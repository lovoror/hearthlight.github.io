> 7,021 acres of national park + 65,165 of preserve) · 1,958,440 recreation visits (2025) · ~245–821 m · NPS region NER (today DOI
> Region 1, North Atlantic–Appalachian)
>
> A deep, forested sandstone canyon carved by a north-flowing river that is older than the mountains it cuts through, spanned by

## Reading the map

The New River runs **north** up the middle of the map, from Hinton at the bottom to Hawks Nest at the top, in a canyon that deepens as it goes. The park and preserve is a long, ragged green corridor following the river for 53 miles, from Bluestone Dam at Hinton to Hawks Nest Lake; it is widest (8 km) around Grandview and Glade Creek in the middle, pinches to the canyon walls elsewhere, and is riddled with private land. The **CSX railroad** (the old Chesapeake & Ohio main line) hugs the river the whole way, on one bank or the other, and the old coal towns sit on narrow benches beside it. Roads mostly stay on the plateau above and only drop into the gorge at a few places, so the park reads as a chain of separate districts, each reached by its own road from US 19, I-64 or WV 20.

At the **north end** (the "Lower Gorge"), the **New River Gorge Bridge** carries US 19 across the canyon, 876 ft (267 m) above the water. The **Canyon Rim Visitor Center** sits on the north rim beside it, with boardwalk stairs down to two overlooks facing the arch. **Fayette Station Road** — the only way across before 1977 — winds one-way down from Lansing to the river, crosses on a low old bridge at the Fayette Station rapids and climbs back up to Fayetteville. Around it: **Long Point** (a rock prow facing the bridge), the **Endless Wall** cliffs along the east rim (Diamond Point), the **Kaymoor** mine site on the west slope (stairs down to the ruins), and **Nuttallburg** on the east bank (a restored coal conveyor and tipple), reached by the narrow Keeneys Creek Road. The rapids here are the big ones: Surprise, Upper and Lower Railroad, the Keeneys, Double Z, Greyhound Bus Stopper, Millers Folly, Fayette Station.

In the **middle**, the river makes a series of great loops. **Cunard** (the rafts' put-in) and **Brooklyn** sit at the river south-east of Fayetteville; **Thurmond**, the ghost railroad town, sits on a shelf between cliff and tracks at the mouth of Dunloup Creek, reached by WV 25 from Glen Jean (park headquarters). Upstream, gravel McKendree Road follows the river past Stone Cliff and Thayer; the loop around Army Camp brings the river to **Prince** and Quinnimont, where WV 41 crosses; high above them on the south rim is **Grandview**, where the Main Overlook looks 1,400 ft (430 m) down onto a horseshoe bend, the deepest part of the gorge. Glade Creek and War Ridge are wild side valleys to the south-east.

At the **south end** the gorge opens into a wider, gentler valley: WV 20 follows the east bank from **Hinton** past Brooks Falls to **Sandstone**, where the green-built **Sandstone Visitor Center** stands near I-64, and **Sandstone Falls** spreads across the whole width of the river, reached by River Road on the west bank and a boardwalk out over the islands. Upstream (off the south edge) lie Bluestone Dam and the Bluestone National Scenic River; north beyond Hawks Nest, the separate Gauley River NRA.

### Water

The **New River** is the park: it rises in North Carolina, crosses Virginia, enters West Virginia and flows **north** (a rarity) through every ridge of the Alleghenies to join the Gauley at Gauley Bridge and form the Kanawha. Geologists count it among the oldest rivers on the continent despite its name. The park and preserve protects **53 miles (85 km)** of free-flowing river from Bluestone Dam at Hinton to Hawks Nest Lake. It has two characters: the **upper (southern) river** is big, wide and gentle — long pools and Class I–III rapids, the wide ledge drops of **Brooks Falls** and **Sandstone Falls** (the widest falls on the river, spread across ~450 m with drops of 3–8 m), islands and flatrock shelves; the **Lower Gorge** below Thurmond narrows between boulders into Class III–V whitewater (Surprise, Upper and Lower Railroad, Upper/Middle/Lower Keeneys, Double Z, Greyhound Bus Stopper, Millers Folly, Fayette Station). Side streams fall steeply off the plateau in rhododendron ravines: Glade Creek (trout), Mill Creek, Dunloup Creek (Thurmond), Arbuckle Creek, Manns Creek, Keeneys Creek, Wolf Creek, Fern Creek, Laurel Creek, Big Branch. The water runs green-brown and clear in low summer flow, chocolate after storms; swimming is discouraged (strong currents, undercut rocks, sudden drop-offs). Hawks Nest Lake (a dam reservoir) closes the north end; the Gauley's famous autumn dam-release season is next door.

### Relief

From ~245 m where the river meets Hawks Nest Lake to 821 m on Backus Mountain, a knob of the preserve east of Grandview (Sullivan Knob near Sandstone reaches 786 m; the Appalachian Plateau rim sits at 550–800 m). The gorge is cut **~300–490 m** deep into flat-lying layers: a hard cap of **Nuttall sandstone** forms a continuous band of vertical cliffs (9–37 m) at the rim — Endless Wall, Bridge Buttress, Junkyard Wall, the Ameless and Head walls below Hawks Nest, Beauty Mountain, Dowdy Bluff near Thayer, Grandview's Castle Rock — and below it steep forested slopes of shale, softer sandstone and **coal seams** (the Sewell seam, where the mine portals are) drop to boulder banks, the railroad bench and the river. It is deepest at Grandview (~430 m below the Main Overlook) and narrowest and most V-shaped in the Lower Gorge; to the south the valley widens with flat bottoms at Sandstone and Hinton. In the game: the rims are flat plateau tiles ending in a one-level **cliff band** of golden sandstone, then 8–15 stepped banks of forest slope with a narrow railroad bench and village shelves near the bottom; the river bed steps down at each rapid (white water) and at Sandstone Falls (a wide stair of ledges); the bridge is a 3D steel arch spanning rim to rim high above everything.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: the river edge has sycamore, river birch and, on scoured sandstone shelves, the rare **Appalachian flatrock** community (sedges, eastern red cedar, Virginia pine); the gorge slopes carry one of the most diverse mixed-hardwood forests in the central Appalachians (tulip poplar, sugar maple, oaks, beech, buckeye), with **hemlock and great rhododendron** crowding the cool ravines and north slopes; the rims and cliff tops are drier: chestnut oak, pitch and Virginia pine, **catawba rhododendron** and mountain laurel on the sandstone; the plateau above is a patchwork of forest, towns and old mine lands.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#C7985A` sunlit Nuttall sandstone cliff · `#8D7C64` shaded, lichened sandstone · `#2E2A27` coal seam and mine portal · `#6F8F4F` summer hardwood canopy · `#35553F` hemlock-and-rhododendron ravine · `#5B7466` green river in low water (`#8A6A4A` after rain) · `#EEF2F0` whitewater foam · `#7E4A2E` weathering-steel bridge (rust brown) · `#8A4B3A` old brick of Thurmond · `#D9A441` October hickory gold.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **black bear** (rims and ravines) · **white-tailed deer** · **river otter** and **beaver** (quiet river edges) · **bobcat** · **Allegheny woodrat** (cliff crevices) · **bald eagle** and **osprey** (over the river) · **peregrine falcon** (restored to the gorge cliffs) · **cerulean warbler** (sky-blue, high in the canopy — the gorge is a stronghold for this declining bird) · **wild turkey** · **smallmouth bass** (famed river fishing), **walleye** and **muskellunge** · fish found only in the New River drainage such as the **bigmouth chub** and **Kanawha minnow** · **timber rattlesnake** and **copperhead** (sunny rocks on the rim) · **eastern box turtle** · bats in abandoned mine portals.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): catawba rhododendron (purple, mid-May at Grandview) · great rhododendron (white-pink, July) · mountain laurel (May–June) · eastern hemlock · eastern red cedar on the flatrock shelves · Virginia pine · tulip poplar · pawpaw (river bottoms, fruit in September) · large-flowered trillium and bloodroot (April ravines) · Christmas fern and other winter greens.
- **Weather & seasons**: spring brings high, cold water and wildflowers; summer is hot, humid and green, with afternoon thunderstorms and morning fog lying in the gorge; autumn is dry and golden (peak colour mid-to-late October, climbing season, Bridge Day); winter is grey and quiet with occasional snow on the rims and ice on the cliffs, the river running high.
- **Night**: the rims are dark enough for good stars away from Fayetteville and Oak Hill (Grandview, Glade Creek, Thayer); the bridge's lights and the headlights crossing it hang in the black gorge; fireflies over the riverside campgrounds in June; owls, whip-poor-wills, bats leaving the mine portals; the headlamp of a night coal train sweeping the canyon walls.
- **Sounds**: the roar of rapids rising from the gorge; freight trains rumbling, whistles echoing up the walls; rafters whooping at Fayette Station; the clink of climbing gear and "on belay!" calls; wind in the hemlocks; the hum of trucks on the bridge; the drip of water from old mine portals; wood thrushes at dusk; the hollow knock of a paddle on a raft.

## Moments

- **Bridge Day** (third Saturday of October; first held 1980): the bridge closes to cars and opens to tens of thousands of walkers; BASE jumpers leap 876 ft to the river and rappellers hang beneath the deck — West Virginia's biggest one-day festival.
- **Rhododendron season at Grandview**: catawba rhododendron blooms purple around mid-May; great rhododendron follows in July.
- **Autumn colour**: mid-to-late October, the gorge walls in gold and red around Bridge Day.
- **Whitewater season**: April–October on the New (big water in spring, warm and technical in summer); next door, the Gauley's dam releases in September–October draw paddlers from everywhere.
- **Climbing seasons**: late April–mid-June and mid-September–late October on the sandstone.
- **Morning fog in the gorge**: summer and autumn mornings, the river hidden under cloud seen from Long Point, Diamond Point and Grandview.
- **Trains through Thurmond**: coal trains day and night; the Amtrak Cardinal three days a week each way, stopping at Thurmond by request.
- **Summer theatre at Grandview**: outdoor dramas in the Cliffside Amphitheatre from June through August.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a tall **1024 × 2048-tile** map with the river as its spine, flowing from the bottom (Hinton, south) to the top (Hawks Nest, north), compressed ~30× along its length (85 km → ~2,000 tiles) and ~10× across (the gorge 1–1.5 km wide → 100–150 tiles, rim to rim). Near true scale (1 tile ≈ 1.5 m): the Canyon Rim VC and boardwalk, Thurmond's one-street town and depot, the Kaymoor and Nuttallburg ruins, the Sandstone Falls boardwalk, the Fayette Station rapid; the **bridge** at ~×2 (924 m → ~300 tiles long, the arch spanning rim to rim) with its height exaggerated relative to the rest; the gorge depth rendered as ~10–15 height levels. Compressed hard: the plateau farmland and towns (a thin rim strip with Fayetteville and Lansing as small hubs), the long gentle upper valley between Hinton and Sandstone, the side valleys. Cut: most private land, the Gauley and Bluestone units (a signpost at each edge).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: hubs at **Canyon Rim** (VC, overlooks, the bridge above), **Fayetteville** (outfitters, the rafting bus), **Thurmond** (depot and Commercial Row, the train stop), **Grandview** (overlooks, amphitheatre, picnic shelters), **Sandstone** (VC, falls). 3D landmarks: the New River Gorge Bridge (rust-brown steel arch and deck trusses, the catwalk under the deck), the low Fayette Station bridge, the Nuttallburg conveyor and tipple, the Kaymoor headhouse and haulage ruins, Thurmond's brick Commercial Row and wooden depot, the Endless Wall cliffs, Turkey Spur's rock knob, Sandstone Falls' wide ledges, coal trains (animated, long). Interiors: Canyon Rim VC, Thurmond Depot (waiting room, ticket office), the Sandstone VC, a mine portal (a short safe tunnel exhibit, not a real mine), a raft outfitter's shed.
- **Activities**: **rafting the Lower Gorge** as the signature activity (a party of 4–8 in a raft, a guide calling paddle commands through named rapids, a swim at a calm pool, the take-out under the bridge); paddling the gentle upper river by canoe or kayak with camps on sandbars; **climbing** the sandstone (Endless Wall, Bridge Buttress) as a rope mini-game with a belay partner; the **Bridge Walk** on the catwalk beneath the deck; watching (or, on Bridge Day, joining) the BASE jumps; driving Fayette Station Road down and up; mountain-biking the Arrowhead loops; exploring ghost towns (Thurmond, Nuttallburg, Kaymoor, Brooklyn) for history plaques; riding the train to Thurmond; smallmouth bass fishing; primitive camping by the river; ranger talks. Photo spots: the bridge from Long Point, from the lower Canyon Rim overlook and from the river at Fayette Station; Diamond Point on Endless Wall; Grandview Main Overlook's horseshoe bend; Turkey Spur; Thurmond's main street with a passing train; the Nuttallburg tipple; Sandstone Falls. Junior ranger ideas: identify the rock layers (sandstone cap, shale, coal) on a cliff, count the cars of a coal train, learn rapid classes I–V, find five Thurmond buildings on an old photo, pick up nothing at the mine ruins.
- **Passport stamps**: the park's visitor centres — **Canyon Rim** and **Sandstone** (year-round), **Grandview** and **Thurmond Depot** (seasonal); the park's own stamp list is not published online.
- **With friends / with the world**: a raft is the perfect party vehicle (everyone paddles, everyone gets wet); Canyon Rim's overlooks and Long Point are the world's natural meeting spots, all facing the bridge; Bridge Day is the ultimate open-world event (a crowd on the deck, jumpers dropping, a festival on the span); Thurmond's depot platform when the train pulls in; climbing partners on Endless Wall; group photo on Turkey Spur's rock.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): never walk or cross the railroad (active CSX line — a hard "no trespassing" boundary); respect the many private properties inside the boundary; wear a life jacket near the river and don't swim in the rapids; leave mine ruins and artifacts untouched (no climbing on walls or conveyors); climbers stay on established routes and respect seasonal closures near nesting peregrines; camp only in the designated primitive sites (none within 100 ft of cliffs or water); store food from bears; pack out everything.
- **Implementation challenges**: a **deep, narrow canyon** (many height levels in a small width, readable from the oblique camera); a **3D arch bridge** hundreds of tiles long and far above the ground (shadows, silhouette, a walkable deck and catwalk); a river that **flows north** with **rapids** (current, standing waves, rafts that follow the flow); animated trains on a rail line through the whole map; roped climbing on vertical cliffs; separate districts linked by long roads (fast travel via the train and the roads); ghost-town ruins half-hidden in dense forest; fog filling the gorge below the rims; Bridge Day crowds and parachutes.
