# Grand Teton National Park — `GRTE`

> Wyoming · established February 26, 1929 · 1,254.7 km² (310,044.36 acres) · 3,800,648 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~1,925–4,197 m · NPS region IMR
>
> A 64 km wall of granite peaks shooting 2 km straight out of a flat sagebrush valley, with a string of glacier-carved lakes at its foot and the Snake River braiding down the middle. In Hearthlight it should feel like a panorama you can walk into: the Tetons are always on the horizon, every turnout frames them differently, and the valley floor is a stage for moose, bison, elk and pronghorn.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a tall shape about 60 km from north to south, hanging below Yellowstone, to which it is tied by the John D. Rockefeller Jr. Memorial Parkway: a 13 km green strip along the Snake River at the top edge of the brochure, with Flagg Ranch in its middle and the unpaved Grassy Lake Road heading west to Idaho. The **west third** of the park is the Teton Range, drawn as grey shaded rock and small white glaciers: a straight wall running north–south whose crest is the park's west boundary (Idaho's Teton Valley and the Caribou-Targhee forest beyond). Its highest knot, the Cathedral Group — Grand Teton (4,197 m), Mount Owen, Teewinot, with Middle and South Teton behind — stands halfway down the park at 43.74° N; Mount Moran (3,842 m), with its glaciers and black dike, rises alone further north above Jackson Lake. Canyons cut east into the range, from south to north: Granite, Open, Death, Avalanche, Garnet, Cascade (the big one, forked), Paintbrush, Leigh, Moran, Snowshoe, Waterfalls, Colter, Webb, Owl and Berry. Each canyon opens onto a lake at the foot of the range.

The **rest of the park** is Jackson Hole, a flat valley of sage drawn in pale green. At the mountains' foot lies a chain of lakes: from north to south, **Jackson Lake** (24 km long, the big blue shape filling the valley's north end, with islands and bays on its west side and a dam at its south-east corner), then **Leigh**, **String**, **Jenny**, **Bradley**, **Taggart** and **Phelps** lakes, each held in by a forested moraine. The **Snake River** leaves Jackson Lake at the dam, loops east round Oxbow Bend, then braids south-south-west down the middle of the valley to Moose and out of the park. East of the river the ground rises gently: **Signal Mountain** (2,355 m) by Jackson Lake's south-east shore, the kettle ponds of the Potholes, the forested **Blacktail Butte**, and the open **Antelope Flats** with Mormon Row. The south-east corner takes in the Gros Ventre River, its campground and the village of **Kelly**; the east boundary follows the foothills (Shadow Mountain, the Mount Leidy highlands in Bridger-Teton forest); the **National Elk Refuge** lies south of the Gros Ventre, and the town of **Jackson** at the bottom edge.

The **roads make a long loop** with two sides. The outer side is the highway, open all year: US 89/191 comes north from Jackson along the east side of the valley, past Gros Ventre Junction and Moose Junction, then 29 km across the sage flats (Glacier View, Teton Point, the Snake River Overlook, Cunningham Cabin, Elk Ranch Flats) to **Moran Junction**. There, US 26/287 leaves east for Dubois, and the highway swings west past **Oxbow Bend** to **Jackson Lake Junction** (8 km), then north as the North Park Road along Jackson Lake's east shore: Jackson Lake Lodge, Colter Bay, Leeks Marina, Lizard Creek, the Parkway and Flagg Ranch, and Yellowstone's South Entrance (26 + 13 km). The inner side is the **Teton Park Road**, which starts at Moose (Craig Thomas centre, entrance station) and runs north-west along the foot of the peaks: Menors Ferry, Windy Point, Taggart Lake trailhead, Teton Glacier Turnout, Lupine Meadows, **South Jenny Lake** (13 km). A one-way scenic drive then passes Jenny Lake's east shore, the Jenny Lake Overlook, the Cathedral Group Turnout and Jenny Lake Lodge to North Jenny Lake Junction (String and Leigh lakes). From there the road runs north-east past the Mount Moran and Potholes turnouts, Signal Mountain Lodge and the Signal Mountain summit road, the Chapel of the Sacred Heart and the dam, to Jackson Lake Junction (19 km). Side roads: the narrow **Moose-Wilson Road** south-west from Moose (Murie Ranch, Death Canyon trailhead, the Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve, the Granite Canyon entrance, then Teton Village), with an unpaved stretch and no trucks, RVs or trailers; Antelope Flats Road to **Mormon Row**; the Gros Ventre Road to Kelly; the 4WD River Road along the Snake's west bench from the Bar BC Ranch to the R.K.O. Road near Signal Mountain; the Pacific Creek and Two Ocean Lake roads. A paved multi-use pathway runs from Jackson to Moose and on to South Jenny Lake.

The **hubs** line the inner road. **Moose** in the south (park headquarters, the Craig Thomas Discovery and Visitor Center, Dornan's, the ferry and the log chapel). **Jenny Lake** in the centre, the busiest place in the park (visitor centre, climbing ranger station, the shuttle-boat dock, store, tent campground, Jenny Lake Lodge up the road). **Signal Mountain Lodge** and its marina on Jackson Lake's south-east shore. **Jackson Lake Lodge** on a bluff over Willow Flats, facing Mount Moran. **Colter Bay Village** on the lake's east shore (visitor centre, marina, cabins, campground). **Flagg Ranch** in the Parkway. The south-west has the quiet Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve on Phelps Lake. The **wild parts** are the range itself (trails climb every canyon to the Teton Crest Trail along the top) and the whole west shore of Jackson Lake, reachable only by boat or on long trails; the north-eastern corner (Pilgrim Creek, Two Ocean and Emma Matilda lakes) is forested and bear-rich.

**Relief** is almost binary. The Teton Fault runs along the range's east foot, so the mountains rise with no foothills, ~2,100 m above the valley in a few kilometres; the valley floor is only gently stepped (flat outwash plains of cobbles and sage, forested moraine ridges round the lakes, kettles, a few buttes). The lowest point, ~1,925 m, is where the Snake River leaves the park south of Moose.

### Water

The **Snake River** comes down from Yellowstone through the Rockefeller Parkway, entering **Jackson Lake** at its north end. It leaves by the **Jackson Lake Dam** at the lake's south-east corner, loops east through **Oxbow Bend**, gathers **Pacific Creek** and **Buffalo Fork** at Moran, then runs south-south-west for ~45 km as a braided river of gravel bars, side channels and cottonwood islands: past Deadmans Bar, Schwabacher Landing and Moose, and out of the park south of Moose (toward the Columbia and the Pacific). The **Gros Ventre River** comes in from the east past Kelly to join it south of the park. **Jackson Lake** is a natural glacial lake raised by the dam (built 1906–1916, rebuilt 1989): 24 km long, 11 km wide, up to 133 m deep, surface ~2,064 m. Its level rises in early summer and is drawn down for irrigation in late summer, uncovering mud flats (in late September 2026 the lake was low and most marinas closed). Islands (Elk, Marie, Grassy, Moose, Arizona) and bays (Colter, Spalding, Moran) line it. The **piedmont lakes** — Leigh, String, Jenny, Bradley, Taggart, Phelps — sit in basins scoured by glaciers at the mouths of the canyons, dammed by moraines. Jenny (~4.8 km², ~129 m deep) is drained south by Cottonwood Creek; String Lake is shallow and warm, a canoe channel between Jenny and Leigh. Above them, cirque lakes: Amphitheater, Surprise, Solitude, Holly, Lake of the Crags, Delta. There are about a dozen small glaciers (Teton, Middle Teton, Schoolroom, Skillet, Falling Ice, Triple, Teepe, Petersen), all shrinking. Waterfalls: Hidden Falls, Ribbon Cascade, Wilderness Falls, Columbine Cascade. Beaver ponds and spring creeks fill the bottoms (Schwabacher, Blacktail Ponds, the Moose-Wilson Road).

### Relief

From ~1,925 m (the Snake leaving the park south of Moose) to 4,197 m (Grand Teton; 13,770 ft on the brochure, 13,775 ft in recent surveys). The **range** is a tilted block lifted along the Teton Fault: its east face is a straight wall with no foothills, rising ~2,100 m in 5–8 km, cut by deep U-shaped canyons with cirques at their heads. Summits over 3,600 m: Grand Teton, Mount Owen (3,940 m), Middle Teton (3,902 m), Mount Moran (3,842 m), South Teton (3,814 m), Teewinot (3,756 m), Thor Peak (3,666 m), Buck Mountain (3,639 m), Nez Perce (3,627 m). The **valley** is nearly flat, stepping down gently from north to south (~2,100 m at Jackson Lake to ~1,950 m at Moose): outwash plains of cobbles under sage, forested **moraine** ridges curling round each lake (Burned Ridge, Timbered Island), the Potholes' kettles, river terraces along the Snake, and isolated hills — **Signal Mountain** (2,355 m), **Blacktail Butte** (2,343 m), Uhl Hill, Lozier Hill.

In game terms: the range is the tallest relief anywhere near a valley in the set — the 0–7 levels should go almost all to the mountains (the valley floor at levels 0–1, moraines and terraces at 1–2 as banks, Signal Mountain and Blacktail Butte at 2–3, the range from 3 to 7 with painted cliffs and 3D peaks on top). 3D peaks: the Cathedral Group as one sculpted cluster (Grand, Owen, Teewinot, Middle and South Teton), Mount Moran with its glaciers and dike, Buck Mountain, Rendezvous Peak with the tram beyond the boundary. Stairs: the Inspiration Point climb, the Amphitheater Lake switchbacks, the Signal Mountain summit road. Cliffs by the water: Jenny Lake's west shore, the canyon mouths.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: valley floor (1,925–2,100 m): big sagebrush and bitterbrush on cobbly outwash, bunchgrasses and spring wildflowers; narrowleaf cottonwood, blue spruce and willow thickets along the Snake and its channels; aspen groves at the edges of the flats. Moraines and lake basins (2,050–2,400 m): lodgepole pine forest, Douglas-fir, Engelmann spruce, huckleberry understory. Canyons (2,300–2,900 m): spruce-fir forest, avalanche paths, flower meadows, boulder fields. Above ~2,900 m: whitebark pine krummholz, then alpine tundra, talus, snowfields and small glaciers on the north faces.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#8E98A6` granite and gneiss grey · `#3B3F46` the diabase dike's black · `#F3F5F8` snowfield · `#CFE3EE` glacier ice · `#A9B08F` sagebrush · `#CFB57C` dry grass gold · `#35523F` lodgepole green · `#E6B83E` aspen gold · `#3E8FA5` Jenny Lake teal · `#5E9BA3` Snake River green-blue · `#B8B1A4` river cobble · `#7B5B44` weathered barn wood.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): moose (Willow Flats, Oxbow Bend, the Moose-Wilson Road, Gros Ventre River; rut September–October), elk (the flats at dawn and dusk; bugling mid-September–mid-October), bison (Antelope Flats and Mormon Row; rut in August), pronghorn (Antelope Flats, May–October), mule deer, grizzly bear (Pilgrim Creek, Oxbow Bend, the north end), black bear (Moose-Wilson Road, berries in August), gray wolf, coyote, red fox, beaver and river otter (Schwabacher, Oxbow), pika and yellow-bellied marmot (talus in the canyons), Uinta ground squirrel, greater sage-grouse (dancing leks in April–May), bald eagle and osprey (the Snake), trumpeter swan (Christian Pond), American white pelican (Oxbow Bend), sandhill crane, Snake River fine-spotted cutthroat trout.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): arrowleaf balsamroot (sheets of yellow in late May–June), silky lupine, scarlet gilia and Indian paintbrush (July; paintbrush is Wyoming's flower), glacier lily at the snow's edge, alpine forget-me-not on the crest, fireweed in the burns, huckleberry (bears' August food), big sagebrush (the valley's smell after rain), quaking aspen (gold in late September), whitebark pine, narrowleaf cottonwood.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter (November–April) buries the valley; the Teton Park Road becomes a ski track, moose browse the willows, elk move south to the refuge, clear frigid days between storms. Spring (May–June) is muddy, green flats, balsamroot, sage-grouse dancing, bears waking, high water. Summer (July–August): warm days, afternoon thunderstorms over the range, wildflowers in the canyons, wildfire smoke some years. Autumn (September–October): golden aspens and red hawthorns, bugling elk, first snow dusting the peaks, the pronghorn leaving.
- **Night**: dark skies over the flats (Mormon Row and the Snake River Overlook have no light at all), the Milky Way standing over the Grand, moonlight on the snowfields; elk bugling and owls in autumn nights.
- **Sounds**: elk bugles, wind in the aspens, the Jenny Lake boat's motor, Hidden Falls, pikas' squeaks in the talus, sandhill cranes' rattle over the flats, the sage-grouse's popping, the Snake's riffles, thunder rolling off the range, climbers' gear clinking on the Lower Saddle.

## Moments

- **Sunrise on the Tetons**: the range faces east, so dawn lights the peaks pink while the valley is still blue (Mormon Row, Schwabacher Landing, Oxbow Bend, the Snake River Overlook); at sunset the range stands in silhouette.
- **The elk rut**: mid-September to mid-October, bugling at dawn and dusk across the flats (Timbered Island, Willow Flats, the Gros Ventre).
- **The pronghorn migration**: they arrive in May and leave in October–November along the ~240 km « Path of the Pronghorn » from the Upper Green River basin, one of the longest land migrations in the lower 48.
- **Sage-grouse leks**: April–May, at first light on the flats near the airport and Antelope Flats, the males puff and pop.
- **Balsamroot bloom**: late May–June the sage flats and Signal Mountain turn yellow.
- **Aspen gold**: late September to early October (Oxbow Bend, Signal Mountain, the Moose-Wilson Road).
- **Moose season**: autumn rut in September–October; winter moose in the willows along the Snake and the Gros Ventre.
- **Jackson Lake's drawdown**: full and blue in June–July; low in late summer, with mud flats (and most marinas closed by late September 2026).
- **Winter**: from November 1 to April 30 the Teton Park Road is closed to cars and becomes a ski and snowshoe route; at Flagg Ranch the plowed highway ends and guided snowcoaches and snowmobiles carry on into Yellowstone.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: 768 × 1280 tiles (24 × 40 chunks), portrait, north up, with the Parkway as a short top strip toward Yellowstone (so the two maps can join later on the road-trip). Across: ~250 tiles of range (crest to foot), ~430 of valley, ~90 of eastern hills. Hubs at 1 tile ≈ 3–4 m: Jenny Lake's hub (boat dock, Hidden Falls, Inspiration Point, 1.6 km of trail and a 1.5 km-wide lake) becomes ~350 × 250 tiles, with the crossing a ~40 s boat ride; Moose, Mormon Row, Oxbow Bend, Signal Mountain and Colter Bay each ~150–250 tiles. Roads between hubs compressed ~12–15× (Moose → Jenny Lake ~40 s by car, Jenny Lake → Jackson Lake Junction ~60 s). Jackson Lake and its forested east shore compressed ~20× (the lake ~300 × 450 tiles), the north end and the Parkway ~30×. The canyons become short dead-end valleys (150–250 tiles) climbing into the range, with Cascade Canyon the one developed in full. Cut: the town of Jackson and the Elk Refuge (edge scenery), the Gros Ventre Slide (outside), most of the northern range (views only).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Jenny Lake (the plaza: the visitor centre's lawn, the boat dock, a lakeside path), Moose, Colter Bay, Jackson Lake Lodge, Signal Mountain, Mormon Row. 3D landmarks: the Cathedral Group and Mount Moran as sculpted peaks (the silhouette must read from every turnout), the T. A. Moulton barn and the other Mormon Row barns, the Chapel of the Transfiguration (window framing the Grand), Menors Ferry with its cable, Cunningham Cabin, Jackson Lake Dam, the Chapel of the Sacred Heart, the Signal Mountain summit road, the Jenny Lake shuttle boats. Interiors: Jackson Lake Lodge's lobby with its huge window, the Craig Thomas centre's glass wall and relief model, the Jenny Lake Lodge cabins, the Menors store.
- **Activities**: hiking (the lake loops, Cascade Canyon, Amphitheater Lake, the crest for experts); photo spots — the Moulton barn at sunrise, Schwabacher's reflection, Oxbow Bend with Mount Moran, the Snake River Overlook (the Ansel Adams frame), the chapel window, Inspiration Point, the Cathedral Group Turnout; wildlife spotting at dawn and dusk on the flats (a binocular count: moose at Oxbow, bison on Mormon Row, pronghorn on Antelope Flats); ranger programmes (Jenny Lake geology walks, Colter Bay evening talks, Menors Ferry history, a climbing ranger's talk at the Jenny Lake ranger station); Junior Ranger ideas for the game's Young Ranger: name the Cathedral Group's peaks from the turnout, count the lakes in the moraine chain, sketch a pronghorn, ride Menors Ferry; water: the Jenny Lake shuttle boat, canoes on String and Leigh (portage between them), a Jackson Lake cruise to Elk Island from Colter Bay, a Snake River float from Deadmans Bar to Moose, swimming at String Lake; vehicles: car, bike on the pathway and the Teton Park Road, horse rides from Colter Bay and Jackson Lake Lodge; winter: skiing the closed Teton Park Road, snowshoe walks from Taggart Lake; climbing: a guided climb of the Grand as the park's big challenge.
- **Passport stamps**: Craig Thomas Discovery & Visitor Center, Jenny Lake Visitor Center, Jenny Lake Ranger Station, Colter Bay Visitor Center, Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve Center, Flagg Ranch Information Station (the Rockefeller Parkway's stamp), and the interagency Jackson Hole and Greater Yellowstone Visitor Center in Jackson (to confirm on the Passport stamp finder).
- **With friends / with the world**: Jenny Lake's boat dock is where strangers naturally meet (the shuttle queue, then the climb to Inspiration Point together); Oxbow Bend and Schwabacher Landing at sunrise gather photographers; the Mormon Row barn is the obvious group-photo spot. Party ideas: a canoe convoy String → Leigh with the portage carried together; a shared elk count at dusk; a roped party of 2–4 on the Grand, each carrying part of the kit; a float trip where one steers and the others spot wildlife.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): 100 yd / 91 m from bears and wolves, 25 yd / 23 m from moose, elk and bison (moose are the most likely to charge); stop only in turnouts; hike in groups and carry bear spray; food in bear boxes at camp; stay on trails in the fragile alpine; no pets on trails; boats need an aquatic-invasive inspection; quiet near wildlife at dawn.
- **Implementation challenges**: the range's vertical front (a wall of 3D peaks and painted cliffs rising straight from flat ground, readable from a north-facing camera even though the peaks lie west of the valley); glaciers and snowfields; a braided river with gravel bars and side channels, and a lake whose level changes with the season (mud flats in late summer); the shuttle boat as a scheduled vehicle; reflections on still water at dawn (Oxbow, Schwabacher, String Lake); great herds on open flats; the pronghorn migration as a seasonal flow across the map; winter mode (the road turned ski track); the Parkway as a seam with Yellowstone.
