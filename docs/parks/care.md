## Reading the map

The brochure map is a tall strip that follows the **Waterpocket Fold**, a line of tilted rock running almost due north– south for ~100 miles. Its west edge is a row of cliffs, domes and canyons; its east side slopes down into long strike valleys and grey badlands. The park is widest in the **north** (the Cathedral district), narrows to 10–15 km in the middle, and ends in a thin tail in the **south** along Halls Creek, pointing at Lake Powell.

The only paved road across is **UT-24**, which crosses the map from west to east about a third of the way down, following the **Fremont River** through the fold. From Torrey on the west it passes the west-entrance pullouts, Twin Rocks, Chimney Rock, Panorama Point (stargazing, with the Goosenecks of Sulphur Creek and Sunset Point just south), the **visitor centre** and the green valley of **Fruita**, then continues east through the fold past the Fremont petroglyphs, the Hickman Bridge trailhead under Capitol Dome, Behunin Cabin and the mouth of Grand Wash, leaves the park at an east orientation pullout and runs on to the River Ford, Caineville and Hanksville. From Fruita the paved **Scenic Drive** runs south along the foot of the cliffs for ~8 miles, with dirt spurs into **Grand Wash** (1.3 mi) and **Capitol Gorge** (2.4 mi) and a rougher road to **Pleasant Creek**; the Fruita inset shows the orchards spread along the river between the visitor centre and the campground.

The **Cathedral (north) district** is a loop of dirt roads north of UT-24: the Hartnet Road starts 11.7 miles east of the visitor centre with a **ford** across the Fremont River and climbs north-west past the Bentonite Hills and the Lower and Upper South Desert overlooks to Hartnet Junction near Thousand Lake Mountain; the Cathedral Road comes back south-east past Upper Cathedral Valley (campground), the Gypsum Sinkhole and the **Temples of the Sun and Moon** and Glass Mountain, down Caineville Wash to UT-24 near Caineville (57.6 miles in all). The **Waterpocket (south) district** is reached by the Notom–Bullfrog Road, which leaves UT-24 east of the fold and runs south along its east side (Notom, Burro and Cottonwood washes, Sheets Gulch, Cedar Mesa campground) to the junction with the **Burr Trail Road**; the Burr Trail climbs west up the fold's face by the Burr Trail Switchbacks toward Boulder, while the road south continues past Strike Valley and Muley Twist Canyon, the Post Corral and the Halls Creek Overlook to Bullfrog Marina. Driving UT-24, the Notom–Bullfrog and Burr Trail roads and UT-12 over Boulder Mountain makes the 124-mile "Loop-the-Fold".

### Water

The **Fremont River** comes from Fish Lake and the high plateaus to the west, passes Torrey, and crosses the fold from west to east: it waters Fruita (where Sulphur Creek joins it at the visitor centre), cuts the Fremont Gorge, runs past Hickman Bridge and Behunin Cabin, and leaves the park toward Hanksville, where it becomes the Dirty Devil River on its way to Lake Powell. It is the reason Fruita exists: pioneers dug gravity-fed ditches in the 1880s, and the park still flood-irrigates the orchards through them. East of the park there is **no bridge** on the road to Cathedral Valley — the Fremont is forded (usually a foot deep or less; closed when high). **Sulphur Creek** meanders in the Goosenecks below Panorama Point; **Pleasant Creek** and **Oak Creek** are perennial streams that cut through the fold farther south; **Halls Creek** runs south along the fold's east side to Lake Powell, entering deep narrows near the park's tail.

The fold's name comes from its **waterpockets** — potholes in the sandstone, from puddles to deep "tanks" (the Tanks in Capitol Gorge, Muley Tanks, Cottonwood and Willow tanks) that hold rainwater for weeks. Summer storms cause **flash floods** in Grand Wash, Capitol Gorge and the slot canyons (Burro Wash, Cottonwood Wash, Sheets Gulch), and wash out the dirt roads. Up north, **Glass Mountain** is a mound of gypsum crystals and the **Gypsum Sinkhole** is a deep collapse where buried gypsum dissolved.

### Relief

The park runs from 1,182 m at Halls Creek on its southern tip to ~2,700 m where its north-west corner climbs onto the flank of Thousand Lake Mountain. Its backbone is a **monocline**: rock layers that lie flat to the west and to the east but bend sharply down between them along a north–south line, as if the land had been folded like a rug; erosion of the bent edge left a ~100-mile wall of cliffs and domes (the "reef"). From west to east you cross: the **cliffs** (chocolate- brown banded Moenkopi at the base, purple-grey Chinle slopes, red Wingate walls, then Kayenta ledges), the **white domes** of Navajo Sandstone on top (Capitol Dome, the Golden Throne at 2,146 m, Navajo Knobs 2,127 m), and on the east side tilted slabs and **strike valleys** (Strike Valley, Halls Creek) running parallel to the fold. Canyons cut across it (the Fremont, Grand Wash, Capitol Gorge, Pleasant Creek, Muley Twist). Fruita lies at ~1,650 m in the valley floor. In the north, softer layers have eroded into the **Middle and South deserts**: badlands and freestanding Entrada monoliths (Cathedral Valley). The skyline has two great mountains: Boulder Mountain/Aquarius Plateau (~3,400 m) to the west and the Henry Mountains (Mount Ellen, ~3,500 m) to the east.

In the game: the fold as a long ridge whose layers **tilt** (a sloped stratigraphy painted on cliff faces and on the tilted slabs of the east side), cut by narrow canyons (Grand Wash: a few-tile slot between tall walls); the white domes as rounded 3D caps; badlands as soft, banded mounds (banks, not cliffs); the Burr Trail switchbacks as a zigzag road up a cliff; 3D landmarks for Capitol Dome, Hickman Bridge, Cassidy Arch, Chimney Rock, Twin Rocks, the Castle, the Temples of the Sun and Moon, Glass Mountain.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: river bottoms (1,550–1,700 m) — Fremont cottonwood, box elder, willows, pasture grass and the historic orchards (apricot, cherry, peach, pear, apple, plum, mulberry, almond, walnut). Slickrock and benches (1,500–2,150 m) — pinyon pine and Utah juniper, cliffrose, Mormon tea, buffaloberry, rabbitbrush, single-leaf ash; hanging gardens at seeps. Badlands — nearly bare, a few saltbush and shadscale on the grey clay. North-west corner (2,100–2,700 m) — ponderosa pine and aspen coming down from Thousand Lake Mountain.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#8C4A36` chocolate-brown Moenkopi (Fruita's cliff base) · `#C0582E` red Wingate walls · `#EDE3D1` white Navajo domes · `#8C7A8E` purple-grey Chinle and bentonite badlands · `#9AA7B0` blue-grey Mancos hills · `#D38A62` salmon Entrada monoliths (Cathedral Valley) · `#3C302A` desert varnish · `#6FA048` orchard and pasture green (`#F4D6E0` blossom in spring) · `#E4B640` cottonwood gold in fall · `#8A7650` silty Fremont River.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): mule deer grazing in the orchards at dusk; desert bighorn sheep (reintroduced) on the cliffs along UT-24 and Capitol Gorge; yellow-bellied marmots and rock squirrels around Fruita; ringtails; beavers on the Fremont; coyote, gray fox and mountain lion (rarely seen); bats over the river at dusk; chukar on the talus (introduced); wild turkeys in the orchards; golden eagles and peregrine falcons over the cliffs; ravens; canyon wrens; collared and side-blotched lizards; Great Basin rattlesnake; red-spotted toads in waterpockets after rain.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): the orchards' blossoms (apricots from early March, peaches, pears and apples to early May) and fruit (below); Wright fishhook cactus (a federally listed cactus found only in this region, pink flowers in spring); Winkler cactus; sego lily (May–June); globemallow and claret cup (spring); cliffrose (spring); rubber rabbitbrush (yellow, September); Fremont cottonwood (gold in late October).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — windy, orchards in blossom, busy; summer — hot (32–38 °C), July–September storms with flash floods and red waterfalls, roads washed out; fall — mild, harvest time, cottonwoods gold; winter — quiet and cold, light snow on the domes and the Henry Mountains white, ice in the canyons.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park (2015): astronomy programmes with telescopes several nights a week in summer, the **Heritage Starfest** each September; the Milky Way over Panorama Point, the Goosenecks and the Temples of Cathedral Valley (light painting is not allowed).
- **Sounds**: the Fremont River; irrigation water gurgling down the orchard ditches; deer crunching fallen apples; the canyon wren's falling song; chukar calling from the talus; ravens; a screen door and the bell at the Gifford House; wind in the cottonwoods; gravel under tyres on the dirt roads; thunder and the roar of a flash flood.

## Moments

- **Orchard blossom** — early March to early May: apricots first (early March–mid April), then peaches, pears and apples; pink and white trees under red cliffs.
- **U-pick harvest** — cherries first (June), apricots late June–mid July, peaches late July–early September, pears early August–early September, apples mid August–mid October; "U-Pick Fruit" signs go up, hand-pickers and ladders are provided, and you weigh and pay for fruit you take away (eating in the orchard is free). Dates shift by weeks with the weather; the park runs a fruit hotline.
- **Pies at the Gifford House** — spring through fall, fresh-baked daily (and gone fast).
- **Heritage Starfest** — usually in September: telescopes and night-sky talks in a certified dark sky.
- **Sunset at Sunset Point and Panorama Point** — the fold's cliffs turn red, then purple.
- **Sunrise in Cathedral Valley** — the Temples of the Sun and Moon light up first.
- **Flash floods** — July–September storms send torrents down Grand Wash and Capitol Gorge (the roads close).
- **Fall colours** — cottonwoods along the Fremont and the orchard leaves turn gold in late October.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a tall 512 × 1024-tile map following the fold, true north up. The centre (rows ~300–650) holds Fruita, the UT-24 corridor and the Scenic Drive at ~1:4–1:6: Fruita's valley (~2 km of orchards, schoolhouse, Gifford House, campground) becomes ~350 tiles wide, with Hickman Bridge, Grand Wash, Cassidy Arch and Capitol Gorge close by. The Cathedral district (north, ~40 × 30 km) is compressed ~1:30 into a loop of dirt road with its landmarks; the Waterpocket district (south, ~60 km) ~1:50 into a strip with the Burr Trail switchbacks, Strike Valley, Muley Twist and Halls Creek. Torrey and Boulder Mountain form the west edge, Caineville, Notom and the Henry Mountains skyline the east.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the visitor centre; **Fruita** as the park's village — the Gifford House (interior: pioneer kitchen and a shop selling pies), the Fruita Schoolhouse (interior: one-room school), the blacksmith shop, the Ripple Rock Nature Center, the orchards with ditches and ladders, the campground and amphitheatre, the petroglyph boardwalk; Behunin Cabin; the Cathedral Valley and Cedar Mesa campgrounds; Morrell Cabin. 3D landmarks: Capitol Dome, Hickman Bridge, Cassidy Arch, Chimney Rock, Twin Rocks, the Castle, the Golden Throne, the Temples of the Sun and Moon, Glass Mountain, the Burr Trail switchbacks, the Henry Mountains and Boulder Mountain on the horizon.
- **Activities**: **fruit picking** — a seasonal orchard loop: blossoms in spring, ripe fruit only in orchards with a U-pick sign, a picker pole and ladder, the self-pay scale; buying a pie; historic-building visits; hiking (table); the **Cathedral Valley loop** with its river ford and the **Loop-the-Fold** by car; ranger archaeology talks and evening programmes; astronomy nights and the Heritage Starfest; Junior Ranger booklet (with translations in 13 languages); canyoneering, climbing and bouldering by permit; biking the paved roads. Photo spots: Capitol Dome from the Hickman Bridge trailhead, Hickman Bridge, the orchards in blossom under the cliffs, the schoolhouse, the Gifford barn, Chimney Rock, Panorama Point and the Goosenecks, Sunset Point, Cassidy Arch from above, the Pioneer Register, the Tanks, the Temples of the Sun and Moon at sunrise, Upper Cathedral Valley, the Burr Trail switchbacks, Strike Valley.
- **Passport stamps**: Capitol Reef Visitor Center (bookstore of the Capitol Reef Natural History Association).
- **With friends / with the world**: a harvest party in the orchard (one holds the ladder, one picks, one carries the basket to the scale); pie picnics on the Gifford House lawn; a convoy on the Cathedral Valley loop, crossing the ford together; star parties at the amphitheatre. Open-world gathering spots: the orchards and campground at Fruita, the Gifford House porch, the petroglyph boardwalk, Panorama Point after dark.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): pick only ripe fruit, only in orchards with "U-Pick Fruit" signs; pay for fruit you carry out; never climb the historic trees or hang hammocks on them; view the Fremont petroglyphs and the Pioneer Register without touching — adding a new name is vandalism; stay out of narrow canyons when storms threaten; don't cross the ford at high water; no ATVs or off-road driving; don't feed the deer and marmots; camp only in campgrounds or with a permit; pets only in developed areas and orchards.
- **Implementation challenges**: a 100-km strip that is mostly remote dirt road around one dense hub; tilted rock layers (a monocline) rather than flat plateaus; soft banded badlands; a river ford for vehicles; fruit trees with seasonal states (bare → blossom → fruit → harvest) and picking interactions; narrow slot canyons with flash floods; rock-art and inscription decals.
