# Dry Tortugas National Park — `DRTO`

> Florida · established October 26, 1992 (Fort Jefferson National Monument January 4, 1935) · 261.8 km² (64,701.22 acres) ·
> 89,355 recreation visits (2025) · 0–3 m (the fort's walls ~14 m) · NPS region SER (Southeast)
>
> Seven low sand-and-coral keys ~70 mi / 113 km west of Key West, reached only by boat or seaplane, where a gigantic unfinished
> brick fort sits in a turquoise lagoon ringed by reefs. In Hearthlight it should feel like a sea voyage to the edge of the
> map: the ferry ride out, a day of snorkelling around the moat wall, the roar of 80,000 terns next door, and — for the few who
> camp — the Milky Way over the fort when the boats have gone.

## Reading the map

The park is an **irregular octagon of yellow boundary buoys** ~20 km wide, with a squared-off south-west corner: the south edge runs along 24°34′ N from 82°58′ W to 82°54′ W, the west edge up 82°58′ W to 24°39′ N, a long diagonal climbs north-east along the **Northwest Channel** to the straight north edge at 24°43.5′ N, and the east side angles back south-west from 82°46′ W past the **Southeast Channel**. Outside it lie the Tortugas Ecological Reserve (north-west) and the Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary (east and south). A dashed line cuts the park in two: west of 82°51′ W and north of 24°36′ N is the **Research Natural Area** (about half the park; no fishing, no anchoring), except a one-nautical-mile circle around the Garden Key harbour light.

The **keys make a rough west–east line across the middle**. In the west, the long, thin **Loggerhead Key** with its lighthouse sits on the curving **Loggerhead Reef**, which bends north past White Shoal and Brilliant Shoal to **Texas Rock**; the **Windjammer wreck** lies on the reef ~1.6 km south-west of the island. Five km east, at the centre of the map, **Garden Key** is almost entirely covered by the hexagonal **Fort Jefferson**; immediately east of it is **Bush Key** (sometimes joined to Garden Key by a sand bar) and, south of Bush Key's east end, the sliver of **Long Key**. North-east of the fort lie **Middle Ground** (a daybeacon on a shoal) and **Hospital Key** (~2.5 km away); then, 2.5 km apart in a row to the east, **Middle Key** and **East Key**. **Pulaski Shoal** with its light and "good scuba diving" marks the north-east corner, and **Northkey Harbor** is a sheltered anchorage basin north of the eastern keys. Hospital, Long, Middle and East keys are closed all year; Bush Key closes during tern nesting.

The **inset** zooms on Garden Key: the fort with its moat and the harbour light on the east bastion; Bush Key across a narrow channel with a shoal; channel daymarks (red triangles, green squares) leading into **Bird Key anchorage** south-west of the fort (over Bird Key Bank, where Bird Key washed away in the 1930s) and the **Tortugas anchorage** between the fort, Bush Key and Long Key; the **Brick wreck** with "good snorkeling"; and two outlined special protection zones south-east of Long Key (nurse sharks, closed Jun–Oct, and corals, always closed). There is no road anywhere: routes are the ferry and seaplane approach from Key West (off the map, 70 mi east), the marked channels, and short paths on Garden and Loggerhead keys.

### Water

The Dry Tortugas are an atoll-like ring of reefs and shoals around a central basin: sandy flats 1–3 m deep hug the keys, the harbour and channels are 5–10 m (deep enough, in the 1800s, for a battle fleet), and the basin and outer channels deepen to 20 m and more. The water is exceptionally clear (visibility often 15–30 m) because the park is far from land and swept by the easterly current passing just south; that current and the tides make **strong flows between the keys**, especially on the Loggerhead crossing. Tides are small (a few decimetres). There is **no fresh water at all** — hence "Dry": the fort stored rain in cisterns under its walls. The keys are made of sand and coral rubble and **change shape**: Bush Key is joined to Garden Key by a sand bar at times and cut off at others, Middle Key disappears for weeks or months, and former keys (Bird, North, Northeast, Southwest) have washed away. Winter fronts (Dec–Mar) pile up big seas; summer is calm between squalls; hurricanes (Ian in 2022) rearrange beaches and wreck docks.

### Relief

Almost none: the highest natural ground is ~3 m (10 ft) on Loggerhead Key; the other keys are 0–2 m. All the height is built: **Fort Jefferson** rises ~14 m from the moat in three levels — two tiers of arched gun rooms (casemates) and the open **terreplein** on top — with six walls (two of ~325 ft and four of ~477 ft) joined by corner **bastions** that hold granite spiral stairs; the **harbour light** stands on a bastion; the **Loggerhead lighthouse** is a 157-ft (48 m) brick tower. For the game: sea, one sand level and one low bank level; the fort as a stack of three walkable floors with stairs in the bastions (the oblique camera needs cut-away walls); the **moat** as a water ring 1 level below the seawall walkway. Underwater relief (reef ridges, sand channels, the moat wall's base, wrecks) matters more than land relief.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation** (by salt and sand): turtle-grass beds and coral heads under water → bare white sand and coral rubble at the tide line → low beach strand of sea purslane, railroad vine (beach morning glory), sea lavender and bay cedar (where noddies nest) → coconut palms, buttonwood and a few shade trees; inside the fort, a mown lawn parade ground with palms and buttonwood.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#B5653E` sun-lit brick · `#7E432A` shaded brick in the casemates · `#D9CBB3` lime mortar and cut stone · `#F3EEDD` white coral sand · `#7FE0D6` aquamarine shallows · `#3FB5C9` turquoise channels · `#1F4F8F` deep Gulf blue · `#5FA7A0` reef patches seen from above · `#8FAF5A` parade-ground grass · `#2A2A2A` iron (harbour light, cannon) · `#E8E1CF` Loggerhead lighthouse's white base.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **sooty tern** (Bush Key, up to ~80,000–100,000, ≈ Feb–Sep) · **brown noddy** (nests in bay cedar, same season) · **magnificent frigatebird** (soars over the fort; nests on Long Key — the only colony in the continental US) · **masked booby** (Hospital Key) and brown booby · roseate tern, brown pelican, double-crested cormorant · **spring migrants** (warblers, tanagers, buntings, cuckoos) resting in the fort's trees in April, with peregrine falcons hunting them · **green and loggerhead sea turtles** (feeding in the basin; nesting on the beaches in summer), hawksbill · **goliath grouper**, tarpon and great barracuda (coaling-dock pilings) · **nurse shark** (moat wall; own protection zone) · reef squid, octopus and basket stars (night) · parrotfish, hogfish, sergeant majors · spiny lobster · invasive lionfish.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): **coconut palm** · **bay cedar** (the noddies' nest shrub) · **sea lavender** · **railroad vine**, the purple beach morning glory (summer flowers) · **sea purslane** · **buttonwood** · **geiger tree** (orange flowers, Loggerhead Key) · **turtle grass** (the turtles' pasture) · prickly-pear cactus on the dry keys.
- **Weather & seasons**: *Winter* — trade-wind days broken by cold fronts: 20–30-knot northerlies, spray over the moat wall, ferries sometimes cancelled. *Spring* — calm, clear, the migration and the terns' return. *Summer* — hot and still, glassy water, far-off squalls, turtle nesting. *Hurricane season* (Jun–Nov) — storms that erase beaches and join or split keys. Rain is scarce all year.
- **Night**: among the darkest skies in the eastern US — the Milky Way arches over the hexagon; the harbour light's dark lantern stands against the stars; from mid-January the sooty terns wheel noisily overhead at night; sea turtles crawl up the beaches in summer; snorkellers' lights glow along the moat wall.
- **Sounds**: the non-stop screeching of the tern colony across the channel, surf on coral rubble, wind whistling through the casemate embrasures and footsteps echoing under the arches, flags snapping, the ferry's horn and the seaplane's engine, halyards in the anchorage, the hiss of a snorkel.

## Moments

- **The daily tide of people** — the ferry arrives ~10:15–10:30 am and leaves ~3 pm; seaplanes land and take off beside the fort; after 3 pm only campers and anchored boats remain.
- **Tern season (≈ Feb–Sep; night fly-overs from mid-January)** — up to ~80,000 sooty terns and ~4,500 brown noddies nest on Bush Key; watch from the fort's walls with binoculars.
- **Spring migration (April–early May)** — exhausted songbirds pour into the fort's trees and water; peregrines hunt them; a world-class birding week.
- **Turtle nights (summer)** — loggerheads and greens nest on the keys' beaches; hatchlings run for the sea weeks later.
- **Night snorkel on the moat wall** — octopus and basket stars come out; fish sleep in the crevices.
- **Sunset from the terreplein** — the hexagon glows orange, Loggerhead's lighthouse on the western horizon.
- **The Milky Way over the fort** — new-moon nights, especially in summer.
- **Winter fronts (Dec–Mar)** — seas burst over the seawall; the boats may not come.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: **896 × 640 tiles**. Garden Key near true scale (~1.25 m per tile): the fort ≈ 200 × 200 tiles including the moat, the whole Garden–Bush–Long cluster ≈ 400 × 250 tiles in the centre-east of the map. **Loggerhead Key** at the west edge, shrunk ~4× (≈ 300 × 80 tiles) and brought ~450 tiles from the fort (≈ 11:1 for the crossing). Hospital, Middle and East keys as small closed sand spots 150–250 tiles apart to the east; reefs and wrecks as underwater set pieces. The **Key West crossing** becomes a ferry cut-scene or a short sailing mini-map (the Marquesas passing by) that ends at the Garden Key dock. Cut: most of the open basin, the far north-east shoals.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: **Garden Key** — the only hub: the dock (ferry, seaplane beach, dinghy beach), the visitor centre in the casemates, the campground with its wheeled gear carts. The **Yankee Freedom** ferry as a moving hub (deck, snack bar, rinse shower). 3D landmarks: **Fort Jefferson** (hexagon, bastions, two casemate tiers, terreplein with cannon, moat and seawall, the sally-port bridge), the iron **harbour light**, the **Loggerhead lighthouse**, the coaling-dock pilings, the Windjammer wreck. Interiors: casemate galleries (the park's best "corridors of arches"), the powder magazines, Dr. Samuel Mudd's cell above the sally port, the hot-shot furnace.
- **Activities**:
  - *Hiking*: the fort tour (a slow loop up through three levels) and the moat-wall walk; Bush Key's beach in the off-season; Loggerhead's paths.
  - *Photo spots*: arches repeating into perspective in the casemates; the hexagon from a bastion corner; the seawall with the sea on one side and the moat on the other; the tern cloud over Bush Key; the harbour light at sunset; Loggerhead's lighthouse from the water; a turtle under the ferry dock; the Milky Way over the walls.
  - *Wildlife & rangers*: a tern count with binoculars, a migration checklist in April, turtle-nest night patrol (from a distance), ranger fort talks.
  - *Junior Ranger*: the park's handbook plus the **Underwater Explorer** badge. Game tasks: count the fort's sides, tiers and bastions; spot where the brick colour changes (bricks came from different sources over the 30 years of building); explain why "Dry" and why "Tortugas"; identify five fish on the moat wall; learn the Bush Key closure and why.
  - *Snorkelling*: the signature activity (less than 1% of the park is land). Start from the swim beach; follow the **moat wall** round the fort; dive the **coaling-dock pilings**; swim to the **Brick wreck** in Bird Key Harbor; by boat, snorkel **Little Africa** off Loggerhead Key and the **Windjammer** wreck. Outside the swim area, a dive flag is required; night snorkelling needs a light.
  - *Boating*: the long ferry crossing as the park's opening; seaplane arrival as a premium alternative; a private boat for the Loggerhead run (mooring buoys only in the RNA, anchor only within 1 nmi of the harbour light, dock only 2 h by day).
  - *Paddling*: kayaks brought on the ferry: a lap around Bush and Long keys, or the exposed 5-km crossing to Loggerhead (currents, wind; required PFD, whistle, VHF).
  - *Camping*: 8 primitive sites + overflow on Garden Key; bring everything (2 gallons of water per person per day); charcoal grills only; composting toilets closed while the ferry is in.
- **Passport stamps**: Dry Tortugas · Garden Key · Key West (the NPS lists the Florida Keys Eco-Discovery Center as a DRTO visitor centre) · and an Underground Railroad **Network to Freedom** stamp for Dry Tortugas.
- **With friends / with the world**: the ferry deck as the party's lobby (dolphin and flying-fish spotting on the way out); a group photo on the terreplein; a snorkel "chain" around the moat wall where each player must spot a different creature; a night-snorkel party with lights; a camp-out after the ferry leaves. In the shared world, strangers gather at the **dock at 10:30** when the ferry lands, on the **terreplein at sunset**, and at the campground's picnic tables after dark.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace as gameplay): keep off Bush Key during nesting and 100 ft / 30 m from closed keys; never touch coral or stand on it; take nothing — shells, coral, bricks, artifacts; no swimming in the moat; stay off crumbling walls (loose bricks, unprotected edges); dive flag outside the swim area; no fishing or anchoring in the RNA; give turtles and nests a wide berth at night; pack out every scrap (there is no trash service); fires only in the campsite grills; carry all your water.
- **Implementation challenges**: the **fort** — a large multi-level building with arches, stairs in bastions and a moat, that must read from an oblique camera (cut-away walls, roof on/off); an **underwater layer**; sand keys that **change with season and storms** (Bush Key's land bridge); **seasonal closures** that move the playable area; a huge **bird colony** as a sound-and-particle system; a **long sea crossing** that must feel like a voyage without being boring; night snorkelling with light cones.
