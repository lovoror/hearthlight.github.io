# Lake Clark National Park & Preserve — `LACL`

> Alaska · established December 2, 1980 (national monument since December 1, 1978) · 10,602.0 km² (2,619,816 acres) of park, 16,309 km² (4,030,006 acres) with the preserve · 19,778 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 0–3,108 m · NPS region AKR (Alaska)
>
> A roadless land between Cook Inlet and Bristol Bay where two steaming volcanoes rise straight from the sea, turquoise lakes feed the world's greatest red-salmon run, the Dena'ina have lived for a thousand years, and one man built a log cabin by hand. In Hearthlight it should feel like a string of bush-plane landings: a gravel airstrip village on a 50-mile lake, a quiet cabin on a mountain lake, and a beach where bears graze like cattle under a volcano.

## Reading the map

The brochure map is almost square, north up. The park is a tall, jagged green shape on the west side of **Cook Inlet**, whose blue water fills the right third of the map, with the Kenai Peninsula (Kenai, Soldotna, Kasilof, Ninilchik, Anchor Point, Homer and the Sterling Highway) on its far shore. The park's eastern half is mountains: the **Alaska Range** in the north (Neacola Mountains, Mount Spurr and Mount Torbert just outside), the **Chigmit Mountains** in the middle, and the **Aleutian Range** in the south — with two white-capped volcanoes near the coast: **Redoubt Volcano** (10,197 ft / 3,108 m, Dena'ina Bentuggezh K'enulgheli, « the one with a notched forehead ») above Redoubt Bay, and **Iliamna Volcano** (10,016 ft / 3,053 m, Ch'naqał'in, « the one that stands above ») above Tuxedni Bay. Large parts of both halves are labelled **Jay S. Hammond Wilderness Area**. The western third is a lighter green **preserve** of rolling lake-and-river lowlands (the Bonanza Hills, the Mulchatna and Chilikadrotna rivers flowing west to Bristol Bay).

The centrepiece is **Lake Clark** (Qizhjeh Vena, « people gather lake »), a long blue lake running south-west to north-east through the middle of the map, ~50 miles long, 254 ft above the sea and up to 870 ft deep. On its south shore, a third of the way up, is **Port Alsworth** (teal label « Visitor Center, National Park Field Headquarters; Tanalian Falls Trail, Post Office, lodgings and private airstrips »), at Hardenburg Bay, with Tanalian Mountain (Tanq' Nunst'in, 3,960 ft) and Kontrashibuna Lake behind it. Across the lake is **Kijik** (Qizhjeh) National Historic Landmark and Archeological District by Kijik Lake and Kijik Mountain; up-lake, Little Lake Clark and the Tlikakila River lead north-east towards **Lake Clark Pass**. At the south-west end, the lake drains into Sixmile Lake and the Newhalen River past **Nondalton** (Nuvendaltun) and Newhalen to **Iliamna Lake** (Nila Vena) at the bottom left, with Iliamna and Pedro Bay villages. South of Lake Clark lie the Tazimina Lakes (a conservation easement) and Roadhouse Mountain.

North of Lake Clark, a chain of mountain lakes sits between forest and tundra: **Twin Lakes** (Niłqidlen, « flows together lakes ») with the ranger-station symbol and « Richard Proenneke Historic Site » on Upper Twin Lake; Snipe, Fishtrap and Lachbuna lakes; **Turquoise Lake** (Vandaztun Vena, « caribou hair lake ») and **Telaquana Lake** (Dilah Vena, « fish swim into lake ») with an « Old Village site » and a ranger station; Telaquana Pass and Telaquana Mountain (8,070 ft) above. The **coast** is drawn from the Drift River and Redoubt Bay south past Crescent Lake (ranger station), the Tuxedni River and Tuxedni Bay (Chisik Island), Slope Mountain, Iliamna Point, **Silver Salmon Creek** (ranger station), Hickerson Lake and Spring Point to **Chinitna Bay** (ranger station); Native Corporation lands (orange) cover several coastal blocks. Red notes warn that « private property is interspersed with public lands » around Port Alsworth and that hunting and commercial activities are prohibited on the Tazimina Lakes conservation easement.

The legend (top left) shows the national park, preserve, Native corporation and private lands, ranger stations (« hours vary »), the wilderness boundary and a 0–20 km / 0–20 mi scale. There are **no roads and no trails** except the short Tanalian network at Port Alsworth (6.9 miles in all); the « Telaquana Trail » of old maps is only a route. The **Dena'ina place-names table** (top right) is the map's cultural key: besides the lakes above, Chulitna River = Ch'alitnu (« flows out river »), Kijik Lake = K'q'uya Vena (« red salmon lake »), Kijik River = Ch'ak'daltnu (« animals-walk-out stream »), Kontrashibuna Lake = Qenłghishi Vena (« boiling lake »), Tanalian River = Tanilen Vetnu (« flows into water stream »), Tlikakila River = Łiq'a Qilanhtnu (« stream where salmon are »), Tyonek = Tubughnenq' (« beach land »).

### Water

Lake Clark sits in the **Kvichak watershed**: its water flows west through Sixmile Lake and the **Newhalen River** into Iliamna Lake, then down the Kvichak to **Bristol Bay**, and every summer from 147,000 to 3.1 million **red (sockeye) salmon** swim back up past the Newhalen counting station to spawn in the lake's tributaries — part of the world's largest sockeye run. The lakes are glacial and often **turquoise** from rock flour: Lake Clark (77 m surface, 265 m deep), Kontrashibuna, Upper and Lower Twin (603 m), Telaquana (372 m), Turquoise, Crescent (183 m). Three wild rivers (Chilikadrotna, Mulchatna, Tlikakila) run through the preserve and park; the Tanalian River falls over Tanalian Falls into Lake Clark. The **Cook Inlet coast** has some of the largest tides in North America (up to ~9 m in the inlet), exposing wide mud and sand flats with razor clams and salt marshes of sedge; its creeks (Silver Salmon, Johnson, Crescent, Polly) carry silver and pink salmon in late summer. Glaciers and snow cover ~20 % of the park; the four largest glaciers of Iliamna Volcano alone hold about three times the ice of Mount Rainier. Lakes freeze in November and melt in April — ski-planes then floatplanes.

### Relief

From sea level on Cook Inlet to **Redoubt Volcano, 3,108 m**, within ~15 km of the shore. The land steps from the coastal flats and marshes to steep, glacier-carved Chigmit Mountains (1,500–2,500 m, with the two volcano cones above 3,000 m), then west down through deep glacial troughs holding long lakes (Lake Clark at 77 m, Kontrashibuna at ~140 m), to the rolling tundra plateau and lake country of the north-west (Twin Lakes at 603 m, above most trees) and the preserve's low hills. Tanalian Mountain rises 1,130 m straight above Port Alsworth. In the game: a long lake with forested banks and gravel beaches, a waterfall gorge with a two-level drop, a steep switchback climb to a tundra summit, high-country lakes in open U-shaped valleys with scree slopes, 3D volcano cones with steam and glaciers, and a flat coast of sand, mud and sedge edged by low bluffs.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: around Lake Clark (77–400 m) white spruce, paper birch, balsam poplar and cottonwood with alder and willow, fireweed clearings; the lake country (400–800 m) is spruce thinning into tundra, dwarf birch and willow, lichen and crowberry; alpine tundra and fellfield higher, then glaciers; the coast has salt marshes of Lyngbye's sedge and beach rye, beach pea and Sitka spruce stands on the bluffs. Land cover: bare gravel and rock 24 %, ice 20 %, shrubland 19 %, tundra 15 %, forest 11 %.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#3FA7B5` turquoise glacial lake · `#1F5E77` deep Lake Clark blue · `#2F4B38` spruce forest · `#C8B04A` autumn birch gold · `#8A9A5B` tundra · `#B03A2E` red salmon in the creeks · `#9FB870` coastal sedge meadow · `#8B7F6A` tidal mud flats · `#E9EEF1` volcano snow · `#5A5552` volcanic ash rock.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **brown bear** (coast: sedge meadows May–July, clams, salmon August–September; up to 20+ in view at Chinitna Bay), black bear (interior forests), moose, **caribou** (Mulchatna herd), Dall sheep (above Twin Lakes), gray wolf, wolverine, lynx, red fox, beaver (Beaver Pond), river otter, porcupine; **sockeye salmon** and silver salmon, Arctic grayling, lake trout; birds — bald eagle, common and Pacific loons (Silver Salmon Lakes), trumpeter swan, shorebirds on the coastal flats, seabirds on Chisik Island, willow ptarmigan; in Cook Inlet, **beluga whales** (an endangered population), harbor seals and sea otters.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): fireweed (July–August), chocolate lily (coastal meadows, June), wild iris / blue flag (June–July), Nootka lupine (June), Lyngbye's sedge (the bears' spring food), beach pea, dwarf dogwood, cloudberry and blueberry (August), Labrador tea, paper birch turning gold in September.
- **Weather & seasons**: the coast is cloudy, windy and wet, the interior drier with warm summer days; spring break-up in May (floats replace skis), June bears on the coastal sedge, July–August salmon and berries, September gold birches and termination dust on the peaks, ice on the lakes by November. Fog and wind often ground the planes.
- **Night**: ~19 h of sun at Port Alsworth on the June solstice (sunrise ~4:50, sunset ~23:47 AKDT); dark autumn and winter skies with aurora over Lake Clark; Redoubt's ash plumes darkened the sky in 1989–90 and 2009.
- **Sounds**: a single-engine plane's drone and splash landing, the roar of Tanalian Falls, loons calling on Twin Lakes at midnight, an axe and a crosscut saw (Proenneke's work), bears munching sedge, waves and gulls on the Cook Inlet beach, the rattle of an ATV trailer on the sand.

## Moments

- **Coastal bear season**: in June bears graze the sedge meadows at Silver Salmon Creek and Chinitna Bay like cattle and dig clams at low tide; mating season shows « cowboy-walking » boars; in August–September they fish silver salmon in the creeks.
- **The red-salmon run** (July–August): the Newhalen and the lake's tributaries turn red with spawning sockeye; the Dena'ina fish cache and subsistence nets at Nondalton and Port Alsworth.
- **Proenneke's cabin open house** (late May–late September, daily 10 am–4 pm): rangers show the hand-built cabin, cache and woodshed from the 1967–68 build; his journals and films (« One Man's Wilderness »).
- **A volcano steams**: Redoubt (last eruption 2009) and Iliamna often show plumes; 4 recorded Redoubt eruptions (1902, 1966, 1989–90, 2009).
- **Freeze-up and break-up**: lakes freeze in November and open in April–May, switching the planes from floats to skis and back.
- **Midsummer on the lakes**: loons calling in the bright night at Twin Lakes; Dall sheep on the slopes above.
- **Rock paintings**: red-ochre pictographs over 1,700 years old at Clam Cove in Chinitna Bay and in Tuxedni Bay — the only two rock-painting sites known in Alaska's national parks.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a curated map of **~1,024 × 768 tiles** made of three islands linked by **plane hops**, plus an optional fourth. (1) **Port Alsworth & the Tanalian Trails** ~420 × 300 tiles: the village near true scale (two airstrips ~1 km long, the visitor center and headquarters, Hardenburg Bay), Beaver Pond, Tanalian Falls and Kontrashibuna Lake with distances compressed ~2×, and the Tanalian Mountain climb as a steep terrace staircase; a stretch of Lake Clark with a canoe route to Kijik (12 km compressed to ~200 tiles). (2) **Twin Lakes** ~256 × 192 tiles: the Proenneke cabin, cache and woodshed at true scale, Hope Creek campsite, Teetering Rock, the upper lake for paddling. (3) **Silver Salmon Creek** ~288 × 224 tiles: beach landing strip, ranger station, lodges, the (closed) sedge meadow as a bear stage, tidal flats and the creek, Iliamna Volcano as the backdrop. Optional (4) Chinitna Bay or Crescent Lake. Links: Port Alsworth ↔ Twin Lakes (float, 30 min), Port Alsworth ↔ the coast (wheels via Lake Clark Pass or around the volcanoes), and to the other Alaska vignettes (Brooks Camp in Katmai is 125 mi away).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the Port Alsworth visitor center beside an active airstrip (planes landing as ambient traffic), floatplanes at Hardenburg Bay, lodges and cabins, Tanalian Falls; the **Proenneke cabin** (a small, perfect log cabin with a sod-and-moss roof, a raised food cache on posts, a woodshed, hand-carved hinges — a detailed interior); the Silver Salmon Creek ranger station, lodge ATV trailers, the beach with parked planes; 3D landmarks: Redoubt and Iliamna volcanoes (steam), Tanalian Mountain, Telaquana Mountain, Chisik Island.
- **Activities**: bush-plane travel (a « circle of safety » pre-flight briefing mini-game, weather delays), hiking the Tanalian Trails and Tanalian Mountain, canoe/kayak on Lake Clark and Twin Lakes, the Proenneke cabin tour and a « build your own cabin » crafting nod (notching logs with hand tools), bear viewing on the coast (read a bear's body language: standing, yawning, huffing, jaw-popping), coho fishing (cut your line when a bear watches), clam digging at low tide, birding (loons, shorebirds), photo spots — Tanalian Falls, Kontrashibuna from the mountain, the cabin from the lake, bears on the sedge with Iliamna behind, Redoubt's plume; Junior Ranger activities; learning Dena'ina place names as a collectible.
- **Passport stamps**: « Port Alsworth » (Lake Clark Visitor Center), « Twin Lakes » (Proenneke cabin site), « Silver Salmon Creek » (ranger station), « Homer » and « Anchorage » (park offices outside the park).
- **With friends / with the world**: a party shares one plane (seats and cargo weight to balance — who leaves their heavy bag?), a group bear-viewing session where everyone must stay within a few arm's lengths and quiet, canoeing in pairs, a campfire at Hope Creek; gathering spots — the Port Alsworth visitor center deck, the cabin beach at Upper Twin Lake (the classic lunch spot), the Silver Salmon beach at low tide; group photo in front of Proenneke's cabin.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): keep food, garbage and fish in bear-resistant containers within ½ mile of the coast and the listed lakes (containers lent free at the visitor center); stay out of the sedge meadows (closed June 15–September 15 at Silver Salmon, closed at Chinitna Bay) and use designated viewing sites; give bears multiple escape routes and never block their path or crowd them; stay together in a tight group; never sleep in or cook inside the Proenneke cabin, be gentle with its hand-made door, stay on his trails; camp limits (21 days near Twin Lakes, 14 at Hope Creek); respect private land in Port Alsworth and Native corporation lands; cross airstrips only at the ends, looking up.
- **Implementation challenges**: a plane-hop network with landing-gear rules (floats vs wheels) and weather holds; huge tides that move the shoreline and can strand planes and boats; bears in high density with readable body language; turquoise glacial lakes and water colour by depth; volcanoes with steam and occasional ash; a detailed log-cabin interior; seasonal freeze-up changing the lakes into snowfields.
