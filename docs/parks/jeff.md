# Gateway Arch National Park — `JEFF`

> Missouri (with a sliver of the Illinois riverbank) · established February 22, 2018 (Jefferson National Expansion Memorial since December 21, 1935) · 0.8 km² (192.83 acres; ~91 acres of grounds) · 2,209,028 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 118–144 m (ground; the Arch's top stands 192 m above it) · NPS region MWR (Midwest; DOI Regions 3–5)
>
> The smallest national park: a 630-foot stainless-steel arch on a landscaped riverfront lawn in downtown St. Louis, with a museum buried beneath it, a capsule tram inside its legs and the domed Old Courthouse where Dred and Harriet Scott sued for freedom. In Hearthlight it is a street-scale city vignette: stroll the tree-lined allées, ride the tiny tram to the windows at the top, watch barges on the Mississippi and fireworks over the river.

## Reading the map

Use north up (the 2010 plan) to redraw it, and the painted 2018 view (looking west) for how it feels. The park is a long lawn laid along the **west bank of the Mississippi**, which flows south here and runs down the east edge of everything. The grounds form a strip about 1.2 km north–south and 300 m wide between **Memorial Drive and the sunken I-44 lanes** on the west and **Leonor K. Sullivan Boulevard** on the riverfront to the east. Their north end is **Washington Avenue and the Eads Bridge** (1874, carrying MetroLink to Illinois) next to the old warehouses of Laclede's Landing; their south end is **Poplar Street** and the tangle of the Poplar Street Bridge (I-55/I-64) near Busch Stadium.

The **Arch** stands in the middle of the strip, its two legs 630 ft (192 m) apart on a north–south line parallel to the river, and 630 ft tall. West of it, a round sunken plaza is the **Visitor Center entrance** (the only way in, facing the city); the **Museum at the Gateway Arch**, Tucker Theater, store and café are all **underground** beneath the lawn between the legs, and the **tram lobbies** lie under each leg, with the North and South Exits coming up at the leg bases. East of the Arch, the 1960s **Grand Staircase** drops about 15 m to the cobbled levee, where the replica paddlewheel riverboats and helicopter tours tie up. Curving paths lined with **London plane trees** frame two **reflecting ponds**, one near each end, with circles of bald cypress; the **North Gateway** (where a parking garage stood until 2015) is now a native meadow and pollinator "Explorer Garden" leading to Laclede's Landing and its MetroLink station.

West of the Arch, the 2018 **lid over I-44** turned **Luther Ely Smith Square** into a lawn bridge that walks you straight across the highway to the **Old Courthouse** (4th Street, between Market and Chestnut), whose green dome sits exactly on the Arch's axis; the **Dred and Harriet Scott statue** stands in front of its east steps. Beyond it, **Kiener Plaza** and the Gateway Mall continue the axis west into downtown. The **Old Cathedral** (Basilica of St. Louis, King of France, 1834) sits inside the grounds' south-west corner on Walnut Street. Across the river, the park boundary also takes in a strip of the **East St. Louis riverfront** in Illinois (mostly undeveloped, not visitor space), facing the city's tall fountain, the Gateway Geyser. There is no wilderness and almost no relief: the "cliffs" are the levee wall and the Grand Staircase, and the tallest things are the Arch and the downtown towers behind it.

### Water

- **Mississippi River**: flows **south** along the whole east edge, ~450–500 m wide, brown with silt, full of barge tows; the Missouri River joins it ~24 km upstream (north) and the combined river carries it past the Arch. Normal surface ~118 m; flood stage at the St. Louis gauge is 30 ft, and the levee, lower Grand Staircase and Leonor K. Sullivan Blvd go under water in big floods (record 49.58 ft in 1993). No tides.
- **Reflecting ponds**: two landscaped ponds near the north and south ends of the grounds (part of Dan Kiley's designed landscape); stormwater from Luther Ely Smith Square is filtered and stored in an underground basin.
- No streams, springs or falls.

### Relief

Lowest ~118 m (the river and levee), highest ground ~144 m (the Old Courthouse and Kiener Plaza on the bluff edge) — and the **Arch itself rises 192 m** above its base, the tallest monument in the United States. The land steps only twice: the natural bluff/terrace on which the Arch and old city stand, and the levee 15 m below, joined by the Grand Staircase and ramps; the I-44 lanes run in a cut beside the grounds, lidded at Luther Ely Smith Square. In the game: one HIGH terrace (the grounds) with a painted levee wall and stairs, a walkable ramp at each end, the sunken highway as a cut with a lawn bridge, the Arch as a single huge 3D landmark (reflective, visible from the whole map), the Old Courthouse dome as a second landmark, downtown towers as a backdrop wall.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: all designed: 74 acres of fescue lawn; allées of London plane trees (planted from 2016, replacing Rosehill ash removed ahead of the emerald ash borer); circles of bald cypress (some planted before 1980) that turn rust-red in fall; shady zen gardens of grey gravel and square stones; a ~3-acre native meadow of big and little bluestem, switchgrass and sideoats grama; the Explorer Garden of native pollinator perennials (black-eyed Susan, butterfly milkweed, asters, goldenrod, blue vervain); willows and cottonwoods on the river's edge.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#C9CDD2` brushed stainless steel of the Arch (sky-coloured reflections: `#E7C08A` at sunset) · `#6E9B4E` lawn · `#3E6B3A` plane-tree allée shade · `#B9542C` bald cypress in November · `#8C7A5B` Mississippi silt-brown · `#6F7D86` river under grey sky · `#BFB6A8` limestone of the Old Courthouse · `#5E8C7E` its verdigris dome · `#8B8F94` levee cobbles and granite steps · `#B3A57E` bluestem meadow in fall.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): 142 bird species recorded on the grounds — peregrine falcon (nests on downtown towers), red-tailed hawk, bald eagle (over the river in winter), Canada goose and mallard (ponds), great egret (river edge, summer), chimney swift (summer evenings), ring-billed gull (river, winter), 27 warbler species in April–May migration, Northern mockingbird, cardinal; mammals from the park's wildlife cameras — squirrels, coyote, Virginia opossum, raccoon, eastern cottontail, woodchuck; big brown and eastern red bats; monarch butterflies in the meadow; river fish like blue catfish and paddlefish.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): London plane tree (mottled bark, all year), bald cypress (feathery needles, rust in November), big bluestem (bronze in fall), little bluestem, switchgrass, common and butterfly milkweed (summer), black-eyed Susan (July–August), partridge pea (late summer), aromatic aster (October).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — thunderstorms, floods on the levee, migrating warblers; summer — hot and humid, heat shimmering off the steel, afternoon storms, July 4 crowds; fall — clear blue days, gold meadow and rusty cypress; winter — grey river, occasional snow outlining the curve of the Arch, eagles along the Mississippi, fog hiding the top.
- **Night**: city glow, few stars (the park's "Sky Ranger" blog points out planets and eclipses that are still visible); the Arch floodlit from below — its lights are switched off during peak bird-migration weeks; city lights reflected in the river; fireworks on Independence Day.
- **Sounds**: towboat horns and diesel rumble from the river, MetroLink bells on the Eads Bridge, traffic hum from the I-44 cut, the clack of the tram capsules and their gentle tilt, wind whistling at the top, crowds on the Grand Staircase, Cardinals game roars from Busch Stadium, riverboat calliope-style whistles, geese on the ponds.

## Moments

- **Ride to the Top**: 4 minutes up in a five-seat capsule that pivots ~155° to stay level, 8–10 minutes at the windows, 3 minutes down; last tram about an hour before the 6 pm closing.
- **Sunrise over the Mississippi** from the east windows and **sunset behind downtown** from the west windows; the Arch glows gold and pink from the ground at both ends of the day.
- **The Arch's shadow** sweeps across downtown in the morning and across the river in the afternoon; reflections in the ponds on calm days.
- **Independence Day**: fireworks over the riverfront with crowds on the grounds (Fair Saint Louis).
- **Spring floods** push the Mississippi up the levee and the Grand Staircase; barges and debris race by.
- **Bird migration** (April–May, September–October): warblers in the plane trees; the Arch's lights go dark for the birds.
- **Winter bald eagles** along the river (January–February).
- **Old Courthouse ranger tour** daily at 2:15 pm (the Dred Scott story in the restored courtrooms).
- **Riverboat cruise** (1 hour) and helicopter hops over the Arch from the levee.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a small vertical city map, **512 × 768 tiles** (~0.8 × 1.15 km at 1.5 m/tile), north up: the grounds, the courthouse block and Kiener Plaza at nearly true scale (1.2 km of lawn, 250–350 m wide); the Mississippi compressed from ~480 m to ~150 m; the Illinois bank a thin strip on the east edge with the geyser as a set piece. The Arch at true footprint (legs 128 tiles apart) but modelled at reduced height if the camera cannot frame it — its full curve should be seen from the far bank or the north end. Cut: downtown beyond Kiener Plaza (a backdrop wall of towers), the highway interchanges (reduced to the I-44 cut and two bridge stubs).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the West Entrance plaza and underground lobby (main hub), the Grand Staircase (gathering steps facing the river), the Old Courthouse rotunda, the levee landing. 3D landmarks: the Arch (stainless, catching the sky colour), the Old Courthouse with its verdigris dome, the Old Cathedral, the Eads Bridge arches with a passing MetroLink train, paddlewheel riverboats, barge tows, the Poplar Street Bridge. Interiors: the underground museum (six galleries: Colonial St. Louis, Jefferson's Vision, New Frontiers, The Riverfront Era, Manifest Destiny, Building the Dream), Tucker Theater, the tram capsule and the observation deck (a long narrow room with 32 slit windows), the Old Courthouse courtrooms and galleries.
- **Activities**: the tram ride as a special scene (a capsule that tilts as it climbs the leg, then the view); photo spots — the Arch framing the Old Courthouse dome from the Grand Staircase, looking straight up a leg, the Arch reflected in the north pond, the Arch from the Illinois bank at sunset, the view down from the top windows; museum & ranger talks; junior ranger (two badges: the Arch and the Old Courthouse); riverboat cruise; helicopter hop; MetroLink ride across the Eads Bridge; counting barges; birding in the plane trees in May; the Fourth of July fireworks; picnics on the lawn.
- **Passport stamps**: the 2025 list gives "St. Louis" and "Old Courthouse", plus Amtrak Trails & Rails stamps for the routes the park's volunteers ride from St. Louis: "Kansas City Mule", "Ann Rutledge", "Texas Eagle", "Trails and Rails Lincoln Service", "Trails and Rails Missouri River Runner". In the game: the St. Louis stamp at the Arch visitor center desk, the Old Courthouse stamp in its rotunda, the rail stamps on a train at the edge of the map.
- **With friends / with the world**: a party of up to five fits exactly in one tram capsule — ride up together; group photo at the base of a leg, everyone looking up; "touch the Arch" (local legend: it pulls you back to St. Louis like a boomerang); watch fireworks together on the Grand Staircase; strangers meet in the tram line, on the steps at sunset and at the top windows.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): touch the Arch but never scratch or tag it (graffiti is a federal offense); stay off the levee when the river is high; leash pets (not inside); no glass on the grounds; no drones; quiet in the courtroom; don't feed the geese; keep the lights out for migrating birds.
- **Implementation challenges**: a 192-m-tall, thin, reflective catenary landmark far beyond the usual camera height (LOD, silhouettes, framing from afar); an underground museum under a lawn and a curved tram shaft inside the legs; a big river with barges, floods and bridges; a highway cut with a lawn lid; dense city edges and crowds; night lighting and fireworks.
