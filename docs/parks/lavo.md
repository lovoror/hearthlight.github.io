# Lassen Volcanic National Park — `LAVO`

> California · established August 9, 1916 (Lassen Peak and Cinder Cone national monuments since May 6, 1907) ·
> 431.4 km² (106,589.02 acres) · 504,777 recreation visits (2025) · ~1,520–3,187 m · NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10,
> California–Great Basin)
>
> The southern end of the Cascades, where all four kinds of volcano stand side by side — a giant lava dome that erupted
> in 1914–17, a black cinder cone with painted dunes, broad shield volcanoes and the broken stump of an ancient
> stratovolcano — around boiling mud pots, roaring steam vents and clear snowmelt lakes. In Hearthlight it should feel
> like a sleeping giant's garden: a road that climbs between walls of snow to a sulphur-steaming valley, a boardwalk over
> a paint-box of hot pools, and a real volcano you can walk up in an afternoon.

## Reading the map

The park is a rough rectangle, ~31 km wide and 20 km tall, cut by one road. **Lassen Peak** (3,187 m) stands in the western third, a bare grey dome with its 1915 craters; the highway wraps round its south and east flanks. West of the peak lies the old volcano **Mount Tehama**, now a ring of summits — Brokeoff Mountain, Mount Diller, Pilot Pinnacle, Eagle Peak — around its eroded core, where the hot ground of **Sulphur Works**, **Little Hot Springs Valley** and **Bumpass Hell** still steams. North-west of the peak, the pale domes of **Chaos Crags** rise over the rockfall of Chaos Jumbles and **Manzanita Lake**. The eastern two thirds are a gentle forested plateau of lakes and low shield volcanoes (Prospect Peak, Mount Harkness, Crater Butte, Mount Hoffman), almost all wilderness, with **Cinder Cone** and the **Fantastic Lava Beds** in the north-east corner and **Juniper Lake**, the largest lake, in the south-east. The green dashes of the **Pacific Crest Trail** cross it south–north through Warner Valley and the Twin Lakes; the old **Nobles Emigrant Trail** runs east–west along the north.

**The park highway.** From the **Southwest Entrance** (the Raker Memorial Gateway, then the **Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center** at 2,044 m, open all year), Hwy 89 climbs north past the roadside mud pots of **Sulphur Works** and switchbacks up the old caldera past Diamond Peak and the Little Hot Springs Valley overlook to **Emerald Lake**, the **Bumpass Hell** trailhead and **Lake Helen** (2,500 m), then reaches its high point, 2,594 m, at the **Lassen Peak trailhead**. It swings east under Reading Peak and drops to the **Kings Creek** meadows (picnic area at 2,256 m; the Kings Creek Falls trailhead), turns north past **Summit Lake** (two campgrounds, ranger station) and the Hat Lake trailhead to the **Devastated Area**, where the 1915 blast and mudflows came down Lost Creek and Hat Creek, then bends west past **Hot Rock** and Anklin Meadows along Lost Creek, round the flank of Chaos Crags, to **Manzanita Lake** (Loomis Museum, Discovery Center, the largest campground, camper store and gas) and the **north-west entrance** by Lassen Crossroads. The whole road is 30 mi (48 km), closed from ~1 mi inside each entrance from November–December until it is dug out, usually in late May–June.

**The south: Warner Valley.** From Chester a long road enters the park in a green valley south of the Flatiron Ridge: the Warner Valley ranger station and campground (1,719 m), **Drakesbad Guest Ranch** with its hot springs, and short trails to **Devils Kitchen** (steam vents and mud pots, 1,838 m), **Boiling Springs Lake** and, on the Pacific Crest Trail, **Terminal Geyser**. The lower slopes here burned in 2021.

**The east: Juniper Lake and Butte Lake.** A dirt road from Chester climbs to **Juniper Lake** (2,058 m) — the campground, ranger station, and the trail up **Mount Harkness** (2,452 m) to its fire lookout, burned in 2021. In the north-east corner, a gravel road from Hwy 44 ends at **Butte Lake** (1,859 m: campground, ranger station, boat launch): from there the Nobles Emigrant Trail leads west to **Cinder Cone** (2,105 m), a perfect black cone with the rust and violet **Painted Dunes** at its foot, and the jagged **Fantastic Lava Beds** that pour south between Butte Lake and **Snag Lake**, which they dammed. Between the outposts the brochure shows only trails and lakes: Twin, Rainbow, Swan, Echo, Cluster, Horseshoe, Widow and dozens more.

### Water

Snow is the water story: 10–13 m falls in a normal winter and melts into **200+ lakes and ponds** and 15 perennial streams — the headwaters of four watersheds (the Pit River, the North Fork Feather, Mill Creek and Battle Creek). Lakes range from ice-bound alpine tarns (**Lake Helen**, frozen into July; **Emerald Lake**) and meadow lakes (Summit Lake) to big plateau lakes (**Juniper**, the largest; **Snag** and **Butte**, dammed and seeped through lava; **Manzanita**, dammed by the Chaos Jumbles rockfall). Streams: **Kings Creek** tumbling through its cascades and falls, **Mill Creek Falls** (23 m, the tallest), Hat Creek and Lost Creek (the 1915 mudflow paths), Hot Springs Creek in Warner Valley. The **hydrothermal water** is the park's signature: eight hot areas fed by a reservoir at ~240 °C — fumaroles, boiling pools, mud pots, the acid **Boiling Springs Lake**, **Cold Boiling Lake** (cold water fizzing with gas), Drakesbad's hot spring, Terminal Geyser's steam jet. In spring snowmelt the vents run cooler and wetter; in late summer, hotter and drier.

### Relief

From **~1,520 m** (the park's low edges; Warner Valley at 1,719 m is the lowest visitor area) to **3,187 m** (Lassen Peak). The west is volcanic high country: the dome of **Lassen Peak** rising ~600 m above its skirts, the ring of **Mount Tehama** remnants (Brokeoff 2,815 m, Diller, Eagle Peak) around the eroded caldera with its hot basins, the steep domes of **Chaos Crags** (2,567 m), the lava-block fields of Chaos Jumbles and the Devastated Area. The east is a rolling plateau at 1,800–2,200 m, dotted with low shield volcanoes (Prospect Peak, Mount Harkness, Crater Butte) and cinder cones (Cinder Cone, Red Cinder), and cut by Warner Valley in the south.

For the game: Lassen Peak is a big **3D peak** with a switchback trail and summit craters; Brokeoff, Chaos Crags and Cinder Cone are distinct **3D landmarks** (jagged remnant, pale domes, perfect black cone); the hydrothermal basins are sunken **banks** with boardwalks over coloured, steaming ground; the highway's climb is a stack of switchback **stairs** through snow walls in early summer; lava beds are rough black **plateaus** with blocky edges; the lake plateau is flat with gentle banks. Camera note: Bumpass Hell sits in a bowl entered from the north-west — from a north-looking camera its south rim hides the boardwalk; the basin wants a cut-away rim.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: below ~1,830 m (Manzanita Lake, Warner Valley, Butte Lake): Jeffrey, ponderosa and sugar pine, white fir, incense cedar; 1,830–2,590 m: red fir (the park's signature big tree, often « flagged » by mistletoe) with lodgepole, western white pine and mountain hemlock, and pinemat manzanita underneath; subalpine to ~3,050 m: whitebark pine and mountain hemlock, then alpine fell-fields and bare pumice near the summits. Aspens and cottonwoods line Hat Creek and fill the Devastated Area; wildflower meadows at Kings Creek and Paradise; burned snags across the east and south since 2021.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#8A8480` Lassen dacite grey · `#2B2724` black basalt and cinders · `#9C3B2E` Painted Dunes red · `#6B4E7A` Painted Dunes violet · `#E6C229` sulphur yellow · `#EDEAE0` white silica and clay · `#C9722E` iron-orange crust · `#6FA7A0` milky hot-pool blue-green · `#3C7A5A` red fir green · `#EAF1F4` snow walls · `#2F6E8E` Manzanita Lake blue.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): black bear (~50; meadows, and the steaming Little Hot Springs Valley in early spring); mule deer (the most common sighting); golden-mantled ground squirrel (subalpine, bold); American pika (talus above treeline, heat-shy); Douglas squirrel (busy all winter); Sierra Nevada red fox (one of only two known populations, travelling on winter ski tracks); American marten; porcupine; Clark's nutcracker (caching whitebark seeds); Steller's jay (picnic areas); mountain chickadee (all winter); black-backed woodpecker (in the burned snags); sooty grouse; osprey (Manzanita Lake); gray-crowned rosy-finch (above treeline); Pacific tree frog (wet meadows); rainbow trout (Butte Lake).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): red fir, mountain hemlock, whitebark pine, quaking aspen (gold in autumn), woolly mule's ears (early, rustling), snow plant (scarlet, just after the melt), corn lily, silverleaf lupine, paintbrush, pinemat manzanita, round-leaved sundew (a carnivorous bog plant), golden draba near the summits, and the bacteria and algae that colour the hot pools.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter (November–May) buries the park — snow walls, a closed highway, snowshoe walks from the visitor centre and ski touring up the empty road; spring is plowing season, with walkers and cyclists on the cleared road; the highway opens in late May–June between walls of snow; July–August bring wildflowers by elevation, mosquitoes at Summit Lake and thunderstorms; September–October are clear, cool and golden with aspens; first snow in October.
- **Night**: dark skies (the park is working toward a formal dark-sky designation), August star parties and the Perseids at the visitor centre, telescopes at the Bumpass Hell lot during past Dark Sky Festivals; steam plumes under moonlight; owls (great gray, saw-whet); bats (8 species).
- **Sounds**: the jet-engine hiss and gurgle of fumaroles and mud pots at Bumpass Hell, sulphur-scented quiet, snow plows beeping in May, Clark's nutcrackers' grating calls, wind on the bare summit, mule's ears rustling in autumn, woodpeckers drumming on burned snags, the plop of rising trout in Manzanita Lake at dusk.

## Moments

- **The road through the snow**: plowing starts in March–April in three sections (crews clear 9–12 m of snow); walkers and cyclists get the cleared road first (~April–July); cars follow in late May–June (median ~June 2) between towering snow walls, and the road closes again mid–late November.
- **The eruption anniversary**: May 19–22 (the Great Explosion of May 22, 1915, ~4 pm, a column above 9 km seen 240 km away).
- **Bumpass Hell and Lassen Peak open**: usually mid–late July, when the snow lets go.
- **Wildflowers by elevation**: Warner Valley and Manzanita Lake late May–early July; the south-west and Butte Lake June–July; Summit and Juniper lakes late June–early August; the Lassen Peak area late July–September.
- **Star parties and the Perseids**: early–mid August at the Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center.
- **Aspen gold**: September–October at Manzanita Lake, Hat Creek and the Devastated Area.
- **Alpenglow on Lassen Peak** from Manzanita Lake at dusk; its mirror image on the lake at dawn.
- **Snowshoe season**: December–April from the visitor centre (2 mi to Sulphur Works) or round Manzanita Lake.
- **The fumaroles' seasons**: cooler and wetter in spring melt, hotter and drier in late summer.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: ~1,024 × 768 tiles. The high country along the highway — Kohm Yah-mah-nee, Sulphur Works, Bumpass Hell, Lake Helen and the Lassen Peak trail — near true scale (~1:2; Bumpass Hell's boardwalk basin ~150 × 100 tiles), since it is the heart of the park; the 30 mi highway compressed to ~700 tiles with its 16 stops; Manzanita Lake with Chaos Crags in the north-west corner (~250 × 200); the east plateau squeezed to a belt of lakes crossed by the PCT, with Cinder Cone and the lava beds (NE), Juniper Lake (SE) and Warner Valley (S) as three outposts at the ends of their own dirt roads.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center, the Loomis Museum and Manzanita Lake camp store, Summit Lake, Drakesbad Guest Ranch (lodge interior, hot-spring pool), Butte Lake; 3D landmarks: Lassen Peak with its craters, Brokeoff Mountain, Chaos Crags, Cinder Cone and the Painted Dunes, Hot Rock, the Mount Harkness lookout (burned), Bumpass Hell's boardwalk and Big Boiler; interiors: the visitor centre, the Loomis Museum (eruption photographs and a seismograph), the ranch.
- **Activities**: hiking (the list above) and a summit day on Lassen Peak; photo spots (Lassen Peak mirrored in Manzanita Lake, the Bumpass Hell boardwalk, Sulphur Works, Lake Helen under the peak, Kings Creek Falls, the Cinder Cone rim over the Painted Dunes, Hot Rock); a « colour chemistry » herbarium of hot-pool colours (yellow sulphur, white silica, red iron, green algae); wildlife (pikas, nutcrackers, the rare red fox on winter tracks); ranger programmes (Sulphur Works walks, the solar telescope, volcano talks, evening programmes at Manzanita Lake); Junior Ranger (ages 5–12, a wooden badge; « Chipmunk Club » for the youngest; « Rock Detectives » at the Devastated Area); water (kayak and swim at Manzanita, Summit and Butte lakes); vehicles (car, bike on the spring road, horse at the stock corrals and on the PCT); winter (snowshoeing, ski touring, winter camping along the closed road).
- **Passport stamps**: Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center (Lassen Association bookstore, all year) and the Loomis Museum (summer); the official list also has « California » and « 100th Anniversary 1916–2016 » stamps.
- **With friends / with the world**: « first car over the pass » — the open world's spring event when the highway opens between snow walls; a sunrise summit party on Lassen Peak; a hand-holding line on the Bumpass Hell boardwalk (the ground either side is lethal); racing up and sliding down the loose cinders of Cinder Cone; the August star party as the evening gathering; a Nobles Emigrant Trail wagon trek re-enactment across the north.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on the boardwalks and trails in hydrothermal areas — the crust is thin and the water boils (people have been badly burned off-trail at Sulphur Works and Devils Kitchen); 100 yards from bears, 25 from other animals; food in the bear lockers; no pets on trails; stay off snow bridges over creeks; no campfires in the backcountry.
- **Implementation challenges**: hydrothermal ground (steam plumes, bubbling mud, colour patches, heat that kills off-path); a volcano with summit craters; deep snow that closes the only road for half the year (snow walls, a plowing calendar, a road walkable before it is drivable); black lava beds and loose cinder slopes; seasonal wildflower bands by elevation; burned forest across the east (the 2021 Dixie Fire burned 69 % of the park); a road with no guardrails along drops.
