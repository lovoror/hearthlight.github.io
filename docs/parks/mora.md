# Mount Rainier National Park — `MORA`

> Washington · established March 2, 1899 · 956.6 km² (236,382 acres) · 1,635,342 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~520–4,392 m · NPS region PWR (today DOI Region 9, Columbia–Pacific Northwest)
>
> A single ice-clad volcano, 4,392 m high and wrapped in 28 major glaciers, fills the middle of a square park; a
> ring of wildflower meadows circles it at 1,500–2,100 m and old-growth forest fills the valleys below. In Hearthlight
> it should feel like living at the foot of a giant: whichever meadow you stand in — Paradise, Sunrise, Spray Park —
> the white mountain looms over you, and the day is about the light on it.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is almost a square about 37 km on a side, with straight surveyed boundaries on the north, west and south and a ragged east side that follows the Cascade crest (the Pacific Crest Trail runs along it from Chinook Pass south). **Mount Rainier** sits a little west of centre: a white star of ice about 15 km across, its summit (Columbia Crest, 4,392 m) ringed by glaciers that radiate like spokes — Carbon and Russell to the north, Winthrop and Emmons to the north-east, Fryingpan, Ingraham and Cowlitz to the east and south-east, Nisqually, Wilson and Kautz to the south, Tahoma, Puyallup and the Mowich glaciers to the west. **Little Tahoma** (3,395 m) is the sharp second summit on its east shoulder. Below the ice, each glacier becomes a milky river running out of the park in its own direction: the Carbon and Mowich north-west, the Puyallup west, the Nisqually south-west, the Cowlitz and Ohanapecosh south-east, the White north-east.

Roads only touch the lower flanks, in a « C » that is open on the west. The **southern arc** is the main visitor route: from the **Nisqually Entrance** in the south-west corner the Paradise Road follows the Nisqually River 10 km east to **Longmire** (the historic first headquarters: National Park Inn, museum, wilderness centre), passes Cougar Rock campground, Christine Falls, the Ricksecker Point one-way loop and Narada Falls, and climbs to **Paradise** (1,650 m) on the south slope, ~28 km from the entrance: the Henry M. Jackson Visitor Center, the Paradise Inn and the meadows under the Nisqually Glacier. From Paradise the one-way Paradise Valley Road and **Stevens Canyon Road** continue east past **Reflection Lakes**, under the Tatoosh Range (Pinnacle, Unicorn and Stevens peaks) and through the Box Canyon tunnel, 30 km down to the **Stevens Canyon Entrance** and SR 123 in the south-east corner, beside the Grove of the Patriarchs; **Ohanapecosh** lies 5 km south on SR 123 at the park's lowest south-east corner.

The **east side** is two state highways. SR 123 runs 18 km north up the Ohanapecosh–Chinook Creek valley to **Cayuse Pass** (1,431 m), where SR 410 (the Mather Memorial Parkway) comes in: east 5 km to **Chinook Pass** (1,657 m) and **Tipsoo Lake** on the crest, and north down the White River to the **White River Entrance**. From there the **Sunrise Road** climbs 24 km west: past the White River campground turn-off, then in switchbacks up Sunrise Ridge to **Sunrise** (1,950 m), the highest point reached by car, facing the Emmons Glacier across the White River valley. The **north-west corner** has two dead ends from SR 165: the gravel Mowich Lake Road (9 km to **Mowich Lake**, the park's largest and deepest lake) and the Carbon River Road, closed to cars at the **Carbon River Entrance** since the 2006 floods (its 6 km to Ipsut Creek are now a trail for hikers and bikes). On the west side the **Westside Road** is open to cars only 5 km to Dry Creek. Since April 2025 the whole north-west corner has no public road access at all (the Fairfax Bridge outside the park is permanently closed).

Everything else is wilderness (97 % of the park), stitched together by the **Wonderland Trail**, a 150 km loop around the mountain at ~750–2,070 m that goes up and down across every glacier valley: Longmire, Indian Henry's Hunting Ground, Klapatche Park, Golden Lakes, Mowich Lake, Ipsut Creek and the Carbon Glacier's snout, Mystic Lake, Sunrise, Summerland, Indian Bar, Box Canyon, Reflection Lakes and back to Longmire. Relief organises everything in rings: forest valleys at 550–1,000 m, the hubs and meadows at 1,500–2,000 m, pumice and rock at 2,000–2,500 m, then only ice. In winter only the Nisqually–Paradise road is kept open (the Longmire gate closes at night), and every other road on the map is closed from roughly November to late May or June.

### Water

- **Glaciers**: 28 major glaciers and many snowfields cover ~78 km² (30 sq mi), the most glaciated peak in the contiguous US. Emmons has the largest area (4.3 sq mi / 11 km²); the Carbon has the lowest terminus (3,600 ft / ~1,100 m) of any glacier in the lower 48; the Nisqually is the one watched from Paradise. Summit steam vents melt caves under the crater ice.
- **Rivers** run out radially and are milky with glacial flour, braided on wide gravel floors, and prone to floods and debris flows: the **Nisqually** (from the Nisqually Glacier past Longmire, south-west to Puget Sound); the **Paradise River**; the **Cowlitz** and **Muddy Fork** (south-east, Box Canyon); the clear green **Ohanapecosh** (from springs and snowmelt, south-east); the **White** (from the Emmons, north-east, to the Puyallup); the **Carbon** (north-west, from the Carbon Glacier); the **Mowich** and **Puyallup** (west); Tahoma and Kautz creeks (south-west, known for debris flows — the Kautz flow of 1947, the Carbon River Road washed out in 2006).
- **Lakes**: Mowich (largest and deepest), Reflection Lakes, Tipsoo, Eunice, Mystic, Frozen Lake (Sunrise's water supply), Snow and Bench, Louise, the Crystal Lakes, Golden Lakes, Lake George, Lake James, Owyhigh Lakes.
- **Falls**: 29 named in the NPS data — Comet (~98 m), Narada, Christine, Myrtle, Sluiskin, Carter & Madcap, Martha, Silver (Ohanapecosh), Spray, Ranger and Chenuis (Carbon River).
- **Springs**: iron-red mineral springs at Longmire (the reason for the first resort); warm seeps at Ohanapecosh (the old hot-springs resort site).

### Relief

From ~520 m where the Carbon River leaves the park (the NPS gives 1,700 ft for the boundary forests) to 4,392 m at Columbia Crest — nearly 3,900 m of relief in 20 km. The land steps in rings around the cone: steep forested valley walls and river terraces (550–1,200 m); benches and ridges carrying the meadows and hubs (Paradise 1,650 m on a broad sloping bench, Sunrise 1,950 m on a ridge-top plateau, Spray and Grand parks as flat high meadows); rock ridges and cleavers that divide the glaciers (Burroughs Mountain tableland, Emerald Ridge, Cowlitz Cleaver, Disappointment Cleaver); then the ice cone with its crater rim. The Tatoosh Range is a small jagged wall just south of Paradise (Pinnacle Peak 2,000 m, Unicorn 2,108 m), Box Canyon a deep narrow slot, the east boundary a line of crest passes (Cayuse, Chinook). In the game: Mount Rainier as the biggest 3D peak of the mode (visible from every hub, with glacier tongues reaching down valleys), the meadow benches as broad plateaus with banks, the Tatoosh and the lookout summits as small 3D peaks, cliffs for canyon walls and moraines, stairs for the Skyline Trail's steep sections, and the snow line moving with the season.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: lowland old-growth of western hemlock, Douglas-fir and western redcedar (500–800 m; 1,000-year giants at the Grove of the Patriarchs; moss, sword fern and devil's club in the Carbon River rain forest) → montane forest of Pacific silver fir, noble fir, Alaska yellow cedar and western white pine (800–1,400 m) → subalpine fir, mountain hemlock and (east side) whitebark pine clumps in the meadow parkland (1,500–2,100 m: heather and huckleberry on the south and west, lush valerian–lupine meadows all round, green-fescue grassland on the dry east side) → alpine sedge, moss and pumice fields (2,100–2,500 m) → rock and ice.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#2B4A32` old-growth canopy · `#6F9B4E` summer meadow green · `#7C6FB5` lupine blue-violet · `#D2467A` magenta paintbrush · `#F2EEDC` avalanche lilies & western anemone · `#EEF4F8` glacier snow · `#86B9D8` crevasse and ice-cave blue · `#7F7B77` grey andesite cleavers · `#B79F7C` Sunrise pumice & tundra · `#A9BFB8` milky glacial river · `#3E8F80` the Ohanapecosh's clear green · `#B8342A` autumn huckleberry red.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): hoary marmot (Paradise & Sunrise meadows, whistling from boulders) · American pika (talus by the Paradise Valley Road and at Sunrise) · Cascade red fox (Paradise; red, silver and « cross » colour phases) · mountain goat (cliffs above Sunrise, Mount Fremont, Burroughs) · Roosevelt elk (east side, Sunrise and Shriner Peak meadows in summer and autumn) · black-tailed deer (Longmire, Paradise) · black bear (huckleberry slopes in August–September) · Clark's nutcracker (whitebark pines at Sunrise) · Canada jay & Steller's jay (picnic areas — the moochers) · sooty grouse (hooting in the firs in spring) · white-tailed ptarmigan (Muir Snowfield, Burroughs) · Cascades frog (Reflection Lakes and meadow ponds) · northern spotted owl & marbled murrelet (old growth) · golden-mantled ground squirrel (Sunrise).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): avalanche lily and glacier lily (right at melt-out, early–mid July at Paradise) · western anemone (white cups in July, shaggy « mop-top » seed heads in August) · magenta paintbrush · subalpine lupine (peak late July) · pink mountain heather · Sitka valerian · Lewis's monkeyflower (along streams) · mountain huckleberry (berries late August–September, scarlet leaves in October) · whitebark pine · Alaska yellow cedar · old-growth western redcedar.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter is long and deep (snow on the meadows November–June, 16 m a year at Paradise; Paradise Inn buried to its roof; avalanches; only the Nisqually–Paradise road open). Spring is snowshoe season high up and flowers low down. Summer is short: melt-out in July, bloom late July–mid-August, busy weekends; afternoon clouds often hide the summit, and a lenticular « cap cloud » on the summit warns of weather. Autumn: clear September days, scarlet huckleberry slopes, first snow in October, heavy autumn rain can trigger floods and debris flows. In 2026 wildfires (the Wonderland Complex, left to burn out naturally) closed the north side around Grand Park and Sunrise.
- **Night**: very dark skies at Paradise and Sunrise (the park limits artificial light; night-sky programmes); moonlit glaciers; after midnight in climbing season a string of climbers' headlamps crawls up from Camp Muir toward the summit.
- **Sounds**: marmot whistles and pika « eeps »; meltwater everywhere; the grinding roar of glacial rivers moving boulders; thunderous ice and rockfall from the glaciers; sooty grouse hooting; wind across the snowfield and crunching snow; creaking old-growth; the evening hum of the Paradise Inn's great hall.

## Moments

- **Wildflower peak**: Paradise and Sunrise meadows burst into bloom soon after melt-out; the peak is usually late July to mid-August (Tipsoo Lake and the Naches Loop a little earlier); western anemone seed heads and huckleberries follow in late August.
- **Alpenglow**: at sunrise the Emmons Glacier turns pink from Sunrise Point; at sunset the mountain glows over Reflection Lakes and from the Paradise Inn's porch.
- **« The mountain is out »**: the summit hides in cloud for days; clearing is an event (and a lenticular cap cloud means a storm is coming).
- **Summit night**: in the climbing season (≈ May–September) teams leave Camp Muir around midnight–2 am, a line of headlamps up the Cowlitz and Ingraham glaciers, to reach the crater at sunrise.
- **Autumn colour and elk**: huckleberry and mountain ash turn scarlet late September–early October; elk bugle on the east side.
- **Winter at Paradise**: ranger-led snowshoe walks on winter weekends (late December–March), the snowplay sledding area, snow camping; 5–6 m snowpacks by March.
- **Ice and debris**: glacier outburst floods and debris flows in hot late summers or autumn rains (Tahoma and Kautz creeks) — a « geohazard » the park signs.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a **1280 × 1280-tile** square (~1.9 km at ~1.5 m a tile) for a 37 × 33 km park, ~20× compression, with **Mount Rainier as a 3D massif ~400–500 tiles across in the centre-west** and its glaciers as white tongues down every valley. Keep near true scale: the Paradise meadows (the 8.8 km Skyline loop → ~3 km, the Jackson VC, Paradise Inn, Myrtle Falls), Longmire's historic district and Trail of the Shadows, Reflection Lakes, Box Canyon, Sunrise's lodge and Sourdough Ridge, Tipsoo Lake. Compressed ×10–20: the road arcs (Nisqually–Paradise 28 km → ~2 km, Stevens Canyon 30 km → ~1.5 km, SR 123/410, Sunrise Road's switchbacks), the Wonderland loop (150 km → ~8 km of trail). The north-west corner (Mowich, Carbon River) can stay as a wilderness corner reached only on foot, as it is today.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: hubs at Longmire, Paradise, Sunrise and Ohanapecosh (plus White River as the north-east gate). 3D landmarks: Mount Rainier with its crater rim and Little Tahoma; the Tatoosh spires; the fire lookouts (Mount Fremont, Tolmie Peak, Gobblers Knob, Shriner Peak); the Christine Falls stone bridge; Narada and Comet falls; the Box Canyon slot and bridge; the PCT log bridge over the highway at Chinook Pass; the Nisqually Entrance log arch; Camp Muir's stone hut; the Grove of the Patriarchs' suspension bridge (and its washed-out gap); Longmire's suspension bridge. Interiors: Paradise Inn's great hall (log beams, fireplaces, the old piano), the Jackson VC, the Longmire Museum, the National Park Inn, the Sunrise Day Lodge, a patrol cabin, the Camp Muir hut.
- **Activities**: hiking (above); **photo spots**: Reflection Lakes at dawn, Myrtle Falls, Panorama Point, Christine Falls under its bridge, Narada Falls, Box Canyon, Tipsoo Lake, Sunrise Point, Emmons Vista, the Fremont and Tolmie lookouts, Paradise Inn buried in snow; **wildlife**: the « meadow five » (marmot, pika, fox, goat, ptarmigan); **ranger programmes**: meadow walks, glacier talks at Nisqually Vista, evening talks at Cougar Rock, winter snowshoe walks; **Young Ranger** ideas: find the safe path through a meadow without stepping on flowers, read the glacier-retreat markers along the Nisqually valley, learn the lahar evacuation signs, match glaciers to the rivers they feed, pack out a blue bag from Camp Muir; **climbing** as a guided expedition (rope team, crampons, crevasse ladders); **water**: canoe on Mowich Lake, fish mountain lakes; **bike** the former Carbon River Road and Westside Road; **winter**: sledding in the Paradise snowplay area, snowshoeing, skiing, snow camping.
- **Passport stamps**: the official cancellation list (2025) has ten for Mount Rainier: Ashford · Paradise · Longmire · Sunrise · Ohanapecosh · Wilkeson · Carbon River · White River · Tahoma Woods (HQ at Ashford) · « 125th Anniversary 1899–2024 ». In the park they sit at the Jackson VC (Paradise), the Longmire Museum and WIC, the Sunrise and Ohanapecosh VCs, the White River WIC and the Carbon River Ranger Station.
- **With friends / with the world**: a **roped summit expedition** for a party — leave the Camp Muir hut at midnight, cross crevasses on ladders one at a time, arrive on the crater rim for sunrise; a **Wonderland relay** where each friend takes a section of the loop and hands over at the camps; a meadow-restoration work party; snowball fights and sled races at Paradise. Open-world gathering spots: the Paradise Inn lobby and the Jackson VC plaza, the Skyline loop's Panorama Point, Sunrise Point at dawn, Reflection Lakes' shore, Camp Muir at night. Group photos: at Myrtle Falls, at Reflection Lakes, on the summit crater rim.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on trails in the meadows — the park's great rule (one step off can kill plants that took decades); never feed jays, foxes, marmots or chipmunks; keep well back from goats, elk and bears; no pets on trails; permits for camping and climbing; pack out everything, including human waste above the tree line (blue bags); don't stack rocks or pick flowers; heed geohazard signs in river valleys and leave if you hear a roar upstream.
- **Implementation challenges**: the **giant central volcano** (a multi-chunk 3D model, readable from 20 km, with 28 glaciers, crevasse fields and a crater); **glaciers** as walkable-but-dangerous terrain; **deep snow** most of the year (buildings buried, snow bridges, a moving snowline) and roads closing by season; **clouds hiding the summit** and alpenglow lighting; **fragile meadows** that must be visible but not walkable off-path; **debris flows / lahars** in river valleys; roped **climbing** gameplay; waterfalls and braided glacial rivers.
