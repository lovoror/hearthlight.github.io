## Reading the map

The park is a long, thin strip on the **east edge of the Paunsaugunt Plateau**: the flat, forested plateau top is on its west side, and on its east side the plateau breaks off into the pink cliffs and hoodoo-filled amphitheatres that drop toward the Paria Valley and the town of Tropic. On the brochure the strip runs straight up the page because the map is rotated about 20°; in true north-up terms it runs from the north-north-east (UT-12 and Fairyland) to the south-south-west (Rainbow Point), about 30 km long and 2–8 km wide. Dixie National Forest surrounds it on three sides and Grand Staircase–Escalante National Monument touches its east side.

Everything enters from the **north**. UT-12 runs east–west across the top: from US-89 and Red Canyon in the west to the UT-63 junction, then across the park's northern tip past **Mossy Cave** and down Water Canyon to Tropic. UT-63 turns south from the junction through **Bryce Canyon City** (Ruby's Inn, the shuttle station, shops) to the park entrance sign; a paved shared-use path runs beside it. Just inside are the fee stations and the **visitor centre** (2,406 m). From there the single park road runs south along the rim for 18 miles to **Rainbow and Yovimpa points**, and every viewpoint is on the left (east) side when driving south.

The first three miles are the **Bryce Amphitheater**, the busy heart of the park: a 1-mile spur east to **Fairyland Point** (closed to cars in winter); North Campground and the General Store; **Sunrise Point**; **Bryce Canyon Lodge** set back in the pines; **Sunset Point** and Sunset Campground (west of the road); and a shared turn at mile 1.5 for **Inspiration Point** and the 2-mile road to **Bryce Point** and Paria View (no trailers). Below the rim, a web of trails fills the amphitheater: the Queen's Garden trail from Sunrise Point, the Navajo Loop from Sunset Point (Wall Street and Two Bridges), the Peekaboo Loop from Bryce Point, the Fairyland Loop under Boat Mesa to the north, and the Rim Trail linking Fairyland Point to Bryce Point along the edge. Bryce Creek drains the amphitheater eastward to Tropic.

South of Bryce Point the road leaves the crowds: the **southern scenic drive** climbs through forest past Swamp Canyon (mile 6), the Whiteman picnic area, Farview and Piracy points (mile 9–10), **Natural Bridge** (12.5), Agua Canyon (13.5), Ponderosa Canyon (15), Black Birch Canyon (16) and ends at **Rainbow Point** (9,115 ft / 2,778 m, the highest point) with Yovimpa Point beside it and the Bristlecone Loop. Below the rim the whole length is backcountry: the **Under-the-Rim Trail** (22.9 mi) runs from Bryce Point to Rainbow Point through the breaks, with connecting trails up to the road and campsites at springs; the **Riggs Spring Loop** circles the southern tip. East of the strip the land steps down into the Paria Valley; west of the road is plateau forest draining to the East Fork of the Sevier River and Tropic Reservoir.

### Water

Bryce has almost no standing water. The rim is a **divide between two worlds**: rain falling west of the park road flows to the East Fork of the Sevier River and the landlocked Great Basin, while everything east of the rim drains through the breaks — Bryce Creek from the amphitheater, Yellow Creek, Sheep Creek, Willis Creek and the rest — east and south into the **Paria River**, which runs past Tropic and Cannonville to the Colorado River at Lees Ferry. These creeks are small and often dry; summer storms turn them into muddy flash floods. At the northern tip, the **Tropic Ditch** (dug by Mormon settlers in 1890–92) carries East Fork Sevier water through the plateau into Water Canyon, where it falls over a ledge as the Mossy Cave waterfall; the grotto nearby drips moss in summer and hangs with icicles in winter. Springs feed the backcountry campsites (Riggs, Yovimpa, Iron, Birch, Yellow springs). **Ice** does the carving: water seeps into cracks and freezes on roughly 180 nights a year, prying the rock apart (frost-wedging). Snow covers the plateau much of the winter; Tropic Reservoir lies outside the park to the west.

### Relief

The park runs from ~2,018 m where Yellow Creek leaves it at the north-east to 2,778 m at Rainbow Point. The **rim climbs southward**: ~2,370 m at Fairyland Point, 2,400–2,530 m around the amphitheater (Sunset Point 2,438 m, Bryce Point 2,529 m), 2,680–2,780 m on the southern drive. East of the rim the **Pink Cliffs** of the Claron Formation — soft, iron-stained limestone — drop 200–300 m in steep "breaks": amphitheatres of ribs (fins), windows and **hoodoos** (spires capped by harder rock, 5–45 m tall), with forested benches and creek beds at the bottom. The amphitheater floor sits around 2,200–2,300 m, and the land keeps stepping down to the Paria Valley (Tropic ~1,920 m). This rim is the top step of the Grand Staircase, the cliffs that descend through Zion to the Grand Canyon. West of the road the plateau is nearly flat forest and meadow.

In the game: the plateau top is the base level; the rim is a cliff edge facing east; below it, terraced slopes (4–8 levels in steps) covered with hundreds of instanced hoodoo spires, ribs and small arches; the Navajo Loop and Queen's Garden trails as switchback stairs (Wall Street as a stair slot between two walls). Model as 3D: Thor's Hammer, Queen Victoria, the Hat Shop, Tower Bridge, Natural Bridge, the Wall of Windows, Boat Mesa's white cliff; Navajo Mountain and the Grand Staircase as a skybox from the southern viewpoints.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: 2,000–2,300 m (north end, Mossy Cave, below the rim) — pinyon pine, Utah juniper, Gambel oak, manzanita, rabbitbrush. 2,300–2,600 m (the amphitheater rim and plateau) — open ponderosa pine forest with greenleaf manzanita and grassy meadows (prairie-dog towns); limber pine and Douglas-fir clinging in the breaks (Douglas-firs reaching for light in Wall Street). 2,600–2,780 m (southern drive) — white fir, Douglas-fir, blue and Engelmann spruce, quaking aspen, and Great Basin bristlecone pines on the windy edge at Rainbow and Yovimpa points.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#E27D48` orange Claron hoodoos · `#F0A98A` pink cliffs · `#F3E6D6` white limestone caps and snow-dusted rims · `#B85A3A` rust shadows and iron bands · `#D9C3A0` pale ground below the rim · `#3F5E3A` ponderosa and fir green · `#7E9A6A` sagebrush meadows · `#B7A388` dusty trail tread · `#8A6C4F` muddy Paria creeks · `#20305A` deep night sky.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): Utah prairie dogs in the meadows along the road (a threatened species, asleep in winter); pronghorn in the open meadows at the north end; mule deer at dusk; Steller's jays and Clark's nutcrackers at the viewpoints; violet-green swallows and white-throated swifts wheeling over the amphitheater; ravens; golden- mantled ground squirrels and Uinta chipmunks begging at the rim (don't feed); peregrine falcons on the cliffs; short-horned lizards on warm ground; Great Basin rattlesnake (rare, below the rim); mountain lion (rarely seen).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): Great Basin bristlecone pine (Rainbow Point; some over 1,000 years old); ponderosa pine (vanilla-scented bark); greenleaf manzanita; sego lily (Utah's state flower, June); Markagunt penstemon (blue, found only on these plateaus, June–July); rock columbine (June–July); bronze evening primrose; scarlet gilia (summer); quaking aspen (gold in late September–early October).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — snow patches, muddy trails, late storms; summer — warm days (~25 °C), cold nights, afternoon thunderheads and lightning on the rim; fall — crisp, aspens gold, the clearest air; winter — deep snow on the plateau, hoodoos frosted white, the Wall Street side of the Navajo Loop and some rim sections closed, snowshoe and ski programmes. Dawn to afternoon the temperature can rise by ~22 °C (40 °F).
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park (2019) and one of the darkest places in North America reachable by paved road: the Milky Way arcs from horizon to horizon, Venus and Jupiter cast shadows; Dark Rangers with big telescopes, full-moon hikes among silver hoodoos, an Astronomy Festival in June.
- **Sounds**: wind in the ponderosas; ravens and nutcrackers calling at overlooks; swifts screaming overhead; the crunch of gravel and frost underfoot on the switchbacks; small stones clattering down after a thaw; horses' hooves and bells on the Peekaboo Loop; thunder rolling over the breaks; deep silence below the rim at night.

## Moments

- **Sunrise over the amphitheater** — the rim faces east, so dawn is the show: from Bryce, Inspiration, Sunset or Sunrise Point the hoodoos catch fire one row after another.
- **Snow on the hoodoos** — December–March (sometimes October–May): white caps on orange spires, the brochure's cover.
- **Freeze–thaw** — about 180 days a year the temperature crosses freezing overnight; small rockfalls after thaws.
- **Full-moon hikes** — monthly (spring–fall), rangers lead 1–2-mile walks among moonlit hoodoos (ages 8+, sign-up required).
- **Astronomy Festival** — around the new moon in June; telescopes on the rim; the Milky Way from Inspiration Point.
- **Geology Festival** — each July.
- **Prairie-dog spring** — Utah prairie dogs wake in April; pups appear above ground in June.
- **Summer storms** — July–August afternoons: thunderheads over the Paria Valley, lightning (get off the rim), then steaming, glowing rock.
- **Mossy Cave waterfall** — flows roughly May–October with ditch water; icicles fill the grotto in winter.
- **Aspen gold** — late September–early October along the southern drive.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a 512 × 1024-tile map, true north up, a thin strip with the rim running NNE–SSW. The Bryce Amphitheater and Fairyland (~5 km of rim and the breaks below) take ~450 tiles of height at ~1:7, where the hoodoos and switchbacks stay near life size (a hoodoo 5–45 m → 3–25 levels); the 24 km southern drive shrinks to ~400 tiles (~1:40) with its seven viewpoints kept as small pullouts; Mossy Cave and Bryce Canyon City sit at the northern edge; the plateau forest west of the road is a narrow band; the Under-the-Rim backcountry becomes a few connected benches.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the visitor centre; Bryce Canyon Lodge (interior: lobby, dining room, cabins); the General Store; Sunset Point (the crowd spot); Bryce Canyon City's shuttle station (just outside, a gateway strip with Ruby's Inn). 3D landmarks: Thor's Hammer, Queen Victoria, the Silent City's crowd of spires, Wall Street's slot with its tall firs, Two Bridges, the Wall of Windows, Tower Bridge, the Hat Shop, Natural Bridge, Boat Mesa; instanced hoodoo fields everywhere below the rim; Navajo Mountain and the Grand Staircase as distant scenery.
- **Activities**: rim walks and below-rim loops (table above); the **"Hike the Hoodoos" benchmark hunt** — find special benchmarks on nine trails and make a pencil rubbing or selfie for a reward: a ready-made in-game collectible; Junior Ranger booklet; wrangler-led **horse and mule rides** from the corral near Sunrise Point into the Peekaboo Loop (spring–fall); the **shuttle** as the amphitheater's fast travel; biking the shared-use path; full-moon hikes and telescope nights; **winter**: snowshoe walks (with ≥14 in of snow), cross-country skiing on the rim (never below it), a snowy Fairyland road; backpacking the Under-the-Rim Trail. Photo spots: Bryce Point at sunrise, Thor's Hammer from Sunset Point, the Wall Street switchbacks from above, Queen Victoria, the Silent City from Inspiration Point, Tower Bridge, the Hat Shop, Natural Bridge, Rainbow Point, the Mossy Cave waterfall, snow on the hoodoos.
- **Passport stamps**: Bryce Canyon Visitor Center (bookstore).
- **With friends / with the world**: the sunrise crowd at Bryce and Sunset points is the natural gathering; a party hiking the Queen's Garden/Navajo loop together (down through the tunnels, up Wall Street); horse rides in a line; a telescope night where each player takes a turn at the eyepiece; a shared benchmark hunt. Open-world meeting spots: Sunset Point, the General Store porch, the lodge lawn, the shuttle station, Inspiration Point after dark.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on trails and never shortcut switchbacks (the slopes are crumbling); stay back from the rim edge; don't feed the chipmunks and squirrels; yield to horses on the Peekaboo Loop; pets only on paved surfaces; wheels only on pavement; don't climb on or carve the hoodoos; get off the rim and away from lone trees when lightning comes; carry water and go slowly — the air is thin at 2,500 m; firewood is bought, never gathered.
- **Implementation challenges**: hundreds of thin, tall, overhanging spires (instanced 3D hoodoos rather than tiles); the rim faces **east** while the game camera looks north, so the great amphitheater view is seen side-on — overlooks may need a turned camera; steep switchback stairs in a slot (Wall Street); tunnels through thin fins (Queen's Garden); deep seasonal snow on the plateau and below the rim, with trails that close in winter; frost and thaw as a visible daily cycle; a strong night-sky mode.
