# Grand Canyon National Park — `GRCA`

> Arizona · established February 26, 1919 · 4,862.9 km² (1,201,647 acres) · 4,430,653 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 358–2,793 m · NPS region IMR (DOI Region 8, Lower Colorado Basin)
>
> A mile-deep chasm cut by 446 km (277 river miles) of the Colorado River: a historic rim village of log and stone
> lodges on the South Rim, a forested and fire-scarred North Rim 16 km away across the void, and a roadless inner
> canyon reached only on foot, by mule or by raft. Walking it in Hearthlight should feel like leaning on a railing
> above an impossible drop, then taking the switchbacks down through bands of colour to a green oasis by a cold river.

## Reading the map

The park is a ragged ribbon following the Colorado River. It starts in the north-east at **Lees Ferry** (below Glen
Canyon Dam and Page) as nothing but the slot of **Marble Canyon**, running ~100 km due south with the Navajo Nation
on its east rim. At the **Little Colorado** confluence the river turns west and the canyon opens into the Grand
Canyon proper, between the **South Rim** (Coconino Plateau, ~2,100–2,270 m) and the **North Rim** (Kaibab Plateau,
~2,400–2,700 m). West of the centre the river bows north-west around the Great Thumb Mesa (Havasupai Reservation to
the south; the park keeps a 0.25-mile rim strip), passes Kanab Creek and **Tuweep**, then loops south around the
Shivwits and Sanup plateaus (Hualapai Reservation on the south bank) to Lake Mead at the Grand Wash Cliffs. Only the
central ~40 km, the brochure's heart, has paved roads on both rims. Far west, **Tuweep** (Toroweap Overlook) waits
at the end of 61 mi of dirt road. **Supai and Havasu Falls** are Havasupai land, not park (8 mi on foot from Hualapai
Hilltop, tribal permit); only lower Havasu Creek below Beaver Falls is park. The Skywalk and Diamond Creek are Hualapai.

**South Rim.** AZ 64 comes 6.6 mi (10.6 km) north from Tusayan through the South Entrance to a 5 km strip of rim
village. At its east end the **Visitor Center** plaza sits behind **Mather Point**, with **Yavapai Point** (Geology
Museum) the next promontory west; **Market Plaza** (store, post office, bank, Yavapai Lodge) and the campgrounds lie
inland to the south. The historic **Village** is 3.4 km farther west. Along the rim from east to west stand
Verkamp's, Hopi House, El Tovar (the rail depot just below it, the line arriving from the south-west), Kachina and
Thunderbird lodges, Bright Angel Lodge, then Lookout and Kolb studios at the **Bright Angel Trailhead**. Village Loop
Drive is a one-way loop. The paved **Rim Trail** hugs the edge for 12.8 mi (20.6 km) from the South Kaibab Trailhead
to Hermits Rest, and between Yavapai Point and Verkamp's it carries the **Trail of Time**. West, **Hermit Road** runs
7 mi (11 km) past nine viewpoints to **Hermits Rest**, shuttle-only (red) from March 1 to November 30. East,
**Desert View Drive** follows the rim ~22 mi (35 km): the **Yaki Point / South Kaibab** spur (shuttle-only all
year), Shoshone, Grandview (the road swings south round the side-canyon heads), Moran, Tusayan Pueblo, Lipan and
Navajo points, then the **Watchtower** and the East Entrance. Free shuttles: Village (blue) and Kaibab/Rim (orange)
all year, Hermits Rest (red) March–November, Tusayan (purple) in summer.

**The inner canyon and the rim-to-rim line.** Below the South Rim the walls fall in steps (pale cliffs, red Supai
ledges, the massive **Redwall**) to a broad grey-green bench, the **Tonto Platform**, ~900 m down, cut by the black
**Inner Gorge** where the river runs 5 km north of the Village. Three maintained « corridor » trails cross along one
near-straight north–south line. The **Bright Angel Trail** zig-zags down a fault from Kolb Studio (two tunnels, the
1½-Mile and 3-Mile resthouses) to the cottonwoods of **Havasupai Gardens** (ex-Indian Garden), then down to the
river and along the River Trail to the **Silver Bridge**. The **South Kaibab** starts 5.4 km east on the Yaki spur
and follows an open ridge (Ooh Aah Point, Cedar Ridge, Skeleton Point, the Tipoff) to the **Black Bridge**. Both land
at **Bright Angel Campground** and **Phantom Ranch**, at the mouth of Bright Angel Creek. The **North Kaibab** climbs
22 km north-north-east up Bright Angel Canyon (The Box, Ribbon Falls, Cottonwood, Roaring Springs, Redwall Bridge,
Supai Tunnel) to its trailhead, 2.7 km by road north of the old lodge. The **Tonto Trail** runs east–west along the
bench, linking the unmaintained Hermit, Grandview, New Hance and Tanner trails. **2026**: since the August 29, 2026
flash flood in Bright Angel Canyon, Phantom Ranch, both campgrounds, both bridges and the North Kaibab are closed with
no reopening date. The Bright Angel is open only to the Tonto junction and the South Kaibab only to the Tipoff (NPS,
Sept. 3, 2026), so there is no rim-to-rim this year.

**North Rim.** AZ 67 runs 44 mi from Jacob Lake over the Kaibab Plateau's meadows to the **Bright Angel Point**
peninsula, between The Transept (west) and Roaring Springs Canyon (east). The developed area strings along its last
3 km: North Kaibab Trailhead, gas, Backcountry Information Center, General Store and campground, then the parking at
the former **Grand Canyon Lodge**. **Cape Royal Road** climbs east 5.4 mi to a « Y ». From there it is 2.6 mi north
to **Point Imperial** (2,683 m, the highest rim viewpoint), or 14.5 mi south down the **Walhalla Plateau** to **Cape
Royal** and Angels Window. Dirt roads lead west to Point Sublime (17.8 mi). The lightning-caused **Dragon Bravo
Fire** (from July 4, 2025; ~149,400 acres) destroyed the 1928/1937 lodge, the visitor center, the cabins and the
water systems on July 12. The North Rim reopened on **May 15, 2026** for day use until October 15, with the
campground, store and roads open but no lodging, no potable water and several trails closed (Bright Angel Point,
Widforss, Uncle Jim, Ken Patrick west, most of the Transept). Pink burn closures cover the plateau north and east. The
lodge's future goes through a separate NEPA process.

### Water

The **Colorado River** enters at Lees Ferry (950 m) flowing south down Marble Canyon, turns west at the Little
Colorado (river mile 61.5), dips to its southernmost point below Moran Point (Hance Rapid, mile 76.5), runs
west-north-west past Phantom Ranch (mile 88), bends north-west past Havasu Creek (157) and Lava Falls (179), then
south-west into Lake Mead (~360 m) at the Grand Wash Cliffs (mile 277). Released from deep in Glen Canyon Dam, it is
cold (8–13 °C) and clear green; flash floods from the Paria, the Little Colorado and side canyons turn it brick red.
Rapids are rated 1–10: Hance, Granite, Hermit, Crystal (born of a 1966 debris flow) and **Lava Falls** are the big
names. Side streams are set pieces: the turquoise **Little Colorado** (sacred to the Hopi), **Bright Angel Creek**
with **Ribbon Falls** and **Roaring Springs** (piped up to both rims), **Vasey's Paradise** (a spring bursting from
the Redwall, mile 32), **Deer Creek Falls** and **Thunder River** (miles 134–136), blue-green **Havasu Creek**. Lakes
are only sinkhole ponds on the Kaibab Plateau (Greenland, Swamp). The NPS warns against swimming in the Colorado
(cold, strong eddies); wading in Bright Angel Creek is the canyon-bottom treat.

### Relief

From **358 m** at Lake Mead to **2,793 m** at the fire lookout near the North Rim entrance; ~1,400 m deep at the
Village (rim ~2,100 m, river 732 m at Phantom Ranch). The North Rim is ~300 m higher and drains toward the canyon, so
its side canyons are longer and the river runs ~5 km from the South Rim but 10–13 km from the North Rim. The walls
are a layer cake to build as terraces, top down: **Kaibab limestone** caprock (cream rim), **Coconino sandstone** (a
pale ~100 m cliff), **Hermit shale and Supai** (red slopes and ledges, ~300 m), the **Redwall** (a sheer ~150 m cliff,
stained red), the **Tonto Platform** (a wide walkable bench of soft shale), the brown **Tapeats** ledge and the
black-and-pink **Vishnu schist** of the Inner Gorge. Cliffs = Coconino, Redwall, Tapeats, Inner Gorge; banks = the
Supai and shale slopes; stairs = the switchbacks (« Jacob's Ladder » through the Redwall on the Bright Angel). The
free-standing **temples** are the 3D peaks, many nearly level with the South Rim: Vishnu (~2,390 m), Brahma (~2,300 m),
Wotans Throne (2,353 m), Shiva (2,308 m), Zoroaster (2,173 m), Isis (2,138 m), Cheops Pyramid (1,646 m), plus O'Neill
Butte and Horseshoe Mesa (NPS map heights; OSM differs by up to ~150 m). Behind the North Rim the Kaibab Plateau
rolls, pocked with sinkholes; at Toroweap a cinder cone and frozen lava cascades hang over the gorge.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: river (730 m): sand beaches, tamarisk, coyote willow, arrowweed, mesquite, Phantom
  Ranch's planted cottonwoods · Tonto Platform (1,100–1,300 m): blackbrush, Mormon tea, prickly pear, Utah agave ·
  slopes (1,300–2,000 m): pinyon, Utah juniper, cliffrose, banana yucca · South Rim (2,100 m): ponderosa pine,
  pinyon-juniper, Gambel oak, sagebrush · North Rim (2,400–2,790 m): ponderosa, then white fir, Douglas-fir, spruce
  and quaking aspen around open meadows — and since 2025 black snags, fireweed and aspen sprouts across the burn.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#E6D9BC` Kaibab limestone rim · `#D9BE8C` Coconino sandstone cliff · `#B4573B`
  Supai red slopes · `#C2714A` iron-stained Redwall · `#9AA07B` Tonto Platform · `#6F5A4B` Tapeats ledges · `#3D383E`
  Vishnu schist · `#4E8E7E` the dam-cold green river · `#9C5E3E` the river after a flash flood · `#44BFC6` Havasu and
  Little Colorado turquoise · `#3F5B3A` ponderosa canopy.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): California condor (thermals over the Village and Navajo Bridge, numbered wing tags,
  spring–autumn) · common raven (every viewpoint) · Rocky Mountain elk (Village and Desert View Drive, bugling
  Sept–Oct) · mule deer (both rims at dusk) · Abert's squirrel (South Rim) and the black, white-tailed Kaibab squirrel
  (North Rim only) · rock squirrel (viewpoints — the park's top biter) · desert bighorn sheep (inner-canyon cliffs) ·
  American bison (House Rock herd, North Rim meadows) · pink Grand Canyon rattlesnake (only here, warm months) · bark
  scorpion (glows under UV at Phantom Ranch) · ringtail (Phantom Ranch at night) · canyon wren · bald eagle (winter,
  on the river) · humpback chub (native fish at the Little Colorado).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): ponderosa pine (vanilla-scented bark) · quaking aspen (gold late Sept–early Oct) · Utah
  agave (one tall bloom after 20–25 years, May–July) · cliffrose (April–June) · banana yucca (spring) · Engelmann
  prickly pear (April–May) · sacred datura (white night blooms, summer) · sentry milk-vetch (a tiny endangered pea
  found only on the canyon's rim limestone) · redbud (pink, April, side canyons) · Fremont cottonwood (gold in
  November).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter: snowy South Rim, icy trails, North Rim road closed, rare cloud inversions · spring:
  wind, late snow, agave and cactus blooms, North Rim opens mid-May · summer: rims at ~25 °C, Phantom Ranch >40 °C,
  then the monsoon from early July (afternoon storms, lightning, rainbows, flash floods) · autumn: the best hiking,
  aspens, elk rut, first snow in November.
- **Night**: International Dark Sky Park (2019): the Milky Way over the chasm, El Tovar's lit porch, bats at dusk,
  ringtails and glowing scorpions at Phantom Ranch, the June Star Party's telescopes, moonlit temples.
- **Sounds**: the canyon wren's falling whistle · ravens' croaks and wingbeats · wind in ponderosa needles · shuttle
  air brakes, the depot's bell and whistle · mule shoes on stone · the river's roar rising faintly at Mohave and Pima
  points, full volume in the Inner Gorge · Bright Angel Creek · elk bugles · monsoon thunder rolling down the canyon.

## Moments

- **Sunrise and sunset**: sunrise at Mather and Yaki points and Point Imperial; sunset at Hopi Point (the red shuttle
  runs until an hour after sunset), Lipan Point and Cape Royal. The temples light up one by one above a blue floor.
- **Monsoon storms** (early July–mid-September): afternoon thunderheads, lightning across the canyon, rainbows below
  the rim, red waterfalls off the cliffs, a brown river.
- **Cloud inversion**: on a few winter mornings after a storm, fog fills the canyon to just below the rim and the
  temples stand up like islands. Rare.
- **Condor fly-by** (spring–autumn): condors with 3 m wings ride the late-morning thermals over the Village,
  Lookout Studio and Navajo Bridge.
- **Elk rut** (mid-September–October): bulls bugle on the Village lawns and along Desert View Drive.
- **The train arrives**: the Grand Canyon Railway pulls into the 1910 depot around midday and leaves mid-afternoon.
- **Grand Canyon Star Party**: eight nights every June, telescopes lined up behind the Visitor Center.
- **North Rim aspens** (late September–early October), with green shoots in the 2025 burn.
- **Running Lava Falls** (river mile 179): the biggest rapid, scouted from shore before the plunge.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a **1536 × 1024**-tile map of the central canyon, Hermits Rest to Desert View (~35 km) by
  Tusayan to Point Imperial (~36 km). Near true scale (~1.5–2 m per tile): the Village's historic strip (El Tovar to
  Kolb Studio, ~600 m), the Visitor Center plaza and Mather Point (~500 m), Phantom Ranch and Bright Angel Campground
  (~700 m), the North Rim developed area (~2 km). Compressed 5–15×: Hermit Road (11 km → ~350 tiles, nine stops),
  Desert View Drive (35 km → ~500 tiles, six stops), the corridor trails (15–22 km → ~250–350 tiles of switchbacks
  each, every resthouse kept), the 16 km rim-to-rim gap (→ ~500 tiles). The 1,750 m drop becomes ~12–14 height
  levels. Split off: Tuweep (a 256 × 256 vignette) and the river (a linear 256 × 2048 instance: Lees Ferry → Phantom
  → Lava Falls → Diamond Creek). Leave Supai and Havasu Falls (Havasupai land) off the map; the river passes the
  mouth of Havasu Creek.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the Visitor Center plaza (shuttle hub), the Village (El Tovar's porch and lobby, Hopi House,
  Verkamp's, the depot and its train, Bright Angel Lodge's geology fireplace, Lookout and Kolb studios), Hermits Rest,
  the Watchtower (spiral interior with Hopi murals, roof terrace), Phantom Ranch's canteen, and the North Rim's
  rebuilding (stone foundations, a ranger tent at the old lodge parking). 3D landmarks: the temples, Angels Window,
  the Black Bridge and its tunnel, the Watchtower, Vulcan's Throne.
- **Activities**: the Rim Trail as a promenade of viewpoints; day hikes to the resthouses; rim-to-rim as a 2–3-day
  expedition with water planning; photo spots (Mather Point sunrise, Hopi Point sunset, Lookout Studio, Ooh Aah
  Point, Plateau Point, Angels Window, Point Imperial, the Watchtower's windows, Toroweap's edge); a condor log by
  wing-tag number; ranger talks (condors at Lookout Studio, geology at Yavapai); Junior Ranger (South Rim booklet,
  the « Canyon Explorer » edition for inner-canyon hikers, a Trail of Time task: sketch each layer's rock, oldest
  first); rafting with a paddle crew or in a wooden dory; vehicles: car, the four colour-coded shuttles, Greenway
  bikes, mules, the steam train, rafts.
- **Passport stamps**: the official list: Grand Canyon (Visitor Center Plaza), Verkamp's, Kolb Studio, Yavapai
  Geology Museum, Desert View Watchtower, Tusayan Ruin (now Tusayan Pueblo), Indian Garden (now Havasupai Gardens),
  Phantom Ranch, North Rim, Tuweep. In 2026 the North Rim Visitor Center is gone and Phantom Ranch is closed.
- **With friends / with the world**: sunrise at Mather Point and sunset at Hopi Point, where strangers gather anyway;
  a party rim-to-rim with the hikers' real « key swap » (half the party starts on each rim, they meet at Phantom Ranch
  and trade car keys); up to eight paddlers in one raft through a rapid; a mule string in single file; postcards
  « mailed by mule » from Phantom Ranch, delivered to friends a day later; the Star Party; group photos on the
  Watchtower terrace and at Angels Window.
- **Rules of the park**: stay behind the railings; never throw rocks (hikers below — the throw action is off on
  rims); 100 ft / 30 m (two bus lengths) from elk, bison and condors; don't feed squirrels or ravens; mules have
  the right of way (step to the uphill side and wait), and so do uphill hikers; in summer hike before 10 a.m. or
  after 4 p.m., never rim-to-river-and-back in a day; fill bottles at resthouses (none on the South Kaibab); pack
  everything out; leave rocks, fossils and potsherds; no drones; respect the fire and flood closures.
- **Implementation challenges**: the **camera looks north**, so the South Rim's own cliffs face away and hide
  behind the rim while the North Rim's walls face the player: the descent from the Village needs a cutaway · 12–14
  stacked terraces, switchback stairs and haze layers (bluer with depth) to sell a mile of depth · big free-standing
  temples · whitewater rapids and a raft mini-game · soaring condors · a steam train and mule strings · a fog-plane
  weather state for the cloud inversion · burned forest · rims 16 km apart but ~340 km by road (a Trans-Canyon
  shuttle fast travel) · heat that rises with depth · tribal lands on three sides left respectfully off the map.
