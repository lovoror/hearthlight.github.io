# Wrangell–St. Elias National Park & Preserve — `WRST`

> Alaska · established December 2, 1980 (national monument since December 1, 1978) · 33,682.6 km² (8,323,146 acres) of park, ~53,300 km² (13.2 million acres) with the preserve · 108,840 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 0–5,489 m · NPS region AKR (Alaska)
>
> The largest US national park, where four mountain ranges and nine of the country's sixteen highest peaks meet, with glaciers covering a quarter of the land, living Ahtna, Upper Tanana, Eyak and Tlingit homelands, and a red copper-mill town at the end of a 59-mile gravel road. In Hearthlight it should feel like a road trip into the frontier: dust and one-lane railway bridges, a footbridge into a quirky village, then a ghost-industrial mill town at the edge of a glacier you can walk onto.

## Reading the map

The park map is nearly square, north up, and shows a vast green territory shaped like a fat boot: its **west edge** runs along the Copper River (the Richardson Highway and the towns of Glennallen, Copper Center, Kenny Lake and Chitina lie just outside it), its **north edge** along the Tok Cutoff (Slana) and the Tetlin lowlands, its **east edge** is the straight 141st-meridian border with Canada's Yukon (Kluane National Park beyond), and its **south** reaches the Gulf of Alaska from the Bering Glacier area to Yakutat Bay, with a narrow coastal strip around the Malaspina Glacier to Yakutat. Light green (preserve) and darker green (park) alternate: the preserve fills the northern and western lowlands (sport hunting allowed), the park the high mountains. Beige-grey dotted blocks mark Native Corporation lands along the Copper River and the roads.

Four mountain systems meet in the middle. The **Wrangell Mountains** form a round white volcanic massif in the centre-north: **Mount Sanford** (16,237 ft / 4,949 m), **Mount Drum** (12,010 ft) closest to the highway, **Mount Wrangell** (14,163 ft / 4,317 m, the active volcano), Mount Zanetti, Mount Jarvis, and to the south-east **Mount Blackburn** (16,390 ft / 4,996 m), Atna Peaks, Rime and Parka peaks, Regal Mountain. The **Chugach Mountains** run west–east along the southern third above the Bagley Icefield and the Bering Glacier; the **St. Elias Mountains** rise in the south-east with **Mount Saint Elias** (18,008 ft / 5,489 m) on the border, Mount Bona and Mount Churchill (volcanoes over 15,000 ft) north of them, and Mount Logan (19,551 ft, Canada) just across the line; the Nutzotin and Mentasta ranges (the Alaska Range) close the north-east. Between the Wrangells and the Chugach, the **Chitina River** (Ahtna Tsedi Na') flows west in a wide braided valley to join the Copper River at Chitina.

Roads enter only from the west and north. From Copper Center (Wrangell–St. Elias Headquarters and Visitor Center with the Ahtna Cultural Center, Mile 106.8 Richardson Highway), the **Edgerton Highway** (route 10) runs east past Kenny Lake and Liberty Falls to **Chitina** (ranger station, 581 ft); there the **McCarthy Road** crosses the Copper River and runs ~59 mi east along the old railroad grade — Strelna, the **Kuskulana Bridge**, the **Gilahina Trestle**, the Crystalline Hills, Long Lake — to the Kennicott River and the **McCarthy Road Information Station**. Beyond: the small town of **McCarthy** (1,400 ft) and, 5 mi up the valley on the edge of the Kennicott Glacier, **Kennecott** (2,100 ft, « Kennecott Visitor Center, summer only »), under Donoho Peak and Bonanza Ridge (the company town and mines are spelled Kennecott, the glacier, river and valley Kennicott, after the naturalist Robert Kennicott); the dirty-looking Kennicott and Root glaciers flow down from the Stairway Icefall. In the north, the **Nabesna Road** leaves the Tok Cutoff at **Slana** (ranger station) and runs 42 mi south-east through lake country (Jack, Copper and Tanada lakes, Kendesnii Campground) between the Mentasta Mountains and the Wrangells to the old mining site of **Nabesna** (3,100 ft). Isolated inholdings (Chisana, May Creek, Dan Creek) are marked « Private property interspersed with public lands ».

The **coast** occupies the bottom right: the enormous **Malaspina Glacier** (Tlingit Sít' Tlein, « Big Glacier ») — the world's largest piedmont glacier — spreads like a white fan onto the coastal plain, fed by the Seward and Agassiz glaciers from Mount Saint Elias; Icy Bay and Taan Fiord cut into its west side, Sitkagi Bluffs and Point Manby edge it, and to the east **Yakutat Bay** leads up Disenchantment Bay to the **Hubbard Glacier** (a tidewater glacier that repeatedly blocks Russell Fjord). Yakutat (ranger station, 33 ft) is just outside. Map extras: a 0–30 km / 0–30 mi scale, a note that ~750,000 of the park's 13+ million acres are non-federal (Native Corporations, private owners, the State), and the Native place-name table: Mount Wrangell is **K'ełt'aeni** (« The One that Controls Weather ») or **Uk'eledi** when smoking (« The One with Smoke on It »), Mount Sanford **Hwniindi K'ełt'aeni** and Mount Drum **Hwdaandi K'ełt'aeni** (Upriver and Downriver K'ełt'aeni), Mount Blackburn **K'a'si Tl'aadi** (« The One at Cold Headwaters »), Mount Saint Elias **Was'eitushaa** (Tlingit, « Mountain Inland of Was'ei »), the Nabesna River **Naabiah Niign** (Upper Tanana, « Along the Muddy River »).

### Water

The whole west and centre drain to the **Copper River**, a huge silty river that loops around the Wrangells from Slana to Chitina and cuts through the Chugach to the Gulf (one of the very few rivers to breach the coastal ranges); it carries a famous salmon run (sockeye and king, June–August) harvested by Ahtna fish wheels and the Chitina dipnet fishery. Its tributaries are wide **braided glacial rivers** — the Chitina (Tsedi Na'), Nizina, Kennicott, Kuskulana, Gilahina, Nabesna (Naabiah Niign), Chisana — grey with rock flour and swollen on hot afternoons. Glaciers cover about a quarter of the park: valley glaciers like the Kennicott (its lower 10 km buried under dark rubble) and the clean white Root Glacier; the Nabesna Glacier (one of the longest valley glaciers in North America); the Bagley Icefield; on the coast the **Malaspina** piedmont lobe and the tidewater **Hubbard Glacier**, which has twice dammed Russell Fjord (1986, 2002). Ice-dammed Hidden Creek Lake above the Kennicott Glacier drains suddenly most summers, flooding the Kennicott River at McCarthy. Lakes: Twin, Jack, Copper and Tanada lakes on the Nabesna Road, Long Lake on the McCarthy Road, Skolai lakes in the east, Malaspina Lake on the coast. Falls: Liberty Falls (roadside), Chitistone Falls (backcountry).

### Relief

From sea level on the Gulf of Alaska to **Mount Saint Elias, 5,489 m (18,008 ft)**, only ~16 km from the ocean — one of the greatest coastal reliefs on Earth. In the interior the land steps from the Copper River basin (~140–400 m, flat spruce plateau cut by the river's bluffs) to the rolling volcanic highlands and then the ice-capped Wrangell volcanoes (4,300–5,000 m), broad and dome-like rather than spiky. Along the McCarthy Road the ground is gentle except where the old railway crosses gorges on bridges and trestles (Kuskulana, Gilahina). Kennecott clings to a steep slope on the Kennicott Glacier's lateral moraine (~600 m) with Bonanza Ridge rising to ~2,100 m behind it; the mill building itself steps down the hill in terraces. In the game: bluffs and terraces for the Copper River, a gravel road with 3D trestles, a steep hillside town built of stepped levels, a rubble-covered glacier (grey-brown hummocks) turning into white ice with crevasses and blue pools, 3D volcano domes with a steam plume on Mount Wrangell, and the St. Elias giants as a far backdrop on the coastal vignette.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: 150–900 m boreal forest of white and black spruce with aspen, paper birch and balsam poplar on the river terraces, willow and alder on gravel bars; fireweed carpets around Kennecott and along the road; ~900–1,200 m shrub tundra with dwarf birch; above ~1,200 m alpine tundra and fellfield (Dall sheep country); ice above. The Yakutat coast is Sitka spruce–hemlock rainforest and beach meadows.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#9E3B2A` Kennecott barn red · `#E7E1D2` white window trim · `#6A6A62` glacial rubble (moraine-covered ice) · `#DCE9F2` clean glacier ice · `#4FA3D1` supraglacial meltwater pools · `#8C8878` silty braided-river gravel · `#A7A48F` grey Copper River water · `#2E4A35` spruce forest · `#C9559A` fireweed · `#3E7F66` copper-green malachite on ore rocks.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **Dall sheep** (ridges above the Nabesna Road and Bonanza Ridge — the park has one of the largest populations), mountain goat (Chugach and coast), **caribou** (Mentasta and Chisana herds), moose, grizzly and black bears (Kennecott valley, berries in August), gray wolf, wolverine, lynx, beaver, the Copper River bison herd, **trumpeter swans** on the Nabesna Road lakes, bald eagle, willow and rock ptarmigan, Arctic ground squirrel, pika; **sockeye and king salmon** in the Copper River; harbor seals on Hubbard Glacier's ice, sea otters and sea lions on the coast.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): fireweed (July–August, the Kennecott colour), wild rose, Arctic lupine and mountain avens (June), alpine forget-me-not, moss campion, Labrador tea, crowberry and blueberry (August), soapberry (bear food), aspen gold in early September.
- **Weather & seasons**: interior summers are the sunniest and warmest of the Alaska parks (long days, thunderstorms on the Wrangells, dust on the roads), with smoke from wildfires some years; September turns aspen and birch gold, first snow on the volcanoes; winters bring −40 °C cold snaps, the McCarthy Road unmaintained and the Kennecott shuttle stopped. The coast is rain and fog most of the year.
- **Night**: ~19.5 h of sun at Kennecott on the June solstice (sunrise ~3:47, sunset ~23:17 AKDT); dark, clear winter skies with strong aurora over the Wrangells; steam from Mount Wrangell glowing at sunset.
- **Sounds**: gravel popping under tires and the rattle of an old railway bridge, the shuttle van's diesel, wind through the empty mill, creaking corrugated iron, meltwater gurgling into moulins, the crunch of crampons, ravens, a Dall ram's horns clashing in autumn, a fish wheel's paddles turning in the Copper River.

## Moments

- **Walking onto the Root Glacier** (June–September): from rubble to white ice with blue pools, moulins and crevasses — with crampons, a short walk from Kennecott.
- **The mill tour**: guided tours climb through the 14-storey Concentration Mill where ore from Bonanza Ridge came down by aerial tram (1911–1938).
- **Mount Wrangell steaming**: on clear days a plume rises from the summit craters — K'ełt'aeni, « the one that controls weather ».
- **Copper River salmon run** (June–August): fish wheels turning and dipnetters on the rocks at Chitina, a living Ahtna tradition.
- **Hidden Creek Lake outburst** (most summers, often July): the ice-dammed lake drains under the Kennicott Glacier and the Kennicott River rises at the McCarthy footbridge.
- **The midnight-sun road trip**: the 59-mile McCarthy Road's bridges and trestles, then the footbridge into a car-free village.
- **Dall sheep rut** (November–December) and summer lambs on Nabesna Road ridges; trumpeter swans on the lakes (May–September).
- **Aurora and −40 °C** (October–March): an empty Kennecott under snow, reached on skis.
- **Hubbard Glacier calving** (summer, from boats or cruise ships out of Yakutat): a 10-km, ~100 m-high ice front.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a curated map of **~1,536 × 1,024 tiles** in linked pieces. (1) **Kennecott & McCarthy** ~640 × 480 tiles: the mill town near true scale (~800 × 500 m of buildings on a slope), the Root Glacier's edge and first icefield of the Root Glacier (~2 km compressed ×2), the start of the mine trails (their upper parts compressed as climbs to ruin set pieces), the Old Wagon Road compressed ×3 down to McCarthy and the Kennicott River footbridge. (2) **The McCarthy Road** as a compressed drive (95 km → ~800 tiles) with true-scale set pieces: Chitina and the Copper River bridge with fish wheels, Kuskulana Bridge, Gilahina Trestle, Long Lake, the road-end kiosk. (3) **Copper Center** ~256 × 192 tiles (visitor center campus, Ahtna Cultural Center, the bluff view of three volcanoes). Optional later: (4) the **Nabesna Road** (Slana, Kendesnii Campground, Skookum Volcano) and (5) a **Yakutat/Hubbard** vignette by plane. The Wrangell volcanoes and Mount Saint Elias are backdrop peaks.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the Concentration Mill (a 3D landmark: stepped red building with white trim climbing a hillside, conveyor galleries, the tramway terminal), the Leaching Plant, the General Store & Post Office (exhibits interior), the Blackburn School visitor center, bunkhouses, hospital, power plant, the National Creek trestle, Silk Stocking Row cottages; McCarthy's false-front main street and footbridge; the Copper Center visitor center and the Ahtna fish wheel; the Kuskulana steel bridge and Gilahina wooden trestle; 3D landmarks: Mount Wrangell (with steam), Sanford, Drum, Blackburn, Donoho Peak, the Stairway Icefall.
- **Activities**: road trip (car on the gravel road, then walking or the shuttle — a fun « leave your car at the bridge » moment); glacier walk with crampons (a gear check at the trailhead), guided mill tour (interiors), mine-ruin hikes with a tramway story; photo spots — the mill from the Root Glacier trail, the glacier's blue pools, Kuskulana Bridge, Gilahina Trestle, Mount Wrangell's plume from Copper Center, fireweed with red buildings; fishing and fish wheels at Chitina (Ahtna guides); packrafting the Kennicott River; flightseeing over the Wrangells; ranger talks and walks at Kennecott; Junior Ranger book; winter mode with skiing into a snowbound Kennecott; backcountry fly-in at Skolai Pass.
- **Passport stamps**: « Copper Center » (Wrangell–St. Elias Visitor Center), « Chitina » (Chitina Ranger Station), « Kennecott » (Kennecott Visitor Center), « Slana » (Slana Ranger Station) and « Hubbard Glacier – Yakutat » (Yakutat Ranger Station).
- **With friends / with the world**: a party road trip with one car and a flat tyre to fix together on the McCarthy Road; roped-up glacier walking (one player leads with the ice axe); the mill tour as a group climb through the building; gathering spots — the footbridge (everyone arrives on foot), McCarthy's main street, the Kennecott visitor center porch, the Root Glacier's first blue pool; a group photo in front of the mill.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): respect private property — much of McCarthy, Kennecott and the roadsides is private (stay on marked trails, don't enter unlabelled buildings); never enter unstable mine ruins or collapsed structures; leave artifacts and ore rocks where they lie; cross glaciers only with crampons and away from moulins; carry bear spray, use bear-resistant food containers (required in the backcountry); keep distance from moose (they injure more people than bears); ATVs only on permitted trails with a permit (Nabesna); pack out everything.
- **Implementation challenges**: debris-covered glaciers (grey-brown hummocky ground that is really ice) turning into clean white ice with crevasses, moulins and meltwater pools; a hillside mill town with many interiors and terraces; a long gravel road with dust, potholes and historic trestles; the private/public land patchwork; volcanoes with steam; a car-free village reached by a footbridge; the scale problem of the largest park (most of it only as backdrop and plane hops); winter isolation.
