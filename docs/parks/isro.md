# Isle Royale National Park — `ISRO`

> Michigan · established April 3, 1940 · 2,314.0 km² (571,790 acres, of which the main island is ~535 km²; the rest is Lake Superior out to 4.5 mi) · 29,091 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 183–425 m · NPS region MWR (Midwest; DOI Region 3, Great Lakes)
>
> A roadless 72-km island of parallel volcanic ridges in the middle of Lake Superior, reached only by ferry or seaplane, where moose wade in beaver ponds and wolves are heard more than seen. In Hearthlight it should feel like an expedition: step off the boat at a tiny harbour, shoulder your pack and walk the Greenstone Ridge from one end of the island to the other, camping on fjord-like coves.

## Reading the map

The island lies diagonally across the sheet, north up: a long, narrow body 72 km from its south-west tip (The Head, Cumberland Point, Washington Harbor) to its north-east tip (Blake Point, The Palisades), 15–20 km off the Minnesota/Ontario shore and ~90 km from Michigan's Keweenaw Peninsula. The whole island is combed into **parallel ridges running SW–NE**, like a washboard: the **Greenstone Ridge** is the central spine running almost the full length; the **Minong Ridge** parallels it along the north-west shore; **Red Oak**, **Feldtmann** and **Houghton** ridges form the south-west lobe. Between the ridges lie long, narrow lakes and swamps inland, and at the ends of the valleys the lake floods in as **fjord-like harbours and coves**. The ridges have steep north-west faces and long gentle south-east slopes, so the north shore is cliffy and the south shore is ragged with bays.

There are **no roads**. The two hubs sit at the two ends: **Windigo** in the south-west, at the head of Washington Harbor (visitor centre, store, camper cabins, Washington Creek campground, dock for the Minnesota boats), and **Rock Harbor** at the north-east, on the long sheltered channel of Rock Harbor (visitor centre, Rock Harbor Lodge and cottages, marina, restaurant, the busiest docks, where the Michigan ferries and the Voyageur II land). Park summer headquarters is on **Mott Island** in Rock Harbor channel, and the winter/mainland headquarters and visitor centre are in **Houghton, Michigan** (bottom of the inset). A string of classic spots lines Rock Harbor channel south-west of the lodge: Three Mile, Daisy Farm (with the Mount Ojibway tower above it), the Rock Harbor Lighthouse and Edisen Fishery on the far shore, Moskey Basin at the channel's end; Tobin Harbor lies just north of Rock Harbor, and Lookout Louise, Scoville Point and Blake Point are at the north-east tip.

The trail system reads like a ladder: the **Greenstone Ridge Trail** runs the spine from Washington Creek (Windigo) to Lookout Louise (40 mi); the **Minong Ridge Trail** runs the rugged north-west ridge from Windigo to McCargoe Cove; **Rock Harbor Trail** follows the south-east shore of the channel; the **Feldtmann** loop circles the south-west lobe past Feldtmann Lake, the Big Siskiwit River and Siskiwit Bay; and "rungs" cross between them at Island Mine, Ishpeming Point (lookout tower), Hatchet Lake, Chickenbone Lake, Lake Richie and Daisy Farm. Campgrounds sit at the coves (Huginnin Cove, Todd Harbor, McCargoe Cove, Belle Isle, Duncan Bay, Lane Cove, Chippewa Harbor, Malone Bay, Siskiwit Bay) and on inland lakes (Desor, Feldtmann, Hatchet, Chickenbone, Richie, Whittlesey). **Siskiwit Lake**, the largest inland lake, fills the middle of the south shore behind Malone Bay; **Lake Desor** sits under Mount Desor, the high point (425 m).

Around the island, the map dots four lighthouses (Rock of Ages off the SW end, Isle Royale Light on Menagerie Island in Siskiwit Bay, Rock Harbor Light, Passage Island Light off the NE tip) and draws the boat routes: from Grand Portage straight to Windigo, from Windigo around the north shore to Rock Harbor (5 h) and back along the south shore (4.5 h), from Copper Harbor and Houghton to Rock Harbor.

### Water

- **Lake Superior** (surface ~183 m): the island sits in its north-west quarter. Cold (surface often 4–13 °C even in summer), clear, with fog, big storms, waves and seiches but no real tides; a dozen major shipwrecks lie in the park waters (America, Algoma, Monarch, Kamloops, Emperor, Chester A. Congdon, George M. Cox…) and are dived with permits. The lake freezes between the island and the Canadian shore only in hard winters (the "ice bridge" wolves crossed around 1948–1949).
- **Harbours and coves**: Washington Harbor (Windigo), Rock Harbor (a 16-km channel sheltered by a chain of islands), Tobin Harbor, Moskey Basin, Chippewa Harbor, Malone Bay, Siskiwit Bay (south shore); McCargoe Cove, Todd Harbor, Five Finger Bay, Duncan Bay, Robinson Bay, Huginnin Cove (north shore).
- **Inland lakes**: Siskiwit Lake (largest, with Ryan Island), Lake Desor, Feldtmann, Richie, Chickenbone, Livermore, LeSage, Intermediate, Whittlesey, Ojibway, Sargent, Harvey, Hatchet; countless beaver ponds and bogs. Portages link the central lakes for canoes.
- **Streams**: Washington Creek (west to Washington Harbor — the moose creek), Big and Little Siskiwit rivers (south to Siskiwit Bay), Grace Creek, Tobin Creek; no waterfalls of note.
- Old beach ridges and Suzy's Cave (a wave-cut arch now far from the water) record higher post-glacial lake levels.

### Relief

Lowest point 183 m (Lake Superior); highest 425 m (Mount Desor, 1,394 ft) — the whole island stands only ~240 m above the lake, but it is all ridges. The rock is a stack of ancient basalt lava flows and conglomerates tilted gently to the south-east: each ridge is a **cuesta** with a steep, cliffy north-west face and a long gentle south-east back slope, and the soft layers between them became the valleys, lakes and flooded harbours. Summits (Mount Desor 425 m, Ishpeming Point 416 m, Sugar Mountain 415 m, Mount Siskiwit 367 m, Mount Ojibway 345 m, Mount Franklin 327 m, Pine Mountain 283 m) are forested humps, three with fire lookout towers. In the game: stepped HIGH plateaus in long SW–NE bands, each with one cliff face on its north-west side and a walkable bank/slope on the south-east; stairs where trails climb the steep sides; long narrow lakes in the troughs; the coast of fjords made of flooded troughs; 3D landmarks are the lookout towers and lighthouses rather than peaks.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: shores and the cold NE end: boreal forest of balsam fir, white spruce, paper birch and aspen, hung with old-man's-beard lichen, with rock pools and lichen-crusted basalt at the waterline; interior south-west ridges (warmer): northern hardwoods — sugar maple and yellow birch (the "sugar bush" around Mount Desor and Sugar Mountain), red oak on Red Oak Ridge; open ridge tops with thin soil, juniper, bearberry and wind-bent spruce; valleys with cedar swamps, alder, beaver ponds, sphagnum bogs; thimbleberry thickets along trails and shores.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#3E4A45` dark basalt shoreline rock · `#6F7B5E` lichen-covered ridge rock · `#2F4F3A` spruce-fir forest · `#6E8F4A` maple ridges in summer · `#D9892E` / `#C4452F` September maples · `#1F4E6B` deep Lake Superior · `#5E9AA8` clear shallows over rock · `#8BA7A0` fog grey-green · `#B9463A` thimbleberry red · `#5C7D5A` greenstone pebble (chlorastrolite).
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): moose (wading in Washington Creek, Hidden Lake, Ojibway and Feldtmann lakes at dawn and dusk; 524 counted in 2026), gray wolf (island-wide, rarely seen — tracks and scat on the trails; 37 counted in 2026 after the 2018–2019 relocation), beaver (ponds everywhere), red fox (bold around campgrounds), snowshoe hare, red squirrel, river otter, mink, common loon (inland lakes, summer), herring gull, bald eagle, osprey (Windigo), common merganser, pileated woodpecker; lake trout and coaster brook trout in the lake, northern pike in the inland lakes. No bears, deer, raccoons or skunks: only ~20 mammal species reached the island.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): thimbleberry (fruit late July–August), wild blueberry (August), calypso orchid and pink lady's slipper (June), wild iris (June, shorelines), old-man's-beard lichen (all year), devil's club (a disjunct Pacific plant, shady NE ravines), butterwort and other arctic-alpine plants on the spray-cooled rocks, sugar maple (red-orange in mid–late September), balsam fir (Christmas-tree smell).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring (from mid-April) — ice-out, snow on the ridges, few visitors; June — cold fog, black flies and mosquitoes, wildflowers; July–August — the warm season, calm evenings, thunderstorms, the ferries full; September — cold nights, colour on the maples, the moose rut, fewer bugs; October — gales, last boats; November–mid-April — closed, deep snow, the lake sometimes frozen to Canada.
- **Night**: among the darkest skies in the Midwest — the Milky Way down to the lake horizon, frequent **northern lights** from late August to October, loons wailing across the inland lakes, owls, and on rare nights a wolf howl; no light pollution except a distant ship.
- **Sounds**: waves slapping basalt, foghorns and ferry horns at the docks, the seaplane's drone, loons, gulls, the creak of the lighthouse and the lodge dock, wind in spruce, beaver tail slaps, moose splashing, mosquitoes' whine, thunder rolling over the lake.

## Moments

- **The crossing**: 1.5–6 hours by ferry (or 35 minutes by seaplane) from Minnesota or Michigan, arriving at Windigo or Rock Harbor; storms and fog can cancel boats for days.
- **Moose at dawn**: cows and calves wading in Washington Creek (Windigo) and Hidden Lake (Tobin Harbor) in summer; bull fights during the rut in September–October.
- **Wolf season**: tracks after rain, pups born in April–May; the Winter Study counts wolves and moose from the air every winter (37 wolves and 524 moose in 2026).
- **Northern lights** over the lake on clear nights, most often late August–October.
- **Thimbleberry season** (late July–August): the trails turn into a snack bar.
- **Sunrise at Scoville Point or Lookout Louise**, **sunset over Washington Harbor** at Windigo.
- **Fall colour** on the maple ridges (mid–late September).
- **Closing day**: the island closes November 1; ferries stop in late September and the last people leave.
- **Ranger programs**: Edisen Fishery demonstrations, Rock Harbor Lighthouse museum, MV Sandy trips to Passage Island.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a north-up water map **1280 × 832 tiles** (~1.9 × 1.25 km at 1.5 m/tile) with the island running diagonally from the lower left to the upper right, compressed ~32× along its length. Near true scale: Rock Harbor village (VC, lodge, marina, ~400 m), Windigo (VC, store, dock, creek, ~300 m), Edisen Fishery and the Rock Harbor Lighthouse, Daisy Farm and the Ojibway tower, Suzy's Cave, Lookout Louise. Compressed: the ridges and inland lakes (keep the ladder: 3–4 parallel ridges with the Greenstone spine, a dozen key lakes including Siskiwit and Desor), the harbours as fjords. Cut: most islets (keep a few in Rock Harbor channel and Washington Harbor), most campgrounds (keep ~10 with docks), Houghton and Grand Portage as off-map ports reached by the ferry scene.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Rock Harbor (main hub: ferries, seaplane, lodge), Windigo (second hub), Daisy Farm (backpacker crossroads), McCargoe Cove & Malone Bay (boat stops). 3D landmarks: the Ranger III and Queen IV ferries, a floatplane, Rock Harbor Lighthouse (white tower), Passage Island and Rock of Ages lights, three steel fire lookout towers (Ojibway, Ishpeming, Feldtmann), the Edisen Fishery net house and docks, moose antlers in the water. Interiors: Rock Harbor and Windigo visitor centres, Rock Harbor Lodge dining room, the lighthouse museum, Edisen's cabin, Ranger III's deck/lounge during the crossing.
- **Activities**: backpacking between campgrounds with a permit (the Greenstone traverse as the signature quest), canoe and kayak in Rock Harbor channel and across the inland lake portages, boat tours (MV Sandy), fishing (lake trout from a boat, pike in inland lakes), scuba diving on shipwrecks (a special underwater mode), photo spots — moose in Washington Creek, Scoville Point surf, Lookout Louise, the Ojibway tower view, Rock Harbor Lighthouse at sunset, aurora over a cove; junior ranger (the park's Wilderness Adventure Training); wildlife tracking (wolf tracks); no cars, no bikes, no pets.
- **Passport stamps**: the 2025 list gives "Houghton", "Rock Harbor", "Windigo" and "USNPS Ranger III" (on the ferry itself). In the game: Houghton on the mainland pier before boarding, the two island visitor centres, and a stamp kept aboard the Ranger III during the crossing.
- **With friends / with the world**: the party shares a ferry crossing (a social lounge scene) and a canoe per two; a cross-island relay (one group from Windigo, one from Rock Harbor, meet at Mount Desor); campfire circles at Daisy Farm; the open world gathers on the Rock Harbor dock when the ferry arrives and at the Ojibway tower; group photo at the Rock Harbor Lighthouse; "aurora watch" nights.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): no pets at all (dogs carry diseases to the wolves); keep 25+ yards from moose, never between a cow and calf, give bulls room in the rut; if a wolf approaches, back away and make noise, never feed; store food properly; filter or boil lake water; camp only at designated campgrounds with a permit; take nothing — rocks, greenstones, antlers, flowers stay; pack out all trash; no fires except in designated rings; respect the closure November 1–April 15.
- **Implementation challenges**: a roadless island whose only arrival is a long boat or seaplane trip (a transition scene with weather cancellations); open lake with cold water, waves, fog and storms that must stop paddlers; long parallel ridges with one-sided cliffs (cuesta relief) over a large area; many inland lakes linked by portages (carrying a canoe); underwater shipwrecks; a park that closes for half the year (seasonal access); aurora; a wolf-and-moose population model worth simulating lightly.
