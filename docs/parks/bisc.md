# Biscayne National Park — `BISC`

> Florida · established June 28, 1980 (national monument October 18, 1968) · 700.0 km² (172,971.11 acres) · 486,567 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 0–~3 m on land, sea floor to −18 m at the reef edge · NPS region SER (Southeast)
>
> A park that is 95% water, within sight of Miami: a shallow turquoise bay, the northernmost string of true Florida Keys and
> the start of the Florida reef, with shipwrecks, a toy-like 1930s lighthouse and a village of houses on stilts. In Hearthlight it
> should feel like a small archipelago you explore by boat, paddle and snorkel mask, where every island is a little story and the
> city skyline hangs on the northern horizon.

## Reading the map

The map is a tall rectangle ~26 × 42 km with north up, its north-west corner cut away. The **west side is the mainland**: a thin green strip of mangrove shore from Cutler Ridge (SW 168th–184th streets, Old Cutler Road) south past Black Point to Mangrove Point, backed by beige suburbs and farmland (Florida's Turnpike, US 1, Homestead, Homestead Air Reserve Base). Three public doors open on it: **Black Point Park** (county marina and jetty, at SW 248th St / Coconut Palm Drive), **Convoy Point** with the **Dante Fascell Visitor Center** and park headquarters at the east end of **SW 328th Street**, and, right next to it on the south, **Homestead Bayfront Park** (Herbert Hoover Marina). South of that the boundary skirts the **Turkey Point** power plant and its closed grid of cooling canals, then continues to Mangrove Point, with the small closed **Arsenicker** bird keys offshore. A yellow line along the whole shore marks a slow-speed zone within 1,000 ft of land.

The **middle** of the map is **Biscayne Bay**: open, very shallow water (white and pale blue on the map) with the marked **Intracoastal Waterway** running north–south down its centre, pink spoil islands and shoals from old dredging, and a non-combustion-engine zone near the Ragged Keys. Its **north edge** is the tip of **Key Biscayne** (Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park and its lighthouse, outside the park); south of it spreads the **Safety Valve**, a band of sand-and-seagrass flats cut by channels where **Stiltsville** stands in the water, with **Soldier Key** (closed) two-thirds of the way down.

The **keys** form a chain on the east side of the bay, north to south: the private **Ragged Keys**; **Boca Chita Key** with its round harbour and ornamental lighthouse; **Sands Key** (the only real sand beach, with a popular anchorage) across Sands Cut; long, narrow **Elliott Key** (~11 km, the largest, with its harbour and campground on the bay side at mid-length, University Dock at the north end and the dashed "hiking trail" — the Spite Highway — down its spine); then **Adams Key** (day-use dock) and the Rubicon, Reid and Porgy keys at the mouth of **Caesar Creek**; the twin mangrove keys **Totten** and **Old Rhodes** around the enclosed **Jones Lagoon**; and Swan and Gold keys at the south boundary, where Broad Creek and Angelfish Creek separate the park from **Key Largo** and John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park.

**East of the keys** the water turns blue: **Hawk Channel**, a natural lane 6–10 m deep, runs parallel to the chain, and beyond it the **reef line** — Fowey Rocks (with its 1878 iron lighthouse), Brewster, Star, Triumph, Long, Ajax, Pacific, Elkhorn and Ball Buoy reefs, dotted with reef symbols, mooring buoys and hatched Coral Reef Protection Areas. The **Maritime Heritage Trail** wrecks sit on this line: Arratoon Apcar by Fowey Rocks, Lugano and Mandalay on Long Reef, Erl King and Alicia further south, and an unnamed 19th-century wooden sailing vessel off Elliott Key. The **east boundary** follows the 60-ft (18 m) depth contour; the Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary lies beyond. Relief is nil — what organises the map is depth: shallow bay, keys, channel, reef, deep ocean, west to east.

### Water

Biscayne Bay is a semi-enclosed **lagoon averaging ~3 m (10 ft)**, floored with turtle-grass and shoal-grass meadows and patches of hard bottom; its water is green-blue and often slightly hazy. Fresh water enters from the west through canals (Mowry, Black Creek, Goulds) and seeps from the aquifer along the mangrove shore; the bay mixes with the ocean through the **Safety Valve** flats in the north and the passes between the keys (**Lewis Cut, Sands Cut, Caesar Creek, Broad Creek, Angelfish Creek**), where tidal currents run. Tides are small (on the order of half a metre) but decisive: the Boca Chita and Elliott Key harbours shrink to ~1.2 m and ~0.8 m at low tide, with times offset from Government Cut in Miami. East of the keys the water clears and deepens into **Hawk Channel** (6–10 m) and the **reef tract**, whose crests come near the surface; the park ends at the 18 m (60 ft) contour, with the Gulf Stream offshore. There are no rivers, falls or springs. Colour tells depth: white-turquoise flats, pale-blue bay, blue channel, deep blue Atlantic.

### Relief

Flat. The keys are the upper tip of the Pleistocene **Key Largo Limestone** — a fossil coral reef — rising only 1–3 m; their shores are rocky and cut by mangroves (Elliott and Old Rhodes), with real sand only at Sands Key. The mainland shore is a low mangrove fringe; the spoil islets of the ICW make small mounds. In the game the relief should come from **depth tiers** (dry land, 0–2 m flats, 2–4 m bay, 6–10 m channel, 3–18 m reef) rather than height levels; land gets one low bank level at most. Vertical accents are built: the **Boca Chita lighthouse** (65 ft / 20 m), the **Fowey Rocks** skeletal iron tower (light 110 ft / 34 m above water), the **stilt houses**, the jetty at Convoy Point, and on the horizon the **Miami skyline** (north) and the **Turkey Point** stacks (south-west).

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation** (really by salt and depth): seagrass meadows (turtle grass, manatee grass, shoal grass) under the bay → red mangroves on prop roots at the waterline, black mangroves with breathing roots behind, white mangrove and buttonwood above the tide → on the keys' higher fossil-reef rock, tropical hardwood hammock: gumbo limbo, mahogany, strangler fig, poisonwood, torchwood, satinleaf, wild lime → beach strand of sea grape and coconut palms (Boca Chita, Sands Key) → coral heads, sea fans and sponges on the reef.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#EDE3C8` coral sand and rubble · `#BFB39A` grey fossil-reef limestone · `#D8CBB0` Boca Chita's coral-stone lighthouse · `#3E6E3A` mangrove green · `#2F5A36` hammock canopy · `#9FE0D8` turquoise flats · `#6FC2C8` pale-blue bay · `#3F8F7E` seagrass beds seen from above · `#2E7FB8` Hawk Channel blue · `#1F5E8C` deep Atlantic · `#A9B7C6` hazy Miami skyline.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **West Indian manatee** (bay and canals near Convoy Point, especially in winter) · **bottlenose dolphin** (bay and Hawk Channel) · **American crocodile** (mangrove shore; the Turkey Point canals nearby) · **green, loggerhead and hawksbill sea turtles** (reef and seagrass) · **spotted eagle ray** and southern stingray (flats) · **nurse shark** (reef ledges) · **upside-down jellyfish** (Jones Lagoon) · **spiny lobster** (reef crevices) · reef fish — **parrotfish, blue tang, sergeant major, grunts, snapper, queen angelfish, barracuda** · **brown pelican**, **magnificent frigatebird**, osprey and double-crested cormorant (everywhere) · white ibis and herons (mangroves) · **Schaus' swallowtail**, an endangered butterfly of Elliott Key's hammocks (May–June) · mangrove cuckoo (keys) · the rare smalltooth sawfish.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): **red mangrove** (propagules drift in the bay year-round) · **black mangrove** · **turtle grass** · **sea grape** (beaches; grapes in late summer) · **gumbo limbo** · **torchwood**, the Schaus swallowtail's host · **key lime** gone wild from the Jones family groves · **coconut palm** (Boca Chita) · **strangler fig** · **poisonwood** (don't touch).
- **Weather & seasons**: *Winter* — clear, breezy, cooler fronts with choppy seas, the clearest bay water, fewer bugs. *Summer* — hot, humid, flat-calm mornings, towering afternoon storms over the Everglades to the west, lightning over the water, the odd waterspout. *Hurricanes* (Jun–Nov) — Andrew (1992) shredded the mainland and Stiltsville; the park keeps storm flags on its map. No snow or ice.
- **Night**: the Miami skyline glows orange on the northern horizon while the eastern sky over the ocean stays dark; the Fowey Rocks light and channel markers blink; campers on Boca Chita and Elliott Key watch the moon rise out of the Atlantic; on calm nights the harbour water mirrors the lighthouse.
- **Sounds**: halyards clinking in Boca Chita harbour, outboards and idle-speed burbles, waves on coral rubble, pelicans hitting the water, osprey whistles, laughing gulls, mangrove crabs rustling, palm fronds in the trade wind, the snorkeller's own breathing and the crackle of snapping shrimp underwater, a jet from the air base now and then.

## Moments

- **Sunrise on the Atlantic** — from Boca Chita, Elliott Key or the Stiltsville flats, the sun rises straight out of the ocean (rangers have photographed the green flash there), with Miami catching the first light to the north.
- **Glass-calm summer mornings (Jun–Sep)** — the best visibility on the reef and the Mandalay wreck, before afternoon storms build over the mainland.
- **Manatee season (roughly Nov–Mar)** — manatees gather in the warmer canals and along the mainland shore near Convoy Point.
- **Schaus' swallowtail flight (late spring, ≈ May–June)** — the big black-and-yellow butterfly patrols Elliott Key's hammock trails.
- **Low tide at the harbours** — Boca Chita's entrance drops to ~4 ft and Elliott Key's to ~2.5 ft; boats wait for the tide.
- **The lighthouse opens** — when rangers are on Boca Chita, the 65-ft tower's deck opens for the view of islands, bay and skyline.
- **Coral spawning** — Florida's reef corals spawn on a few nights after the late-summer full moons (approximate; a rare night-dive event).
- **Hurricane season (Jun–Nov)** — storm flags go up at the visitor centre; after big storms the islands close for clean-up.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: **768 × 1152 tiles** (~1.5–2 m per tile at the hotspots). North up, like the map: the mainland shore as a strip on the west edge with Convoy Point at x≈90, y≈720; the bay compressed ~20× (the 14-km crossing becomes ~450 tiles of open water); the keys chain down the east-centre (Boca Chita ≈ 60 × 40 tiles at near true size with its round harbour and lighthouse; Elliott Key shortened from 11 km to ~350 tiles but kept 30–60 tiles wide; Adams, Totten and Old Rhodes with Jones Lagoon ≈ 150 × 150 tiles); Stiltsville as 7 houses on a 200 × 150-tile flat at the top; Hawk Channel and the reef line down the east edge with the six wrecks and Fowey Rocks at readable spacing (~100–150 tiles apart). Cut: the suburbs, most spoil islets, the closed keys except as scenery, the Turkey Point plant (a horizon silhouette).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: **Dante Fascell VC** (porch with rocking chairs, gallery, touch table, dock where the tour boat leaves), **Boca Chita harbour** (the social island: bulkhead, lighthouse, picnic pavilion, campground), **Elliott Key harbour** (campground, ranger station, showers). 3D landmarks: the Boca Chita lighthouse (coral-rock tower with a lantern), the Fowey Rocks iron screw-pile lighthouse, 7 Stiltsville houses on pilings, the wrecks (Mandalay's hull ribs, Lugano's plates, Apcar's boilers), channel markers and mooring buoys, the Miami skyline and Turkey Point stacks as backdrop billboards. Interiors: the visitor centre, one Stiltsville house (by permit), the lighthouse stair.
- **Activities**:
  - *Hiking*: short island walks — the jetty, Boca Chita loop, Elliott Key loop, the long Spite Highway tunnel, Adams Key.
  - *Photo spots*: the Boca Chita lighthouse framed by palms; the skyline from the lighthouse deck; Stiltsville at sunrise; a pelican on a channel marker; the Mandalay wreck from above the water; Fowey Rocks at sunset; the jetty's end at Convoy Point.
  - *Wildlife*: manatee noses at the jetty, dolphins riding the tour boat's bow, a crocodile on the mangrove shore, the Schaus swallowtail as a seasonal rarity, a reef-fish checklist for the field guide.
  - *Junior Ranger*: the shared Everglades–Biscayne–Big Cypress book, plus park tasks: name the four ecosystems (mangrove shore, bay, keys, reef); learn the channel-marker rule (red on the right returning from sea); find a turtle, a ray and a parrotfish while snorkelling; collect a stamp at each Maritime Heritage Trail wreck.
  - *Snorkelling*: the core verb. Put on a mask at a mooring buoy and swim in a shallow underwater layer: the **Mandalay** skeleton on Long Reef (easy), coral heads at Alina's, Anniversary and Elkhorn reefs, the base of **Fowey Rocks** and the **Arratoon Apcar** debris, the calm **Jones Lagoon**. Deeper wrecks (Erl King, Alicia, Lugano at ~8 m) can be seen from the surface or unlocked as "scuba" later. A diver-down flag must be up.
  - *Boating*: the park's road network is water. Drive a skiff with **depth tiers** straight from the map legend (white flats run you aground unless you drift or pole; pale blue is safe at idle; blue is open): follow channel markers, slow down in the idle-speed zones at Elliott and Boca Chita harbours, tie up only to mooring buoys over the reef, never anchor on coral or seagrass, keep out of the Legare Anchorage and closed keys, and time the harbour entrances with the tide table. The guided tour boat from the VC is the shared "shuttle" of the park.
  - *Paddling*: kayak the mangrove shore from Convoy Point (Crocodile Creek, Black Point), cross to Elliott Key when the wind is down (a real open-water challenge), or glide over Jones Lagoon's glassy shallows (no engines allowed).
  - *Camping*: Boca Chita and Elliott Key campgrounds, reached only by boat; day-use fees on busy days, overnight docking and camping fee.
- **Passport stamps**: Homestead (Dante Fascell Visitor Center) · Boca Chita Historic District (Boca Chita Key).
- **With friends / with the world**: one boat, several roles — a captain reading the depth colours while the others snorkel from the buoy and point out turtles; a group sunrise at Stiltsville; a relay along the Maritime Heritage Trail (each player finds one wreck's plaque); a kayak crossing where the party must stay together in the chop. In the shared world, **Boca Chita harbour** is the natural meeting place (boats "raft" two deep along the wall — a built-in social layout), with the lighthouse deck as the group-photo spot, and the **Elliott Key harbour** campground at dusk.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace as gameplay): use reef-safe sunscreen; never touch or stand on coral — stand only on sand; use mooring buoys, don't anchor on reef or seagrass; respect idle and slow-speed zones (manatees); fly the diver-down flag; no collecting — wrecks, artifacts, coral and shells are protected; stay out of the Legare Anchorage and closed keys (Soldier, Arsenickers); give crocodiles, manatees and nesting birds room; pack out all trash (no bins on the islands); no pets on Boca Chita; follow fishing and lobster rules; watch for poisonwood and mosquitoes.
- **Implementation challenges**: an **underwater layer** (swim, look down, dive a few metres) with reef and wreck models; **depth-coded water** and grounding on flats; boat docking, rafting and mooring; **tides** changing harbour access; wind and chop for kayaks; stilt houses on pilings; distant **skyline backdrops** (Miami, Turkey Point) with night lights; a map that is 95% sea yet must not feel empty.
