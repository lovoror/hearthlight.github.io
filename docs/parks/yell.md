> Wyoming, Montana, Idaho · established March 1, 1872 · 8,983.2 km² (2,219,790.71 acres) · 4,762,988 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 1,610–3,462 m · NPS region IMR
>
> The world's first national park is a high forested plateau inside a supervolcano's caldera, holding about half of the
> planet's geysers, a 341 km² mountain lake, a yellow canyon with a 94 m waterfall and valleys full of bison and wolves. In
> Hearthlight it should feel like a road trip round a steaming figure-eight: each village is a boardwalk hub with its own clock
> (the next eruption), and the drives between them are wildlife theatre.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a near-rectangle about 87 km wide and 102 km tall. Its west and south edges are ruled lines (the Idaho line runs just inside the west edge, and Montana takes a strip along the north), while the north and east edges are jagged: the Gallatin Range makes a bulge in the north-west corner, crossed by US 191 along the Gallatin River, and the whole east side follows the crest of the Absaroka Range. National forests and wildernesses surround it on every side; along the bottom edge the John D. Rockefeller Jr. Memorial Parkway leads down to Grand Teton, whose Jackson Lake peeks into the bottom-centre of the map. A translucent purple band marks the approximate caldera (72 × 48 km): an oval lying east–west from the Madison Plateau west of Old Faithful to Lake Butte beyond Yellowstone Lake's north shore. Hayden Valley and the northern half of the lake sit inside it, Canyon on its northern rim, Mammoth and Tower well outside.

The road network is the Grand Loop Road, a 229 km figure-eight in the middle of the park, with five spokes to the entrances. The **upper loop** (113 km) runs Mammoth (north-west) → Tower-Roosevelt (north) → over Dunraven Pass (2,700 m, the highest point of the loop) → Canyon (east) → Norris (west) → back to Mammoth. The **lower loop** (155 km) runs Norris → Madison → Old Faithful → West Thumb → along the lake to Lake Village and Fishing Bridge → up Hayden Valley → Canyon. The 19 km Norris–Canyon road is the shared crossbar. The spokes: North Entrance to Mammoth, 8 km; from Tower, 47 km east through the Lamar Valley to the Northeast Entrance (this Gardiner–Cooke City road is the only one kept open all winter); from Fishing Bridge, 43 km east over Sylvan Pass to the East Entrance; from West Thumb, 35 km south past Lewis Lake to the South Entrance; from Madison, 23 km west along the Madison River to the West Entrance. Every junction-to-junction stretch has its distance printed on the brochure (table below). One-way side drives hang off the loop: Upper Terrace Drive at Mammoth, the unpaved Blacktail Plateau Drive east of Mammoth, Firehole Canyon Drive and Firehole Lake Drive on the Firehole, North Rim Drive at Canyon, Virginia Cascade Drive on the crossbar.

The hubs sit where the loop meets water or heat. Mammoth, in the north-west corner, is the park's year-round headquarters: Fort Yellowstone's lawns, the Albright Visitor Center, a hotel, and the travertine terraces climbing the hill west of the road. Tower-Roosevelt, 29 km east, is a lodge at the Lamar junction. Canyon Village is 1.5 km north of the canyon, whose two rims are lined by the North Rim and South Rim drives. Norris is the geyser basin at the western junction of the loops. At Madison, the Gibbon and the Firehole meet to make the Madison River. Old Faithful, south-west of centre, is the biggest village (inn, lodges, visitor centre) facing the geyser. West Thumb and Grant Village sit on Yellowstone Lake's west bay, and Lake Village, Fishing Bridge and Bridge Bay line its north shore. The five insets along the top zoom into exactly these villages.

Everything off the loop is roadless: the Gallatin Range (north-west, Electric Peak), the Washburn Range (north-centre), the Mirror Plateau and the Lamar's headwaters (east), the Absaroka crest and the Thorofare (south-east, the most remote place in the lower 48, with Eagle Peak, the park's summit), the Two Ocean and Pitchstone plateaus (south), the Red Mountains with Heart Lake, Shoshone Lake (the largest lake in the lower 48 that no road reaches), and the Bechler « Cascade Corner » in the south-west, a waterfall country reached only from Idaho. The beige patches show open ground, where animals are easiest to see: Lamar Valley, Hayden Valley, the Blacktail Deer Plateau, Gardners Hole and Swan Lake Flat south of Mammoth, Pelican Valley, the Madison valley, and the Thorofare meadows.

Water and relief organise the whole map. The Yellowstone River enters from the south-east, fills Yellowstone Lake (2,357 m), leaves it northward at Fishing Bridge, meanders through Hayden Valley, drops over the Upper (33 m) and Lower (94 m) falls into the Grand Canyon, runs north to Tower, then north-west to leave the park at Gardiner, beside the lowest point (1,610 m). The Firehole flows north through the Upper, Midway and Lower geyser basins, strung like beads along one 26 km road, and becomes the Madison at Madison Junction, flowing west. The Lewis River links Shoshone Lake and Lewis Lake to the Snake River at the South Entrance. The Continental Divide wriggles across the south: the Old Faithful–West Thumb road crosses it twice (Craig Pass, 2,518 m, with Isa Lake on top) and the South Entrance road once (2,435 m).

### Water

Yellowstone straddles the Continental Divide and sends water to two oceans. The **Yellowstone River** is born outside the park south-east of the Thorofare, meanders north through the Thorofare meadows into the **Southeast Arm** of Yellowstone Lake, leaves the lake at **Fishing Bridge**, flows north through Hayden Valley (LeHardy Rapids, then lazy meanders), plunges over the **Upper Falls** (33 m) and **Lower Falls** (94 m) into the Grand Canyon, runs north past Tower Fall and the basalt Narrows, then bends north-west past the Lamar confluence to Gardiner (Atlantic side, via the Missouri). The **Lamar River** comes down from the Absaroka to the south-east and flows north-west through the Lamar Valley, joined by Soda Butte Creek (which the Northeast Entrance road follows). The **Gardner River** runs past Mammoth to Gardiner. The **Firehole** rises south of Old Faithful and flows north through three geyser basins (its water warmed by them), over Firehole Falls (12 m) into a small canyon, and meets the **Gibbon** (from Norris, over Gibbon Falls, 26 m) at Madison to make the **Madison**, which flows west out of the park. On the Pacific side, the **Lewis River** drains Shoshone Lake into Lewis Lake, falls 9 m at Lewis Falls, cuts the Lewis Canyon and joins the **Snake** by the South Entrance; the Bechler and Falls rivers pour off the Pitchstone and Madison plateaus in the south-west (Union Falls 79 m, Ouzel 72 m, Colonnade 30 m, Cave Falls 11 m). The park counts about 290 waterfalls.

**Yellowstone Lake**: 341 km², 32 km north–south × 22 km east–west, 227 km of shore, surface 2,357 m, average depth 42 m and maximum 131 m (the brochure prints 410 ft; the park's facts give 430 ft); it has three long southern arms (Flat Mountain, South, Southeast), The Promontory between the last two, and islands (Stevenson, Dot, Frank; the Molly Islands for pelicans). West Thumb is a small caldera of its own, with hot springs on its shore and on the lake bed. The lake freezes from about December to late May or June. Other lakes: Shoshone (backcountry), Lewis, Heart, Sylvan and Eleanor (on the East Entrance road), Grebe, Trout. The **hydrothermal system** is the park's other water: more than 10,000 features and more than 500 active geysers, fed by snowmelt heated over the magma chamber. Hot springs and pools (blue centres, orange and brown bacterial aprons), geysers (constricted plumbing), fumaroles (steam vents, loudest on cold mornings), mudpots (acid, grey to pink), and travertine terraces (hot water through limestone, only at Mammoth). The runoff channels from the pools are painted by microbial mats in bands of colour by temperature.

### Relief

From 1,610 m at Reese Creek by Gardiner (north boundary) to 3,462 m on Eagle Peak (south-east boundary). The heart of the park is a high volcanic plateau, 2,100–2,500 m, made of rhyolite lava flows that filled the caldera: the Madison, Central, Pitchstone and Two Ocean plateaus are flat-topped and edged with steep flow fronts (the forested walls above Old Faithful and Madison, the tuff cliffs of the Firehole and Gibbon canyons). Mountain ranges ring the plateau: the Gallatin Range in the north-west (Electric Peak 3,343 m), the Washburn Range across the north-centre (Mount Washburn 3,122 m, Dunraven Pass 2,700 m) which is the caldera's northern rim, the Absaroka Range along the whole east side (Avalanche Peak 3,184 m, Top Notch, Mount Doane, Mount Langford, Colter Peak, Eagle Peak), the Red Mountains south of the lake (Mount Sheridan 3,142 m), and the Beartooth-Absaroka peaks over the north-east corner (Barronette 3,171 m, Abiathar 3,331 m, The Thunderer 3,217 m). The Northern Range drops in steps from the plateau to the Yellowstone River: Blacktail Deer Plateau (~2,000 m), then the river at Tower (1,900 m) and Gardiner (1,600 m).

In game terms: the plateau is the base level; the geyser basins are shallow bowls a level or two below the forest (banks and sinter terraces, not cliffs); the Grand Canyon is the one deep cut (240–370 m deep, 0.5–1.2 km wide, 32–39 km long) and needs the tallest painted cliff faces, with the falls as two stepped water drops and switchback stairs down the north rim; Mammoth's terraces are a stack of one-level travertine banks (perfect for the engine's banks, each with a warm pool); Sheepeater Cliff (columnar basalt), Obsidian Cliff (black glass), the Tower Narrows' basalt columns and the Gibbon and Firehole canyons make short cliffs by the road. 3D peaks: Mount Washburn with its lookout, Electric Peak, Bunsen Peak's dome over Mammoth, Avalanche Peak and the Absaroka wall along the east edge, Mount Sheridan over Heart Lake, and the Tetons as a backdrop beyond the south boundary. Passes where the road climbs: Dunraven, Sylvan, Craig.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: 1,600–2,000 m (Gardiner, Mammoth, the Lamar floor): sagebrush steppe with bunchgrass, Rocky Mountain juniper, Douglas-fir on north slopes, aspen groves and cottonwoods along the Lamar. 2,000–2,600 m (the plateau): lodgepole pine everywhere — mature dark stands, silver snag forests from 1988 with dense young pines under them — broken by sedge meadows, thermal barrens (white sinter, bare ground, bacterial mats) and willow flats; Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir in wet hollows. 2,600–3,000 m: subalpine fir, whitebark pine, wildflower meadows (Washburn). Above ~3,000 m: alpine tundra and scree.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#EEE8DA` sinter and travertine white · `#E08A3C` orange bacterial mat · `#9C5B2E` brown mat edge · `#1F7FB8` hot-spring deep blue · `#7FCFC8` hot-spring turquoise rim · `#E2C35B` canyon sulphur yellow · `#D48C6A` canyon pink rhyolite · `#2F4A3A` lodgepole green · `#A3A98C` sagebrush sage · `#C8A860` autumn valley grass · `#4A7FA8` Yellowstone Lake · `#1E1B22` obsidian black.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): bison (Hayden and Lamar valleys all summer, geyser basins in winter; calves in April–May, rut in July–August), elk (Mammoth's lawns; bugling rut in September–October), gray wolf (Lamar Valley and Slough Creek at dawn, best in winter), grizzly bear (Hayden Valley, Washburn's slopes in autumn), black bear (Tower–Roosevelt, spring), pronghorn (the flats by the North Entrance, Lamar), bighorn sheep (Mount Washburn, the Gardner canyon), mountain goat (Barronette Peak on the Northeast road), moose (Lewis River, Pebble Creek), coyote and red fox (mousing in snow), river otter (Trout Lake, Lamar), yellow-bellied marmot (Storm Point), trumpeter swan, American white pelican (Hayden Valley, the lake), osprey (nests on canyon pinnacles), bald eagle, sandhill crane, Yellowstone cutthroat trout (LeHardy Rapids in early summer).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): fringed gentian (the park flower, July–September in geyser basins), Rocky Mountain iris (the wild iris of the brochure, June in wet meadows), arrowleaf balsamroot (May–June on the Northern Range), silky lupine and Indian paintbrush (July, Washburn), glacier lily (at snowmelt), lodgepole pine (serotinous cones that open in fire), whitebark pine (autumn nuts for bears), Ross's bentgrass (endemic, only on warm thermal ground), Yellowstone sand verbena (endemic, lakeshore beaches), heat-loving microbial mats (a « living colour » page for the herbarium).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter (November–April) buries the plateau under 1–3 m of snow; bison plough it with their heads, steam freezes on trees into « ghost trees » round the basins, the Firehole steams in −30 °C air. Spring (April–June) is mud, late snow, newborn bison and bears on the move; the lake stays frozen into June. Summer (July–August) brings wildflowers, daily thunderstorms, crowds, and in some years wildfire smoke. Autumn (September–October): gold aspens on the Northern Range, elk bugling, first snow on the passes, clear cold mornings when every basin is hidden in steam.
- **Night**: dark skies away from the villages (the Milky Way over a steaming basin); geysers erupt at night too (Old Faithful by moonlight); wolves howling in the Lamar; bats over the Firehole; winter nights at −30 °C with auroras on rare occasions; the Old Faithful Inn's lit windows.
- **Sounds**: the hiss, roar and patter of eruptions and the crowd's « ooh »; mudpots blurping; fumaroles whistling (Roaring Mountain); boardwalk planks under feet; the Lower Falls' roar; bison grunts, elk bugles, wolf howls, ravens, sandhill cranes' rattling call; ice booming on the lake in spring; a snowcoach engine in winter.

## Moments

- **Old Faithful** erupts every ~68 or ~94 minutes (± 10 min): the rangers predict the next eruption from the length of the last (a short eruption under ~2.5 min means a short wait); 1.5–5 min, 32–56 m (106–184 ft). Predictions are posted from spring to autumn and in winter; not when the visitor centre is closed (early Nov–mid-Dec, mid-March–mid-April).
- **The other predicted geysers**: Grand (every ~6.5 h ± 1 h, 49+ m, 8–12 min, the tallest predictable), Riverside (~6.5 h ± 30 min, arcs over the Firehole for 20 min), Castle (~13.5 h), Daisy (~2 h 45), Great Fountain (~11.5 h ± 2 h, on Firehole Lake Drive, spectacular at sunset). Steamboat (> 91 m) is unpredictable: its 2018–2025 active phase (177 eruptions) is winding down, and it may now stay quiet for years — a rare, server-wide event.
- **Grand Prismatic's colours**: a sunny, warm midday from the overlook; on cold mornings the spring is a cloud of steam.
- **Wolves in the Lamar**: dawn, best from December to March when grey shapes show on snow; spotting scopes line the pullouts.
- **Elk rut at Mammoth**: September–October, bulls bugling among Fort Yellowstone's buildings.
- **Bison rut**: late July–August in Hayden and Lamar valleys — bellowing, wallowing, road jams.
- **Cutthroat trout leap LeHardy Rapids**: late May–July.
- **Lower Falls rainbow**: sunny mornings, sun at your back, from Artist Point and the south rim.
- **Winter by snowcoach**: from ~December 15 to mid-March the park is reached only by guided snowcoach or snowmobile (from Mammoth, West Yellowstone, the South Entrance and Old Faithful Snow Lodge); ghost trees, frosted bison, warming huts (Madison, Old Faithful, Canyon, West Thumb, Fishing Bridge, Indian Creek, Mammoth), and Old Faithful erupting into −30 °C air.
- **Ice-out on Yellowstone Lake**: late May–June; the lake « breathes » and ice floes push up on shore.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: 1536 × 1536 tiles (48 × 48 chunks, the top of the tier-1 range), north up, keeping the park's near-square shape and the Grand Loop's figure-eight in the middle. Hubs at 1 tile ≈ 2–3 m: the Old Faithful village with Geyser Hill (~800 × 600 m) becomes ~320 × 240 tiles, Grand Prismatic (113 m) ~45 tiles across, the Mammoth terraces ~200 tiles, the canyon rim from the Brink of the Lower Falls to Artist Point (1.5 km) ~250 tiles with the two falls at full relief; inside a basin, the gaps between features shrink ~2× (the 2.3 km boardwalk from Old Faithful to Morning Glory Pool ≈ 450 tiles, a 3–4 min walk). Roads between hubs are compressed ~20–30×: the 229 km Grand Loop becomes ~4,000 tiles, each of its eight legs ~50–70 s by car (the plan's 40–90 s target), ~7 min for the whole loop. The roadless rest is squeezed ~50–100× into generated plateau forest; Yellowstone Lake shrinks to ~450 × 330 tiles but stays the largest surface on the map. Cut or reduced: US 191's Gallatin strip (a border), the Grassy Lake road; the Thorofare and Two Ocean Plateau as a roadless south-east corner reached by boat down the Southeast Arm or on horseback; Bechler as a small waterfall pocket at the end of its own dead-end road in the south-west.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Old Faithful (the open-world plaza: benches in a crescent, the prediction board, the Inn); Mammoth (fort lawns, terraces, the Roosevelt Arch just north); Canyon (both rims and the falls); Lake (hotel, bridge, marina); West Thumb; Norris; Madison; Tower-Roosevelt; Lamar pullouts. 3D landmarks: Old Faithful Inn (log façade and steep roofs), the Roosevelt Arch, Lake Yellowstone Hotel's columned front, Fishing Bridge, the Mount Washburn lookout, Tower Fall's pinnacles, the Fort Yellowstone stone buildings, the Norris museum, Liberty Cap, Fishing Cone in the lake. Interiors: Old Faithful Inn lobby (the huge stone fireplace, log balconies, the crow's nest), the Old Faithful Visitor Education Center, Canyon's supervolcano hall with its relief model, Lake Hotel's sunroom, the Fishing Bridge museum's bird hall, the Norris museum.
- **Activities**: hiking the boardwalks and the trails above; photo spots — Old Faithful mid-eruption from the benches and from Observation Point, Grand Prismatic from the overlook, the Lower Falls from Artist Point and from its brink, the Mammoth terraces, Morning Glory Pool, Tower Fall, the Roosevelt Arch, a bison with Hayden Valley behind, a wolf in Lamar (through a scope), the lake from Lake Butte at sunrise, the Mount Washburn lookout; wildlife spotting with a scope and a distance ruler; ranger programmes (geyser talks at the Old Faithful benches, canyon rim walks, wolf talks at the Lamar pullouts, evening amphitheatre talks at the campgrounds); Junior Ranger (the real badge is an arrowhead with a geyser, a peak and a bison — the game's « Young Ranger » badge needs its own shape, never the NPS arrowhead) and the Young Scientist kit, a natural Young Ranger booklet: take hot-spring temperatures with a thermometer, sort the four feature types (hot spring, geyser, fumarole, mudpot), predict Old Faithful from the last eruption's length, measure a safe distance in bus lengths; water: the Lake Queen-style cruise from Bridge Bay, canoe from Lewis Lake up the channel to Shoshone Lake, catch-and-release fishing for cutthroat where allowed (no longer from Fishing Bridge itself, closed to anglers since 1973), swimming only in the Firehole swim area; vehicles: car on the Grand Loop, the historic yellow touring bus, bike on the old roads (Lone Star, Fountain Flat, Bunsen Peak, Chittenden Road), horses and a covered wagon from Roosevelt Lodge to the Old West cookout, snowcoach and cross-country skis in winter.
- **Passport stamps**: Albright Visitor Center (Mammoth), Old Faithful Visitor Education Center, Canyon Visitor Education Center, Fishing Bridge Visitor Center, Grant Village Visitor Center, West Thumb Information Station, Madison Information Station, Norris Geyser Basin Museum, Museum of the National Park Ranger (Norris), Bechler Ranger Station, the South Entrance, Tower ranger station, the Lamar Buffalo Ranch, the West Yellowstone visitor centre and the Gardiner park store (from a collector's list; to confirm on the Passport stamp finder).
- **With friends / with the world**: the Old Faithful benches are the natural open-world meeting place — a countdown everyone sees, then a shared eruption and a group photo; the Inn's lobby is the indoor lounge. Party ideas: the « geyser gazers » — each player watches a different geyser in the Upper Geyser Basin and radios its eruption to the others (a real volunteer practice), so the party can race to Grand or Riverside in time; a dawn wolf watch at Slough Creek where the party's scopes combine to track a pack; a canoe convoy to Shoshone Lake (tow the boats up the channel together); a bison jam where everyone waits in their cars. Strangers would meet at the Lamar pullouts at dawn, on the Artist Point terrace and on the Washburn summit.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on boardwalks and trails in thermal areas (the crust is thin and the water scalding — off-path steps are simply impossible, with a ranger's warning); 100 yd / 91 m from bears and wolves, 25 yd / 23 m from bison, elk and everything else — an on-screen « bus lengths » ruler like the brochure's; never feed wildlife, not even chipmunks; nothing thrown into hot springs (Morning Glory's faded colours as the lesson); no swimming or soaking in thermal water; stop only in pullouts to watch animals; food in bear boxes at camp; leave obsidian, petrified wood and antlers where they lie; boats only on the lakes that allow them.
- **Implementation challenges**: the game's Prism Springs already has Grand-Prismatic rings, sinter, orange mats, travertine and timed geysers — to add: mudpots and fumaroles, boardwalk tiles with rails, the shared eruption clock with prediction windows (and a rare, unscheduled Steamboat), steam that thickens on cold mornings and drifts with the wind, and eruption columns tall enough to read from Observation Point. Also: a deep V-canyon (240–370 m) that the 0–7 relief levels must read as a canyon, with two waterfalls and switchbacks; Mammoth's stacked travertine pools; a big lake with arms, islands, waves and a winter ice sheet; bison herds that block roads (a traffic jam everyone shares); the winter mode (snow, roads closed to cars, snowcoach routes, ghost trees, warming huts); wolves seen only at long range (a scope view and a field-guide card at distance); the Continental Divide as a named line (Isa Lake draining both ways); and the size — the most compressed map of the set.
