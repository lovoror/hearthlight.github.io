# Theodore Roosevelt National Park — `THRO`

> North Dakota · established November 10, 1978 (national memorial park 1947) · 285.1 km² (70,447 acres) ·
> 729,893 recreation visits (2025) · 591–872 m · NPS region MWR
>
> Three pieces of the Little Missouri River badlands where a young Theodore Roosevelt ranched in the 1880s: striped
> buttes, burning-coal scoria, petrified wood, bison, wild horses and prairie-dog towns. In Hearthlight it should feel
> like a frontier ride along a meandering river — two scenic drives, a cowboy's cabin, and a lonely ranch site in the
> cottonwoods.

## Reading the map

The three units sit on the **Little Missouri River**, which flows north through the badlands. The **South Unit** is the
largest and busiest: a rough pentagon north of I-94 with the river along its west side and the tourist town of
**Medora** (outside the park) at its south-west corner, where the **South Unit Visitor Center** stands with Roosevelt's
**Maltese Cross Cabin** behind it. The **Scenic Loop Drive** (36 mi round trip) leaves Medora north past Skyline Vista
and Cottonwood Campground along the river, then turns east past Peaceful Valley Ranch, prairie-dog towns, Wind Canyon
and the Boicourt Overlook, swings south to Buck Hill and the Coal Vein Trail, and returns west past the Old East
Entrance, Badlands Overlook, Ridgeline Nature Trail and Scoria Point to Medora; a spur continues north past Wind Canyon
up **Beef Corral Bottom** towards Jules Creek and the Roundup Horse Camp (closed beyond a point in winter). A road
collapse near the Ridgeline Nature Trail currently breaks the loop on its south-west side, so the drive is run as
out-and-back legs (the NPS now quotes ~48 mi). The west third of the South Unit, across the river, is the **Theodore
Roosevelt Wilderness**: the **Big Plateau** and the **Petrified Forest** areas, reached by trail (Maah Daah Hey, Petrified
Forest Loop). The **Painted Canyon Visitor Center** and overlook (summer only) sit on I-94 at the south-east corner.

The **North Unit** is a long east–west band on the south side of the river, 50 mi north of I-94, where US 85 crosses the
Little Missouri on the **Long X Bridge** next to the **North Unit Visitor Center** (a temporary contact station for now).
Its **Scenic Drive** (14 mi one way, 28 mi out-and-back) runs west along the valley floor past the Cannonball
Concretions, Juniper Campground and the Little Mo Nature Trail, climbs to the **River Bend Overlook**, then follows the
rim to the **Oxbow Overlook**, where the river makes its famous turn to the east. South of the river and the drive lies
the North Unit Wilderness: the **Achenbach Hills**, Sperati Point and the Achenbach and Buckhorn trails.

The **Elkhorn Ranch Unit** is a sliver of river bottom about halfway between (40 km south-south-west of the North Unit),
reached only by gravel roads; a 0.7 mi mowed path leads through cottonwoods to the foundation stones of Roosevelt's
"home ranch". The **Maah Daah Hey Trail** (~144 mi) ties all three units together through the Little Missouri National
Grassland, which surrounds every unit.

### Water

- **Little Missouri River**: flows **north** through the South Unit (past Medora and Cottonwood), past the Elkhorn Ranch,
  then turns **east** at the Oxbow in the North Unit and on to Lake Sakakawea on the Missouri. A shallow, silty, braided
  prairie river with sandbars and cottonwood bottoms; wadeable in late summer (the Achenbach Trail fords it twice),
  high and muddy in spring runoff, frozen in winter. Before the last ice age it flowed north to Hudson Bay; the ice
  sheet diverted it east at the Oxbow.
- **Creeks** (intermittent): Paddock, Jones, Talkington, Jules, Knutson and Government creeks in the South Unit; Appel
  Creek and Corral Creek in the North Unit.
- **Springs**: Boicourt Spring, Jones Creek Well, Tomamichael Well (South Unit); Stevens, Overlook, Hagen and Achenbach
  springs (North Unit). Water must be treated.

### Relief

Lowest ~591 m (1,940 ft) where the Little Missouri leaves the North Unit; highest ~872 m (2,860 ft, Peck Hill) — Buck
Hill (870 m) is the visitor's high point. The badlands are cut 100–180 m into the surrounding plains: a flat prairie
"upland" surface at the rim, then steep slopes of banded clay, silt, sand and black lignite coal down to the river
bottoms. Coal seams that burned underground baked the rock above into red **scoria** (clinker), which caps many buttes;
round **cannonball concretions**, bentonite "popcorn" clay, petrified stumps and slumped blocks dot the slopes. The North
Unit is deeper, greener and more rugged (juniper on north slopes); the South Unit is a wider jumble of buttes around the
river. In the game: a flat prairie top with 2–4-level banded cliff faces down to wide river bottoms, red-capped buttes
as 3D props, walkable banks on slumps and coulees, the river as a shallow meandering water band with fords.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation**: mixed-grass prairie on the uplands and bottoms (western wheatgrass, blue grama, needle-and-thread,
  little bluestem), silver and big sagebrush flats, Rocky Mountain juniper thickets on cool north-facing slopes, green
  ash and chokecherry in the draws, plains cottonwood galleries along the river (the old giants at Elkhorn), buffaloberry
  and rabbitbrush; badland slopes mostly bare.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#9A9489` grey-blue bentonite clay · `#D8C9A7` buff sandstone band · `#2E2A27` black
  lignite coal seam · `#B5553A` red scoria (clinker) caprock · `#C98D5A` orange baked clay · `#A8A56F` summer prairie ·
  `#D1B679` autumn grass · `#56694B` juniper · `#E4C35A` cottonwoods in October · `#8C8466` silty Little Missouri.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): American bison (both units; the source of Badlands NP's 1963 herd), feral "wild"
  horses (South Unit bands; foals in spring), elk (South Unit, bugling in September–October), bighorn sheep (North Unit
  cliffs), pronghorn, mule deer (dawn and dusk), white-tailed deer (river bottoms), black-tailed prairie dog (many towns),
  coyote, bobcat, porcupine, golden and bald eagles, wild turkey, sharp-tailed grouse, prairie rattlesnake, and the
  North Unit's demonstration herd of longhorn steers.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): pasque flower (April), prairie crocus-to-coneflower succession: purple coneflower (July),
  blazing star (August), prickly pear (yellow, June), soapweed yucca (June), chokecherry (white May flowers, dark fruit in
  August), rubber rabbitbrush (yellow, September), plains cottonwood (gold in October).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — wet, windy, green, foals and calves; summer — hot days, thunderstorms, gumbo mud after
  rain; autumn — golden cottonwoods, elk bugles, crisp nights; winter — deep cold, snow on striped buttes, road
  sections closed, bison frosted white.
- **Night**: very dark skies; the park's Dakota Nights Astronomy Festival; the aurora borealis sometimes shows this far
  north; coyotes, great horned owls.
- **Sounds**: prairie-dog chirps, meadowlarks, horses whinnying, bison grunting, wind through juniper, the river's
  riffles, elk bugles in fall, coyote choruses; hoofbeats on the Maah Daah Hey.

## Moments

- **Wild horses at Buck Hill and along the loop**: bands with foals in spring and early summer.
- **Elk bugling** (September–October) in the South Unit; **bison rut** (July–August); prairie-dog pups (May–June).
- **Sunset at Wind Canyon**, **Painted Canyon at sunrise**, **River Bend Overlook** at golden hour.
- **Maltese Cross Cabin tour** (ranger-led in summer): the young ranchman's cabin, 1883.
- **Elkhorn Ranch solitude**: cottonwood leaves rustling over the stones where Roosevelt wrote and ranched.
- **Dakota Nights Astronomy Festival** (annual, late summer) and the occasional aurora.
- **Winter**: snow-striped buttes, frosted bison, closed road sections; cottonwoods golden in October before it.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: two maps and a vignette. **South Unit: 640 × 512 tiles** (real ~24 × 20 km; the loop at ~1:4, the
  Medora gate, Cottonwood and Peaceful Valley near 1:2, the western wilderness ~1:8). **North Unit: 640 × 256 tiles**
  (real ~24 × 12 km; the scenic drive valley and rim at ~1:4, the Achenbach Hills compressed). **Elkhorn Ranch: a
  128 × 128 vignette** reached by a long gravel "travel" link or the Maah Daah Hey. Meta size given for the South Unit.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the South Unit Visitor Center with the **Maltese Cross Cabin** (an enterable log cabin), Medora
  as a gateway edge (Old West false fronts, outside the park), Peaceful Valley Ranch buildings, the CCC stone shelter at
  **River Bend Overlook**, the Old East Entrance Station, Painted Canyon's rim building, the Elkhorn foundation stones;
  3D landmarks: red scoria-capped buttes, Buck Hill, cannonball concretions, petrified stumps.
- **Activities**: scenic drives with wildlife jams (bison, horses), short hikes (Wind Canyon, Buck Hill, Coal Vein,
  Caprock Coulee), backcountry treks and river fords (Achenbach), horseback riding (Peaceful Valley's corral history,
  Roundup Horse Camp, the Maah Daah Hey), biking the loop and the Maah Daah Hey outside wilderness, canoeing the Little
  Missouri in spring high water, photo spots (Wind Canyon sunset, River Bend, Oxbow, Painted Canyon, a wild-horse band,
  a scoria butte, the cabin), wildlife field guide, Junior Ranger (booklet; a "Roosevelt's ranch" history badge), ranger
  bison chats and cabin tours, astronomy festival, camping at Cottonwood and Juniper.
- **Passport stamps** (Eastern National list, August 2025): Medora, North Unit, South Unit, Painted Canyon, Watford City,
  Elkhorn Ranch.
- **With friends / with the world**: a wild-horse and bison "safari" convoy on the loop, sunset gathering at Wind
  Canyon, a group ride on the Maah Daah Hey from the South Unit to the Elkhorn, a canoe float on the Little Missouri, a
  star party at Painted Canyon, a quiet group visit to the Elkhorn (no talking inside the circle of stones as a gentle
  rule of respect).
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay 25 yd from wildlife and farther from bison and horses, never
  feed prairie dogs, collect nothing (petrified wood, fossils, antlers), treat all water, pack out trash, respect winter
  road closures and gumbo after rain, no drones, time zones differ between units (a clock gag).
- **Implementation challenges**: three disconnected units (a travel system between maps), a meandering braided river
  with fords, burning-coal scoria and clinker as a distinct red rock set, herds of horses and bison on roads, a road
  collapse as persistent state, and two time zones in one park.
