# Voyageurs National Park — `VOYA`

> Minnesota · established April 8, 1975 · 883.1 km² (218,222 acres, about 84,000 of them water) · 206,326 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 338–430 m · NPS region MWR (Midwest; DOI Region 3, Great Lakes)
>
> A water park on the Canadian border: four big interconnected lakes (Rainy, Kabetogama, Namakan, Sand Point) wrapped around the roadless Kabetogama Peninsula of ancient granite, pine and bog, travelled by canoe, houseboat and, in winter, by car on ice roads. In Hearthlight it should feel like a lake-country holiday: the map is mostly water, you pick a boat at the dock, fish, camp on an island, find a lone historic hotel across the lake and watch the northern lights from the shore.

## Reading the map

North is up and the map is mostly **water**. Across the top runs **Rainy Lake**, the biggest lake, its northern half in Ontario (the border is a dashed line zig-zagging through the islands); across the bottom-left lies **Kabetogama Lake**, a long lake stretching west–east; on the right, **Namakan Lake** runs east to the Canadian border and turns south into **Sand Point Lake**, which ends in **Crane Lake** at the bottom-right corner. Between Rainy Lake to the north and Kabetogama/Namakan to the south lies the park's heart, the **Kabetogama Peninsula** — about 35 km long west–east, roadless, a maze of rock ridges, bogs and 26 small inland lakes (Locator, War Club, Quill, Shoepack, Cruiser, Brown, Peary, Beast…). Everything drains **north-west**: from Crane Lake through Sand Point and Namakan lakes, over the dams at **Kettle Falls** at the peninsula's east tip, into Rainy Lake and out the Rainy River at International Falls toward Hudson Bay.

The **road network only touches the edges**, from the south-west: US 53 runs north from Orr to International Falls along the map's left side; SR 11 leaves International Falls east along the Rainy River to the **Rainy Lake Visitor Center** (12 mi / 19 km, at the mouth of Black Bay, with the park headquarters in town); SR 122 reaches the village of **Kabetogama** and the **Kabetogama Lake Visitor Center** on the south shore of Kabetogama Lake (with Woodenfrog campground and Echo Bay trail on the lake's west arm); the Ash River Trail (CR 129) runs east to the **Ash River Visitor Center** (the historic Meadwood Lodge) at the east end of Kabetogama Lake, where Meadwood Road passes the Voyageurs Forest, Beaver Pond and Kabetogama Lake overlooks; CR 24 comes up from Orr to **Crane Lake** at the far south-east (Crane Lake Visitor Center, opened 2025, and the U.S. customs station). Nothing else: the peninsula, the islands and the east side are reached by water.

Scattered around the lakes are the historic stops a boater visits: **Little American Island** (1893 gold-rush mine, by the Rainy Lake VC), **Harry Oveson Fish Camp** (north shore, Cranberry Bay), the **Ellsworth Rock Gardens** (terraced gardens and rock sculptures on the peninsula's south shore, straight across Kabetogama Lake from the visitor centre), **Kettle Falls** (the red-roofed 1913 hotel between two dams at the peninsula's east end, on the border), **Hoist Bay** and the **I.W. Stevens Resort** (Namakan), **Casareto Cabin** and the **Grassy Bay Cliffs** (Sand Point Lake), and the **Vermilion Gorge** trail at Crane Lake. The Cruiser Lake trail system crosses the peninsula from Lost Bay (Kabetogama) north to Anderson Bay (Rainy Lake); the Kab-Ash trail links the Kabetogama and Ash River communities on the mainland. In winter the lakes themselves become the roads: a plowed **ice road** runs out from the Rainy Lake VC and another links the Kabetogama and Ash River visitor centres, and 110 miles of staked snowmobile trails cross the ice and portages.

### Water

- **Four big lakes, all connected**: Rainy Lake (north, shared with Ontario; the largest), Kabetogama Lake (south-west, entirely in the park), Namakan Lake (east, on the border), Sand Point Lake (south-east, on the border), with Crane Lake at the southern tip. Water is ~38 % of the park. The lakes are tea-dark, rocky-shored and island-studded (hundreds of islands; ~80 named islets on the map), with long bays and narrows (Brule, Namakan, Harrison, King Williams, Blind Indian narrows).
- **Flow**: north-west — Vermilion River → Crane Lake → Sand Point → Namakan (fed also by Kabetogama via the narrows near Ash River) → **Kettle Falls and Squirrel Falls dams** → Rainy Lake → Rainy River at International Falls → Lake of the Woods → Hudson Bay. Lake levels are regulated by the dams at Kettle Falls and International Falls.
- **Interior lakes**: 26 on the Kabetogama Peninsula (Locator, War Club, Quill, Loiten, Shoepack, Little Shoepack, Jorgens, Ek, Agnes, Cruiser, Brown, Peary, Beast, Oslo, Weir…), reachable only by trail, some with park-provided canoes.
- **Ice**: lakes freeze from November–December; average ice-out May 3; ice 60+ cm thick carries the winter ice roads.
- Wild-rice beds in shallow bays; beaver ponds everywhere; bogs with sphagnum and pitcher plants.

### Relief

Lowest 338 m (Rainy Lake, 1,108 ft); highest ~430 m (1,410 ft, near the Meadwood Road entrance tower south of Ash River). Low relief but rugged: the park sits on the **Canadian Shield**, 2.7-billion-year-old granite, schist and greenstone scraped bare by glaciers into rounded knobs, ridges and basins. Shores are rock slabs and low cliffs (Grassy Bay Cliffs, Anderson Bay, the Vermilion Gorge); the peninsula is a jumble of 10–40 m rock knobs separating bogs and lakes. In the game: the lake level as the base; islands and shores as 1–2-level HIGH rock plateaus with painted granite faces (pinkish-grey), walkable banks at the beaches and landing slabs, stairs at the overlooks; a single 3D landmark rock (Grassy Bay Cliffs) and the Kettle Falls dams as a stepped fall; no peaks.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: all within ~90 m of relief — shoreline and rock knobs: jack pine, red pine, scattered giant white pine, blueberry and reindeer lichen on bare granite; upland forest: quaking aspen, paper birch, balsam fir, white spruce (the boreal–northern hardwood transition); lowlands: black spruce and tamarack bogs with sphagnum, Labrador tea, pitcher plants; shallow bays: wild rice, water lilies, bulrush; fire-renewed stands of young jack pine and fireweed after burns.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#2E4A5E` tea-dark lake water · `#6F93A8` lake under summer sky · `#B8A89A` pinkish granite · `#7E7C7A` grey schist and greenstone · `#2E5236` jack/red pine · `#D9C24A` / `#E3A63B` aspen and birch gold in late September · `#F2F1EA` birch bark and snow · `#8A9A5B` bog sedge · `#C9D8E0` lake ice · `#B53A2E` the Kettle Falls Hotel's red roof.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): gray wolf (packs studied by the Voyageurs Wolf Project; they ambush beavers in summer), beaver (dams everywhere), moose (few, on the peninsula), black bear (store food), white-tailed deer, river otter, snowshoe hare, pine marten and fisher, Canada lynx (rare), common loon (every lake, calls at dusk), bald eagle (dozens of nests), osprey, American white pelican (summer, Rainy Lake), double-crested cormorant, great blue heron, ruffed and spruce grouse, Canada jay; walleye, northern pike, smallmouth bass, muskellunge and lake sturgeon (54 fish species in the park).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): wild rice (manoomin, harvested late August–September), blueberry (July–August), showy lady's slipper (June, bog edges), pitcher plant (bogs), bunchberry (June flowers, August berries), fireweed (July, on burns), white water lily (summer bays), reindeer lichen (all year on rock), tamarack (gold needles in October), white pine (the giants of the islands).
- **Weather & seasons**: late spring — ice-out in early May, loons return, black flies; summer — long days (sunset after 9 pm), warm calm lakes, thunderstorms and wind on big water, wildfire smoke some years; fall — gold aspen and birch late September, first snow late October, freeze-up; winter — −30 °C nights, deep snow, lakes frozen solid: ice roads, snowmobiles, ice-fishing shacks, skis and snowshoes.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park (designated 2020): Milky Way over the water in summer, **northern lights** possible any clear night and more often in the long winter darkness; designated dark-sky viewing at the Meadwood Road day-use area and the Rainy Lake VC upper lot; ranger star parties; loons and wolves calling in the dark.
- **Sounds**: loon tremolos, outboard motors fading across the lake, lapping waves on granite, the houseboat's creak at mooring, beaver tail slaps, eagles' chatter, wind in pines, the Kettle Falls spillway roar, snowmobile whine and the boom and crack of lake ice in winter.

## Moments

- **Ice-out** around May 3: the lakes open, loons and pelicans arrive, the boating season starts.
- **Northern lights**: any clear dark night, best in the long nights from September to March; summer brings the Milky Way instead. Ranger star parties in the dark-sky areas.
- **Houseboat season** (mid-May–September): anchor in a cove, grill on the deck, swim off the back.
- **Kettle Falls**: lunch on the 1913 hotel's veranda, then stand at the dam and look *south* into Canada.
- **Ellsworth Rock Gardens** in summer bloom, reached by boat across Kabetogama Lake.
- **Wild-rice harvest** in late August–September; blueberries in July–August.
- **Fall colour** in late September (aspen and birch gold; tamarack gold in October).
- **Winter** (roughly January–March): drive the ice roads, ski the Black Bay and Echo Bay trails, sled Sphunge Island, ice-fish, snowmobile 110 miles of lake trails.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a north-up water map **1280 × 896 tiles** (~1.9 × 1.35 km at 1.5 m/tile) with the ~55 × 37 km park compressed ~30×. Keep the geography readable: Rainy Lake across the top (Canada beyond a buoy line), the long Kabetogama Peninsula in the middle, Kabetogama Lake below it, Namakan and Sand Point on the right, Crane Lake in the bottom-right corner; the mainland strip with roads only along the south-west edge. Near true scale: the four visitor-centre harbours (docks, launch, lodge), Kettle Falls (hotel + two dams, ~400 m), the Ellsworth Rock Gardens (~150 m), Little American Island, a handful of island campsites and houseboat coves. Compressed: open water, the peninsula interior (keep ~8 of the 26 lakes with the Cruiser Lake trail), island clusters. Cut: most of the Canadian side (a rocky horizon), Quetico and the highway south.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Rainy Lake VC (main hub, the one open in winter), Kabetogama and Ash River VCs, Kettle Falls (the social destination), Crane Lake. 3D landmarks: the Kettle Falls Hotel (white clapboard, red roof, veranda) and its dams, the Meadwood Lodge (Ash River VC), rock cairns and terraces of the Ellsworth Rock Gardens, the Grassy Bay Cliffs, tour boats and houseboats, floatplanes, fish-camp net sheds, ice-fishing shacks in winter. Interiors: the Rainy Lake and Ash River visitor centres, Kettle Falls Hotel bar and rooms, Harry Oveson's fish house, a rented houseboat.
- **Activities**: boating as the core loop — rent a canoe, kayak, motorboat or **houseboat** at a gateway, navigate by buoys, camp at numbered island sites (tent pads, fire rings, food lockers), fish for walleye and pike, visit historic stops; the Cruiser Lake traverse and "boats on interior lakes" canoes; photo spots — Grassy Bay Cliffs from a houseboat, Kettle Falls from the dam, Ellsworth's rock sculptures, a loon at sunset, aurora over Rainy Lake, the ice road at dawn; ranger boat tours and star parties; junior ranger (and night explorer); winter mode with ice roads (drive a car on the lake), snowmobiles, skis, snowshoes, sledding, ice fishing.
- **Passport stamps**: the 2025 list gives "Crane Lake", "Kabetogama Lake", "Ash River", "Int'l Falls", "Kettle Falls", "Rainy Lake Visitor Center" and "Crane Lake Visitors Center". In the game: one at each visitor centre, the International Falls headquarters, and a Kettle Falls stamp at the hotel (reached only by boat).
- **With friends / with the world**: the party shares one houseboat (bunks, a deck, a grill) and splits into canoes for errands; fishing contests; a convoy on the ice road; aurora nights with everyone on the dock. The open world meets at the Kettle Falls veranda, the Rainy Lake VC docks and the Ellsworth gardens; group photo on the Kettle Falls dam.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): camp only at designated sites with a permit; store food in the bear lockers; no-wake near docks and loon nests; keep away from eagle nests; wear life jackets; don't cross into Canada without the border check-in (the buoy line); check ice conditions before going out and stay on the plowed ice road; leave rock sculptures and artifacts untouched; pack out trash; clean boats of invasive species.
- **Implementation challenges**: a map that is mostly water, with navigation (buoys, narrows, shallows), many boat types including a slow houseboat you can live on; hundreds of islands; seasonal freeze turning lakes into roads (vehicles on ice, cracks, slush closures); an international border inside the lakes; aurora and dark-sky rendering; wildfire smoke; wolves hunting beavers as an ambient wildlife event.
