# Death Valley National Park — `DEVA`

> California & Nevada · established October 31, 1994 · 13,793.3 km² (3,408,396 acres) · 1,320,134 recreation visits (2025) ·
> −86–3,368 m · NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10, California–Great Basin)
>
> The largest national park in the lower 48: a long, below-sea-level trough of salt flats, dunes and painted badlands
> between two mountain walls, the hottest and driest place in North America, with snow on the peaks above. In
> Hearthlight it should feel vast, silent and luminous: long drives between strange wonders, dawn light on golden
> badlands, and a heat that is itself a character you must respect.

## Reading the map

The park is a tall, lopsided shape leaning north-west, pressed against the Nevada line on its north-east side. Down
its middle runs **Death Valley** itself, a ~200 km trench walled by the **Panamint Range** on the west (Telescope
Peak, 3,368 m) and the **Amargosa Range** on the east (the Grapevine, Funeral and Black mountains). West of the
Panamints, a second, parallel trough, **Panamint Valley**, holds Panamint Springs. North-west of that lie the high,
lonely **Saline** and **Eureka** valleys with the Last Chance and Saline ranges, and the **Racetrack**. The
Nevada-side detached unit of **Devils Hole** sits east, near Ash Meadows.

**The central valley.** **CA 190** is the backbone. It enters from Death Valley Junction (east), crosses the
Amargosa Range past the **Dantes View Road** junction, and drops by **Zabriskie Point** to **Furnace Creek**, the
oasis hub (visitor centre, the Inn and the Ranch at Death Valley, the Borax Museum, three campgrounds, the Timbisha
Shoshone Village, the lowest golf course). From Furnace Creek, **Badwater Road** runs south along the east edge of
the salt pan. It passes Golden Canyon, the one-way **Artists Drive** loop (9 mi, into the painted hills and **Artists
Palette**), the turn-off west across the salt to **Devils Golf Course**, and **Natural Bridge**, reaching **Badwater
Basin** (17 mi / 27 km, −86 m). It then continues south past Mormon Point to Ashford Mill and climbs over Jubilee Pass
to Shoshone. Above Badwater, the dead-end Dantes View Road climbs 13 mi (21 km) to **Dantes View**, 1,755 m straight
above the salt. Northward from Furnace Creek, CA 190 passes Harmony Borax Works, Salt Creek and the **Devils
Cornfield** to the **Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes** and **Stovepipe Wells Village** (24 mi / 39 km). It then climbs west
over Towne Pass (1,511 m) past Emigrant into Panamint Valley and Panamint Springs, and exits west at Father Crowley
Vista Point above Rainbow Canyon.

**North and east.** At the junction by the dunes, **Scotty's Castle Road** (North Highway) heads 36 mi (58 km)
north-north-west up the valley to **Grapevine**, **Scotty's Castle** in Grapevine Canyon (closed since the October
2015 flood; limited reservation tours only), and 5 mi (8 km) west to **Ubehebe Crater**. From Ubehebe the dirt
**Racetrack Valley Road** runs 27 mi (43 km) south to Teakettle Junction and **The Racetrack**, with its Grandstand
outcrop and moving rocks (sharp rocks: heavy-duty tyres). The Big Pine road goes north-west into Eureka Valley
(**Eureka Dunes**). To the east, the **Beatty Cutoff** and **Daylight Pass Road** climb through Hells Gate to Nevada
and Beatty, near the Rhyolite ghost town. The famous one-way dirt **Titus Canyon Road** comes back west from NV 374
through the Leadfield ghost town and the narrows (27 mi / 43 km). Near Hells Gate a spur reaches the **Keane Wonder
Mine**.

**West and south.** From Emigrant, the paved **Emigrant Canyon Road** runs south up the Panamints (the Skidoo and
Aguereberry Point spurs) to **Wildrose**. The **Charcoal Kilns** stand up Wildrose Canyon, and a steep rough road
continues to **Mahogany Flat**, the trailhead for Telescope Peak. The dirt **West Side Road** traces the salt pan's
west shore (Eagle Borax Works, Hanaupah and Johnson canyons). The south end is **Owlshead Mountains** wilderness with
**Harry Wade Road**, the Amargosa River, and **Saratoga Spring** and the Ibex Dunes in the south-east corner. The
backcountry map counts over 1,000 miles of paved and dirt roads, the dirt ones often 4WD, fanning out into the Saline Valley (hot
springs), Hunter Mountain, Butte Valley and the Greenwater Valley.

**2026 conditions.** Road status (NPS, Sept. 28, 2026): Scotty's Castle and Bonnie Claire Road are closed for flood
recovery with no reopening date. Titus Canyon Road is open for now, but its one-way section closes from October 2,
2026 to May 2027 for repairs. The Darwin Falls road is gone until about summer 2027 (hikers walk in from CA 190).
Emigrant Campground is closed indefinitely.

### Water

Death Valley is a closed basin: no water leaves it. **Salt Creek** flows seasonally south from the Mesquite Flat
area to the Cottonball Basin. The **Amargosa River** comes from Nevada, loops south past Shoshone and turns back
north-west into the south end of the valley along Harry Wade Road, reaching Badwater only in rare floods. The
evaporated remains of Pleistocene **Lake Manly** (once ~180 m deep) are the white salt pan, about 518 km²; its old
shorelines are etched on Shoreline Butte. After exceptional storms the lake comes back as a sheet of water a few
centimetres to half a metre deep (2005; 2023–24, when people kayaked at Badwater). Springs make oases: **Furnace
Creek** and Texas Spring (palms), **Grapevine** (cottonwoods and willows), **Darwin Falls**, **Saratoga Spring**,
Saline Valley's warm springs, and **Devils Hole**, a water-filled cave. Relict **pupfish** live in them (Salt Creek,
Saratoga, Devils Hole). Summer flash floods have repeatedly destroyed roads (2015 Scotty's Castle; August 2023,
Hurricane Hilary).

### Relief

From **−86 m** at Badwater (the lowest point in North America) to **3,368 m** on **Telescope Peak**, only ~25 km
apart. The valley floor is a flat salt pan (hexagonal salt polygons, the jagged Devils Golf Course), rimmed by
**alluvial fans** spilling from every canyon mouth (walkable gentle banks). On the east, the Amargosa Range rises as
steep fault scarps with eroded **badlands** at its foot (Zabriskie Point, Golden Canyon, Artists Palette). On the
west, the Panamints climb to 3,000+ m through canyons (Hanaupah, Johnson, Trail). The crest carries the Wildrose
plateau, the Charcoal Kilns and Mahogany Flat. Beyond lie more parallel range-and-trough pairs: Panamint Valley,
Saline and Eureka valleys, with playas (the Racetrack) and dune fields. Volcanic features: **Ubehebe Crater** (a maar
~200 m deep), the lava cliffs of Rainbow Canyon. In game terms: the salt pan and playas are the lowest flat tiles
(white, reflective); badlands are banks cut into ribbed steps; fault scarps and canyon walls are cliffs; switchbacked
roads to Dantes View and Towne Pass are stairs; Telescope Peak, Wildrose Peak and the Grandstand are 3D landmarks;
dunes are soft sculpted height fields.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: salt pan (−86 m): nothing, then pickleweed and saltgrass at the springs · valley edges
  and dunes (0–300 m): honey mesquite hummocks, arrowweed, desert holly (silver leaves) · fans and badlands (300–1,200
  m): creosote bush, brittlebush, desert gold after rain · uplands (1,200–2,000 m): Joshua trees (Lee Flat), blackbrush,
  cacti · Panamints (2,000–3,368 m): single-leaf pinyon and Utah juniper, then limber and bristlecone pine on Telescope
  Peak · oases: fan palms, cottonwoods, willows.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#F4F1E8` salt crust · `#D8CFC0` grey salt pan mud · `#E2B96F` Zabriskie's golden
  badlands · `#C98E5C` rust canyon walls · `#9EB39A` Artists Palette mint green · `#C46F8C` Artists Palette pink ·
  `#7B6F8E` volcanic purple ash · `#EAD3A3` dune sand · `#3E3A36` Ubehebe black cinders · `#8CB6C4` the rare Badwater
  lake.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): desert bighorn sheep (Titus Canyon, Badwater cliffs) · coyote (roadside at dusk —
  don't feed) · kit fox (dunes at night) · black-tailed jackrabbit · kangaroo rat (dune tracks) · roadrunner ·
  common raven (camps and car parks) · Salt Creek pupfish (spawning Feb–April) · Devils Hole pupfish (the rarest fish:
  a few hundred at most) · desert tortoise (southern valleys, rare) · chuckwalla and zebra-tailed lizard ·
  sidewinder (dunes, tracks at dawn) · Panamint chipmunk (Wildrose and the high Panamints) · golden eagle · bats at
  the springs.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): desert gold (the sunflower of the « superbloom », Feb–April in rare wet years) ·
  Panamint daisy (endemic, spring) · notch-leaf phacelia (purple, spring) · desert five-spot · gravel ghost (white,
  night-scented) · creosote bush · desert holly · honey mesquite (pods in summer) · bristlecone pine (Telescope Peak,
  thousands of years old) · Joshua tree (Lee Flat).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter: mild days, cold nights, snow on Telescope Peak, clear air · spring: wildflowers
  (rare superblooms), wind and sandstorms, pupfish spawning · summer (May–Sept): extreme heat, 45–50 °C by day and
  above 30 °C at night, hot winds, occasional monsoon storms and flash floods · autumn: warm, calm, clear from late
  October.
- **Night**: a Gold Tier International Dark Sky Park (2013): among the darkest skies in the lower 48, the Milky Way
  over the dunes and the salt flat, star-trails over Badwater; the annual Dark Sky Festival; kit foxes and kangaroo
  rats in the headlights; warm nights in summer.
- **Sounds**: silence so deep you hear your pulse · salt crust ticking and crackling as it warms and cools · wind
  hissing over the dunes and the low « booming » of Eureka's sand · ravens · coyote yips at dusk · gravel crunching
  on dirt roads · the hum of the heat · rare thunder rolling between the ranges, the rush of a flash flood.

## Moments

- **Sunrise at Zabriskie Point**: the Panamints turn pink, then the golden badlands and Manly Beacon light up.
- **Sunset on the Mesquite Flat dunes** (and at Dantes View): ridges edged in orange, long blue shadows, tracks
  everywhere.
- **Superbloom** (Feb–April, only after an unusually wet winter, a few times a decade): desert gold carpets the
  fans south of Furnace Creek and along Badwater Road.
- **Lake Manly returns**: after exceptional storms a shallow lake mirrors the mountains at Badwater (2005, 2023–24).
- **Sailing stones**: on rare winter days, thin ice on a flooded Racetrack breaks up and the wind pushes rocks along,
  carving new trails.
- **Pupfish spawning** (Feb–April): males turn bright blue and defend territories in Salt Creek.
- **The heat on the thermometer** (July): the Furnace Creek thermometer passes 120 °F (49 °C) and visitors line up for
  a photo; the 134 °F (56.7 °C) record dates from July 10, 1913.
- **Snow and salt**: in winter, snow-capped Telescope Peak rises 3.4 km above the white salt at Badwater.
- **Dark Sky Festival** (annual, late winter): night programmes under a Gold Tier sky.

- **Scale & shape**: a portrait **1024 × 1536**-tile map following the valley's north–south grain, from Ubehebe and
  the Racetrack (north) to Ashford Mill (south), Panamint Valley (west) to Dantes View (east). Near true scale (~2 m
  per tile): Furnace Creek (~1.5 km), Zabriskie Point and Golden Canyon, the Badwater boardwalk and the first 1–2 km of
  salt, the dune field's heart around the high dune (~1 km), Stovepipe Wells, Ubehebe Crater (800 m), the Racetrack's
  Grandstand corner. Compressed 20–40×: the long drives (Furnace Creek → Stovepipe Wells 24 mi, the 36 mi North
  Highway, the 27 mi Racetrack road), the salt pan's width, the empty southern valley. Split off as vignettes: Eureka
  Dunes and Saline Valley, Titus Canyon as a one-way canyon drive, Devils Hole (a single fenced overlook), Saratoga
  Spring and the Ibex Dunes.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Furnace Creek (visitor centre with the giant thermometer, the Inn's palm garden and pool, the
  Borax Museum's wagons), Stovepipe Wells Village, Panamint Springs. 3D landmarks: Manly Beacon and the Zabriskie
  badlands, the Artists Palette hills, Badwater's « sea level » sign high on the cliff, Natural Bridge, the Grandstand
  and the moving rocks, Ubehebe Crater, the ten Charcoal Kilns, the Harmony Borax ruins and a Twenty Mule Team wagon,
  Scotty's Castle (behind construction fences), Leadfield's shacks, the Keane Wonder tramway towers, Telescope Peak.
  Interiors: the visitor centre, the Borax Museum, the Inn's lobby, a charcoal kiln (echoes!), Scotty's Castle's music
  room on a reserved tour.
- **Activities**: salt-flat and dune walks at dawn; canyon hikes (Golden, Mosaic, Titus narrows); the Telescope Peak
  summit hike (the lowest-to-highest challenge); photo spots (Zabriskie sunrise, dune ridges, Badwater polygons,
  Artists Palette, the Racetrack's rock trails, Ubehebe's rim, the kilns, Dantes View); the field guide (pupfish,
  kit fox tracks, bighorn); the herbarium (a superbloom log); ranger programmes at Furnace Creek, night-sky talks;
  Junior Ranger booklet at the visitor centres; vehicles: car (paved loops, Artists Drive), a high-clearance 4WD for
  the Racetrack, Titus Canyon and the backcountry roads (with tyre-puncture risk), bike on the dirt roads; kayaking
  only in a « Lake Manly » year.
- **Passport stamps**: the official list has Death Valley, Furnace Creek, Stovepipe Wells, Scotty's Castle and Devils
  Hole (Nevada). Scotty's Castle's own visitor centre has been closed since the 2015 flood (check with rangers).
- **With friends / with the world**: the Zabriskie Point sunrise and the dunes at sunset are where strangers gather;
  a party « lowest to highest » challenge (Badwater at dawn, Telescope Peak by dusk); a convoy of 4WDs to the
  Racetrack, sharing spare tyres; a dune race to the high crest; stargazing on the salt; group photos at the Badwater
  sign and inside a charcoal kiln.
- **Rules of the park** — **heat is the game rule**: from May to September a heat gauge fills whenever you are
  outside at low elevation after 10 a.m.; the NPS asks visitors not to hike in the low valley after 10 a.m. in summer
  and to drink ~4 L of water a day. Shade, water, salty snacks and an air-conditioned car refill it; hikes above
  ~1,500 m (Wildrose, Telescope Peak) are the summer escape; if your car breaks down, stay with it. Also: drive only
  on roads; never move or take Racetrack rocks; stay on the Salt Creek boardwalk (pupfish); don't feed coyotes or
  ravens; leave mining relics and artifacts; stay out of mine shafts; no fires outside rings; respect flood and heat
  closures.
- **Implementation challenges**: extreme, readable heat (shimmer, a heat gauge, time-of-day danger) · vast empty
  distances (a fast-travel road network or a car with cruise speed) · a reflective white salt pan with polygon
  patterns and a rare water-sheet state · sculpted dunes that ripple and keep footprints · ribbed badlands · the moving
  rocks with trails · 4WD roads with tyre damage · canyon narrows seen from a camera looking north · a Gold Tier
  night sky.
