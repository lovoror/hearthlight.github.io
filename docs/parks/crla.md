# Crater Lake National Park — `CRLA`

> Oregon · established May 22, 1902 · 741.5 km² (183,224 acres) · 632,242 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~1,340–2,721 m · NPS region PWR (today DOI Region 9, Columbia–Pacific Northwest)
>
> The deepest lake in the United States (592 m), unbelievably blue, fills the collapsed summit of Mount Mazama,
> a volcano that blew itself apart 7,700 years ago; a single scenic road circles the caldera rim 300–600 m above the
> water. In Hearthlight it should feel like standing on the edge of a giant blue bowl: every overlook is a different
> frame of the same impossible colour, with a wizard's-hat island and a ghost ship of rock floating in it.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a tall rectangle about 27 km wide and 36 km high, with a thin tail hanging off its south-east side where OR 62 runs down the Annie Creek canyon toward Klamath Falls. **Crater Lake** sits a little north and east of the centre: an almost round blue caldera about 9 km east–west by 7 km north–south, its rim a ring of cliffs and peaks 300–600 m above the water. On the western side of the lake floats **Wizard Island**, a steep cinder cone with a crater on top, separated from the rim by the narrow Skell Channel; near the south shore, under Dutton Cliff at Chaski Bay, is the small **Phantom Ship**, a jagged island of old lava. The north shore holds **Cleetwood Cove**, the only place where a trail reaches the water and where the tour boats dock. The rim's high points are named all the way round: the Watchman and Hillman Peak (the highest point of the rim, 2,484 m) on the west, Llao Rock on the north, Skell Head and Cloudcap on the east, Dutton Cliff, Applegate and Garfield peaks on the south; **Mount Scott** (2,721 m), the highest point in the park, stands 4 km east of the rim.

The road network is simple. **Rim Drive** (33 mi / 53 km, open summer only) circles the caldera clockwise or anticlockwise with ~30 overlooks; the map splits it into **West Rim Drive** (from Rim Village north past Discovery Point, the Watchman and Merriam Point to North Junction) and **East Rim Drive** (from North Junction east past Cleetwood Cove, Skell Head and Cloudcap, then south past Phantom Ship Overlook and Kerr Notch, and back west past Sun Notch and Vidae Falls to park headquarters). From **North Junction** the **North Entrance Road** runs 14.5 km north across the grey **Pumice Desert** to the North Entrance on OR 138 (summer only). From **Rim Village** the **Munson Valley Road** runs south 11.6 km down past **Park Headquarters** and the **Steel Visitor Center** (the only road kept open to the rim in winter) to **Annie Spring**, where it meets OR 62: there sit the Annie Spring Entrance Station and **Mazama Village** (campground, cabins, store, restaurant, gas). OR 62 crosses the park's south-west corner from Medford (west) to Fort Klamath and Klamath Falls (south-east, down the tail). Two spurs leave East Rim Drive: the **Pinnacles Road** from Kerr Notch 10 km south-east down Kerr and Pinnacle valleys to the Pinnacles overlook at the park's eastern edge (with Plaikni Falls and Lost Creek campground on the way), and a short spur up toward **Mount Scott** and the **Cloudcap Overlook**, the highest paved viewpoint. **Grayback Drive**, the old gravel rim road between Vidae Falls and Lost Creek, is now a hiking and biking route.

**Rim Village** on the south-west rim is the heart: Crater Lake Lodge (1915) on the cliff edge, the Rim Visitor Center and the Sinnott Memorial Overlook built into the caldera wall below it, the café and gift shop, the Kiser Studio. Park headquarters and the Steel Visitor Center sit 3 km below in Munson Valley at 1,970 m, where snow buries everything each winter (the administration building is entered through a « snow tunnel »). Everything else is forest wilderness: the **Pacific Crest Trail** crosses the park north–south along its western side (~53 km), backcountry trails reach **Union Peak** in the south-west, **Boundary Springs** (source of the Rogue River) in the north-west corner, and the Bald Crater and Sphagnum Bog country in the west.

Relief organises everything around the missing mountain: the rim is the highest ring (2,100–2,500 m) and the land falls away from it in every direction as gentle pumice-covered slopes cut by deep, steep canyons where hot ash flows once ran (Annie Creek, Sand Creek, Wheeler Creek — with fossil fumarole spires at the Pinnacles). Inside the ring the cliffs drop straight into the water; the lake surface is at 1,882 m and its floor 592 m lower. No stream enters or leaves the lake: it is filled only by snow and rain and balanced by evaporation and seepage.

### Water

- **Crater Lake**: surface 1,882 m (6,173 ft), deepest point 592 m (1,943 ft) — the deepest lake in the US, one of the ten deepest in the world and the deepest volcanic lake; ~18.6 trillion litres. No inlet or outlet: it is filled only by snow and rain (~13 m of snow, ~1.7 m of water a year) and balanced by evaporation and seepage. Its famous blue comes from depth and purity; average clarity ~31 m (102 ft). It rarely freezes (last in 1949); surface 13–15 °C in summer, ~3 °C at depth. Beneath the surface: Wizard Island's submarine base, Merriam Cone (a submerged volcano 148 m below the surface), deep moss gardens (26–140 m) and hot-spring vents on the floor.
- **Islands**: Wizard Island (cinder cone, last active ~4,800 years ago) and the Phantom Ship.
- **Life in the lake**: introduced rainbow trout and kokanee salmon (fishing encouraged, no licence); signal crayfish introduced in 1915 are pushing out the endemic Mazama newt.
- **The Old Man of the Lake**: a 9 m mountain-hemlock log floating upright (1 m above water) since at least the late 1800s, drifting around the lake with the wind.
- **Springs & falls**: Annie Spring (the village's water), Lightning Spring, Boundary Springs (source of the Rogue River, north-west corner), Castle Crest springs; Vidae, Plaikni, Duwee, Annie, Stuart and Red Blanket falls.
- **Streams** radiate from the rim down pumice canyons: Annie, Sun, Sand and Wheeler creeks to the Klamath side (south-east), Castle and Bybee creeks to the Rogue (west).

### Relief

The highest ground is Mount Scott (2,721 m), then the rim itself (2,100–2,486 m, Hillman Peak highest). The lowest is ~1,340 m where OR 62 leaves the park's southern tail. The caldera is the defining shape: a bowl 8–10 km across whose inner walls drop 150–600 m nearly vertically to the water (sheer lava cliffs like Llao Rock and Dutton Cliff, talus and pumice slopes elsewhere), with notches in the rim (Kerr Notch, Sun Notch) where glaciers once flowed out. Outside the rim, gentle cones and shields (Timber Crater, Crater Peak, Union Peak, Mount Scott) rise from a rolling pumice plateau, and deep narrow ash-flow canyons (Annie Creek, Sand Creek, Godfrey Glen) are lined with pinnacles. In the game: the rim as a ring of high plateau with a sheer inner cliff face (several levels of cliff, deeper than the engine's usual steps), the lake as a single flat sheet of water far below, Wizard Island as a 3D cinder cone with a summit crater, Mount Scott and Union Peak as 3D peaks, the Pinnacles as spire props in a canyon, the Cleetwood Cove switchbacks as the one long stair down.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: ponderosa pine and white fir in the low south-east canyons (1,300–1,600 m) → lodgepole pine on the pumice flats (the Pumice Desert's edges, Pumice Flat) → Shasta red fir and mountain hemlock forest (1,700–2,200 m) → wind-flagged whitebark pine and mountain hemlock krummholz on the rim, with pumice slopes bare but for mat plants → spring-fed meadows in pockets (Castle Crest, Munson Valley).
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#1B4F9C` the lake's deep blue · `#2E86C8` shallow turquoise-blue at Wizard Island and Cleetwood Cove · `#D9C9A3` cream pumice (Pumice Desert, Cleetwood switchbacks) · `#B5652E` orange-red pumice & scoria bands (Pumice Castle, Wizard Island cinders) · `#5B5552` dark andesite cliffs (Llao Rock, Dutton Cliff) · `#2F4A38` hemlock–fir forest · `#9FAE8A` silvery whitebark pine needles · `#F2F4F6` deep winter snow.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): Clark's nutcracker (planting whitebark pine seeds on the rim) · Canada jay & Steller's jay · common raven · golden-mantled ground squirrel & Townsend's chipmunk (Rim Village) · American pika (talus on Garfield Peak and Mount Scott) · yellow-bellied marmot · Roosevelt elk & mule deer (meadows at dusk) · black bear · pine marten (winter tracks) · snowshoe hare · porcupine · bobcat & coyote · peregrine falcon (nesting on the caldera walls) · bald eagle & osprey · Mazama newt (endemic, near the shore) · kokanee & rainbow trout.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): whitebark pine (a keystone tree, declining) · mountain hemlock · Shasta red fir · lodgepole pine · pumice sandwort and spreading phlox (mats on bare pumice, July) · Newberry's knotweed (Pumice Desert) · Lewis's monkeyflower, lupine, paintbrush and larkspur (Castle Crest, late July–August) · pinemat manzanita.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter lasts October–June: ~13 m of snow a year (record 22.25 m in 1932–33; 6.5 m on the ground in 1983), snow walls along the plowed road, the lake hidden by cloud half the time. Spring comes in June–July as plows open Rim Drive section by section. Summer is short, sunny and dry, with cold nights and afternoon thunderstorms; August–September can bring wildfire smoke. Autumn is crisp and quiet, first snows by October.
- **Night**: very dark skies over the lake; the Milky Way and stars reflected on a glass-calm surface; ranger night-sky programmes in summer; pine martens and owls in winter.
- **Sounds**: wind in the whitebark pines on the rim, the chatter of nutcrackers and ravens, pikas « eeping » in the rocks, near silence over the lake (no inlets, no surf), the tour boat's motor echoing off the cliffs, snow squeaking underfoot in winter.

## Moments

- **The blue**: on calm, sunny summer mornings the lake is at its most intense blue and reflects the rim perfectly; wind ripples by afternoon.
- **Sunset from the Watchman** (the crowded one) and **sunrise from Cloudcap or Mount Scott** over the whole caldera.
- **The Phantom Ship in fog**: when mist lies on the water, the island really looks like a ghost ship.
- **Rim Drive opening**: plows clear the rim road segment by segment in late spring; the full 33-mile loop usually opens in July and closes on November 1.
- **Wildflowers**: Castle Crest and Munson Valley bloom late July–August; the pumice mats flower in July.
- **Winter on the rim**: 13 m of snow, ranger-led snowshoe walks from Rim Village on winter weekends, the lake rarely freezing (last 1949) and showing through the clouds; skiing the whole rim is a multi-day tour.
- **The Old Man of the Lake**: the upright floating log wanders; spotting it from the rim or boat is a treat.
- **Boat season** (in normal years, July–September): ranger-narrated boat tours circle the lake from Cleetwood Cove and drop hikers on Wizard Island — suspended in 2026 and 2027 while the trail and dock are rebuilt.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a **1024 × 1024-tile** map (~1.5 km at ~1.5 m a tile) around a real 27 × 36 km park, keeping the caldera as the centre of everything: the lake compressed ~8× to ~1.1 km × 0.9 km (still reading as vast), the rim ring kept as a continuous walkable/drivable loop (~4 km of road in the game for the real 53 km), the inner cliffs kept tall (the drop matters more than the width). Near true scale: Rim Village (lodge, VC, Sinnott Overlook, ~400 m of rim walk), the Cleetwood Cove switchbacks (1.8 km → ~600 m, with the full 213 m feel via cliffs), Wizard Island's cone and crater, the Watchman and its lookout, Mazama Village. Compressed ×20+: the Pumice Desert and North Entrance Road, the Pinnacles Road, the west backcountry (Union Peak and Boundary Springs as edge « doors »).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: hubs at Rim Village (lodge, VC, Sinnott terrace), Mazama Village (campground, store) and Park HQ (Steel VC); Cleetwood Cove as the lakeside hub. 3D landmarks: the caldera walls, Wizard Island (with crater), the Phantom Ship, Mount Scott's lookout, the Watchman lookout, Llao Rock's cliff, Pumice Castle, the Pinnacles' spires, the Old Man of the Lake (a floating, drifting prop), the historic stone Rim Drive walls and overlooks. Interiors: Crater Lake Lodge's great hall with its stone fireplace and lake windows, the Sinnott Memorial's small museum in the cliff, the Steel VC, the Kiser Studio, the Watchman lookout cab.
- **Activities**: hiking (above); **photo spots**: Sinnott Overlook, Discovery Point, the Watchman at sunset, Wizard Island from Merriam Point, Phantom Ship Overlook, Cloudcap, Mount Scott, Llao Rock, Pumice Castle, Plaikni Falls, the Pinnacles; **the Rim Drive** by car, bike or trolley with a « 30 overlooks » photo book; **water**: the boat tour round the lake and the Wizard Island drop-off, swimming and the jumping rock at Cleetwood Cove (icy!), fishing (no licence, artificial bait only); **ranger programmes**: rim talks at Sinnott, boat-tour rangers, night sky, winter snowshoe walks; **Young Ranger** ideas: measure the lake's clarity with a Secchi disk from the boat, find the Old Man of the Lake, follow the eruption story in the layers of Pumice Castle, count nutcracker caches, match the rim peaks to their ages; **winter**: snowshoe and ski on the buried Rim Drive, snow camping, sledding near Rim Village; snowmobiles on the North Entrance Road.
- **Passport stamps**: the official cancellation list (2025) has a single stamp for the park, « Crater Lake », kept at the Steel Visitor Center (all year) and in summer at the Rim Visitor Center. Junior Ranger badges at the Steel VC.
- **With friends / with the world**: a **Rim Drive convoy** or bike relay round the 30 overlooks; a **boat trip** with a party dropped on Wizard Island to race to the crater; a « find the Old Man » hunt from the rim (players at different overlooks call out where it drifts); group photo at the Sinnott terrace; a winter snowshoe loop of the rim over several in-game days. Open-world gathering spots: the lodge terrace and Rim Village promenade, the Watchman at sunset, Cleetwood Cove's beach, the Mazama campground amphitheatre.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay behind the rim walls — the caldera edge is loose and undercut; never throw anything into the lake; swim only in bathing suits (no floats, no gear that could carry invasive species); keep off the fragile pumice slopes and meadows; don't feed the ground squirrels and jays; no pets on trails; pack out everything; don't touch the whitebark pine seedlings.
- **Implementation challenges**: a **caldera with near-vertical walls 150–600 m high** (deeper than normal cliff steps; the lake visible far below from everywhere on the rim); **extreme water colour and clarity** (seeing the bottom near shore, deep blue beyond); a **floating, drifting log**; **13 m of snow** with snow walls, buried buildings and a seasonal road that opens in segments; clouds that fill or hide the bowl; a boat circuit and a dock; pinnacle and pumice terrain.
