# Denali National Park & Preserve — `DENA`

> Alaska · established February 26, 1917 (as Mount McKinley National Park; enlarged and renamed December 2, 1980) · 19,185.8 km² (4,740,911 acres) of park, 24,464 km² (6,045,153 acres) with the preserve · 543,300 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~73–6,190 m · NPS region AKR (Alaska)
>
> Six million acres of taiga, tundra and ice crossed by a single gravel road, 92 mi (148 km) long, that runs west along the north foot of the Alaska Range towards North America's highest mountain. In Hearthlight it should feel like riding a green bus through a vast, quiet land: stop for a grizzly on a river bar, get off anywhere to walk the tundra, and wait for the clouds to lift off « the Mountain ».

## Reading the map

The brochure map is almost square, north up. The protected land is a huge irregular block whose spine is the **Alaska Range**, drawn as a white, heavily shaded arc running from the south-west corner up to the north-east. The **Mount McKinley massif** (South Peak 20,310 ft / 6,190 m, North Peak 19,470 ft / 5,934 m) sits just below the centre of the map, with Mount Foraker (17,400 ft / 5,303 m) 30 km to its south-west and Mount Hunter (14,573 ft) to its south. A thin green « Wilderness boundary » line cuts diagonally from the north-west to the south-east across the range: it is the old 1917–1980 park, now the Denali Wilderness. Everything north of the range is brown-green tundra and forest drained by braided rivers flowing north (Toklat, Teklanika, Sanctuary, Savage) or west (the McKinley River along the range's foot, then the Muddy and Foraker rivers into the lake-dotted **Minchumina flats** of the north-west preserve). The second preserve unit fills the south-west corner around Mount Dall and the Kichatna Mountains' granite spires.

The **only road** leaves the Parks Highway at the park's east edge, beside the Nenana River, and runs west for 92 miles, keeping 25–40 km north of the crest so that the Mountain is always to the south. It is drawn red for the first 15 miles (paved, open to private cars: « Private vehicles restricted beyond mile 15 ») and yellow beyond (« Park Road (restricted section) », buses only). Mileposts printed in magenta: the **Denali Visitor Center** at mile 1.5 (1,677 ft / 511 m), Park Headquarters with the « Detail » box around mile 3, **Mountain Vista** (picnic symbol) and **Savage River** (campground) around mile 13–15, then **Sanctuary River** (22) and **Teklanika River** (29) campgrounds. The road turns south up the Teklanika to **Igloo Creek** (34–35) between Igloo and Cathedral mountains, crosses **Sable Pass** (~38), drops to the East Fork Toklat (43) and climbs to **Polychrome Overlook** (~46), then crosses the wide braided **Toklat River** (mile 53, ranger-station symbol), climbs over **Highway Pass** (58, the road's high point, 3,980 ft) and past **Stony Hill** and **Thorofare Pass** to the **Eielson Visitor Center** (mile 66, 3,733 ft / 1,138 m), 53 km from the summit. From there the road drops west along the north bank of the McKinley River bar to **Wonder Lake** (mile 85, campground) and ends at **Kantishna** (mile 92, airstrip), an old gold-mining camp in the Kantishna Hills. The McKinley Bar Trail leads south from Wonder Lake to the river; the Gorge Creek and Eielson Alpine trails start at Eielson.

The massif is labelled like a climbing topo: West, East and South Buttress, **Muldrow Glacier** curling north-east from the summit towards Eielson (the classic 1913 route, via McGonagall Pass), Peters, Straightaway, Foraker and Herron glaciers on the north-west, and on the south side the long **Kahiltna Glacier** (44 mi / 71 km, the range's longest; base camp at ~7,200 ft), the Tokositna, the **Ruth Glacier** squeezing through « The Great Gorge » from the **Don Sheldon Amphitheater**, and the Buckskin and Eldridge glaciers. Summits named around them: Mount Silverthrone (13,220 ft), Mount Mather (12,123), Mount Brooks (11,940), Mount Deception, Mount Eldridge, Mount Koven, Peters Dome, Kahiltna Dome, Mount Crosson, Mount Huntington (12,240), Mount Dan Beard, Mooses Tooth (10,335), Mount Barrille, Mount Dickey, Mount Stevens, Mount Russell (11,670) and Mount Dall (8,756).

The **east and south edges** are the travel corridor: the Parks Highway (route « 3 ») and the Alaska Railroad follow the Nenana River north past the entrance to Healy (11 mi / 18 km, clinic and airstrip) and south through Broad Pass to Cantwell (27 mi / 43 km, junction of the Denali Highway to Paxson, « road not plowed beyond this point in winter »), then down the Chulitna valley, past Hurricane Gulch and **Denali State Park** (a separate state park: Denali Viewpoint North and South, Byers Lake campground and the Alaska Veterans Memorial, the dashed Kesugi Ridge trail, K'esugi Ken campground, Troublesome Creek trailheads), to the Talkeetna turn-off. **Talkeetna** (346 ft / 105 m, 14 mi / 22 km off the highway, at the Susitna–Chulitna–Talkeetna confluence) carries the teal label « Walter Harper Talkeetna Ranger Station », the historical museum, the railroad depot and an airport; the Petersville Road heads west from Trapper Creek. The north edge is roadless apart from the old Stampede Trail west of Healy.

Four insets and extras matter for a builder: the « Headquarters Detail » (the entrance detail map), the Native place-name table (bottom right), a 0–20 km / 0–20 mi scale bar, and red notes on seasonal limits (« Weather conditions affect openings », « Private vehicles restricted beyond mile 15 »). Since August 2021 the road has been closed to vehicles at mile 43 (the **Pretty Rocks landslide**, mile 45.4, below Polychrome Pass); a 475-ft (145 m) steel truss bridge over the slide was completed on September 2, 2026, hikers and cyclists may cross it since August 27, 2026, and full bus service to Eielson, Wonder Lake and Kantishna is expected to return in 2027.

### Water

Every river north of the range is **glacier-fed and braided**: wide grey gravel plains (the Toklat's bar is ~1 km wide at mile 53, the McKinley River's ~1.6 km below Wonder Lake) where milky channels split and re-join, swelling on warm afternoons and shifting from year to year. They flow **north** (Savage, Sanctuary, Teklanika, Toklat and East Fork Toklat, into the Tanana basin) or **west** (the McKinley River along the foot of the range, turning north-west to the Kantishna). The **Nenana River** runs north past the entrance in its canyon (the rafting town of Nenana Canyon « Glitter Gulch » is just outside), and Riley Creek (Tsenesdghaas Na') joins it at mile 0. Clear-water creeks and **kettle lakes** hold most of the park's ten fish species (Arctic grayling in the creeks, lake trout, a few salmon streams in the west and south): Horseshoe Lake (an oxbow with beaver dams), Triple Lakes, the Teklanika and Polychrome kettle ponds, **Wonder Lake** (~4 km long) and **Reflection Pond**. South of the range, the Kahiltna, Tokositna and Ruth glaciers feed the Yentna, Chulitna and Susitna rivers. Glaciers cover about 16 % of the park; the Muldrow (32 mi / 51 km) has surged twice in a century. Rivers freeze from October to May and become the dog-team highways of winter.

### Relief

From ~73 m on the Yentna River (south-west preserve) to 6,190 m: the greatest base-to-summit rise of any mountain on land (~5,500 m). Along the road, the land steps like this: the entrance terraces at ~480–540 m above the Nenana canyon; a 400 m climb to treeline at Mountain Vista (~840 m); a sequence of **passes and river crossings** — braided valleys at 750–920 m (Sanctuary, Teklanika, East Fork, Toklat) separated by tundra passes at 1,130–1,215 m (Sable, Polychrome, Highway, Thorofare); Eielson at 1,138 m on a bench facing the range; then down to the kettle-lake lowland of Wonder Lake (~610 m) and Kantishna (~480 m). The road clings to **cliff shelves** at Polychrome Pass (a narrow ledge above a 300 m drop to the Plains of Murie) and at Pretty Rocks. In the game: terraces and one-level banks for the tundra ridges, wide flat gravel plains with shifting shallow channels for the rivers, a painted cliff band for Polychrome, a few 3D mid-size peaks near the road (Mount Healy, Igloo & Cathedral, Polychrome Mountain, Stony Dome), and the massif as a colossal backdrop: Mount McKinley's twin summits with its buttresses, Foraker to the right, the Muldrow Glacier's grey tongue flowing towards Eielson. Permafrost under the road makes it sag and slide (Pretty Rocks); black spruce lean on it (« drunken forest »).

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: 450–750 m boreal taiga — white spruce on river terraces, stunted **black spruce** in muskeg (leaning « drunken » trees), quaking aspen and paper birch on the sunny entrance slopes, willow and alder along creeks; 750–1,100 m shrub tundra — dwarf birch, willow thickets, blueberry; above ~1,000–1,200 m alpine tundra — cushion plants, lichens, scree; above ~2,000 m rock, snow and glacier. Treeline is only ~760 m, so most of what players see is open tundra.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#2F4A3A` black-spruce taiga · `#8C9A5B` summer tundra · `#B5462E` autumn bearberry red · `#D8A534` autumn dwarf-birch gold · `#A89F91` braided-river gravel · `#8FA3A8` milky glacial water · `#3D6E8C` kettle-lake blue · `#C47A3D` Polychrome orange volcanic rock · `#7A5A8C` Polychrome purple rock · `#EEF3F7` the Mountain's snow (shadows `#A9BCD6`).
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): the « Big Five » — **grizzly bear** (river bars and Sable Pass, digging ground squirrels, soapberries in August), **moose** (willow flats at the entrance and Savage; rut in September), **caribou** (Highway Pass to Eielson tundra, summer), **Dall sheep** (white dots on Polychrome, Igloo, Cathedral and Primrose), **gray wolf** (East Fork and Toklat packs, studied by Adolph Murie); plus red fox, Arctic ground squirrel (everywhere on the tundra), hoary marmot and collared pika (rock fields at Savage), snowshoe hare and lynx (taiga), beaver (Horseshoe Lake), wolverine (rare), **willow ptarmigan** (Alaska's state bird), golden eagle and gyrfalcon (Polychrome cliffs), long-tailed jaeger (Highway Pass), Arctic warbler (willows, from Asia), wood frog (the park's only amphibian).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): fireweed (magenta spikes July–August; dwarf fireweed on the river bars), mountain avens (Dryas, June), alpine forget-me-not (Alaska's state flower, June–July), moss campion (June), Arctic lupine, Labrador tea, soapberry and blueberry (bear food, late July–August), bearberry and dwarf birch (the red-and-gold carpet of late August).
- **Weather & seasons**: late May–June green-up with lingering snow on the passes; July wettest, often overcast, rain showers and dust on the gravel road; mid-August–early September the tundra turns red and gold and the first « termination dust » whitens the peaks; mid-September–April snow, plowing only to mile 3, −40° cold snaps; February brings longer days and the road reopening to Mountain Vista. The Mountain makes its own clouds: it shows fully on only a minority of summer days.
- **Night**: no true night from mid-May to late July (at the summer solstice the sun rises at ~3:30 and sets after midnight at the entrance); alpenglow on the summit at 11 pm; from late August the aurora season begins and runs to April; winter nights of ~19–20 h, very dark skies west of the entrance.
- **Sounds**: the bus's diesel and gravel crunch, the driver's radio calling a sighting; wind on the tundra passes; rushing braided channels and the clack of stones rolling in them; Arctic ground squirrels' alarm « sik-sik », ptarmigan's stuttering « go-back go-back », a wolf howl at East Fork, huskies barking and howling in chorus at the kennels, a train horn at the depot.

## Moments

- **« The Mountain is out »**: the summit is fully visible to only about a third of visitors (and cloud-free top to bottom for ~10 %); clear spells come most often on cold mornings, late evenings and in winter-spring. Eielson's window carries an etched outline showing where it would be.
- **Midnight sun and alpenglow** (late May–July): ~20.8 h of sun at the entrance on June 21 (sunrise ~3:32, sunset ~0:21 AKDT); the summit glows pink around 11 pm–midnight from Wonder Lake and Reflection Pond.
- **Sled dog demonstrations**: 30-minute programs at the kennels at 10 am, 2 pm and 4 pm in peak season (2026: 10 am May 20–Sep 17, 2 pm May 15–Sep 20, 4 pm June 1–Sep 7); the free bus leaves the visitor center 40 minutes before. Dogs have patrolled the park since 1922.
- **Wildlife jams on the road**: a bus stops whenever someone calls out an animal; grizzly sows with cubs on Sable Pass, Dall ewes on Polychrome in June, caribou bulls in velvet on Highway Pass in July.
- **Autumn blaze** (~August 20–September 10): the tundra turns red, orange and gold, the moose rut begins, the first snow dusts the range.
- **Road Lottery** (four days in mid-September, after the bus season): permit winners drive their own cars as far as the road is open (to East Fork during the closure; farther once the west end reopens).
- **Pretty Rocks reopening** (2026–2027): the steel truss bridge over the landslide (September 2, 2026) reopens the west end; the first buses to Eielson and Wonder Lake since 2021 are expected in 2027.
- **Aurora and Winterfest**: northern lights from late August to April; the Winterfest celebration every February; skiers, fat-bikers and mushers on the snow-covered road.
- **Climbing season** (May–July): tent city at Kahiltna base camp, ski planes shuttling from Talkeetna; the first ascent reached the South Peak on June 7, 1913, led on top by Walter Harper (Koyukon Athabascan).

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a curated, long east–west map of **~1,536 × 640 tiles**, built as a string of true-scale hotspots on a compressed road ribbon, with the Mountain as a vast backdrop to the south. Pieces: (1) **Entrance hub** ~360 × 300 tiles, near true scale for the visitor-center campus (~400 × 300 m), Riley Creek, the depot and the kennels, with distances between them squeezed about 3× (the real entrance area is ~5 × 3 km); (2) **Mountain Vista–Savage River** ~200 × 160 tiles (true scale for the loop and the canyon); (3) the **road ribbon** miles 15–66 compressed ~1:40 (123 km → ~2 km of play), punctuated by stop-able set pieces: Teklanika rest stop, Igloo, Sable Pass, East Fork, Pretty Rocks bridge, Polychrome ledge, Toklat bar, Highway Pass, Stony Hill; (4) **Eielson** ~180 × 140 tiles; (5) **Wonder Lake–Reflection Pond–McKinley Bar–Kantishna** ~300 × 240 tiles. Cut: the preserves, the Kantishna Hills' 4WD roads, the north-west flats. A separate **south-side vignette** (~384 × 384 tiles): Talkeetna's main street and ranger station, a ski plane, and a landing in the Don Sheldon Amphitheater on the Ruth Glacier (optionally Kahiltna base camp), linked by a **plane hop** from the Kantishna or McKinley Park airstrip.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Denali Visitor Center (timber-and-glass building with an exhibit hall), the Bus Depot, Murie Science and Learning Center, the railroad depot and a docked train, HQ log buildings and the **kennels** (rows of dog houses, a demonstration loop); Savage Cabin; Toklat rest tents; **Eielson** (a low building dug into the slope with a big south window — an interior with the etched-outline window); Wonder Lake ranger station; Kantishna roadhouse and Fannie Quigley's cabin. 3D landmarks: **Mount McKinley** (twin summits, three buttresses), Mount Foraker, Mount Healy, Polychrome cliffs, the **Pretty Rocks steel truss bridge** (145 m long, 15 m high). Vehicles: green transit bus, tan tour bus, the free shuttles on loops, the train, bush plane.
- **Activities**: ride the bus and call stops (a shared « spot & stop » mini-game: the first to press on an animal stops the bus for everyone); hop off anywhere past mile 15 to hike off-trail; the entrance trails (Horseshoe Lake, Mount Healy, Triple Lakes); photo spots — Mountain Vista, Polychrome Overlook, Stony Hill, Eielson window, Wonder Lake, **Reflection Pond at alpenglow**, Horseshoe Lake beaver dam, a husky portrait; field guide with the Big Five; herbarium of tundra flowers; ranger programs — sled dog demo, campground talks, Discovery Hikes (off-trail, sign-up); Junior Ranger book (in person or online) with Discovery Packs; winter mode — ski, snowshoe or **mush a dog team** along the snowy road, aurora at night; bike the road (allowed on the whole road); raft the Nenana at the edge; flightseeing and a glacier landing; an optional Denali expedition vignette on the West Buttress.
- **Passport stamps**: the official list (2026) has three Denali cancellations — « Denali NP », « Denali Park » and « Talkeetna » — found at the Denali Visitor Center, the Murie Science and Learning Center (winter visitor center), the Denali Bus Depot, and the Walter Harper Talkeetna Ranger Station; Eielson, Toklat and Wonder Lake ranger station have carried stamps when the west end is open.
- **With friends / with the world**: a whole party boards one bus and shares its windows (split screen by seat); co-op wildlife spotting and « hold still » photo moments; fording a braided river together (link arms, face upstream — the party crossing is safer); the sled dog team pulled by one player while others ride; gathering spots for strangers — the Bus Depot queue, the visitor center plaza, the kennels demo stands, Eielson's window, the Wonder Lake shore at 11 pm waiting for the Mountain; a group photo in front of the park entrance sign at mile 0.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay 300 yards (275 m) from bears and 25 yards (23 m) from other animals — the bus stays closed near wildlife; never feed anything; no walking on Sable Pass (wildlife closure); spread out on the tundra instead of trampling one path; cross rivers at braided, shallow places; pack out everything, bear-proof food containers in the backcountry; backcountry units have quotas (a permit screen); pets only on roads and campgrounds; in winter, yield to dog teams.
- **Implementation challenges**: the camera looks north but **every view here is south** (the range lies south of the road) — the Mountain needs a special south-facing photo/viewpoint mode or a sky layer drawn behind the northern horizon; a 5,500 m relief backdrop far beyond the tile world (a skybox-scale 3D peak with its own weather: cloud caps, the « Mountain is out » state); braided rivers with shifting, fordable channels and glacial silt; tundra without trees (the open ground must still read well); black-spruce « drunken forest »; a timetabled bus system on one road with stops, hop-on/hop-off and NPC traffic; midnight sun (no night in June) and aurora; winter road closure and dog-sledding; glaciers and a glacier landing strip; a landslide and bridge.
