# Wind Cave National Park — `WICA`

> South Dakota · established January 9, 1903 · 137.5 km² (33,971 acres) · 606,258 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~1,065–1,528 m on the surface (the cave reaches ~160 m below the visitor center) · NPS region MWR
>
> Two worlds in one small park: above, rolling mixed-grass prairie and ponderosa hills of the southern Black Hills
> with free-roaming bison, elk and prairie dogs; below, one of the world's longest and most complex caves, lined with
> boxwork, which "breathes" through a tiny hole. In Hearthlight it should feel like a prairie safari at dawn followed by
> a ranger-led descent into a honeycomb labyrinth.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a blocky polygon with a panhandle to the south-east. The **west third** is the Black Hills'
edge: ponderosa ridges and the park's main road corridor. **US 385** enters from the north-west (from Pringle and
Custer), joins **SD 87** in the north-west quarter, and the combined highway runs south past the **visitor center**
(on a short spur west of the highway, with the elevator building 200 yd south of it) and on across **Bison Flats** and
**Gobbler Pass** to Hot Springs. North of the junction, SD 87 climbs north along **Rankin Ridge** (the park's high
point, a fire lookout tower at 1,528 m, reached by a short spur) and loops through a bridge underpass near Reaves
Gulch before entering **Custer State Park**, which borders the whole north edge. **Elk Mountain Campground** sits in
ponderosas west of the highway, 1 mi north-west of the visitor center.

The **east two-thirds** is open, rolling prairie drained by **Beaver Creek** (flowing east across the middle of the
park) and its tributary **Highland Creek** (from the north). **Wind Cave Canyon** cuts east from right beside the visitor
center; the cave's **natural entrance** is a small hole in its head, a few hundred metres from the visitor center. The
gravel **NPS 5** road leaves SD 87 near the Custer State Park line and runs south-east past **Lone Pine Overlook** into
the **Red Valley** (a red-soil racetrack valley ringing the Black Hills) and out of the park south-east; **NPS 6** branches
north-east from it to Custer State Park; **Boland Ridge** rises east of NPS 6. The south-east panhandle (Red Valley Road,
Beaver Creek's exit) holds the park's newest land, around the historic Sanson Ranch.

Trails connect the two halves: the **Centennial Trail** (the southern end of the 111-mile Black Hills trail to Bear
Butte) crosses from SD 87 over Beaver Creek to NPS 5; the **Highland Creek Trail** (8.6 mi, the longest) links Wind
Cave Canyon to NPS 5 through prairie, pine and creek bottoms; **Lookout Point**, **Sanctuary**, **East Bison Flats**, **Cold
Brook Canyon** and **Boland Ridge** trails fill in; three short nature trails sit at the visitor center (Prairie Vista),
the campground (Elk Mountain) and Rankin Ridge. Wind Cave is an open-hike park: off-trail walking is allowed.

Underground (not on the brochure), the cave lies under the visitor center area in three main levels of maze passages.
All visitors enter with a ranger: by the elevator for the Garden of Eden, Fairgrounds and Accessibility tours, or by a
walk-in entrance near the natural entrance for the Natural Entrance Tour (which exits by elevator); the Candlelight
Tour explores the unlit Blue Grotto route, the Wild Cave Tour crawls through undeveloped passages.

### Water

- **Beaver Creek**: the park's main stream, flowing east across the middle of the park from the Black Hills to the
  plains (and the South Fork Cheyenne), with cottonwoods, willows and a few pools; **Highland Creek** joins from the
  north. **Cold Brook** and the dry **Wind Cave Canyon** drain south-east. Water must be treated; springs are few.
- **Underground**: Wind Cave is a dry cave overall (few dripstones), but its lowest level reaches the water table, with
  lakes discovered by explorers (Windy City Lake, Calcite Lake) — part of the Madison aquifer that feeds the region.
- **The cave's wind**: when air pressure outside rises (fair weather) air flows in; when it falls (before a storm) the
  cave exhales — the natural entrance gusts noticeably, as the Bingham brothers found in 1881.

### Relief

Surface: lowest ~1,065 m where Beaver Creek leaves the park on the east, highest **Rankin Ridge 1,528 m** (5,013 ft).
The west is the Black Hills' rim: ponderosa ridges and the limestone plateau (the Pahasapa/Madison limestone that holds
the cave) cut by canyons (Wind Cave, Cold Brook, Beaver Creek); eastward the land steps down onto rolling prairie
hills and the red-soil **Red Valley** trough, with **Boland Ridge** a hogback rising beyond it. Underground: three levels
of maze passage stacked in ~200 m of limestone, boxwork fins on walls and ceilings, rooms up to a few tens of metres
wide, and lakes at the bottom. In the game: gentle banks on the prairie, one- and two-level limestone cliffs along the
canyons, a pine ridge with a 3D lookout tower, and the cave as a dark multi-level maze instance with tight stairs.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation**: mixed-grass prairie (western wheatgrass, little and big bluestem, blue grama, buffalograss, needle-
  and-thread) over the east and south; ponderosa pine forest on the western ridges and north (Rankin Ridge, Elk
  Mountain), with Rocky Mountain juniper; bur oak, green ash, box elder, cottonwood and chokecherry in the creek
  bottoms; western snowberry and sumac thickets on slopes; nothing grows in the cave.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#B8B27A` summer prairie grass · `#D2BE86` late-summer golden grass · `#5B6E45`
  ponderosa green · `#C87A5A` Red Valley soil · `#D9D2C2` grey-white limestone cliffs · `#8B7E6A` prairie-dog-town dirt
  mounds · `#6C8FA0` Beaver Creek pools · cave: `#C9B89E` boxwork fins, `#EDE7DA` frostwork and popcorn, `#8A5A3C` red
  paleofill clay, `#211C18` dark passages.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): American bison (~400, one of the few genetically pure, free-roaming herds; founded in
  1913 with bison from the Bronx Zoo), elk (bugling September–October), pronghorn (east prairie), mule and white-tailed
  deer, black-tailed prairie dog (many towns along the roads), black-footed ferret (night; reintroduced 2007),
  coyote, mountain lion (rare), bobcat, badger, porcupine, prairie rattlesnake, wild turkey, red-headed and Lewis's
  woodpeckers (Wind Cave Canyon snags), cliff swallow and canyon wren (canyon cliffs), prairie falcon, burrowing owl.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): pasque flower (March–April), prairie smoke (May), soapweed yucca (June), purple coneflower
  (July), wild bergamot (July), sunflowers (August), bur oak (bronze in autumn), ponderosa pine.
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — wet, greening prairie, bison calves (April–May); summer — hot afternoons, towering
  storms with hail and lightning, prairie-dog pups; autumn — golden grass, elk bugling, crisp nights; winter — snow,
  roads closed at times, bison crusted with frost, the cave the warmest place around at 12 °C.
- **Night**: dark prairie skies away from towns, coyote choruses, elk bugles in autumn, owls; the cave in total
  darkness.
- **Sounds**: prairie-dog barks, meadowlarks, wind in ponderosas, bison grunts and hooves, elk bugles, thunder; in the
  cave, the whoosh of air at the entrance door, drips, the ranger's voice echoing, silence when the lights go out.

## Moments

- **The cave breathes**: at the natural entrance, air rushes out before storms (falling pressure) and in during fair
  weather — a ribbon or the ranger's hat shows it.
- **Elk bugling** (September–October) at dawn and dusk around Elk Mountain Campground.
- **Bison calving** (April–May) and **the rut** (July–August) on the prairie; bison jams on US 385.
- **Prairie-dog pups** (May–June) at the towns along SD 87 and NPS 5.
- **Candlelight and Wild Cave tours** (summer only): exploring an unlit section by candle bucket, or crawling like a caver.
- **Rankin Ridge on a clear day**: the Badlands on the eastern horizon from the lookout trail.
- **Summer hail storms** over the prairie; **winter snow** closing NPS 5 and NPS 6.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a surface map of **512 × 480 tiles** plus a cave instance (~512 × 512 tiles, three levels). Keep
  the visitor-center complex near true scale (visitor center, elevator building and natural entrance within ~400 m →
  ~200 tiles), the Beaver Creek–Lookout Point–Centennial loop at ~1:4, the east prairie (Red Valley, Boland Ridge) at
  ~1:8 as a wide bison range, Rankin Ridge as the north-west high point. Cave: compress the tour routes (each ~1 km)
  to ~300–400 tiles of narrow, stair-stepped passages; the rest of the 270 km maze is implied by side passages behind
  railings.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the stone visitor center (interior with the tour desk, bison and cave exhibits), the elevator
  building, the natural-entrance hole with its wind effect, Elk Mountain Campground and amphitheater, the Rankin Ridge
  lookout tower (3D), prairie-dog towns as living set pieces, the Beaver Creek footbridge; underground: boxwork
  "honeycomb" walls, the Fairgrounds' big rooms, the Garden of Eden, a candle-lit Blue Grotto.
- **Activities**: timed ranger cave tours (tickets, a queue, the elevator, a group walk with a talk and a lights-out
  moment), the Candlelight and Wild Cave tours as harder unlocks; prairie wildlife watching (bison, elk, pronghorn,
  prairie dogs; ferret spotlighting at night); hiking with open off-trail travel; photo spots — the natural entrance,
  boxwork close-up, the lookout tower, bison on Bison Flats, Lone Pine Overlook, elk at Elk Mountain; Junior Ranger
  (booklet, a "cave explorer" and a "prairie ranger" badge); camping at Elk Mountain; driving the scenic roads into
  Custer State Park.
- **Passport stamps**: one cancellation, "Hot Springs", at the visitor center (Eastern National list, August 2025).
- **With friends / with the world**: a cave tour is naturally a group activity (up to a party per tour, the
  Candlelight Tour limited to 10, Wild Cave to 6); a dawn bison-and-elk safari along NPS 5; an evening program at the
  Elk Mountain amphitheater; a squeeze-box challenge (the 25 cm crawl) as a party dare; group photo at the lookout tower.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): no touching the cave walls or formations, no food, drinks or bags
  underground, no clothes worn in another cave (white-nose syndrome), stay on the cave trail; on the surface 25 yd from
  wildlife and far more from bison, don't feed prairie dogs, pets only on two trails, collect nothing (antlers, bones,
  rocks), respect the natural entrance as a sacred Lakota site.
- **Implementation challenges**: a multi-level maze cave with low ceilings, stairs and boxwork texture; air-pressure
  "breathing" tied to the weather system; ticketed, timed group tours; large herds on a small map; representing the
  Lakota emergence story respectfully (use the park's shared version, credited, no invented lore).
