# Yosemite National Park — `YOSE`

> California · established October 1, 1890 (the Valley and the Mariposa Grove were first protected by the Yosemite
> Grant of June 30, 1864) · 3,082.7 km² (761,747.50 acres) · 4,278,413 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 648–3,997 m · NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10, California–Great Basin)
>
> A slab of the High Sierra centred on a glacier-carved granite trench whose kilometre-high walls pour out some of
> the tallest waterfalls on Earth, ringed by giant sequoia groves, a high country of domes, meadows and lakes, and a
> roadless wilderness that covers 95 % of the park. In Hearthlight it should feel like a crowded, cosy village at
> the bottom of a cathedral: every trail climbs out of the Valley toward quieter, wider and higher places.

## Reading the map

The park is a rounded, slightly lopsided block, 60 km wide and 77 km tall, on the west slope of the Sierra Nevada. Its **east edge follows the Sierra crest** (Hoover Wilderness and the Inyo National Forest beyond, Mono Lake 15 km outside); the west edge is ragged, notched where the gateway roads come in. Two great rivers drain it westward: the **Tuolumne** across the northern half and the **Merced** across the southern half, and nearly everything a visitor sees hangs off them. The brochure divides the park into five visited districts: **Hetch Hetchy** in the north-west, **Yosemite Valley** a little south-west of centre, **Glacier Point Road and Badger Pass** on the plateau south of the Valley, **Wawona and the Mariposa Grove** at the southern tip, and the **Tioga Road–Tuolumne Meadows** high country across the middle. The entire northern third and the south-east are roadless wilderness.

**Roads.** All roads funnel toward the Valley from the west. Hwy 140 climbs the Merced gorge through the gateway of El Portal to the **Arch Rock Entrance** and becomes the El Portal Road. Hwy 120 enters at **Big Oak Flat** (an information station and Hodgdon Meadow campground) and runs south-east as the Big Oak Flat Road past the Tuolumne and Merced sequoia groves to **Crane Flat** (gas, the Tioga Road junction), then drops through three short tunnels to the Valley floor, joining the El Portal Road near the Cascades. Hwy 41 enters at the **South Entrance**, passes the **Mariposa Grove Welcome Plaza** and **Wawona**, climbs north as the Wawona Road over **Chinquapin** (junction of the Glacier Point Road) and bores through the Wawona Tunnel to burst out at **Tunnel View**. The **Tioga Road** (Hwy 120 E) leaves Crane Flat and crosses the park eastward for 45.5 mi (73 km) — White Wolf, Yosemite Creek, Porcupine Flat, Olmsted Point, Tenaya Lake, Tuolumne Meadows — to the **Tioga Pass Entrance** at 3,031 m, then falls to Lee Vining; the map marks it « closed approx. November to May east of » Crane Flat. The **Glacier Point Road** runs 16 mi (26 km) east from Chinquapin past Badger Pass to Glacier Point (closed November–May beyond the ski area, and no vehicles over 30 ft past the Sentinel Dome trailhead). The **Hetch Hetchy Road** leaves Hwy 120 just outside Big Oak Flat, goes north past Mather and the Hetch Hetchy Entrance and ends on O'Shaughnessy Dam (vehicles over 25 ft long or 8 ft wide banned). The **Mariposa Grove Road** is closed to private cars: a free shuttle runs the 2 mi (3.2 km) from the Welcome Plaza, from spring (April–May) to November 30.

**Yosemite Valley** (the detail map) is a flat-floored trench ~11 km long (7 mi) and up to 1.6 km wide, running WSW–ENE at 1,200 m with the Merced River meandering west along it. A one-way loop drives it: **Northside Drive** westbound, **Southside Drive** eastbound, cross-linked at El Capitan Bridge (mid-valley) and at Sentinel Bridge and the Village. From the west: Tunnel View and the Wawona Road on the south slope; Bridalveil Fall and Cathedral Rocks on the south wall; **El Capitan** on the north wall facing them across El Capitan Meadow; Three Brothers and Eagle Peak; then **Yosemite Falls** pouring off the north rim above Camp 4 and Yosemite Valley Lodge. **Yosemite Village** sits on the north side just east of the falls (Welcome Center, Wilderness Center, Museum and the reconstructed Ahwahnee village, Ansel Adams Gallery, Village Store, Degnan's, clinic, post office, court). Farther east on the north side, **The Ahwahnee** hotel stands under the Royal Arches and Washington Column. On the south side are the Chapel, Housekeeping Camp on the river, the LeConte Memorial Lodge, **Curry Village**, and the three Pines campgrounds; the east end is a shuttle, bike and foot loop to **Happy Isles** (nature centre, trailhead of the Mist Trail and the John Muir Trail) and to **Mirror Lake** in Tenaya Canyon under the sheer face of **Half Dome**. Above the south wall, **Glacier Point** looks straight down on Curry Village from 980 m higher: 2 km away as the raven flies, an hour away by road — or the Four Mile Trail. South-east of Happy Isles the Merced climbs the « Giant Staircase » of **Vernal** and **Nevada falls** (Liberty Cap and Mount Broderick above) into Little Yosemite Valley.

**The high country.** North of the Valley a granite upland of domes, lodgepole forest and meadows (2,100–3,000 m) carries the Tioga Road. **Tenaya Lake** (2,482 m) lies right on the road below Polly Dome; **Olmsted Point** looks back down Tenaya Canyon to Half Dome and Clouds Rest. **Tuolumne Meadows** (2,614 m), 23 km north-east of the Village as the raven flies but ~90 km by road, is a 4 km-long meadow along the Tuolumne River ringed by domes (Lembert, Pothole, Fairview) with the jagged Cathedral Range to the south (Cathedral Peak, Unicorn Peak) and Mount Dana and Mount Gibbs on the crest to the east. Two canyons meet here: **Lyell Canyon** (the John Muir / Pacific Crest trail south-east to Mount Lyell, 3,997 m, and its glacier) and **Dana Meadows** up to Tioga Pass. Downstream the Tuolumne drops north-west into the **Grand Canyon of the Tuolumne** (Glen Aulin, Waterwheel Falls, Pate Valley) and on to Hetch Hetchy. North of that, the brochure shows only trails: parallel north–south canyons (Jack Main, Stubblefield, Kerrick, Matterhorn, Virginia) rising to Tower Peak, Sawtooth Ridge and Matterhorn Peak on the northern crest.

**The south.** The Glacier Point Road crosses a forested plateau of meadows (Summit, McGurk, Mono) with Badger Pass Ski Area and Bridalveil Creek campground; south and east of it lies the wilderness of the Merced headwaters — Buena Vista Crest, the Clark Range (Mount Clark, Red Peak, Merced Peak), Merced Lake — with the Ostrander ski hut and the Buck Camp patrol cabin. **Wawona** sits in the flat valley of the South Fork Merced near the southern boundary: the Victorian Wawona Hotel, a golf meadow, the Yosemite History Center with its covered bridge, and the visitor centre in Thomas Hill's studio. The **Mariposa Grove** of ~500 giant sequoias fills the hill just east of the South Entrance, the park's southern tip.

### Water

Two westward river systems organise the park. The **Merced** is born under Mount Lyell and the Clark Range, fills **Merced Lake**, crosses Little Yosemite Valley and drops over **Nevada Fall** (181 m) and **Vernal Fall** (97 m) — the Giant Staircase — to Happy Isles, then meanders west down the Valley past Curry Village, the Village and El Capitan Meadow (sandy beaches, gentle summer float) before crashing through the boulder gorge below Pohono Bridge to El Portal. Tenaya Creek joins it from Mirror Lake; Yosemite Creek from the north; Bridalveil Creek and Illilouette Creek from the south. The **South Fork Merced** runs through Wawona under the covered bridge. The **Tuolumne** rises in Lyell Canyon and Dana Meadows, winds through **Tuolumne Meadows**, plunges down the Grand Canyon of the Tuolumne in a string of falls (Tuolumne Falls, White Cascade, California, LeConte and Waterwheel falls — the last throwing « wheels » of water in June) and is dammed in the north-west as **Hetch Hetchy Reservoir** (~13 km long, San Francisco's drinking water: no swimming or boating) behind O'Shaughnessy Dam; **Lake Eleanor** is a second reservoir north of it.

The Valley's **waterfalls** are snowmelt-fed: they roar in May–June and most shrink or dry by August–September (Yosemite Falls usually stops; Bridalveil and Vernal keep a trickle). Lakes: roadside **Tenaya Lake**, high-country Cathedral, May, Elizabeth, Dog and Gaylor lakes, the seasonal **Mirror Lake**, and hundreds of wilderness tarns. **Soda Springs** bubble carbonated water in Tuolumne Meadows. Ice: the **Lyell** and **Maclure** glaciers under the highest peaks (shrinking fast), and « frazil ice » that builds up in Yosemite Creek below the falls on cold spring nights.

### Relief

From **648 m** (the Merced where it leaves the park near El Portal) to **3,997 m** (Mount Lyell). The park is a granite staircase tilted west. The **Valley floor** is flat at ~1,200 m, walled by near-vertical cliffs 900–1,450 m high (El Capitan's face ~900 m; Half Dome 1,470 m above the floor; Glacier Point 980 m above Curry Village), with hanging side valleys spilling falls. The **rims** (1,800–2,500 m) are rolling forested plateaus: Glacier Point Road to the south, the Tioga Road country to the north, both studded with **domes** (Sentinel, North, Lembert, Pothole, Fairview, Polly) — smooth granite bald-heads. The **crest** (3,300–4,000 m) runs along the east edge: Mount Dana, Kuna Crest, Mount Lyell, the Cathedral and Clark ranges, and Sawtooth Ridge in the north. Deep U-shaped canyons (Tenaya, Lyell, Tuolumne, Hetch Hetchy) cut the upland.

For the game: the Valley walls are the tallest continuous cliffs Hearthlight will ever draw — a stack of cliff levels with painted, vertically streaked faces, rising from walkable **banks** along the river. The switchback trails (Yosemite Falls, Four Mile, Mist Trail) become **stairs**; the domes want smooth, rounded height ramps rather than stepped plateaus; Half Dome, El Capitan, Cathedral Peak, Mount Dana and Lyell deserve **3D peak** landmarks. The camera looks north: the Valley's north wall (El Capitan, Yosemite Falls, Royal Arches) faces it and reads beautifully, while the south wall (Cathedral Rocks, Sentinel, Glacier Point) stands *between* camera and floor — it must fade or cut away when the hero walks below it. Half Dome seen from the south shows its classic profile, the sheer cut on the left (north-west) and the dome on the right.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: foothill woodland (550–900 m, the Merced gorge at El Portal and the Hetch Hetchy approach): gray pine, blue oak, canyon live oak, chamise, manzanita, redbud and poppies. Lower montane (900–1,800 m, the Valley, Wawona, Hetch Hetchy): California black oak — the Valley's signature tree — ponderosa pine, incense cedar, white fir, Pacific dogwood, western azalea, bigleaf maple. Giant sequoia groves at 1,650–2,050 m (Mariposa, Tuolumne, Merced). Upper montane (1,800–2,400 m, Crane Flat, Glacier Point Road, White Wolf): red fir, Jeffrey and sugar pine, lodgepole. Subalpine (2,400–3,000 m, Tioga Road and Tuolumne): lodgepole, western white pine, mountain hemlock and flower meadows. Alpine (above ~2,900 m, Dana, Lyell, Gaylor): whitebark pine krummholz and cushion plants.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#D9D6CF` sunlit granite · `#9C9892` granite in shadow · `#4E4A45` dark water-streaks down the walls · `#8DB05A` Valley meadow in May · `#C9B274` meadow gold in late summer · `#3D5A3A` conifer green · `#C06A2B` black oak and maple in October · `#5E9C8D` the Merced's green pools · `#2E5E8C` alpine lake blue · `#F4F5F6` spray and snow · `#F08A3C` the firefall's glow.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): black bear (300–500, mostly brown-coated; meadows and campgrounds at dusk, acorns in autumn); mule deer (Valley meadows, bolder than they look); coyote (Valley and Tuolumne meadows); Sierra Nevada bighorn sheep (Cathedral Range herd reintroduced 2015, Mount Gibbs herd by Tioga Pass); great gray owl (~65 % of California's few hundred, hunting meadow edges at dusk and dawn in April–June); peregrine falcon (on the big walls, spring nesting closures); Pacific fisher (rare, the Wawona and Mariposa Grove forests); Sierra Nevada red fox (camera traps only, northern wilderness); yellow-bellied marmot (Olmsted Point, Tioga); Belding's ground squirrel (Tuolumne Meadows); American pika (talus near Gaylor Lakes); Steller's jay (everywhere, bold); American dipper (bobbing on river rocks and under waterfalls, even in winter); western tanager (summer conifers); Yosemite toad (wet high meadows at snowmelt); Mount Lyell salamander (granite talus near snow, rolls downhill to escape).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): Pacific dogwood (white bracts in May, red leaves in October), California black oak (acorns in autumn, gold in November), redbud and tufted poppies in the Merced canyon from March, western azalea (May), giant sequoia (the Grizzly Giant; cones all year), snow plant (scarlet, Glacier Point Road meadows in early summer), little elephant's heads and shooting stars (Tuolumne, late June–August), whitebark pine (Tioga Pass and treeline), aspens on the Tioga Road (gold in October), lupines (~26 species).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter (December–March) brings snow or rain to the Valley, frosty meadows, the ice rink at Curry Village, skiing at Badger Pass, the Tioga and Glacier Point roads closed. Spring (April–June) is the waterfall season (peak mid–late May), dogwoods, frazil ice and moonbows; the high country stays snowbound until June. Summer (July–September) is hot in the Valley (~32 °C) with afternoon thunderstorms on the crest, the high country open, and often 1–3 weeks of wildfire smoke in August–September; Yosemite Falls usually runs dry. Autumn (October–November) brings black oak, maple and aspen colour, empty falls and the first snow in Tuolumne.
- **Night**: dark skies outside the Valley (Tuolumne is near-pristine); Glacier Point star parties with telescopes on summer weekends, « Stars over Tuolumne »; climbers' headlamps twinkling on El Capitan in spring and autumn; moonbows in the spray of Yosemite Falls on spring full moons; bears on their rounds, great gray owls at dusk, 17 kinds of bats.
- **Sounds**: the roar of Yosemite Falls in May (heard across the Valley), wind gusting spray on the Mist Trail, the shuttle's hiss and doors, Steller's jays scolding, the American dipper's song over the river, woodpeckers, climbers calling « on belay! » far up the wall, the rare rumble of a rockfall, bear-locker doors clanking at the campgrounds, marmot whistles and pika « eeps » at Tioga, the Yosemite toad's trill at snowmelt, afternoon thunder over the crest.

## Moments

- **The Horsetail Fall « firefall »**: for ~2 weeks in mid–late February (projected window February 10–26 in 2026), on clear evenings when the fall is flowing, the setting sun lights the thin fall on El Capitan's east edge orange for ~10 minutes. Viewers walk 1.5 mi (2.4 km) to the El Capitan Picnic Area viewing zone; Northside Drive gives a lane to pedestrians; the south riverbank is closed. (The old Glacier Point Firefall — burning embers pushed off the cliff at 9 pm — ran from 1872 until January 25, 1968.)
- **Peak waterfalls**: mid–late May (fullest May–early June); Yosemite Falls usually shrinks to nothing by August.
- **Moonbows**: full-moon nights at peak flow, April–June, in the spray of Upper and Lower Yosemite Fall.
- **Frazil ice**: spring mornings (mostly April) after freezing nights, Yosemite Creek below the lower fall flows like slush until ~9 am.
- **Dogwood and redbud bloom** in May in the Valley; **Tuolumne wildflowers** late June–August, peak ~July.
- **The Tioga Road opens**: median ~May 23 after 1–2 months of plowing (May 15 in 2026; as late as July 22 in 2023); it closes again ~mid-November.
- **Half Dome cables go up** from the Friday before Memorial Day to the day after the second Monday of October.
- **Autumn colour**: mid–late October (aspens on the Tioga Road, bigleaf maples on the south wall, black oaks).
- **El Capitan by night**: climbers' headlamps in the spring and autumn climbing seasons; spotting scopes at El Capitan Meadow by day.
- **Glacier Point star parties**: summer weekends (July–August), after 8:30 pm.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: ~1,280 × 1,536 tiles, north up, with the park's real arrangement kept: the Valley a little south-west of centre, Tuolumne to the north-east, Wawona and the Mariposa Grove at the southern tip, Hetch Hetchy in the north-west. The Valley floor becomes a ~720 × 150-tile trench (its 11 km squeezed ~8×) whose clusters keep near true scale — Yosemite Village, the Lodge and Camp 4, Curry Village, the base of Yosemite Falls, Happy Isles; the walls stay tall. Glacier Point sits on the south rim right above Curry Village, ~60 tiles away but a long drive round; the Mist Trail's staircase keeps its drama (~250 tiles). The Tioga Road compresses to ~600 tiles of travel with Olmsted Point, Tenaya Lake and Tuolumne Meadows (200 × 80) as its stops; the Mariposa Grove ~160 × 160; Hetch Hetchy a ~250-tile reservoir vignette. The northern and south-eastern wilderness shrink to a band of canyons (the Grand Canyon of the Tuolumne with Glen Aulin, Little Yosemite Valley and Merced Lake), with the crest peaks as 3D backdrop.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Yosemite Village plaza (Welcome Center, Museum and the Indian Village behind it, Degnan's), Curry Village, Yosemite Valley Lodge and Camp 4, The Ahwahnee (the Great Lounge interior), Glacier Point (amphitheatre, geology hut), Tuolumne Meadows (store and grill, Parsons Lodge interior), Wawona (hotel veranda, History Center, covered bridge), the Mariposa Grove Welcome Plaza. 3D landmarks: Half Dome, El Capitan, Yosemite Falls (three tiers), Bridalveil Fall, Sentinel Rock, Cathedral Rocks, Three Brothers, Royal Arches and Washington Column, North Dome, Clouds Rest, Lembert Dome, Cathedral Peak, Mount Dana, O'Shaughnessy Dam, the Wawona Tunnel portal, the Grizzly Giant, the California Tunnel Tree, the Chapel.
- **Activities**: hiking (the list above); photo spots (Tunnel View, Valley View, Glacier Point, Sentinel Bridge at sunset, Cook's Meadow, El Capitan Meadow, Mirror Lake, Taft Point's railing, Olmsted Point, Tenaya Lake, Lembert Dome, the Grizzly Giant); wildlife spotting (bears from a distance, great gray owls at dusk, marmots, pikas); ranger programmes (Valley walks, « Ask a Climber » scopes, Tuolumne walks, campfire talks); Junior Ranger (handbook for ages 4–12: 5 or 7 activities plus one ranger programme → the badge); water (float the Merced from Stoneman Bridge to Sentinel Beach in June–July, swim at Tenaya Lake and the Valley beaches in late summer); vehicles (car, the free Valley, Tuolumne and Mariposa Grove shuttles, the Glacier Point tour bus, YARTS buses, bikes on ~12 mi of Valley paths, horse rides at Wawona, mule strings to the High Sierra Camps); winter (Badger Pass skiing, snowshoe walks, the Curry Village ice rink, Crane Flat sledding); climbing (bouldering at Camp 4; watching El Capitan).
- **Passport stamps**: Yosemite Valley Welcome Center (all year), Tuolumne Meadows Visitor Center (summer), Wawona Visitor Center at Hill's Studio (summer), Big Oak Flat Information Station (summer), and the Mariposa Grove Depot (the Yosemite Conservancy shop at the Welcome Plaza) — the five places the 2026 park newspaper lists.
- **With friends / with the world**: the firefall as the open world's big date — everyone walks to the El Capitan picnic area for a ten-minute sunset that happens only on clear February evenings; the Half Dome cables as a party climb (a queue, passing etiquette, a summit photo); a raft flotilla down the Merced; a shuttle-bus scavenger hunt round all 18 stops; a relay up the Four Mile Trail while friends drive the hour round to meet them at Glacier Point; Camp 4 as the evening gathering place (boulders, stories, a campfire); Glacier Point star parties; group photo at Tunnel View.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): every crumb in the bear locker (a cooler left in the car at night means a smashed window and a « Red Bear, Dead Bear » lesson); 50 yards from bears and coyotes, 25 from other animals; obey the speed limits — bears and great gray owls die on the roads; stay on the boardwalks across the meadows; never swim above the waterfalls (Emerald Pool, Silver Apron) or step past the railings; don't feed the Glacier Point squirrels; respect peregrine closures on the walls; a permit for Half Dome and for any night in the wilderness.
- **Implementation challenges**: kilometre-high vertical walls (the tallest cliffs in the game) and a south wall that stands between the camera and the Valley floor — it must fade or cut away; waterfalls whose flow follows the calendar (roaring in May, gone in August) and wind-blown spray; the firefall's sunset lighting of one waterfall on a few days a year; the Half Dome cables (a near-vertical slab with a queue); seasonal road closures and a ski area; moonbows (a night rainbow in spray); the 1.3 km Wawona Tunnel; a seasonal lake that dries into a meadow; 64 m sequoias; a dammed reservoir; a shuttle loop with 18 stops and crowds.
