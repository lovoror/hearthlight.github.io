# Mammoth Cave National Park — `MACA`

> Kentucky · established July 1, 1941 (World Heritage Site 1981, Biosphere Reserve 1990, International Dark Sky Park 2021) · 291.5 km² (72,042 acres within the boundary, 52,008 federal) · 660,734 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 128–282 m on the surface (the deepest toured passages lie ~110 m below it) · NPS region SER (Southeast; DOI Region 1)
>
> The world's longest known cave system — more than 426 miles of surveyed passages — lies beneath a quiet forested plateau of sinkholes and hollows split by the green Green River. In Hearthlight it should feel like two worlds stacked: a gentle Kentucky forest with a cable ferry, a lodge and a country lane of cave entrances above, and below, lantern-lit halls, domes, a buried river and the frozen waterfall of stone at Frozen Niagara.

## Reading the map

North is up. The park is a rough, rounded block of forest about 25 km across, and the **Green River** crosses it from east to west in big meanders — entering near Dennison Ferry at the north-east (river mile ~210), looping south past the Visitor Center (RM ~195), bending around Turnhole Bend and Sand Cave Island, and leaving at the west near Houchin Ferry and Brownsville (RM ~180); the **Nolin River** comes down from the Nolin River Lake dam and joins it just inside the north-west corner. The river divides the park into two very different halves. **South of the river** is the developed side: the **Visitor Center** cluster on a bluff above the river (Historic Entrance, the Lodge at Mammoth Cave, campground, Caver's Camp Store, the Green River Ferry at the foot of the hill), and, running south-east along **Mammoth Cave Ridge**, the string of other cave entrances reached by tour buses — **Violet City**, **Carmichael**, the **Elevator**, **New Entrance** and **Frozen Niagara** — with Sand Cave, Cedar Sink, Sloans Crossing Pond and Turnhole Bend around them. **North of the river** is the backcountry: a maze of ridges and hollows (Big Hollow, Buffalo Creek, First Creek, McCoy Hollow, Raymer Hollow, Collie Ridge) with ~60 mi of hiking, horse and bike trails, backcountry campsites, Maple Springs group/horse campground and research centre, Great Onyx Cave, the old-growth Big Woods at the north-east and the White Oak trailhead.

Roads come in from the south and east. **Mammoth Cave Parkway** runs 9 mi north from I-65 at Park City (exit 48) past Sloans Crossing Pond to the Visitor Center; **Cave City Road/SR 70** comes west from Cave City (exit 53) and joins it near the Frozen Niagara/New Entrance area; **Brownsville Road** (SR 70) runs west to Brownsville past Cedar Sink and Joppa Ridge; **Flint Ridge Road** heads north-east from the Visitor Center along Flint Ridge (past the Mammoth Cave Baptist Church, Crystal Cave and Great Onyx Cave road) to Dennison Ferry Road. North of the river, **Green River Ferry Road** climbs from the ferry to the Maple Springs Loop and on to the park's north edge (Lincoln, the Great Onyx Job Corps Center), and **Houchin Ferry Road** reaches the western campground. The Mammoth Cave Railroad Bike & Hike Trail follows the old railroad bed 9 mi from Park City to the Visitor Center; the Big Hollow Trail's two loops are the mountain-bike trails. The map warns not to cross the fords when water covers them.

Underground, the brochure's 3D map (drawn looking north, west to the left) runs from the **Historic Entrance** at the Visitor Center south-eastward under Mammoth Cave Ridge to the **Frozen Niagara Entrance**, ~6 km away. From the Historic Entrance, Houchins Narrows opens into the **Rotunda** (140 ft / 43 m below the surface, under the Lodge) with the 1812 **saltpeter works**; from there **Broadway** and the **Main Cave** run south-east past Methodist Church, Booth's Amphitheatre, the **Giant's Coffin**, the Tuberculosis Hospital huts and Wright's Rotunda to Chief City and Kämper Hall near the Carmichael and Violet City entrances; side branches go north to Gothic Avenue and down through the Bottomless Pit, **Fat Man's Misery** and the Great Relief Hall (280 ft) to **River Hall**, the **River Styx**, Lake Lethe and **Echo River** (360 ft, the base level that drains to springs on the Green River), and back up through **Mammoth Dome** (a 192-ft shaft with the Tower and Ruins of Karnak). A lower tube, **Cleaveland Avenue**, runs east from the Carmichael Entrance to the **Snowball Room** (267 ft, under the elevator), then Boone Avenue (a slot canyon), Kentucky Avenue, **Mount McKinley** (195 ft), the Grand Canyon, Aero Bridge, Big Break and **Grand Central Station** to the **New Entrance** shafts, Moonlight Dome and the dripstone cascade of **Frozen Niagara** (130 ft) beside Crystal Lake, next to its own entrance.

### Water

- **Green River**: flows **west** across the park for 25 mi (40 km), averaging ~200 ft (61 m) wide and 10 ft (3 m) deep, green and slow but with strong currents after rain (it can rise 20–50 ft in hours); sandbars, islands (Cave, Floating Mill, Three Sisters, Stice, Sand Cave, Boardcut, Crump) and bluffs; one of the most biologically diverse rivers in the US (150+ fish species, 70+ mussel species). Lock and Dam No. 6 downstream failed in 2016 and was removed, lowering levels. It is the cave's base level: the deepest cave rivers drain to springs along its banks.
- **Nolin River**: 7.5 mi (12 km) from the Nolin River Lake dam tailwater (outside, north-west) to its confluence with the Green inside the park.
- **Cave rivers**: River Styx, Lake Lethe and Echo River at the bottom of the Historic section, 360 ft down, rising and falling with the Green; they resurge at River Styx and Echo River springs below the Visitor Center. **Turnhole Spring** at Turnhole Bend is the largest outlet of the whole system.
- **Karst**: no surface streams on the plateau — rain sinks through thousands of sinkholes (Cedar Sink, Mammoth Dome Sink, Doyle Valley) and sinking streams into the cave; ponds sit on clay plugs (Sloans Crossing Pond); dripwater builds the flowstones of Frozen Niagara and the gypsum flowers of Cleaveland Avenue.
- Small lakes: First Creek Lake (north-west); fords on backcountry roads flood in high water.

### Relief

On the surface, lowest 128 m (the Green River, 421 ft) and highest 282 m (Mammoth Cave Ridge near Park Ridge Road, 925 ft) — a gentle landscape where the drama is below. The park sits on the edge of the **Chester Upland**: flat-topped ridges (Mammoth Cave, Flint, Joppa, Collie ridges) capped by Big Clifty sandstone that protects the soluble Mississippian limestone (Girkin, Ste. Genevieve, St. Louis) in which the cave formed on five levels over ~10 million years; the river has cut a 100-m-deep valley with bluffs, and south of the park the caprock is gone, leaving the **Pennyroyal sinkhole plain**. Underground, passages range from ~40 m below the ridge tops (the Rotunda, Frozen Niagara) to ~110 m (Echo River at base level). In the game: ridges as 2–3-level HIGH plateaus with sandstone cliff faces over grey limestone, sinkholes as walkable bowls with a dark mouth at the bottom, the river valley with bluffs and a ferry crossing; the cave as instanced dungeon levels stacked by depth (Rotunda level, Mammoth Dome shaft, river level; Cleaveland/Kentucky avenues; Frozen Niagara), linked to the surface entrances.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: river bottoms (128–150 m) — sycamore, silver maple, river birch, cane breaks, spring wildflowers (Virginia bluebells); slopes and hollows — mixed mesophytic forest of tulip poplar, sugar maple, beech, buckeye, with hemlock in cool ravines; ridge tops (220–280 m) — oak–hickory, old fields turning to forest, eastern redcedar glades; the **Big Woods** (never logged, big white and chestnut oaks); cave mouths — the twilight zone of mosses, ferns and liverworts in the cool breath of the cave; deep cave — no plants at all.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#8C8A84` grey limestone · `#B69A6E` tan Big Clifty sandstone · `#5E4A3A` cave mud and saltpeter dirt · `#D8C9A8` flowstone and gypsum (Frozen Niagara) · `#1C1A1A` cave darkness · `#E9B96E` lantern light · `#4F7A5C` the green Green River · `#3E6A35` summer forest · `#D8A13A` / `#B8502E` October hickories and maples · `#9FB06B` river-bottom meadow.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): in the cave — Kentucky cave shrimp (endangered, found only here), northern and southern cavefish (eyeless, pinkish), cave crayfish, cave crickets (by the thousand near entrances), cave salamander, bats (Indiana, gray, little brown, big brown, tricolored — once millions, now thousands); on the surface — white-tailed deer (very common), wild turkey, coyote, bobcat, river otter and beaver (river), black bear (returning, rare), eastern box turtle, timber rattlesnake and copperhead, barred owl, pileated woodpecker, wood thrush, prothonotary warbler and belted kingfisher (river), great blue heron; in the river — 70+ freshwater mussels (14 federally listed), muskellunge, catfish, bass.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): bloodroot and Dutchman's breeches (March), trilliums and Virginia bluebells (April, river bottoms), flowering dogwood and redbud (April), fire pink (May), mountain laurel on sandstone bluffs (May), river cane (all year), tulip poplar blossoms (May), goldenrod and asters (September–October), white oak (the Big Woods giants).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — wettest, river floods, wildflowers everywhere; summer — hot and humid, thunderstorms, busy cave tours (the cave is always 54 °F); fall — oaks and hickories colour in October, clear days, low river; winter — grey and quiet, occasional snow and ice, bare trees reveal the sinkholes; the cave "breathes" out cold air in summer and in warm air in winter at the Historic Entrance.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park since 2021 — ranger stargazing with telescopes, the Milky Way over the river valley, fireflies in June, whip-poor-wills, owls, bats pouring from cave mouths at dusk in warm months.
- **Sounds**: dripping water and the hush of the cave (echoes of the ranger's voice in the Rotunda), footsteps on steel stairs, the ferry's cable clanking across the river, the Green River's ripples, spring peepers in Sloans Crossing Pond, cicadas, barred owls ("who cooks for you"), wind in oaks.

## Moments

- **Cave tours all year** (the cave is always 54 °F / 12 °C): the Historic Tour's Fat Man's Misery and Mammoth Dome, the Domes & Dripstones descent through the New Entrance shafts to Frozen Niagara, the 4-hour Grand Avenue Tour with lunch in the Snowball Room.
- **Lantern tours** (Violet City, Star Chamber, Great Onyx): the cave by paraffin light, as the early guides saw it.
- **The cave breathes**: cold air rushes out of the Historic Entrance on summer days and in on winter days.
- **Sunset at Sunset Point** over the Green River valley.
- **Spring wildflowers** in April along the river bottoms and the Echo River Spring trail; fall colour in mid–late October.
- **Bats at dusk** in summer; bat hibernation in winter (caves closed to protect them).
- **Stargazing programs** in the International Dark Sky Park; fireflies in June.
- **River days**: paddling from Dennison Ferry to the Green River Ferry (8 mi), camping on islands and sandbars; the ferry shutting down when the river floods.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a surface map **1024 × 768 tiles** (~1.5 × 1.15 km at 1.5 m/tile) compressed ~16×, north up, with the Green River crossing it west-east; plus **instanced cave levels** (the engine's dungeon instances at x ≥ 3000) for the four signature tours at near true route length but compressed rooms. Near true scale on the surface: the Visitor Center cluster (VC, Historic Entrance hollow, Lodge, campground, ferry, ~800 m), the ridge-top entrances as small bus stops, Cedar Sink, Sloans Crossing Pond. Compressed: the northern backcountry (a few hollows with horse trails and Maple Springs), Flint Ridge's caves, the Big Woods as a patch of giant trees. Cut: the far west and the Nolin River Lake.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the Visitor Center plaza and tour-ticket queue (main hub), the Historic Entrance (stone steps into a dark mouth with a cool breeze and a waterfall dripping over it), the Green River Ferry, Maple Springs. 3D landmarks: the cable ferry, the Lodge, tour buses, the Hercules locomotive, the New Entrance's steel stair shafts in a sinkhole, the Frozen Niagara flowstone, the saltpeter vats in the Rotunda, Mammoth Dome's tall shaft with the stair tower, the Giant's Coffin boulder. Interiors: the cave instances (Historic route: Rotunda → Broadway → Fat Man's Misery → River Hall → Mammoth Dome; Grand Avenue: Cleaveland Avenue → Snowball Room → Kentucky Avenue → Frozen Niagara), the Visitor Center, the Lodge dining room.
- **Activities**: ranger-led cave tours as scheduled group events (join a tour, follow the ranger's lantern, stay with the group), lantern tours (limited light radius), a wild-cave crawl for the adventurous; hiking the surface trails to springs and sinkholes; paddling the Green River and camping on islands; horseback and mountain biking in the north; the ferry crossing; photo spots — Frozen Niagara, the Rotunda with its vats, Mammoth Dome, Sunset Point, the Historic Entrance from below, Cedar Sink, the ferry at dusk; junior ranger (booklet; Night Explorer); birding and bat watching; stargazing.
- **Passport stamps**: the 2025 list gives "Mammoth Cave", "UNESCO World Heritage Site" and "International Biosphere Reserve", all kept at the Visitor Center desk. In the game: at the Visitor Center, with the UNESCO and Biosphere stamps as rewards for completing a cave tour and a surface nature walk.
- **With friends / with the world**: a party of 1–8 is exactly a small cave-tour group: follow one ranger NPC, pass the lantern, squeeze single-file through Fat Man's Misery, all turn lights off for a moment of total darkness in the Rotunda (a classic ranger moment), eat lunch together in the Snowball Room; share a canoe; open-world strangers meet in the ticket queue, on the ferry and at Sunset Point.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): never touch formations (skin oils stop their growth); stay with the tour and on the trail; no food, drink or flash in the cave; walk the white-nose-syndrome decontamination mats at the exits; don't enter closed caves (bat hibernation); don't take rocks, artifacts or saltpeter; respect the river (life jackets; no crossing flooded fords); stay out of sinkholes (they can collapse).
- **Implementation challenges**: caves — multi-level underground passages with stairs, shafts and domes, low ceilings and tight squeezes, darkness and lantern lighting, and a clean link between surface entrances and underground instances (bus rides between entrances); underground rivers; dripping formations; a river with a cable ferry and sudden floods; sinkholes as holes in the terrain; scheduled group tours for many players at once.
