# Black Canyon of the Gunnison National Park — `BLCA`

> Colorado · established October 21, 1999 · 124.6 km² (30,779.83 acres) · 250,086 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~1,650–2,757 m · NPS region IMR
>
> A gorge so deep, steep and narrow that parts of it barely see the sun: the Gunnison River has sawn 550–830 m down
> through some of the oldest, darkest rock in North America, with the Painted Wall, Colorado's tallest cliff, streaked
> with pink veins. In the game it should feel like walking the lip of an abyss — sagebrush and pinyon on a quiet rim,
> then one step to the railing and a black, roaring chasm opening straight down beneath you.

## Reading the map

North up, the **Black Canyon** is a dark, twisting gash that crosses the map from the lower right to the upper left. The **Gunnison River** enters at the south-east corner below Crystal Dam and **East Portal** (the end of Curecanti National Recreation Area's chain of reservoirs), runs north-west past the South Rim visitor centre, squeezes through **the Narrows**, passes under **Chasm View** and the **Painted Wall**, then bends west and north-west through a roadless wilderness stretch and leaves the park into the Gunnison Gorge National Conservation Area (where the Chukar Trail reaches the river for rafters). The park is a narrow band along the gorge, wider on its north side.

The **South Rim** lies south and west of the river and is the developed side. CO-347 comes up from US-50 and Montrose (south-west, off the map) to the entrance station; just inside, the paved **East Portal Road** plunges east in hairpin curves (16 % grades) to the river at East Portal, and the **South Rim Road** runs north past the campground to the **visitor centre** at Gunnison Point, then west along the rim for 7 miles past Pulpit Rock, Rock Point, Devils Lookout, **Chasm View**, **Painted Wall View**, Cedar Point, Dragon Point and Sunset View to its end at **High Point** (2,523 m), where a trail continues to **Warner Point**. Vernal Mesa rises south of the road.

The **North Rim** lies north of the river and is reached from the opposite direction: from Crawford the Black Canyon Road comes south across open sagebrush to the North Rim Ranger Station and campground, where a gravel road runs east along the rim past Chasm View, the Narrows View, Balanced Rock, Big Island and Island Peaks views to **Kneeling Camel View** and the Deadhorse Trail. The North Vista Trail climbs west from the ranger station to **Exclamation Point** and **Green Mountain** (2,610 m, the highest point on a trail; the park's true high ground, Poison Spring Hill at ~2,757 m, rises trail-less at the east end of the North Rim sector). Opposite viewpoints face each other only ~335 m apart across Chasm View, yet no road or bridge links the rims. Everything below the rims — the inner canyon — is designated wilderness, reached only by steep, unmarked routes with a permit.

### Water

The **Gunnison River** made the canyon. It flows north-west through the park from Crystal Dam and East Portal to the Gunnison Gorge, and on to the Colorado River at Grand Junction. Three dams just upstream in Curecanti National Recreation Area (Blue Mesa, Morrow Point and Crystal) now regulate its flow, but it still runs cold, clear and fast over boulders, dropping steeply through the park (on average ~95 ft per mile — far steeper than the Colorado in the Grand Canyon), so its roar rises all the way to the rims. At East Portal the **Gunnison Diversion Dam** sends part of the river into the **Gunnison Tunnel** (5.8 miles, dug 1905–1909) under Vernal Mesa to irrigate the Uncompahgre Valley around Montrose. The river is designated Gold Medal trout water (brown and rainbow trout); American dippers dive in its riffles; river otters and beavers live along it. Kayaking it is for experts only, and rafting in the park is strongly discouraged; there are no lakes, and the rims are dry — no drinking water in the park November–April. Side gulches (Deadhorse, Red Rock, Echo, Grizzly) carry water only after storms and snowmelt.

### Relief

The park runs from ~1,650 m where the river leaves it at the north-west to ~2,757 m on Poison Spring Hill, a trail-less rise at the east end of the North Rim sector (Green Mountain, 2,610 m, is the highest point on a trail; Signal Hill on Vernal Mesa, south of the South Rim entrance, reaches ~2,680 m). The rims are a rolling upland at ~2,300–2,550 m (the South Rim road climbs to 2,536 m at the campground and 2,523 m at High Point); the canyon drops **550–830 m** below them (Gunnison Point ~550 m / 1,800 ft; the deepest reach, below Warner Point, ~830 m / 2,722 ft) while staying astonishingly narrow: **335 m** rim to rim at Chasm View and only **12 m** wide at the river in the Narrows. The walls are Precambrian gneiss and schist about 1.7 billion years old, dark grey-black in shadow, cut by horizontal and diagonal bands of pink **pegmatite** (the "paint" of the Painted Wall, 686 m high). Spires and fins stand in the canyon (Big Island, Island Peaks, Kneeling Camel, Pulpit Rock). The canyon is so deep and narrow that parts of the bottom get only a short burst of direct sun each day.

In the game: the rims as two gently rolling plateaus at a high base level; the canyon as a **very deep, very narrow slot** — depth compressed to the engine's maximum cliff (dozens of levels) but width kept tiny (8–20 tiles at the Narrows, ~50 at Chasm View), with black painted walls veined in pink; overlooks as railed platforms jutting over the void; the inner-canyon routes as near-vertical scree gullies; the river as fast white water between boulders; 3D models for the Painted Wall's face, Big Island, Kneeling Camel and the dams.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: rims and hills (2,300–2,757 m) — pinyon pine and Utah juniper woodland (some trees many centuries old, twisted by wind), Gambel oak, Utah serviceberry, mountain mahogany, with silver-blue big sagebrush and grasses on the North Rim; aspen in cool pockets. Canyon walls — bare rock with pinyon clinging to ledges; Douglas-fir on the shaded, north-facing walls. River bottom (1,650–2,000 m) — box elder, willow, poison ivy, mosses and ferns in the spray. The 2025 South Rim Fire left a burn scar across part of the South Rim.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#2B2A2E` shadowed gneiss (the "black" walls) · `#5C5A60` sunlit schist grey · `#D99A9A` pink pegmatite veins · `#A77D63` weathered rim rock · `#5A6B45` pinyon–juniper green · `#8FA56B` Gambel oak (`#C8642E` in October) · `#AEB6A2` sagebrush silver-green · `#3E7F74` green-white Gunnison River (`#E8F1EE` white water) · `#1F2A44` night sky over the canyon.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): mule deer on the rims; elk in the oak brush; black bears (fall, in the oak and serviceberry); mountain lions and bobcats (rarely seen); bighorn sheep balancing on inner-canyon ledges; yellow-bellied marmots and rock squirrels at overlooks; porcupines; river otters and beavers on the river; American dippers diving in the riffles; peregrine falcons nesting on the walls (climbing closures in spring); white-throated swifts and violet-green swallows riding the updrafts; ravens and golden eagles; great blue herons; Gunnison sage-grouse on the North Rim sagebrush (threatened); ringtails at night.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): pinyon pine and Utah juniper (ancient trees); Gambel oak (acorns, red-orange in fall); Utah serviceberry (white blossoms in May, berries in August); mountain mahogany (feathery seeds); big sagebrush; Douglas-fir; box elder; wildflowers in late spring and summer — Indian paintbrush, penstemons, lupine, sego lily.
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — snow lingering on the rims into April, the river's spring release; summer — warm days, cool nights, almost daily afternoon thunderstorms (lightning on exposed overlooks); fall — oak and serviceberry turn red and gold, clear air; winter — snow on the rims, the South Rim Road beyond the visitor centre becomes a ski and snowshoe track, the North Rim is closed, ice in the canyon.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park (September 2015): summer telescope programmes on the South Rim; the Milky Way framed by the canyon walls; best spots Chasm View and Dragon Point (away from headlights); ringtails, owls, bats.
- **Sounds**: the Gunnison's roar echoing up 550 m of rock (louder at the Narrows); wind in pinyon needles; the white-throated swifts' whoosh past the rim; a peregrine's cry; rockfall clattering in the gorge; ravens; thunder booming between the walls; the quiet of the North Rim.

## Moments

- **Sunset on the Painted Wall** — from Painted Wall View, Cedar Point and Sunset View, the pink veins glow while the gorge fills with shadow.
- **Deepest shade** — parts of the inner canyon receive only a brief spell of direct sunlight each day; midday is the moment to look down.
- **Summer storms** — July–August afternoons: lightning over the rims, rain curtains in the canyon, then steaming rock.
- **Fall colours** — late September–early October: Gambel oak and serviceberry red and orange along both rims.
- **Winter ski on the South Rim Road** — mid-November to mid-April the closed road beyond the visitor centre becomes a cross-country ski and snowshoe route to the overlooks.
- **Stargazing** — summer telescope nights on the South Rim; the Milky Way over the chasm.
- **Peregrines and climbers** — spring: peregrine falcons nest on the walls (some climbing routes close); big-wall climbers inch up the Painted Wall and North Chasm View Wall in spring and fall.
- **Fire recovery** — from 2026 on, green shoots and gambel oak resprouting across the burn scar of the 2025 South Rim Fire (July 10–September 18, 2025, ~4,200 acres).

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a compact 512 × 384-tile vignette map, north up, framing the most dramatic ~10 km of the canyon from East Portal (bottom right) to Warner Point (left). The rims stay close to true width at the key overlooks (Chasm View's 335 m gap → ~220 tiles at 1.5 m/tile, compressed to ~60–80 tiles at ~1:3), the South Rim Road's 7 miles shrink to ~350 tiles of road with its 12 overlooks kept as small railed platforms; the North Rim is a strip along the top with its ranger station and short road; the western wilderness canyon is cut. The canyon depth must be compressed to the engine's deepest possible cliff while keeping the slot extremely narrow.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the South Rim Visitor Center at Gunnison Point (interior: exhibits, the 1901 Fellows–Torrence descent, a relief model); the South Rim Campground (rebuilt after the 2025 fire); the North Rim Ranger Station; East Portal at river level with the diversion dam and tunnel mouth. 3D set pieces: the **Painted Wall** face with its pink pegmatite, Chasm View's facing cliffs, the Narrows slot, Big Island, the Island Peaks, Kneeling Camel, Pulpit Rock, the Gunnison Diversion Dam and Crystal Dam; the West Elk and San Juan mountains on the horizon.
- **Activities**: overlook hopping along both rim drives (a "collect all 12 South Rim views" goal); short rim hikes (table); a **permit route to the river** as the park's big challenge — a steep gully scramble with a chain section, a night by the roaring river, the climb back out; fishing for trout at East Portal or in the inner canyon; watching (or joining, as a roped party) big-wall climbers on the Painted Wall; expert kayaking as a gated challenge; horseback rides on the North Rim's Deadhorse Trail (day use); ranger programmes and summer telescope nights; winter skiing and snowshoeing on the closed South Rim Road; Junior Ranger booklet. Photo spots: the Painted Wall from Painted Wall View and Cedar Point, Chasm View (both rims), the Narrows View, Pulpit Rock, Sunset View at dusk, Exclamation Point, Warner Point, East Portal's dam.
- **Passport stamps**: South Rim Visitor Center; North Rim Ranger Station (seasonal).
- **With friends / with the world**: players on opposite rims waving and calling across Chasm View (so close, yet 2–3 hours apart by road) — a perfect shared-world moment; a party descending the Gunnison Route together (one at a time on the chain), camping at the river; a climbing party on the Painted Wall; telescope night on the South Rim. Gathering spots: the visitor centre patio at Gunnison Point, Chasm View and Painted Wall View, High Point's picnic area, East Portal.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): never throw anything from the rim — even a pebble can kill someone below; stay behind railings, many overlooks have none; wilderness permits for any trip below the rim (routes are limited and some closed after the fire); pack out human waste (WAG bags) in the inner canyon; keep away from wildlife and store food from bears; no drones; stay out of closed burned areas; campfires only in campground grates; carry water (none in the park in winter).
- **Implementation challenges**: a slot canyon far deeper than it is wide, seen from a 45° camera looking north (the canyon runs NW–SE, so its walls face the camera obliquely — overlooks may need a turned camera); near-vertical dark walls with coloured veins; fast white water with boulders; steep scramble routes with a fixed chain; rope climbing on a big wall; a burned-forest state for part of the South Rim; winter road closure turning a road into a ski track.
