# Virgin Islands National Park — `VIIS`

> U.S. Virgin Islands (St. John & Hassel Island) · established August 2, 1956 · 60.9 km² (15,052.33 acres) · 471,074 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 0–389 m · NPS region SER (Southeast)
>
> About 60% of the island of St. John, "from the ridge tops to the reefs": white north-shore beaches, coral bays with sea turtles,
> steep forested mountains, Taíno rock carvings and the stone ruins of Danish sugar plantations built by enslaved Africans. In
> Hearthlight it should feel like a small, steep, sunlit island you cross in a day — winding left-hand roads over green ridges,
> a snorkel in every bay — where the beauty sits right beside a hard history that the park tells with care.

## Reading the map

St. John is drawn as a rough oval ~13 km long, lying east–west, with its eastern end split into long fingers around **Coral Bay** so that the whole island looks like a fat letter C opening south-east. North is up. The **west tip** holds **Cruz Bay**, the only town of any size and the gateway: ferry dock, visitor centre on the north side of the harbour, US Customs, and the car ferry at Enighed Pond (all in the Lind Point inset). **St. Thomas** fills the left edge across Pillsbury Sound; **Tortola** and the Thatch islands (British Virgin Islands) sit across the international border along the top.

**Two main roads** organise the island. **North Shore Road** (Route 20) leaves Cruz Bay north-eastward and follows the north coast in switchbacks past a string of bays, each with its own parking and facilities: Caneel Bay (the former resort), **Hawksnest Bay**, Peace Hill (windmill ruin), Jumbie Beach, **Trunk Bay** with its snorkel trail around Trunk Cay, **Cinnamon Bay** (campground, historic district), then **Maho Bay**; a short one-way section climbs from Maho back to the spine, and a branch continues to **Francis Bay** and the **Annaberg** ruins on Leinster Bay. **Centerline Road** (Route 10) runs along the island's spine from Cruz Bay to Coral Bay, passing the Catherineberg windmill, the top of the Cinnamon Bay Trail and the **Reef Bay trailhead** below Mamey Peak. From its east end, **Bordeaux Mountain Road** climbs over the island's highest point and drops (unpaved, 4WD) to **Lameshur**, while **Route 107** runs south along Coral Bay to **Salt Pond Bay** and on (unpaved) to the Lameshur bays and the VIERS field station. East of Coral Bay a road winds along **Hurricane Hole** to the private **East End**.

**Park land** covers most of the north shore, the central mountains and the south shore from Reef Bay to **Ram Head**, the island's southern tip; the paler private land fills the Cruz Bay–Chocolate Hole–Fish Bay area in the south-west, a block around Bordeaux Mountain and Coral Bay, and the East End. **Trails** (dashed) drop from the ridge to the sea: Lind Point and Caneel Hill (from Cruz Bay), Cinnamon Bay and Maria Hope (north), **Reef Bay** and L'Esperance (south, to the Reef Bay sugar mill and the petroglyphs), Bordeaux Mountain, Lameshur Bay, Yawzi Point and Tektite (south-east), Ram Head and Drunk Bay (from Salt Pond), Leinster Bay, Johnny Horn and Brown Bay (north-east). Pink reef edges wrap nearly every headland; the blue lines of the **Virgin Islands Coral Reef National Monument** enclose the waters off the north-east (Leinster–Haulover), Coral Bay/Hurricane Hole and the south coast.

**Relief** is the island's other organiser: spot heights mark **Bordeaux Mountain (1,277 ft / 389 m)**, Camelberg Peak (364 m), Mamey Peak (350 m), Minna Hill (301 m), Peter Peak (295 m), Margaret Hill (256 m), Gift Hill (252 m), Caneel Hill (219 m), Fortsberg (130 m) and Ram Head (61 m). Steep ravines ("guts") fall from the spine to the bays. The **Hassel Island** map is a separate sheet: a long, thin island (~1.5 km) in Charlotte Amalie's harbour, with trails linking the Creque Marine Railway (north tip), Shipley's Battery, Careening Cove and the Hamburg-America Line coaling station (east side), Cowell's Battery and Prince Frederik's Battery (south).

### Water

St. John has no rivers: dry ravines ("guts") carry water to the sea only after heavy rain, when a **seasonal waterfall** fills the pool at the Reef Bay petroglyphs. The coast alternates **white-sand bays** (Honeymoon, Hawksnest, Trunk, Cinnamon, Maho, Francis on the north; Salt Pond, Lameshur and Reef bays on the south), **rocky points** with fringing reefs (pink on the map), **seagrass meadows** in the calmer bays (Maho, Francis) where green turtles graze, **salt ponds** behind the beaches (Francis Bay, Salt Pond, Europa Bay) and **mangroves** in Mary Creek and Hurricane Hole. Tides are tiny (a few decimetres). The north shore faces the channels toward Tortola and gets winter swells from the Atlantic; the south shore opens to the Caribbean Sea; Coral Bay and Hurricane Hole are the island's sheltered harbours. Around 200 mooring buoys replace anchoring, which is allowed only in designated anchorages at Francis Bay, Lind Point and Cruz Bay. Water is 26–29 °C and clear enough to see turtles from the surface. No ice, no springs of note.

### Relief

The island rises steeply from sea level to **Bordeaux Mountain (389 m / 1,277 ft)** within about 2 km of the shore; a central ridge at 200–350 m (followed by Centerline Road) carries a line of peaks — Camelberg, Mamey, Peter Peak, Margaret Hill — and sends spurs and guts down to every bay. The rock is old volcanic stone (the island began forming ~100 million years ago), with steep coastal cliffs at **Ram Head**, the **White Cliffs** of the south coast and the capes of the East End; plantation-era **stone terrace walls** still climb the slopes. For the game: 12–18 height levels, roads as switchbacks cut into banks, trails as long stair-stepped descents (Reef Bay ~10 levels, Bordeaux Mountain ~14 in a short distance), cliffs on the south-east capes, and **3D peaks** for Bordeaux Mountain and Camelberg seen from the beaches; the ruins' windmill towers (Annaberg, Catherineberg, Peace Hill) are the human verticals.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation** (and by rain): coral reefs and turtle-grass meadows under water → beach strand of sea grape, coconut palms and poisonous manchineel → salt-pond edges with mangroves and glasswort → **dry forest and cactus scrub** on the low, sunny south-east (Ram Head, Salt Pond, Coral Bay slopes): Turk's cap and pipe-organ cacti, century plants, catch-and-keep acacia → **moist forest** in the north-facing valleys and high guts (Reef Bay, Bordeaux): kapok (silk-cotton) giants, bay rum trees, genip, mango, strangler figs, lianas → cloudier, shrubby forest on the summit ridge.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#F4EEDC` white coral sand · `#5FD3CF` turquoise shallows · `#1E6FA8` deep Caribbean blue · `#3F8F6E` seagrass meadow · `#2F6B3C` moist-forest green · `#7C8F4E` dry-scrub olive · `#8C7E6A` plantation stone rubble · `#D8B26A` yellow Danish brick trim · `#6F7F8C` Ram Head's blue cobbles · `#E3D2B0` dusty 4WD road · `#D9462F` flamboyant-tree red.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **green sea turtle** (Maho and Francis bays, early morning and late afternoon) · **hawksbill turtle** (reefs; nests on quiet beaches) · the rare **leatherback** — Trunk Bay is named for these "trunks" · **southern stingray** and **spotted eagle ray** · **nurse shark** · reef fish: blue tang, stoplight parrotfish, sergeant major, French angelfish, trunkfish, squirrelfish, barracuda · Caribbean reef **octopus** and squid · invasive **lionfish** · **brown pelican** (diving in Francis and Cruz bays) · **magnificent frigatebird** and brown booby · **bananaquit** (the territorial bird) and Antillean crested hummingbird · pearly-eyed thrasher, zenaida dove, bridled quail-dove (forest trails) · white-cheeked pintail (salt ponds) · **bats**, the island's only native mammals · feral **donkeys** and goats (Coral Bay, Ram Head) · green **iguana** · hermit crabs.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): **turpentine tree** ("tourist tree", peeling red bark) · **kapok / silk-cotton tree** (buttressed giants on the Reef Bay Trail) · **bay rum tree** (once distilled for bay rum) · **Turk's cap cactus** (red-capped, Ram Head) · **pipe-organ cactus** · **century plant** (its tall yellow flower stalk in spring) · **sea grape** · **manchineel** (toxic — the park's "do not touch" plant) · **genip** (fruit in summer) · **flamboyant** (scarlet blooms, early summer).
- **Weather & seasons**: *Winter–spring* (Dec–May): sunny and breezy, strong trade winds, occasional north swells pounding the north-shore beaches, a greener-to-browner hillside as the dry season deepens. *Summer–autumn* (Jun–Nov): hot and humid, calmer water, brief showers and squalls, the guts running after downpours, the hurricane season — Irma and Maria (Sept 2017) stripped the forest and wrecked the resort and campground; the park reopened within months. Saharan dust can haze summer skies.
- **Night**: dark skies with stars rarely seen further north — the **Southern Cross** low over the southern horizon in the busy season; ranger night-sky programmes on Cinnamon Bay beach; the lights of St. Thomas and Tortola across the water and of Cruz Bay's harbour; a loud chorus of tiny whistling frogs; bats over the salt ponds.
- **Sounds**: the whistling-frog chorus at dusk, trade wind in the palms, small surf on coral sand, pelicans crashing into the water, bananaquits chattering, donkeys braying near Coral Bay, roosters in Cruz Bay, ferry horns, open-air safari taxis grinding up the switchbacks, steel-pan music drifting from town.

## Moments

- **Turtles at Maho (every day, best early morning and late afternoon)** — green turtles graze the seagrass a few metres from the beach.
- **Snorkel trail at Trunk Bay (morning, before the day boats)** — read the underwater plaques in calm, clear water.
- **Reef Bay descent (ranger hike on scheduled days)** — down through the forest to the petroglyphs and the sugar mill, then a 40-minute boat ride back to Cruz Bay.
- **Ram Head at sunrise** — the sun comes up over Drunk Bay and the Atlantic from the island's southern tip.
- **Sunset from Caneel Hill or Lind Point** — the sun drops behind St. Thomas.
- **The Southern Cross (spring nights)** and the ranger **night-sky programme** at Cinnamon Bay (every other Wednesday evening, Nov–May).
- **After the rain (late summer–autumn)** — the guts run and the petroglyph pool fills below its little waterfall.
- **Annaberg living history (weekdays 10 am–2 pm, Nov–Apr)** — docents and cultural demonstrations at the ruins.
- **Emancipation Day (July 3) and the St. John Festival (late June–July 4)** — the island celebrates the 1848 end of slavery in the Danish West Indies and Carnival in Cruz Bay.
- **Winter north swells** — big surf rolls into the north-shore bays; the south shore (Salt Pond, Lameshur) becomes the calm side.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: **1280 × 896 tiles** for St. John (~2 m per tile at the hotspots, ~4.5× compression overall), plus **Hassel Island** as a separate small island (~120 × 240 tiles) off the west edge reached by boat, and the **Red Hook** ferry pier as the arrival point on a strip of St. Thomas. Near true scale: Cruz Bay's waterfront and visitor centre, Trunk Bay (beach ~500 m, cay 60 m off the sand), the Cinnamon Bay campground, Annaberg's ruins, the Reef Bay mill; compressed: the ridge road and the long south-east arm (Coral Bay, Hurricane Hole). The north shore keeps its sequence of bays exactly; the East End (private) is scenery. Tortola and the Thatch islands are a skyline across the channel (no landing — another country).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: **Cruz Bay** (ferry dock, barge, visitor centre with the raised-relief table map and dolphins on the ceiling, shops) is the arrival hub; **Trunk Bay** (lifeguard stand, snack bar, rental hut, the snorkel trail) and **Cinnamon Bay** (eco-tents and cottages, watersports hut, beach programmes) are the social beaches; **Annaberg** is the history hub; **Coral Bay** and **Salt Pond** anchor the east. 3D landmarks: Annaberg's stone windmill tower and animal mill, Catherineberg and Peace Hill windmills, the Reef Bay steam mill, Trunk Cay, the Caneel Hill platform, Ram Head's cliffs, Bordeaux Mountain, the Emmaus church, ferries and the car barge. Interiors: the visitor centre; Hassel Island's shipyard shed.
- **Activities**:
  - *Hiking*: the Reef Bay descent (party returns by boat), Ram Head, Lind Point to Honeymoon Beach, Leinster Bay to Waterlemon, the brutal Bordeaux Mountain climb, Hassel Island's forts.
  - *Photo spots*: the Trunk Bay overlook on North Shore Road (the classic postcard); Annaberg's windmill against Tortola; a turtle in Maho's seagrass; the petroglyphs reflected in their pool; Ram Head's cliff; the Southern Cross over Cinnamon Bay; the Caneel Hill panorama; the ferry entering Cruz Bay.
  - *Wildlife*: turtle watching from the surface, reef-fish checklists per bay, pelicans in Francis Bay, bats at dusk, donkeys on the roads (don't feed).
  - *Junior Ranger*: the park's workbook from the Cruz Bay VC. Game tasks: name the three peoples whose traces are in the park (Taíno, Danish colonists, enslaved Africans and their descendants); read five plaques of the underwater trail; find the manchineel and explain why not to touch it; learn why anchors are forbidden; learn to drive on the left.
  - *Snorkelling*: in nearly every bay — the **Trunk Bay Underwater Trail** (each sea-floor plaque unlocks a field-guide entry), **Maho** and **Francis** (turtles, beginners), **Hawksnest** (elkhorn fingers — swim around them, never over), **Waterlemon Cay** (a circuit with a current on the Tortola side), **Cinnamon Cay**, **Salt Pond** (south-shore reefs), Honeymoon/Salomon (brain and pillar corals). Snorkelling uses the same underwater layer as Biscayne and the Dry Tortugas, with winter swells closing north-shore spots and pushing players to the south shore.
  - *Boating*: arrive by ferry; take the car barge; a day sail around the island; tie up only to mooring buoys (anchoring only at Francis Bay, Lind Point and Cruz Bay); the Reef Bay boat pick-up.
  - *Paddling*: kayak or paddleboard from the Cinnamon Bay watersports hut to Cinnamon Cay or Whistling Cay; glide through the mangrove channels of **Hurricane Hole**; paddle across St. Thomas harbour to Hassel Island.
  - *Driving*: a rental jeep on the left-hand side of steep switchback roads, 4WD tracks to Lameshur; or ride an open-air **safari taxi** (the island's shuttle) from Cruz Bay to the beaches.
  - *Camping*: Cinnamon Bay's eco-tents, cottages and bare sites.
  - *History, told with care*: at Annaberg, Cinnamon Bay, Catherineberg and Reef Bay the ruins are presented as the places where enslaved Africans lived, laboured and resisted — through rangers, docents and quoted voices, not as a production game. Key stories: the **1733 Akwamu insurrection** that began at the Coral Bay fort and, by oral tradition, ended at Ram Head; escapes by boat from **Leinster Bay to Tortola** (a Network to Freedom site); **Emancipation on July 3, 1848**; the living culture of the island today. The **Taíno petroglyphs** are sacred: look, photograph, never touch. No sugar-making mini-game, no "plantation owner" roles.
- **Passport stamps**: St. John (Virgin Islands National Park, Cruz Bay Visitor Center) · St. John (Virgin Islands Coral Reef National Monument, same desk).
- **With friends / with the world**: a party snorkel along the underwater trail where each player reads a different plaque; the Reef Bay hike down together with the boat waiting at the bottom; a jeep convoy (who can drive on the left?) or one safari taxi for the whole group; a kayak crossing to Whistling Cay; a beach camp at Cinnamon Bay with the night-sky talk. In the shared world, strangers meet at the **Cruz Bay ferry dock**, on **Trunk Bay's beach**, at the **North Shore Road overlooks** and on the **Annaberg windmill terrace**.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace as gameplay): drive on the left and walk facing traffic; reef-safe sunscreen only; never stand on or touch coral — stand only on sand; swim around elkhorn, not over it; keep your distance from turtles and never chase or touch them; moor, don't anchor; take nothing — shells, coral, artifacts, rocks from ruins; don't climb on ruins or touch the petroglyphs; stay on trails and off private land; avoid manchineel, catch-and-keep and unknown fruits; don't feed donkeys, iguanas or fish; pay the Trunk Bay fee; pack out your trash.
- **Implementation challenges**: **steep relief** on a small island (many height levels, switchback roads, stair-stepped trails, cliffs); **left-hand traffic** for cars and AI; the shared **underwater layer** with reefs, turtles and plaques; **seasonal swell** that opens and closes snorkel spots; ferries and a car barge on timetables; windmill ruins and stone walls; a sensitive-history presentation system (voices, docents, quiet spaces); a detached second island (Hassel) and a foreign skyline (Tortola); hurricane-damaged forest as a possible state.
