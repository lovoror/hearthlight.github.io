> Virginia · established December 26, 1935 (authorized May 22, 1926; dedicated by F. D. Roosevelt July 3, 1936) · 811.2 km²
> (200,446 acres; the park's own count is 198,754) · 1,682,152 recreation visits (2025) · 163–1,235 m · NPS region NER (today DOI
> Region 1, North Atlantic–Appalachian)
>
> A long, narrow strip of the Blue Ridge, 105 miles from end to end, with Skyline Drive running along its crest from overlook to
> overlook above the Shenandoah Valley and the Piedmont. In Hearthlight it should feel like a slow road trip on top of the world:
> stone-walled pull-offs, deer in a mountain meadow at dusk, waterfalls hidden down every hollow and the Appalachian Trail always
> a few steps from the road.

## Reading the map

The park is a thin, winding ribbon laid diagonally from the north-east (Front Royal, at the top right of the map) to the south-west (Rockfish Gap near Waynesboro, bottom left), following the crest of the Blue Ridge. To its west, 600–900 m below, lies the flat, farmed **Shenandoah Valley** with the South Fork of the Shenandoah River looping through it (Front Royal, Luray, Stanley, Shenandoah, Elkton, Grottoes, Waynesboro) and the long wall of Massanutten Mountain beyond; to its east the rolling **Piedmont** (Sperryville, Washington, Syria, Madison, Stanardsville, Charlottesville). The ribbon is only a couple of kilometres wide in places, bulging to 15–20 km where long side ridges and hollows are protected: around Mathews Arm and Overall Run in the north, around Old Rag (an eastern lobe) and the Rapidan in the centre, and the Big Run wilderness in the south.

**Skyline Drive** runs the full length along the crest, 105 miles (169 km), 35 mph, and it is also the map's coordinate system: concrete **mileposts** start at 0 at the Front Royal entrance and end at 105 at Rockfish Gap, and every facility is known by its mile (Dickey Ridge Visitor Center 4.6, Mathews Arm 22.1, Elkwallow 24.1, Skyland 41.7–42.5, Big Meadows 51, Lewis Mountain 57.5, Loft Mountain 79.5). Three US highways cross the ridge at gaps and divide the park into **North** (MP 0–31.5), **Central** (31.5–65.5) and **South** (65.5–105) districts: US 211 at Thornton Gap (with the Marys Rock Tunnel just south), US 33 at Swift Run Gap, and I-64 at Rockfish Gap, where the Drive hands over to the Blue Ridge Parkway heading 469 miles south to the Great Smokies. Overlooks — stone-walled pull-offs, about 70 of them — alternate sides, so the view swings between the valley (west) and the Piedmont (east).

The **Appalachian Trail** (green dashes, 101 miles in the park) shadows the Drive the whole way, crossing it again and again, with its three-sided huts and the PATC's rentable cabins. The busiest and highest part is the **Central District**: Marys Rock and the Pinnacles, then **Skyland** (the Drive's high point, 3,680 ft at MP 41.7) under Stony Man, **Whiteoak Canyon** falling east, **Hawksbill** (the highest peak), and **Big Meadows** with the Byrd Visitor Center, lodge, campground, Dark Hollow Falls and Hoover's **Rapidan Camp** down a hollow to the east. **Old Rag** stands apart, 6 km east of the Drive, a bare granite summit reached only from its own trailhead on VA 600. The **North District** has Dickey Ridge's visitor centre, Mathews Arm and the tallest falls (Overall Run); the **South District** is lower and wilder, with Loft Mountain's big campground on Big Flat Mountain, the Big Run wilderness, Blackrock's talus summit and the Riprap canyon. Fire roads (gated, for walkers and horses) drop from the Drive into the hollows; everything else is reached on foot.

### Water

The park sits astride the Blue Ridge divide, so its **90+ streams** are short, steep and cold, starting as springs just below the Drive (Lewis Spring, Hawksbill Spring, Rock Spring) and tumbling down "hollows" on both sides. West-side runs (Overall Run, Jeremys Run, Big Run, Madison Run, Naked Creek) drain to the **South Fork of the Shenandoah River**, which meanders north through the valley below and on to the Potomac. East-side rivers drain to the Rappahannock (the Thornton, Hughes, Rose, Rapidan and Conway rivers) or the James (the Moormans, Doyles and South rivers). Waterfalls mark where the streams leave the hard greenstone and granite of the crest: **Overall Run (93 ft, tallest)**, **Whiteoak Canyon** (six falls, the upper one 86 ft), **Dark Hollow (70 ft, most visited)**, Lewis Falls (81 ft), South River Falls (83 ft), Rose River Falls (67 ft), Doyles River, Jones Run and Cedar Run. There are no natural lakes; the Big Meadows swamp is the one real wetland on the ridge. Pools below falls and slides (Cedar Run, Riprap Hollow) are the summer swimming spots; wild brook trout live in the upper streams.

### Relief

From 163 m (535 ft) at the north end near Front Royal to **1,235 m (4,051 ft) on Hawksbill**; 69 peaks top 3,000 ft (914 m). The Blue Ridge here is a single narrow crest 600–1,200 m high, standing 600–900 m above the valley on the west and the Piedmont on the east, notched by **gaps** where roads cross (Thornton 702 m, Swift Run 721 m, Rockfish 579 m). It is highest and widest in the Central District (Hawksbill, Stony Man 1,220 m, Hazeltop 1,149 m, Bushytop, Nakedtop), lower in the south (Hightop 1,091 m, Loft Mountain, Blackrock 944 m). Three rocks shape the look: **greenstone** (ancient lava flows, grey-green, forming the western cliffs of Stony Man, Hawksbill, Franklin Cliffs and Compton Peak's columns), **granite** (pale, over a billion years old — Old Rag's tors and Marys Rock), and **quartzite and sandstone** (the South District's talus slopes at Blackrock and the Riprap canyon). Long side ridges and deep hollows run down both flanks, steepest to the west. In the game: the crest is a plateau band 1–4 tiles above everything else, with the Drive on top and the overlooks as stone-walled terraces at the edge; hollows are stepped valleys of one-level banks with stairs where trails descend and a waterfall at each step; cliffs only at Stony Man, Hawksbill, Franklin Cliffs, Marys Rock and Old Rag; Old Rag's summit is a heap of 3D boulders with gaps to squeeze through; talus fields are grey boulder slopes; the valleys outside the park are a painted backdrop far below.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: valley edges and lower hollows (160–600 m): tulip poplar, sycamore, black walnut and old apple trees, stone walls and chimneys of vanished farms; the bulk of the park (600–1,000 m): **oak-hickory forest** (chestnut oak, northern red oak, hickories, with American chestnut root sprouts that never grow up), mountain laurel and pink azalea understory, ferns in the coves, hemlock and yellow birch along the falls (many hemlocks lost to the woolly adelgid); the crest and summits (1,000–1,235 m): wind-stunted oaks, **red spruce and balsam fir** only on Hawksbill's top, grassy **Big Meadows** kept open by fire and mowing, lichen-covered greenstone cliffs and talus.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#5E7F4C` summer oak canopy · `#8FA0B8` far blue ridges · `#B7C3CF` hazy valley and Piedmont · `#6D7A62` grey-green greenstone cliff · `#C4BCAE` pale Old Rag granite · `#8C8579` quartzite talus · `#A9B96A` Big Meadows grass in June (`#C8A85A` in August) · `#C8532E` October maple red · `#E0A93A` hickory gold · `#3D6B6E` pool below a falls · `#8A8176` the stone walls of the overlooks.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **black bear** (one of the densest populations in the East; Big Meadows, roadsides, oaks in fall) · **white-tailed deer** (Big Meadows at dusk, spotted fawns in June) · **wild turkey** (the largest bird; meadows) · **bobcat** · **red fox** and **gray fox** · **eastern chipmunk** and **woodchuck** (Drive edges) · **striped skunk** · **Shenandoah salamander** (federally endangered, lives only on the talus of Hawksbill, Stony Man and The Pinnacle) · **timber rattlesnake** and **copperhead** (sunny rocks) · **eastern box turtle** · **brook trout** (cold upper streams) · **peregrine falcon** (restored to the cliffs; Hawksbill) · **common raven** (Stony Man cliffs) · **broad-winged hawk** (September migration) · **barred owl** · **wood thrush** (dusk song in the hollows).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): hepatica and bloodroot (late March, first flowers) · large-flowered trillium (April–May) · pink lady's slipper (May) · pink azalea (May, along the Drive) · mountain laurel (June) · Turk's cap lily (summer) · black cohosh (summer spikes) · lowbush blueberry (Big Meadows, ripe in August) · goldenrods and asters (September–October) · chestnut oak (the ridge tree) and the ghost of the American chestnut. 862 wildflower species in all.
- **Weather & seasons**: spring climbs the ridge from the hollows (late March) to the crest (May); summer is green, humid and hazy with afternoon thunderstorms; autumn brings the park's great colour show in mid-to-late October and the heaviest traffic; winter is cold and quiet, with rime ice on the summits, frozen falls and sections of the Drive closed after snow. Fog often lies on the crest while the valleys are clear, or fills the valley below the overlooks on cool mornings (a sea of clouds).
- **Night**: Big Meadows is the park's classic stargazing field (open sky, no trees, ranger night programmes); the Milky Way over the dark Piedmont; the valley towns glitter far below from the western overlooks; fireflies over the meadow in June; bats, whip-poor-wills, barred owls; bears on the move at dusk.
- **Sounds**: wind through oaks and across the overlook walls, the low hum of cars at 35 mph, woodpeckers drumming, ovenbirds and wood thrushes, a waterfall's roar growing as you descend a hollow, acorns pattering down in October, horses clopping at Skyland, deer snorting in the meadow, AT hikers' trekking poles, rain on the lodge roof.

## Moments

- **Autumn colour on Skyline Drive**: typically peaks mid-to-late October, moving from the high central peaks down to the hollows; the park's busiest weekends.
- **The spring wildflower climb**: hepatica and bloodroot in the low hollows from late March, trillium and lady's slippers in April–May, pink azaleas along the Drive in May, mountain laurel in June; the park holds a Wildflower Weekend of walks each May.
- **Dusk in Big Meadows**: deer come out to graze in the meadow every evening; fawns appear in June; in August bears browse the blueberries.
- **Sunset from the western cliffs**: Stony Man, Crescent Rock, Hawksbill, Franklin Cliffs over the Shenandoah Valley; **sunrise** from east-facing overlooks and Old Rag's summit.
- **Valley fog**: on cool mornings the valley fills with cloud and the ridge floats above it.
- **Peregrine season** (spring–summer) on the Hawksbill and Stony Man cliffs; **hawk migration** along the ridge in mid-September.
- **Appalachian Trail thru-hikers** pass north through the park in late spring (May–June).
- **Winter rime and ice**: frozen waterfalls (Dark Hollow, Whiteoak), rime-coated summits, the Drive closed in sections — the park at its emptiest.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a tall **1024 × 2048-tile** map, the Drive as a spine from the top-right corner (MP 0) to the bottom-left (MP 105) — and, as on the real map, **mileposts are the map's coordinates**: a concrete post prop every mile, ~17 tiles apart on average (the 169 km road squeezed ~60×), stretched to 25–40 tiles per mile through Skyland and Big Meadows and shrunk to ~10 per mile in the quiet South District. Near true scale (1 tile ≈ 1.5 m): each overlook (a 40–80-tile stone-walled pull-off), the Skyland and Big Meadows lodges, the Byrd VC and the meadow's edge, the Marys Rock Tunnel, the Dark Hollow Falls trail (~1.1 km → ~500 tiles), the Old Rag scramble (~1.5 km of boulders, kept almost whole), Stony Man's cliff. Compressed ×5–10: the hollows (each a short, steep side valley with its falls), the meadow itself, Rapidan Camp's hollow. Cut or reduced: the long wilderness side ridges, the lowest farm hollows, most fire roads; the valley and Piedmont outside the park as a painted panorama far below the overlooks.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: hubs at **Big Meadows** (Byrd VC, lodge terrace, wayside with the only gas pump, campground, amphitheatre), **Skyland** (lodge, dining room, stables), **Dickey Ridge**, **Loft Mountain**, the **Old Rag trailhead**, **Thornton Gap**. 3D landmarks: Marys Rock Tunnel's stone portals, Stony Man's cliff face (with its "face"), Hawksbill's summit platform, Old Rag's boulder crest, Compton Peak's greenstone columns, Blackrock's talus heap, the CCC stone walls and chestnut-log shelters, AT huts, the concrete milepost markers, the overlook signs. Interiors: Byrd Visitor Center (exhibits on the families displaced to make the park), Big Meadows Lodge's great room, Skyland's dining room, Hoover's Brown House at Rapidan Camp, Corbin Cabin (a mountain family's home), a PATC trail cabin.
- **Activities**: the **road trip** — driving or biking the Drive overlook by overlook, collecting all ~70 overlook views (each with its elevation sign) and the 105 mileposts; hiking to waterfalls down the hollows (Dark Hollow, Whiteoak, Overall Run, Rose River, Lewis, Doyles River, South River); the Old Rag scramble as a puzzle-climb (squeeze, boost a friend, find the route), with a day ticket; summit sunsets (Stony Man, Hawksbill); horseback rides from Skyland; section-hiking the AT hut to hut (with thru-hiker NPCs heading to Maine); deer and bear watching in Big Meadows; stargazing in the meadow; fly-fishing for brook trout; ranger van to Rapidan Camp; fall-colour photo hunt; winter walks on the closed Drive. Photo spots: Stony Man cliff, Hawksbill platform, Old Rag summit, Marys Rock, Dark Hollow Falls, Whiteoak upper falls, Big Meadows at dusk, Hogback Overlook's river bends, Crescent Rock at sunset, Blackrock summit, the tunnel portal. Junior ranger ideas (the real booklet is for ages 7–12): find a milepost ending in every digit, identify greenstone vs granite vs quartzite, count deer in the meadow at dusk, learn a displaced family's story at Corbin Cabin, follow blue (hiking), white (AT) and yellow (horse) blazes.
- **Passport stamps**: the park's cancellation stamps are kept at its two visitor centres — **Dickey Ridge Visitor Center** (MP 4.6) and **Harry F. Byrd Sr. Visitor Center** at Big Meadows (MP 51) — and the rangers' Mobile Visitor Center van; the park publishes no list of stamp variants (collectors report extra designs such as Skyline Drive and the Appalachian Trail).
- **With friends / with the world**: the overlooks are natural meeting spots (a stone wall to sit on, a view, cars pulling in and out); Big Meadows at dusk is the world's gathering place (deer, sunset, then stars); Old Rag's summit and the Stony Man cliff are group-photo peaks. Co-op: an Old Rag scramble where players boost and pull each other through the chutes; a convoy drive down the whole Drive with stops called by milepost; a relay AT hike where each player carries the "thru-hiker's pack" between huts; a group waterfall hunt; a ranger campfire talk at the Big Meadows amphitheatre.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): 35 mph and brake for deer and bears on the Drive; stay 50 yards from bears and never feed any animal (feeding deer at Big Meadows is a classic temptation); stay on the trail — especially on Old Rag's summit and Big Meadows' fragile plants; don't climb on the rocks at waterfalls (they are slick and deadly); leave the stone walls and chimneys of old homesites untouched; pets banned on Old Rag, Dark Hollow and other listed trails; check for ticks; Old Rag needs a day-use ticket; pack out everything from the huts.
- **Implementation challenges**: a 105-mile **linear map** (streaming along a diagonal spine, fast travel by milepost); **overlooks onto vast views outside the map** (the Shenandoah Valley, Massanutten, the Piedmont — need a far backdrop or impostor terrain far below the ridge); haze and valley fog below the player; waterfalls on steep one-tile steps down narrow hollows; a real **boulder scramble** (squeezes between rocks, a crawl under a chockstone); the Marys Rock tunnel; talus fields; the AT crossing the Drive over and over; the Drive closing in snow; October colour change across the whole map.
