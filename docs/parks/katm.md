# Katmai National Park & Preserve — `KATM`

> Alaska · established December 2, 1980 (Katmai National Monument since September 24, 1918) · 14,870.3 km² (3,674,529 acres) of park, 16,564 km² (4,093,077 acres) with the preserve · 34,479 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 0–2,318 m · NPS region AKR (Alaska)
>
> A roadless volcanic wilderness on the Alaska Peninsula where about 2,200 brown bears fish for sockeye salmon, and where the largest eruption of the 20th century (Novarupta, 1912) buried a valley under ash. In Hearthlight it should feel like stepping off a floatplane onto a beach full of bear tracks: bear school first, then a boardwalk over a river where bears walk beneath you, the roar of Brooks Falls, and a bus ride to a silent, pumice-grey valley.

## Reading the map

The brochure map is a rough square, north up, cut out of the base of the Alaska Peninsula. The park is a big green block tilted from south-west to north-east: **Bristol Bay's lake-dotted lowlands** fill the left third (the Naknek River runs west to Naknek and King Salmon on the far left edge), the **Aleutian Range** of glaciated volcanoes forms a white spine near the right edge, and the **Shelikof Strait** coast (blue, with Kodiak and Afognak islands at the bottom right) wraps the south-east side. A pale-green lobe at the top is the **preserve** around Kukaklek and Nonvianuk lakes, drained west by the Alagnak Wild River; McNeil River State Game Sanctuary and the Kamishak Special Use Area (state land) sit at the top right; Becharof National Wildlife Refuge and Becharof Lake fill the bottom left.

The only road on the map outside the park is the paved **Alaska Peninsula Highway** from Naknek to **King Salmon** (visitor center, park headquarters, airport), then a gravel road east to **Lake Camp** at the western tip of Naknek Lake (boat launch, picnic area) where the park begins. **Naknek Lake** is the map's centrepiece: ~60 km long, with the **North Arm** reaching north-east to the Bay of Islands (Fure's public-use cabin and a portage trail to Lake Grosvenor, with Grosvenor Lodge and Lake Coville beyond), and the narrow, glacier-milky **Iliuk Arm** reaching east-south-east to the mouth of the Savonoski River and the abandoned village of Savonoski. **Brooks Camp** is marked on the main lake's south shore, where the short Brooks River pours in from **Lake Brooks** (a separate lake to the south-west); Dumpling Mountain (2,440 ft / 744 m) rises just north-west of it, Mount La Gorce (3,183 ft) across the arm.

From Brooks Camp the park's **only road** — a thin grey line, unpaved — runs 23 mi (37 km) south-east past Margot Falls to the **Three Forks Overlook** (a shelter symbol; the Robert F. Griggs Visitor Center). Beyond it a pink-shaded patch labelled « Valley area of ash flow from 1912 eruption » fills the **Valley of Ten Thousand Smokes**, ~20 km long, between the Buttress Range (south-west) and Mount Griggs (7,602 ft, north-east). Knife Creek and the River Lethe cut down the valley to join the Ukak River at Three Forks. At the valley head sit Baked and Broken mountains, Falling Mountain, Mount Cerberus, the little **Novarupta** dome (2,759 ft / 841 m), Trident Volcano (6,115 ft), Mount Mageik (7,103 ft), **Katmai Pass** to the Pacific side, and the Knife Creek Glaciers climbing to **Mount Katmai** (6,716 ft) whose collapsed summit holds « Crater Lake ». Mount Martin (6,102 ft) and the Kejulik Mountains continue south-west; Yori Pass (west of the valley) leads towards the Contact and Angle creeks.

The **coast** (right side) is a chain of fjords and bays from Cape Douglas in the north-east to Cape Kubugakli in the south-west: Swikshak Bay (patrol cabin), **Hallo Bay** (Hallo Bay Wilderness Camps; Ninagiak Island offshore), Kukak Bay (Katmai Wilderness Lodge), Kaflia Bay, Hidden Harbor, **Geographic Harbor**, Amalik Bay (patrol cabin), Katmai Bay with the abandoned Katmai Village, Dakavak and Alinchak bays. Behind the coast rise the volcanoes with their glaciers: Mount Douglas (7,021 ft), Fourpeaked Mountain (6,903 ft), Devils Desk, Kukak Volcano, Mount Steller, **Mount Denison** (7,605 ft / 2,318 m, the park's high point), Snowy Mountain, the Hallo and Serpent Tongue glaciers. A note says that all islands within 5 miles of the mainland are in the park, but not the sea. Lodges in the preserve and lake district: Royal Wolf, Enchanted Lake, Kulik, Battle River, Grosvenor; patrol cabins at Nonvianuk, Swikshak and Amalik Bay.

The **Brooks Camp vicinity inset** (bottom right, north up, 400 m bar) is the most important drawing for the game: the Brooks River enters from Lake Brooks at the bottom left (float-plane access and a picnic shelter there), loops north then east in big S-bends, drops over **Brooks Falls** (Falls Platform and Riffles Platform on its south bank, reached by the dashed Brooks Falls Trail), and meanders ~1 km more to Naknek Lake (right edge). At the river mouth the **Boardwalk Trail** crosses on an elevated bridge between the North and South platforms. North of the mouth: the **Visitor Center** by the float-plane beach, Brooks Lodge (bed, food, store), the auditorium, a picnic area, the Cultural Site and, farther north, the campground and the way « To Dumpling Mountain ». South of the river: the gravel road with toilets, the stop where the « Bus to Valley of Ten Thousand Smokes meets here », Beaver Pond, and the road south to Lake Brooks and the Valley.

### Water

Everything drains **west to Bristol Bay** through a chain of glacial lakes. **Naknek Lake** (~600 km², clear and deep blue in the main basin) empties by the Naknek River past King Salmon; its **Iliuk Arm** is turquoise-milky with silt from the Savonoski and Ukak rivers. The **Brooks River** is a short clear river, ~1.5 mi (2.4 km) from Lake Brooks to Naknek Lake, broken by **Brooks Falls**, a ~2 m ledge that stalls the migrating **sockeye salmon**: they enter the Naknek drainage from Bristol Bay in late June and July, jump the falls through July, spawn from August to October, then die and drift into the lower river's eddies. In the valley, **Knife Creek** and the **River Lethe** (named for the river of forgetfulness) have cut slot canyons into the soft ash flow, joining at Three Forks to form the **Ukak River**, which drops over Ukak Falls and runs north to Iliuk Arm. The **Pacific coast** has big tides (several metres) that uncover clam flats and sedge meadows at Hallo Bay and Swikshak Lagoon, and fjords under hanging glaciers. The preserve's Kukaklek and Nonvianuk lakes feed the Alagnak Wild River. Lakes freeze in winter; williwaws can raise dangerous waves on Naknek Lake in minutes.

### Relief

From sea level (the coast; Naknek Lake and Brooks Camp sit at ~15 m) to **Mount Denison, 2,318 m**. Brooks Camp is a low, forested river terrace; Dumpling Mountain rises 730 m straight behind it. The Valley road climbs gently through forest to the Griggs overlook knoll (~390 m); below it the **Valley of Ten Thousand Smokes** is a near-flat plain of pumice and welded tuff (the ash flow is up to ~200 m thick, 100 km²) that rises from ~150–250 m at Three Forks to ~800 m at Katmai Pass, **cut by vertical-walled gorges** 10–30 m deep. Around it stand eighteen or so volcanoes, several still steaming: domes, stratocones with ice caps, and Mount Katmai's broken-off summit (a caldera ~600 m deep holding a lake). The coast is steep: fjord walls, glaciers reaching the sea, gravel beaches and flat sedge meadows. In the game: flat ground with fresh-snow-like pumice texture and sharp canyon cuts (1–2-level vertical drops with painted layered faces) for the valley; one-level banks for river terraces; stairs on the Dumpling Mountain trail; 3D volcano landmarks (Katmai with its crater lake, Mageik and Trident with steam plumes, Griggs, Denison) on the skyline.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: 0–300 m white spruce and paper birch forest with alder, willow and tall grass (cow parsnip, fireweed) along the river and lakes; 300–600 m alder thickets and subalpine meadows; above ~600 m crowberry–lichen tundra, then snow and ice; the ash valley is almost bare — pumice, lichens and a few mosses a century on; the coast is treeless: sedge meadows, beach rye, alder slopes.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#2E4F3B` spruce forest · `#5E7F45` birch–grass understory · `#6FA3B8` Naknek Lake blue · `#8CCBC6` Iliuk Arm glacial turquoise · `#B04A3A` spawning sockeye red · `#D6C7A8` pumice plain · `#9C8468` layered tuff canyon walls · `#9DB36B` coastal sedge meadow · `#4E4A48` dark volcanic rock · `#F2F4F6` glacier ice.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **brown bear** (~2,200 in the park; at Brooks in July and September, on the coast June–August; sows with cubs at the river mouth), **sockeye salmon** (July run, red spawners August–October), rainbow trout and Arctic char (Brooks River anglers), bald eagle, glaucous-winged gull (following the bears), common merganser, river otter, beaver (Beaver Pond), red fox, gray wolf (rarely, along the river), moose (willow flats), wolverine; on the coast harbor seals, Steller sea lions, sea otters, tufted puffins on the islands; Arctic ground squirrels at the valley's edges.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): fireweed (July–August), cow parsnip (June–July), Nootka lupine (June–July), chocolate lily (June), dwarf dogwood / bunchberry, crowberry and blueberry (August), Lyngbye's sedge (the coast bears' salad, June), paper birch (yellow in September), the pioneer mosses and lichens on the pumice.
- **Weather & seasons**: June — green-up, few bears at Brooks, coastal bears on the sedges; July — rain, wind and crowds, bears packed at the falls; August — quieter river, salmon spawning red, blueberries; September — birches turn gold, the first snow on the volcanoes, fat bears, fewer people; October–May — camp closed, lakes freeze, bears den (November–April). Fog and low clouds ground floatplanes any day.
- **Night**: long summer twilight (at the June solstice ~18.4 h of sun at Brooks: sunrise ~5:13, sunset ~23:35 AKDT); dark skies and aurora from late August; the platforms close at night (10 pm–7 am, June 15–August 15).
- **Sounds**: the steady roar of Brooks Falls and splashing salmon, bears huffing and jaws snapping, gull squabbles, a ranger calling « bear on the trail! », the rumble of a de Havilland Beaver floatplane, rain on the boardwalk, wind hissing over the pumice, the crunch of ash underfoot, silence in the valley.

## Moments

- **Bears at Brooks Falls** (late June–July, peak mid-July): the biggest bears hold the lip and the « jacuzzi » below, catching leaping sockeye; the Falls Platform holds 40 people, with waits of 1.5 h or more at midday.
- **September's fat bears**: after spawning, dying salmon drift into the lower river; the bears, now heavy and in new fur, crowd the river mouth under the bridge.
- **Fat Bear Week** (late September–early October, online since 2014): a bracket of the Brooks River bears voted by the public — 480 Otis won four times, 747 twice, 128 Grazer in 2023 and 2024, 32 Chunk in 2025.
- **Bear school**: every arrival starts with a ranger's safety talk at the visitor center; nobody walks into camp before it.
- **The bear jam on the bridge**: when a bear lies down near a bridge end, the crossing closes and everyone waits.
- **The Valley bus** (daily 9 am, June 7–September 17): 7–8 h, the Robert F. Griggs Visitor Center, then the ranger hike down 1,000 ft to Ukak Falls and Three Forks.
- **June 6–8, 1912**: a 60-hour eruption, 13–15 km³ of magma, ash as far as Juneau; Mount Katmai collapsed; Robert Griggs found the valley still full of steaming fumaroles in 1916 (they died out within ~15 years). Apollo astronauts trained here in 1965–66.
- **Ranger programs**: Cultural Walk at 2 pm to the barabara, evening program at 8 pm in the auditorium (June 7–September 17); live chats on the bearcams.
- **Coastal clam-digging bears** (Hallo Bay, Swikshak, June–July): at low tide, bears dig razor clams on the flats between sedge grazing.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a curated map of **~1,024 × 768 tiles** holding four linked pieces. (1) **Brooks Camp** ~512 × 384 tiles: true scale for the camp (lodge, visitor center, campground ~400 × 300 m), the bridge and the falls; the 2.4 km river compressed ~2× so that Lake Brooks, the falls and the mouth fit. (2) **The Valley of Ten Thousand Smokes** ~384 × 384 tiles: the Griggs overlook knoll and the Ukak Falls hike at true scale, the ash plain up to Novarupta and Katmai Pass compressed ~1:8. (3) **Hallo Bay** ~256 × 256 tiles: sedge meadow, tidal clam flats, a wilderness camp. (4) a tiny **King Salmon** strip (~128 × 96: visitor center, airport, floatplane dock). Links: **floatplane hop** King Salmon → Brooks (33 mi), **bus** Brooks → Griggs Visitor Center (the 23 mi road compressed into a short drive with the three fords), **floatplane hop** Brooks → Hallo Bay, and the lakes as a canoe route (a heavily compressed Savonoski Loop could come later). Cut: the preserve's lodges, the southern coast, the volcano summits (backdrop only).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the float-plane beach with parked Beavers; Brooks Camp Visitor Center (small wooden building, bear-school benches); Brooks Lodge (red-cedar log lodge with a fireplace lounge and dining room) and cabins; the auditorium; the **elevated bridge and boardwalk** (bears pass underneath); **Falls Platform** and Riffles Platform on stilts; the **barabara** interior (a sod-roofed semi-subterranean house); the fenced campground with cooking shelters and food caches; Robert F. Griggs Visitor Center (stone-and-glass overlook); Baked Mountain huts; Fure's Cabin. 3D landmarks: Dumpling Mountain, Mount Katmai (caldera and crater lake), Mount Griggs, Mageik and Trident with steam, the Novarupta dome, Mount Denison on the horizon. Vehicles: floatplane, Valley bus (4×4 school bus), canoe/kayak, skiff.
- **Activities**: bear watching from the platforms (a « bear book » field guide where each bear has a number, a look and a personality — the bearcam culture); photo spots — Falls Platform salmon-leap shot, Riffles, the bridge, Dumpling overlook, Griggs overlook, Ukak Falls, River Lethe gorge, Novarupta, Hallo Bay clam digging; fly fishing catch-and-release for rainbow trout (with the « cut your line » rule); canoe or kayak on Naknek Lake; the Valley tour and hike; ranger programs (bear school, Cultural Walk, evening talk); Junior Ranger (ages 5–13) and the adults' « Not-So-Junior Ranger » book; a **Fat Bear Week** vote on the phones at the end of a season; backcountry camp at Baked Mountain.
- **Passport stamps**: « Brooks Camp » (Brooks Camp Visitor Center), « King Salmon » (King Salmon Visitor Center), « Valley of Ten Thousand Smokes » (Robert F. Griggs Visitor Center) and « Wilderness ».
- **With friends / with the world**: the party flies in together in one floatplane and sits through bear school; the Falls Platform is a natural MMO gathering spot with a real **40-person cap and a queue** (one-hour turns when full); the bridge bear jam makes strangers wait together; co-op canoe crossings of a windy lake; the Valley hike as a group; group photos on the float-plane beach or at the Griggs overlook; a season-end Fat Bear Week vote shared by everyone online.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay at least 50 yards (46 m) from any bear, and never approach one using a food source; bears have the right of way (step off the trail, back away, never run); no food or drink except water outside buildings and fenced picnic areas; never leave gear unattended; stop fishing and break the line when a bear comes within 50 yards; talk quietly and no flash on the platforms, don't stop on the walkways; respect the Brooks Falls closure (June 15–August 15, often to September 30) and the river permit corridor (June 15–October 31); camp only in the fenced campground within 1.5 mi of the falls; no pets within 1.5 mi of the falls; don't take pumice or artefacts.
- **Implementation challenges**: dozens of animated bears with individual looks and behaviours (fishing spots, hierarchy at the falls, crossing under the bridge, sleeping on trails) and NPC rangers enforcing distances; salmon leaping a waterfall; a queue-and-capacity system for platforms; floatplanes landing and taxiing on water, weather delays; a treeless ash valley with vertical-walled canyons cut into flat ground; volcano steam plumes; big wind-driven waves on lakes; coastal tides uncovering clam flats.
