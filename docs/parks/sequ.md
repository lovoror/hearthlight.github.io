# Sequoia National Park — `SEQU`

> California · established September 25, 1890 · 1,635.2 km² (404,062.63 acres) · 1,378,337 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~418–4,421 m · NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10, California–Great Basin)
>
> The southern half of « Sequoia & Kings Canyon National Parks », run with Kings Canyon as a single park (one brochure,
> one website under `/seki/`; see [`kica.md`](kica.md)): a staircase from hot oak foothills up to the largest trees on
> Earth in the Giant Forest, then on to a granite divide and Mount Whitney, the highest summit of the lower 48. In
> Hearthlight it should feel like shrinking: the hero ambles between trunks as wide as houses, climbs a stone stairway
> onto a dome above the clouds, and slips into a cool marble cave.

## Reading the map

Sequoia is the southern of the two parks: a block ~61 km wide and 45 km tall that climbs west to east from the Kaweah foothills (Three Rivers, 244 m, just outside the south-west corner; Lake Kaweah below it) to the Sierra crest, where **Mount Whitney** (4,421 m) marks the east boundary. Its northern edge is the **Kings–Kaweah Divide** and the Silliman Crest, shared with Kings Canyon. The **Great Western Divide** runs north–south through the middle; west of it the forks of the **Kaweah River** fan out in deep canyons; east of it the **Kern River** runs due south in a long straight trench, the Kern Canyon. Everything with a road sits in the western third, on the ridges between the Kaweah forks.

**The Generals Highway** is the spine. It enters at **Ash Mountain** (Hwy 198), passes the **Foothills Visitor Center and Park Headquarters** (518 m) and follows the Middle Fork Kaweah north-east past Tunnel Rock (a boulder the old road tunnelled under), Potwisha and **Hospital Rock** (Buckeye Flat campground across the river). Then it climbs: about 1,400 m of tight switchbacks up the canyon wall, marked « vehicles longer than 22 feet not advised between Potwisha and Giant Forest Museum », to the **Giant Forest Museum** (1,954 m). The **Giant Forest** is a sequoia-covered plateau between the Marble Fork (north) and Middle Fork (south): the Big Trees Trail rings Round Meadow by the museum; the **Moro Rock–Crescent Meadow Road** branches south-east to **Moro Rock** (a granite dome at the plateau's lip, 2,050 m), through **Tunnel Log** to **Crescent Meadow** and **Tharp's Log** (closed in snow; shuttle-only on summer weekends and holidays); the highway continues north-east to the **General Sherman Tree** (winter parking by the road; the main summer lot is up the Wolverton road, with a stepped trail down to the tree), then to **Lodgepole Village** (2,048 m: visitor centre, market, campground) in the glacial valley of the Marble Fork, with the Tokopah Falls trail running east up the valley. The highway turns west past **Wuksachi Lodge**, Little Baldy and **Dorst Creek** and leaves Sequoia at **Lost Grove**, crossing the Giant Sequoia National Monument (Stony Creek, Montecito Sequoia Lodge, Quail Flat) to Kings Canyon's **Grant Grove**, ~48 km (30 mi) north of Giant Forest.

**Side roads.** The **Crystal Cave Road**, narrow and closed to vehicles over 22 ft, winds ~11 km west from the highway below Giant Forest to **Crystal Cave** (advance tickets required, open only in the tour season). The **Mineral King Road** leaves Hwy 198 in Three Rivers and climbs 25 mi (40 km) east along the East Fork Kaweah through the **Lookout Point Entrance**, the Atwell Grove and **Silver City** (a private summer resort) to the **Mineral King Ranger Station** (2,387 m) at the head of the valley — narrow, winding, partly unpaved, RVs and trailers not advised, gated in winter. **South Fork Drive** leads from Three Rivers to the remote **South Fork** campground (Ladybug Trail, Garfield Grove). **Mineral King** itself is a subalpine glacial valley (2,300–3,000 m) ringed by peaks (Sawtooth Peak, Mineral Peak, Florence Peak, Vandever Mountain, Farewell Gap to the south) and old mining-era cabins, with trails up to Monarch, Crystal, Eagle, Mosquito and Franklin lakes.

**The roadless east.** From Crescent Meadow the **High Sierra Trail** runs east along the Middle Fork's north wall to **Bearpaw Meadow** (a High Sierra Camp, 2,377 m), Hamilton Lakes and over the Great Western Divide at **Kaweah Gap** (3,258 m), down into the Kern Canyon and up to Crabtree and **Mount Whitney**, whose summit is the end of both the High Sierra Trail and the John Muir Trail. North of Lodgepole the Lakes Trail climbs to Heather, Emerald and **Pear Lake** (a winter ski hut) under **Alta Peak** (3,415 m). The ecosystem map shows the whole east as alpine and subalpine country: the Great Western Divide (Mount Stewart, Kaweah Peaks Ridge, Mount Eisen), the Kings–Kern Divide on the north border, the Kern Canyon down the middle and the Sierra crest with Whitney on the east.

### Water

The **Kaweah River** drains all the developed west. Its **Marble Fork** rises on the Tablelands, falls over Tokopah Falls, runs through Lodgepole and down a gorge north of Giant Forest; the **Middle Fork** comes from Hamilton Lakes and Kaweah Gap, runs in a deep canyon under Moro Rock (the two forks meet in the gorge below it) and past Hospital Rock and Potwisha; the **East Fork** drains the Mineral King valley; the **South Fork** and **North Fork** come in from the remote south and from Kings Canyon's Redwood Canyon. All meet around **Three Rivers** and pool in **Lake Kaweah** behind Terminus Dam, outside the park. East of the Great Western Divide the **Kern River** runs due south through its trench (Kern Hot Spring on its bank). The high country holds hundreds of glacial tarns (Pear, Emerald, Heather, Hamilton, Precipice, Monarch, Eagle, Mosquito, Franklin lakes, Nine Lake Basin). Snowmelt peaks in **May–June**; drowning in the swift, cold rivers is the leading cause of death in the parks. Underground, the marble bands hide **200+ caves**, Crystal Cave among them. Meadows (Crescent, Log, Round) are wet and flowery in early summer.

### Relief

From **~418 m** (the Kaweah at the Ash Mountain boundary; NPS pages round it to 1,300–1,370 ft) to **4,421 m** (Mount Whitney, 14,505 ft by the modern datum; NPS pages still print 14,494 ft / 4,418 m). The park climbs west to east in steps: oak foothills (400–1,500 m) cut by V-shaped river canyons; the **Giant Forest plateau** (~1,950–2,150 m) perched on the canyon rim, its southern lip marked by **Moro Rock**, a granite dome that drops ~1,200 m to the Middle Fork; the U-shaped glacial valleys of Lodgepole and Mineral King; granite domes (Little Baldy, Beetle Rock, the Watchtower); the **Great Western Divide** (3,600–4,000 m); the **Kern Canyon** trench; and the Sierra crest on the east edge with Whitney.

For the game: the Generals Highway's 1,400 m climb is a stack of **banks and switchback ramps** up one long wall; Giant Forest is a gently rolling plateau where the **trees are the verticals** (84 m sequoias — taller than most cliffs in the game); Moro Rock is a **3D dome** with a stairway of railings along its crest; the valleys at Lodgepole and Mineral King are flat floors between cliff levels, with Tokopah Falls as a stepped waterfall; the divide and Whitney are distant **3D peaks** on the east horizon. Camera note: in the dense grove, trunks and crowns between the camera and the hero must fade out, or the Giant Forest becomes a wall of bark.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: foothills (400–1,500 m): blue oak, interior live oak, California buckeye, western redbud, chaparral of chamise and manzanita, chaparral yucca, golden grass; lower montane (1,500–1,900 m): ponderosa pine, black oak, incense cedar; the sequoia belt (1,700–2,300 m): giant sequoias with white fir, sugar pine, Pacific dogwood and western azalea; red fir and lodgepole pine around Lodgepole and Mineral King (2,000–2,800 m); subalpine foxtail and whitebark pine (2,800–3,400 m); alpine rock gardens with sky pilot above.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#A4522E` sequoia bark cinnamon · `#6B3A26` bark in shadow and old fire scars · `#2F4A2E` sequoia and fir foliage · `#D4CFC6` granite (Moro Rock) · `#D2B56C` foothill grass in summer · `#8FA487` blue oak · `#F3F1EA` dogwood bracts and snow · `#7FAE6A` spring meadow · `#4E8C83` Kaweah river jade · `#EDEBE6` polished marble (Crystal Cave).
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): black bear (several hundred; grazing Crescent Meadow in spring, berries in summer, acorns in the foothills in autumn); mule deer; yellow-bellied marmot (Mineral King, Tokopah valley); American pika (high talus); Sierra Nevada bighorn sheep (reintroduced to Big Arroyo on the Great Western Divide in 2014); mountain lion, bobcat and ringtail (secretive, night); gray fox (foothills); western rattlesnake and western fence lizard (foothills); western pond turtle and California newts (foothill streams, winter–spring); acorn woodpecker (foothill oaks); pileated woodpecker and western tanager (sequoia belt); Clark's nutcracker (high country); California spotted owl; peregrine falcons nesting on Moro Rock; Douglas squirrels cutting sequoia cones.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): giant sequoia (green cones hold seed ~20 years; after fire they rain seed within 1–2 weeks), western redbud (magenta, February–April), chaparral yucca « Our Lord's candle » (one tall bloom, April–July), California buckeye (flowers late spring, bare by late summer), Pacific dogwood (white in May–June, pink-orange in autumn), sugar pine (the longest cones of any conifer), lupine and corn lily in Crescent Meadow (summer), foxtail pine (twisted, high country), sky pilot (alpine blue).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter brings deep snow to the groves (December–May), chains on the Generals Highway, the Lodgepole–Grant Grove stretch closed January to March, snowshoeing among red trunks and green foothills below. Spring is wildflowers and redbud in the foothills (February–May), rivers rising, bears in the meadows. Summer is hot in the foothills (32–46 °C), mild in the groves (20–24 °C), with afternoon thunderstorms, shuttles and crowds. Autumn has dogwood colour, acorn-fed bears, clear air and the Mineral King road closing at the end of October. Fire is part of the story: the 2020 and 2021 fires killed ~10–19 % of all large sequoias, while Giant Forest, long treated with prescribed burns, came through.
- **Night**: some of the darkest skies in California (no formal certification); the Dark Sky Festival each September with star parties at Giant Forest, Lodgepole and Wuksachi; ranger moonlight walks and « Full Moon on Moro Rock »; 17 species of bats, ringtails, owls, bears on the prowl.
- **Sounds**: the deep hush of the big trees, Douglas squirrels chattering and dropping cones, woodpeckers drumming, the Middle Fork roaring in its gorge, marmot whistles in Mineral King, wind high in the sequoia crowns, snow sliding off branches, the shuttle's brakes at the museum stop.

## Moments

- **Foothill spring**: redbud and wildflowers February–April (NPS: into May), newts in the streams.
- **Bears in Crescent Meadow**: spring and early summer, grazing the new grass.
- **Mineral King marmots**: from early spring to mid-July they chew radiator hoses; drivers wrap their cars in tarps.
- **Snow in the Giant Forest**: December–May; the Generals Highway between Lodgepole and Grant Grove is unplowed from the Friday after New Year to the third Friday of March.
- **Sunset and full moon on Moro Rock**, the Great Western Divide turning pink.
- **High water**: the Kaweah peaks in May–June; Tokopah Falls and Marble Falls at their best.
- **Dogwood bloom** in Giant Forest, ~May–June, and its autumn colour in October.
- **Dark Sky Festival**: mid-September (September 11–13 in 2026), star parties across both parks.
- **After a fire**: sequoia cones open and seeds rain down within 1–2 weeks onto the ash.
- **Crystal Cave season**: normally late May to September.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: ~1,024 × 1,024 tiles (or the southern two thirds of a shared 1,536 × 1,280 « SEKI » map with Kings Canyon to the north). The Giant Forest core — museum, Sherman, Congress Trail, Moro Rock, Crescent Meadow, ~3.5 × 3.5 km — at ~1:4 (~450 × 450 tiles), with the named trees at true size (Sherman's trunk ~11 m ≈ 7–8 tiles across). The Generals Highway's 16 mi of switchbacks become a ~250-tile ramp of 6–8 hairpins; Lodgepole and the Tokopah valley ~300 × 120; the Foothills 200 × 150; Mineral King a separate pocket valley at the end of its own winding road; the High Sierra Trail a ~300-tile strip to Bearpaw and Kaweah Gap, with the Kern Canyon and Whitney as a distant backdrop. Cut the far south (South Fork) and most of the Kern.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Giant Forest Museum and its plaza (interior), Lodgepole (visitor centre, market), Wuksachi Lodge (interior), the Foothills Visitor Center, Mineral King ranger station; 3D landmarks: the General Sherman Tree and the Congress Trail giants, Moro Rock and its stair, Tunnel Log, Auto Log, Tharp's Log (a cabin *inside* a log — an interior), Tunnel Rock, Hospital Rock's painted boulder; interiors: Crystal Cave (marble, the spider-web gate), the Pear Lake ski hut, the Bearpaw tent camp.
- **Activities**: hiking; photo spots (the Sherman base, Moro Rock summit, driving through Tunnel Log, Crescent Meadow, Tharp's Log, Beetle Rock at sunset, Tokopah Falls, Little Baldy, the Mineral King valley, Kaweah Gap); wildlife (bears in the meadows, marmots, bighorn far out); ranger programmes (grove walks, moonlight walks, the Beetle Rock talk, evening campfires); Junior Ranger (ages 5+; bears and food storage, caves, dark skies, sequoias, invasive plants → the SEKI badge); caves (Crystal Cave tour); winter (snowshoe walks, Wolverton sledding, the Pear Lake ski hut); vehicles: car, the free shuttle lines, the Visalia bus.
- **Passport stamps**: Foothills Visitor Center, Giant Forest Museum, Lodgepole Visitor Center, Mineral King (ranger station), Crystal Cave (at the cave, in season), plus the « 125th Anniversary 1890–2015 » bonus stamp.
- **With friends / with the world**: « How many friends round Sherman? » — the trunk is 31 m round at the ground, so a full ring of ~16 heroes hand in hand is a party challenge (the real tree is fenced: do it at a fallen giant or with a rope); a group shot on Moro Rock's summit; a convoy through Tunnel Log; marmot-proofing the party car together with a tarp at Mineral King; a shared cave tour (35 people max); Dark Sky Festival star parties as the open-world night gathering; the High Sierra Trail as a party trek with the Bearpaw camp as the inn.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): every scrap of food and scented item in a bear box (a car with food in view gets broken into); 50 yards from bears and mountain lions, 25 from other wildlife; stay behind the fences at the big trees (shallow roots) and never climb them; wrap your car at Mineral King; step in the disinfectant tray and touch nothing in Crystal Cave (white-nose syndrome); stay out of spring rivers; no pets on trails, no drones.
- **Implementation challenges**: 84 m trees with 11 m trunks (a new « giant tree » model class, and occlusion rules for the camera); a drive-through log and a cabin inside a log; a 1,400 m switchback climb; Moro Rock's cliff-edge stair; a marble show cave; deep snow and winter road gates; burned groves (black snags, seedlings) next to living ones; a skyline of 4,000 m peaks that can never be reached on the map.
