# Carlsbad Caverns National Park — `CAVE`

> New Mexico · established May 14, 1930 · 189.3 km² (46,766 acres) · 410,778 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 1,096–1,992 m on the surface (the cavern reaches 316 m below it) · NPS region IMR
>
> A strip of Chihuahuan Desert on a fossil reef, hollowed by more than 119 caves; the famous one, Carlsbad Cavern, holds
> the Big Room, North America's largest single cave chamber by volume. In Hearthlight it should feel like stepping from
> blinding desert light down a spiral of switchbacks into a cool, echoing, amber-lit underworld — then climbing back
> out at dusk to watch the bats pour out.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a stair-stepped band ~30 km long running west-south-west to east-north-east along the edge of
the Guadalupe Mountains. Its south-east side is the **Capitan Reef escarpment**, a steep front where the fossil reef
drops ~200 m to the desert plain; its north-west side climbs onto the reef's back, the **Guadalupe Ridge**, rising
westward to ~1,990 m where the park meets the Lincoln National Forest (north and west). US 62/180 runs outside the park
along the foot of the escarpment, from Carlsbad (north-east) through **Whites City** at the east corner and on
south-west to Guadalupe Mountains National Park and El Paso; Texas begins a few kilometres south of the park.

At the east end the **Carlsbad Cavern Highway** climbs 7 mi / 11 km west up **Walnut Canyon** to the **visitor
center** on the rim (1,344 m). The **Natural Entrance** is a five-minute walk north-east of it, a gaping hole in a
hillside with the **Bat Flight Amphitheater** on its lip; the elevators start inside the visitor center. West of the
visitor center the one-way gravel **Walnut Canyon Desert Drive** (9.5 mi) loops out along the ridge and back down
Walnut Canyon (closed since the August 2022 flood). The **Old Guano Road Trail** runs east along the rim from the Natural
Entrance down to Whites City. **Lechuguilla Cave**'s entrance lies north of the drive, closed to all but scientists.

The west two-thirds is roadless: parallel canyons cut into the reef (Rattlesnake, North Slaughter, Slaughter, Yucca,
Double), reached only from the desert below. County Road 418 leaves US 62/180 west to **Rattlesnake Springs** (a
detached oasis unit of cottonwoods and lawn), then continues as gravel to the mouth of **Slaughter Canyon**, where a
steep half-mile path climbs to **Slaughter Canyon Cave** (a wild cave, ranger tours only). The **Guadalupe Ridge Trail**
(a ~100-mile National Recreation Trail to Guadalupe Peak in Texas) crosses the park along the ridge; its section from
the visitor center to Slaughter Canyon is closed by flood damage.

Underground (the NPS cutaway), the **Big Room** is a vast cross-shaped chamber reached from the **Underground Rest
Area** (lunchroom and elevator lobby) at 229 m depth. The trail leaves the lunchroom through the **Hall of Giants**
(Giant Dome, Twin Domes) to **Caveman Junction**, where a **shortcut** cuts across. The full loop goes on to the
**Jumping Off Place** above **Lower Cave** (the 1924 explorers' rope ladder), **Top of the Cross** (the room's centre
and highest ceiling), **Mirror Lake** and the **Bottomless Pit**, then back past gypsum blocks to Caveman Junction,
**Crystal Spring Dome**, the **Rock of Ages**, the **Painted Grotto** and the **Chinese Theater** to the elevators. The
**Natural Entrance Trail** descends in tight switchbacks past the dark mouth of **Bat Cave**, through **Devil's Den**,
the **Whale's Mouth**, **Iceberg Rock** and the **Green Lake Overlook**, over the **Boneyard** and **Appetite Hill** to
the lunchroom; the **King's Palace** ranger route branches off near its end into the deepest decorated rooms.

### Water

- **No surface rivers**: canyons run only after storms; the August 2022 flood tore up the desert drive and trails.
- **Rattlesnake Springs**: a permanent spring feeding a pond, a cottonwood grove and a lawn; the park's water supply
  and a birding oasis (no swimming). The Black River flows just east of it, outside the park.
- **In the cave**: still drip pools and lakes — Devil's Spring, Green Lake, Mirror Lake, the Lake of the Clouds at the
  bottom, the lakes of Left Hand Tunnel; rimstone dams and cave pearls; clear water that turns green or turquoise under
  the lights. The caves were dissolved by sulfuric acid rising from below, not by a river — hence the huge rooms, the
  gypsum blocks on the floors and no stream passages.

### Relief

Surface: lowest ~1,096 m (3,596 ft) in the desert near Rattlesnake Springs, highest ~1,992 m (6,535 ft) on the
Guadalupe Ridge at the western boundary. A tilted slab: the fossil **Capitan Reef** faces south-east with a 150–250 m
escarpment from the plain (US 62/180) to the rim where the visitor center sits; behind it the reef's back rises gently
westward, cut by deep, parallel canyons draining south-east (Walnut, Rattlesnake, Slaughter, Yucca). Underground: the
Natural Entrance drops 229 m to the Big Room level; King's Palace is at 253 m; Lower Cave reaches 316 m. In the game:
the escarpment as one long two-level cliff with Walnut Canyon's road as the ramp; canyons as narrow cliff-walled cuts
in the upper plateau; the Natural Entrance as a pit with stairs spiralling down; the cave as a dark instance with
several floor levels, 3D formations (stalagmites, columns, draperies), breakdown boulders and pits.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: plain and escarpment foot (1,100–1,300 m) — creosote, lechuguilla, ocotillo, prickly
  pear and cholla, catclaw, soaptree yucca; escarpment and canyons (1,300–1,600 m) — sotol, agave, algerita, mariola,
  little-leaf sumac, Texas black walnut and desert willow in the washes, alligator juniper; ridge top (1,600–1,990 m)
  — pinyon, juniper, gray oak, grama grass; Rattlesnake Springs — Fremont cottonwoods and hackberries. Underground,
  only a little lamp-flora near the lights.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#D8CCB2` pale reef limestone · `#C6AD86` desert soil and caliche · `#8C9A6E`
  sotol and lechuguilla · `#57694B` juniper on the ridge · `#EAD9B6` cream flowstone and stalagmites · `#C58B57`
  iron-stained orange formations · `#F5EBD3` white gypsum blocks and aragonite · `#5E8C7A` green cave pools · `#1C1916`
  the dark zone · `#F2DDA8` warm trail lighting.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): Brazilian (Mexican) free-tailed bat (April–October, dusk and dawn at the Natural
  Entrance; 17 bat species in the park), cave swallow (nesting in the entrance, spring–summer), cave cricket
  (underground), ringtail (night, rim and canyons), mule deer (evenings along the entrance road), javelina, rock
  squirrel, greater roadrunner, western diamondback rattlesnake and collared lizard (warm months), great horned owl
  (canyons), vermilion flycatcher, summer tanager and wild turkey (Rattlesnake Springs).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): lechuguilla (bloom stalks in early summer; Lechuguilla Cave's namesake), sotol (summer
  spikes), ocotillo (red tips after spring rain), claret cup cactus (scarlet, April–May), algerita (yellow spring
  flowers, red berries), Texas black walnut (Walnut Canyon, nuts in fall), soaptree yucca (white bells, May–June).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — windy, mild, cactus bloom, the bats return; summer — 35 °C+ on the rim, afternoon
  storms from July, the 13 °C cave as a refuge; late summer–early autumn — the wettest, flash floods, the biggest bat
  flights; winter — cold nights, the odd snow on the rim, bats gone to Mexico.
- **Night**: the bat exodus at dusk, ranger night-sky programs under a dark desert sky, ringtails and owls, the
  pre-dawn return of the bats.
- **Sounds**: wind over the rim; swallows chattering in the entrance; inside, drips, echoing footsteps and hushed
  voices; at dusk the whir of thousands of wings; at dawn the buzz of diving bats.

## Moments

- **Bat flight** (every evening, April–October; free ranger program at the amphitheater before sunset; no electronics):
  a few bats, then a spiralling column of hundreds of thousands heading south-east to the Pecos and Black river
  valleys, for 20 minutes to 2.5 hours; best August–September when the young and northern migrants join.
- **Dawn return** (roughly 4–6 am, same season): bats fold their wings and dive into the entrance at 40 km/h+, buzzing.
- **First sight of the Big Room** after the elevator or the long descent: the ceiling lost in the dark, the Giant Dome.
- **Blackout in the King's Palace** (ranger tour, when offered): lights off, total darkness and silence.
- **Summer relief**: from 38 °C on the rim into the 13 °C cave; cave swallows swirling in the Twilight Zone.
- **Late-summer storms** (August–September) and **spring birding at Rattlesnake Springs** (April–May migration).

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a surface map of **640 × 384 tiles** plus a cave instance (~720 × 480 tiles, several levels,
  off-map like the engine's dungeons). Surface: the cavern hub near true scale (visitor center to Natural Entrance 300 m
  → ~150 tiles); Walnut Canyon's 11 km road ~160 tiles; the western reef compressed ~1:20 into three canyon cuts and the
  ridge; Rattlesnake Springs as an oasis vignette on the south edge. Cave: the Natural Entrance route (2 km, 229 m) as
  ~500 tiles of switchbacks stepping down ~8 levels; the Big Room (1,220 × 190 m) at ~1:2 → ~600 × 120 tiles with a
  ~1,000-tile loop and its shortcut; King's Palace and Lower Cave as side rooms behind ranger gates.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the **visitor center** (ticket hall, elevator lobby, film, café — an interior), the **Natural
  Entrance and amphitheater** (a 3D cave mouth with curved stone seats), the **Underground Rest Area** (the cave's
  social hub), and big 3D props: Giant Dome, Twin Domes, Rock of Ages, the Bottomless Pit rail, Top of the Cross, the
  rope ladder, Iceberg Rock, the Whale's Mouth; Rattlesnake Springs' cottonwood grove.
- **Activities**: the two self-guided routes (an audio-guide mode reading the numbered stops), ranger-gated tours
  (King's Palace blackout, Left Hand Tunnel by lantern, Lower Cave by ladder, Slaughter Canyon Cave by headlamp); photo
  spots — Rock of Ages, Giant Dome, Bottomless Pit, Green Lake, the entrance switchbacks from above, Walnut Canyon
  Overlook, and the bat column at dusk as a "memory" (cameras are banned there); wildlife (swallows by day, bats at
  dusk, ringtails at night, birds at Rattlesnake Springs); Junior Ranger (booklet, a bat badge, a formation hunt: soda
  straws, helictites, cave pearls, popcorn); rim hikes; no frontcountry campground (backcountry permits; lodging at
  Whites City).
- **Passport stamps**: one cancellation, "Carlsbad", at the visitor center bookstore (Eastern National list, Aug. 2025).
- **With friends / with the world**: the Bat Flight Amphitheater as the nightly server-wide gathering (a shared silent
  moment); the Underground Rest Area as a meeting room; a party blackout in King's Palace; one player walks down the
  Natural Entrance while the others ride the elevator and meet at the lunchroom; a group photo at the Rock of Ages.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): don't touch formations, no food or drink beyond the lunchroom,
  no coins in pools, stay on the paved trail, speak quietly, walk the bio-cleaning mats on the way out (white-nose
  syndrome), no phones or cameras at the bat flight, 100 yards from wildlife, carry water on desert trails.
- **Implementation challenges**: a big multi-level cave with a sense of vast height (the Big Room's 78 m ceiling) in a
  renderer built for top-down terrain; darkness with trail lighting and a blackout; a swarm of hundreds of thousands of
  bats (instanced particles) on a daily timer; elevators linking a surface map and an instance; dripping-water sounds
  and pools; closures (flood-damaged trails, suspended tours) as state.
