> California · established March 4, 1940 (absorbing General Grant National Park of October 1, 1890; Cedar Grove and
> Tehipite Valley added in 1965) · 1,869.2 km² (461,901.20 acres) · 779,791 recreation visits (2025) · ~1,060–4,343 m ·
> NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10, California–Great Basin)
>
> The northern half of « Sequoia & Kings Canyon National Parks », run with Sequoia as a single park (one brochure,
> one website under `/seki/`; see [`sequ.md`](sequ.md)): a detached grove of giant sequoias around the General Grant
> Tree, a scenic byway that dives into one of the deepest canyons in North America, and beyond the road's end the
> wildest stretch of the High Sierra. In Hearthlight it should feel like two gifts in a row — a cathedral of big trees
> at the rim, then a green, echoing glacial canyon where the road simply stops and only trails go on.

## Reading the map

Kings Canyon is really two places joined by a road through someone else's forest. The **Grant Grove section** is a thin north–south strip on the west edge, wholly surrounded by the Sequoia National Forest and Giant Sequoia National Monument; the **main park** is a huge roadless block of the High Sierra to the east, reached by car at only one point, the bottom of the canyon of the **South Fork Kings River**. The ecosystem map shows the main park's shape: the South Fork and Middle Fork of the Kings drain it south-west from the crest; the Monarch Divide separates them; the Goddard and LeConte divides and the Evolution Basin (headwaters of the San Joaquin) form the northern tip; the Palisades (**North Palisade**, 4,343 m, the highest point) stand on the north-east crest; the **Kings–Kern Divide** and Mount Brewer close the south, against Sequoia.

**Grant Grove.** Hwy 180 enters at the **Big Stump Entrance** (Big Stump Grove and picnic area, where 1880s loggers left their stumps) and runs 3 km north to **Grant Grove Village** (2,008 m): Kings Canyon Visitor Center, market, restaurant, John Muir Lodge and cabins, post office, pack station, and the Azalea, Sunset and Crystal Springs campgrounds. The **General Grant Tree** stands 1 km north-west of the village on a paved loop, with the North Grove beyond. A small road climbs east to **Panoramic Point** (closes with snow), looking out across the canyon country to the Great Western Divide; a fire lookout crowns Park Ridge south of it. At **the Wye**, just south of the village, the **Generals Highway** branches south-east past Redwood Mountain Overlook and Quail Flat (the dirt road down to **Redwood Mountain Grove**, the largest sequoia grove, open summer only) toward Sequoia.

**The Kings Canyon Scenic Byway** (Hwy 180 East) leaves Grant Grove northward, passes the turn to Hume Lake and Converse Basin (Chicago Stump, Boole Tree; national forest), and at **Hume Junction** reaches the winter gate: beyond it the road is closed ~mid-November to late April. It swings round **Junction View** above the meeting of the South and Middle forks, then switchbacks down ~900 m to the river, which it follows upstream east past **Boyden Cave**, Convict Flat and **Grizzly Falls** (all national forest), re-entering the park a few kilometres before Cedar Grove. The canyon floor opens into a flat glacial valley ~1 km wide between granite walls: Sheep Creek, Sentinel, Moraine and Canyon View campgrounds and **Cedar Grove Village** (1,412 m: visitor centre, lodge, market, food, pack station) on the north bank; then **Knapp's Cabin**, **Roaring River Falls** (south side), **Zumwalt Meadow** under the Grand Sentinel and North Dome, **Muir Rock** on the river, and the loop at **Road's End** (permit station, 1,535 m), 10 km past the village by road. A narrow unpaved road (grey on the map) also follows the north bank east of the village. Trails fan out from Road's End: north up the South Fork to **Mist Falls** and Paradise Valley (the Rae Lakes Loop), east up **Bubbs Creek** under **The Sphinx** toward the crest and Kearsarge Pass, and north up Copper Creek to the Monarch Divide.

**The wilderness.** Everything else is trail country, shown on the ecosystem map: **Tehipite Valley** on the Middle Fork, a second Yosemite-style valley under the 1,100 m **Tehipite Dome**, reached by no road; the John Muir Trail running north–south along the east side over Forester, Glen, Pinchot, Mather and Muir passes (the stone Muir Hut on Muir Pass), past Rae Lakes, the Palisade glaciers and Evolution Basin; and cross-crest passes to the Owens Valley.

### Water

The **Kings River** is the park's spine: the **South Fork** (44 mi / 71 km) runs from the crest through Paradise Valley to Road's End, where **Bubbs Creek** joins, then flows west down the flat floor of Cedar Grove (clear green pools, gravel bars, Muir Rock) and into the gorge past Boyden Cavern; the **Middle Fork** (37 mi / 60 km) drains LeConte Canyon and Simpson Meadow, passes roadless Tehipite Valley and meets the South Fork below Junction View. Both are Wild and Scenic Rivers (1987). The north tip drains to the **South Fork San Joaquin** through Evolution Basin. Side creeks tumble in as falls: **Roaring River Falls**, **Mist Falls**, Grizzly Falls (outside, on the byway), Ella and Viola falls near Grant Grove. About 71 % of the runoff comes with the April–July snowmelt — the rivers are cold, fast and deadly then; Muir Rock's pool is for late summer only. The high country holds hundreds of lakes (Rae, Charlotte, Bullfrog, Kearsarge, Evolution, Sapphire, Wanda, the Palisade lakes) and small glaciers under the highest peaks (Darwin, Mount Fiske). Hume Lake, just outside, is an old logging reservoir.

### Relief

From **~1,060 m** (the lowest point, on the Redwood Canyon side of the Grant Grove section) to **4,343 m** (North Palisade, one of five 14,000-foot summits on the Palisade crest). The Grant Grove section is a forested ridge at 1,800–2,300 m. The **canyon of the South Fork** is a glacier-widened U at Cedar Grove — a flat floor ~1 km wide at 1,400–1,550 m, walled by granite rising 1,500 m and more (Grand Sentinel, North Dome, Lookout Peak) — and a river-cut V gorge below it; where the Middle and South forks meet under Spanish Mountain the relief reaches ~2,400 m, « arguably the deepest canyon in North America » (NPS). The rest of the park is the High Sierra: divides at 3,500–4,300 m (Monarch, LeConte, Goddard, Kings–Kern), glacial basins and the Palisades.

For the game: the byway's plunge into the canyon is a sequence of **switchback banks** down one wall; the Cedar Grove floor is flat, with the river's **banks** and meadow; the walls are tall **cliff stacks** with 3D landmarks (Grand Sentinel, North Dome, the Sphinx); Tehipite Dome and the Palisades are distant **3D peaks**. The canyon runs east–west: its north wall faces the camera and reads well, the south wall must cut away when the hero walks beneath it.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: the Grant Grove ridge (1,800–2,300 m): giant sequoia with sugar pine, white fir, incense cedar and dogwood; the canyon walls: chaparral, canyon live oak and chaparral yucca spikes; the Cedar Grove floor (1,400 m): incense cedar (hence the name), ponderosa pine, black oak, cottonwoods and alders by the river, bracken and ferns in Zumwalt Meadow; subalpine basins (2,700–3,400 m): lodgepole, foxtail and whitebark pine; alpine granite with sky pilot and lichens above.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#D6D2CA` pale canyon granite · `#8E8A84` wall shadows · `#3E6B6A` deep green river pools · `#9FD0C8` shallow river over gravel · `#A4522E` sequoia bark · `#E9E2C8` yucca bloom · `#C8932E` black oak in October · `#6E8F4A` Zumwalt ferns · `#2D5C86` alpine lake · `#B04A3A` the red-banded Painted Lady above Rae Lakes.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): black bear (Grant Grove and the canyon; berries and acorns); mule deer; yellow-bellied marmot (Zumwalt, high trails); pika; Sierra Nevada bighorn (the crest, rarely seen); American dipper (South Fork rocks); western rattlesnake and western fence lizard (warm canyon floor and byway); acorn woodpecker; pileated woodpecker (groves); Steller's jay; Clark's nutcracker (high country); California spotted owl; bats at dusk.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): giant sequoia (the Grant Tree), Pacific dogwood (white in spring, red in autumn), chaparral yucca « Our Lord's candle » on the canyon walls (April–July), California black oak and incense cedar (Cedar Grove), western azalea (~June, by creeks), bracken and ferns (Zumwalt), lupine and columbine by the falls, sky pilot (alpine).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter buries Grant Grove (~4.7 m of snowfall a year; sledding at Big Stump and Columbine) and closes the canyon (mid-November to late April/May); spring brings the snowmelt flood, roaring falls and rockfall; summer is hot on the canyon floor (32–37 °C) and opens the passes (Glen Pass from mid–late July); autumn is quiet, golden with black oaks and dogwoods. Fire has marked both ends: the 2015 Rough Fire ringed Grant Grove and the byway, the 2021 fires burned into Redwood Mountain.
- **Night**: dark canyon skies (Dark Sky Festival star parties at Grant Grove, Big Stump and Cedar Grove in September; Grant Grove stargazing programmes); bats over the river, owls, the Milky Way in the canyon's slot of sky.
- **Sounds**: the South Fork's rush and the roar of Mist Falls, rockfall clatter on freezing mornings, the canyon wren's falling trill, woodpeckers in the groves, pack-station horses and mule bells, wind in the sequoia crowns, carols at the Grant Tree in December.

## Moments

- **Trek to the Tree**: every second Sunday of December since 1925 (the 100th on December 14, 2025), a snowy walk and ceremony at the General Grant Tree, the Nation's Christmas Tree.
- **Snowmelt flood**: April–July, with Mist Falls and Roaring River Falls at their biggest in late spring.
- **The canyon opens**: the byway beyond Hume Lake opens from the fourth Friday of April at the earliest (usually May) and closes mid-November.
- **High passes open**: Glen Pass and the Rae Lakes Loop from mid–late July to September.
- **Yucca bloom** on the byway's canyon walls, April–July (peak April–May).
- **Dark Sky Festival**: mid-September, star parties at Grant Grove, Big Stump and Cedar Grove.
- **Autumn in Cedar Grove**: black oaks and dogwoods in October.
- **Late-summer swim** at Muir Rock, when the river finally slows.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: ~1,024 × 768 tiles (or the northern third of a shared 1,536 × 1,280 « SEKI » map). The Grant Grove section on the west edge (~200 × 400 tiles: Big Stump, the village, the Grant Tree loop near true scale, Panoramic Point, Redwood Mountain); the byway as a ~400-tile scenic drive through the national forest that plunges down switchbacks into the canyon (Junction View, Boyden Cavern as a cave interior, Grizzly Falls); the Cedar Grove–Road's End canyon floor ~500 × 120 tiles (its 10 km squeezed ~3×) between tall walls; beyond Road's End a compressed trail web to Mist Falls, Paradise Valley and one Rae Lakes loop over Glen Pass. Tehipite, Evolution and the Palisades stay as backdrop, with the Muir Hut as a far vignette.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Grant Grove Village (visitor centre, lodge, market, restaurant, pack station), Cedar Grove village, the Road's End permit station; 3D landmarks: the General Grant Tree, Gamlin Cabin (1872) and the walk-through Fallen Monarch, the Centennial and Mark Twain stumps, the Panoramic Point railing, the Park Ridge lookout, Knapp's Cabin, Roaring River Falls, the Grand Sentinel and North Dome, Muir Rock, Mist Falls, the Sphinx, the Muir Hut; interiors: the visitor centres, Boyden Cavern (outside the park), the hollow Fallen Monarch.
- **Activities**: hiking; photo spots (the Grant Tree, Panoramic Point, Junction View, Grizzly Falls, Roaring River Falls, Zumwalt Meadow under Grand Sentinel, Muir Rock, Mist Falls, the Sphinx from Bubbs Creek, Rae Lakes); wildlife; ranger talks at the Grant Tree and campfire programmes; Junior Ranger (the shared SEKI badge); horse and mule rides from the Grant Grove and Cedar Grove pack stations; river wading and a late-summer swim; winter sledding and snowshoeing at Grant Grove; the Trek to the Tree.
- **Passport stamps**: Grant Grove, Kings Canyon Visitor Center, Cedar Grove, plus the « 75th Anniversary 1940–2015 » bonus stamp.
- **With friends / with the world**: the Trek to the Tree as the open world's winter gathering (everyone walks through the snow to the Nation's Christmas Tree on the second Sunday of December); a road-trip convoy down the byway with stops at every overlook; tales told from Muir Rock at dusk; a multi-day Rae Lakes trek with a pack string of mules; a group ring round the General Grant Tree's fence.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): food in the bear lockers; stay behind the fences at the big trees; never carve or cut a tree (the stumps of Big Stump and Converse Basin tell why); keep out of the spring river; watch for rockfall in freezing weather; wilderness permits and bear canisters beyond Road's End, no fires above 10,000 ft (3,048 m); no pets on trails, no drones.
- **Implementation challenges**: a park in two pieces joined by a road through non-park land (the forest stretch must be drawn but gated from stamps and park rules); a canyon 1,500–2,400 m deep whose south wall hides the floor from the camera; rockfall; a seasonal road with a winter gate; snowbound passes until late July; burned groves; a stone hut on a pass; a yearly December event with snow and a wreath.
