# Guadalupe Mountains National Park — `GUMO`

> Texas · established September 30, 1972 · 349.5 km² (86,367 acres) · 206,423 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 1,108–2,667 m · NPS region IMR
>
> The four highest summits in Texas, raised on the world's best-exposed Permian fossil reef, with a hidden canyon of
> maples, a desert of white gypsum dunes at its foot and almost no roads inside. In Hearthlight it should feel like a
> hiker's pilgrimage: windy switchbacks to the "Top of Texas", a red-and-gold canyon in autumn, and El Capitan's pale
> prow glowing over the desert.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a rough rectangle whose north edge is the New Mexico line. It splits in two halves. The **west
half** is lowland: a stair-stepped panhandle over the **Salt Basin**, a desert of dry salt lakes (outside the park,
west and south-west) whose gypsum has blown into the **Salt Basin Dunes**, a north–south strip of white dunes ~5 km
long inside the western lobe, reached only by the unpaved Williams Road from Dell City (west) and a 1.5 mi trail;
Crow Flats lie north-west. The dashed **Butterfield Stage Route** (1858) crosses this lowland diagonally from the
north-west to Guadalupe Pass.

The **east half** is the **Guadalupe Mountains**, a wedge of fossil reef pointing south. Its western face is a great
escarpment of cliffs rising ~1,500 m above the Salt Basin, crowned by the four highest peaks in Texas in a north–south
line: **Bush Mountain** (2,631 m), **Shumard** (2,626 m), **Bartlett** (2,593 m) and **Guadalupe Peak** (2,667 m) at the
south end, with **El Capitan** (2,464 m), the famous sheer prow, just south of it. The whole block is **Wilderness**:
no roads, ~80 mi of trails and 10 wilderness campgrounds. Inside, **Pine Spring Canyon** cuts west from Pine Springs to
**Devil's Hall**; the **Bowl** is a forested high basin north of Hunter Peak; long ridges run north to **Lost Peak** and
**Dog Canyon**; the **McKittrick** canyons (South and North McKittrick) drain north-east to the reef front.

The only highway, **US 62/180**, comes up from the south-west (El Paso, Salt Flat) around the southern tip, crosses
**Guadalupe Pass** south-east of El Capitan (TX 54 from Van Horn joins below it) and runs north-east along the reef's
eastern foot: **Pine Springs** (visitor center, campground, the Pinery ruins, the main trailhead) sits right on it;
**Frijole Ranch** is 1 mi north up a gravel road; the **McKittrick Canyon** road leaves 7 mi north-east and climbs 4 mi
to a day-use contact station (gate locked at night); the highway then leaves for Whites City and Carlsbad. **Dog
Canyon** is on the far side of the mountains, at the state line, reached only through New Mexico. The 4WD **Williams
Ranch Road** (7.3 mi, locked gates, keys at the visitor center) runs from US 62/180 north along the western foot to the
old Williams Ranch house under the escarpment.

Trails knit the hubs together: the **Tejas Trail** is the north–south spine (Pine Springs → Pine Top → the Bowl → Dog
Canyon, 11.8 mi); the **Bush Mountain Trail** loops along the western rim; the **McKittrick Canyon Trail** follows the
canyon floor then climbs to McKittrick Ridge and on to the Tejas; the **El Capitan Trail** skirts the southern tip to
Williams Ranch; the **Guadalupe Peak Trail** zigzags up from Pine Springs.

### Water

- **McKittrick Creek** (South McKittrick Canyon): the park's only perennial stream, spring-fed, running in and out of
  its gravel bed toward the north-east; visitors must stay out of the water. Pools and small cascades under maples.
- **Springs**: Frijole Spring (at the ranch), **Manzanita Spring** (a pond), **Smith Spring** (a grotto), Choza Spring;
  the Grotto drips with cave formations; old ranch pipes and tanks (Cox Tank) in the high country.
- **Salt Basin** (west, outside): dry salt lakes that flood briefly after storms and leave white crusts; the source of
  the gypsum dunes. No other surface water; summer storms send flash floods down the canyons (Devil's Hall is closed in
  wet weather).

### Relief

Lowest ~1,108 m (3,636 ft) in the Salt Basin at the south-west corner; highest **Guadalupe Peak 2,667 m** (8,751 ft).
The land is a tilted block: the **Capitan Reef** limestone rises from the south-east (Pine Springs at 1,750 m, McKittrick
at 1,530 m) in a steep eastern front (~800 m), levels out on a forested reef-top plateau (2,100–2,500 m) with the Bowl
and long ridges tilting north towards Dog Canyon, and ends on the west in a sheer escarpment (~1,500 m of cliffs and
talus) above the Salt Basin, where the Salt Basin Dunes stand 1,100 m below the summits. El Capitan is the reef's
knife-edge southern prow. In the game: a high plateau with cliff faces on both fronts (the tallest on the west), stairs
for the switchback trails (Guadalupe Peak, the Permian Reef climb, McKittrick's "Notch"), 3D peaks for Guadalupe Peak,
El Capitan, Hunter Peak and the western crest, a deep narrow canyon for McKittrick with a creek, a slot (Devil's Hall),
and low walkable dunes on the basin floor.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: basin and dunes (1,100–1,300 m) — creosote, mesquite coppice dunes, saltbush, gypsum
  scalebroom; bajadas and foothills (1,300–1,800 m) — lechuguilla, sotol, yucca, agave, grama grass, scattered junipers,
  cholla and prickly pear; canyons (1,500–2,000 m) — bigtooth maple, velvet ash, Texas walnut, chinkapin oak, Texas
  madrone, alligator juniper; reef top (2,100–2,667 m) — ponderosa pine, southwestern white pine, Douglas fir, pinyon,
  gray oak and aspen patches, a relict forest from the last ice age.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#E6DDC9` pale reef limestone (El Capitan's face) · `#CDB997` buff talus and
  sandstone foothills · `#C3A57C` desert soil · `#F4F1E8` gypsum dunes · `#EDEAE2` salt-flat crust · `#8D9A68`
  grassland and juniper · `#3E5A3E` high-country conifers · `#C0432F` maple red · `#D8912E` maple orange · `#5F8FA0`
  McKittrick Creek pools.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): mule deer (everywhere at dusk), elk (the Bowl and Dog Canyon), black bear and mountain
  lion (high country, rarely seen), gray fox, ringtail, javelina, coyote, rock squirrel, golden eagle and peregrine
  falcon (cliffs), wild turkey, canyon wren and white-throated swift, greater roadrunner, collared lizard, Texas horned
  lizard, rock rattlesnake, a white form of the lesser earless lizard (dunes), scorpions (seven species), rainbow trout
  in McKittrick Creek (introduced; no fishing).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): bigtooth maple (red and orange, late October–early November), Texas madrone (red peeling
  bark, white spring flowers, red berries), velvet ash (gold in fall), sotol (summer spikes), agave (bloom stalks in
  summer), claret cup cactus (scarlet, April–May), gypsum scalebroom (dunes), Douglas fir (the Bowl).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter — wind, clear cold days, light snow on the peaks; spring — the windiest season, blooms;
  summer — hot foothills, cool high country, afternoon lightning storms (dangerous on Guadalupe Peak); autumn — calm,
  golden light, the maple display and big crowds on weekends.
- **Night**: very dark, remote skies (the nearest towns are 50+ km away); ringtails, owls and elk bugles in the high
  country in autumn; the Milky Way over El Capitan.
- **Sounds**: howling wind on the crest, canyon wrens' falling notes, the trickle of McKittrick Creek, elk bugles in
  autumn, ravens, the crunch of limestone scree, truck noise faint from US 62/180 at Pine Springs.

## Moments

- **Top of Texas**: summiting Guadalupe Peak (8.4 mi round trip), best at sunrise or sunset (headlamp needed), with El
  Capitan below; the steel pyramid on the summit (placed in 1958 for the centennial of the Butterfield mail).
- **McKittrick Canyon fall colors**: usually mid-October to mid-November, peak the last week of October–first week of
  November; Upper Dog Canyon turns a week or two earlier; the park fills on autumn weekends.
- **El Capitan at sunset** from the west (Salt Basin Dunes, US 62/180 west of the pass): the pale cliff turns gold and
  pink above the white dunes.
- **Windstorms**: winter and spring gusts over 60 mph (100 km/h) — the summit wind is a real hazard.
- **Summer thunderstorms** (July–September) with lightning on the peaks; flash floods in Devil's Hall.
- **Elk in the Bowl** at dawn and dusk; bugling in September–October.
- **Dark-sky nights** at Pine Springs and Dog Canyon.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: **768 × 544 tiles**, north up. Keep the Pine Springs–Guadalupe Peak–El Capitan corner near 1:3
  (the 6.8 km peak trail → ~800 switchback tiles would be too long; use ~1:5 → ~450 tiles and ~12 height levels);
  McKittrick Canyon's 5.5 km to the Grotto at ~1:5 (~300 tiles of canyon floor); Frijole Ranch and Smith Spring as a
  compact 120-tile loop; the high country (the Bowl, Pine Top, Lost Peak) compressed ~1:10 into a forested plateau;
  Dog Canyon as a small north hub reached by trail (and a "road via New Mexico" fast-travel); the Salt Basin Dunes as a
  west-edge vignette (~150 × 200 tiles) under the cliffs. Cut most of the north-west lowland.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Pine Springs (visitor center interior with the reef diorama, campground, the Pinery ruins);
  Frijole Ranch (the stone house, orchard, spring); McKittrick Canyon contact station, Pratt Cabin (stone interior) and
  the Grotto (a walk-in alcove); Dog Canyon's ranger station; Williams Ranch house; 3D landmarks: El Capitan's prow,
  Guadalupe Peak with its pyramid, Hunter Peak, the western escarpment wall seen from the dunes.
- **Activities**: the Top of Texas climb (a wind meter on the summit), McKittrick fall-color photo hunt, Devil's Hall
  scramble, fossil spotting on the Permian Reef Trail (sponges, algae, brachiopods for the field guide), dune walks,
  horseback riding (horse corrals at Frijole and Dog Canyon; stock trails), backpacking between wilderness camps (10
  sites), ranger talks at Pine Springs, Junior Ranger (booklet, a fossil-reef badge), the Butterfield stage history
  (the Pinery), photo spots — El Capitan from US 62/180, the summit pyramid, Pratt Cabin in fall, the Notch, the dunes
  under the west face, Devil's Hall's staircase, the Bowl's pines.
- **Passport stamps** (Eastern National list, August 2025): Salt Flat (Pine Springs), Dog Canyon, Frijole Ranch,
  McKittrick Canyon, Butterfield Overland Mail Route – The Pinery, Salt Basin Dunes, Williams Ranch, Guadalupe Peak – The
  Top of Texas.
- **With friends / with the world**: a dawn group summit on Guadalupe Peak (the natural gathering spot, a group photo
  by the pyramid); a fall-colors walk to the Grotto; a party backpacking relay Pine Springs → the Bowl → Dog Canyon; a
  dune sunset party at the Salt Basin; a Guadalupe Ridge Trail event linking to Carlsbad Caverns.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): pack out human waste in the wilderness (bags), stay on the trail
  and out of McKittrick Creek, don't cut switchbacks, no pets on trails, turn back when half your water is gone, respect
  wind and lightning on the peak, McKittrick closes at 5 pm (Mountain Time — the park's clock quirk as a gag), camp only
  in designated sites.
- **Implementation challenges**: very tall stacked cliffs (1,500 m western escarpment), strong wind as a gameplay force,
  seasonal foliage (a McKittrick palette swap), a time-zone joke that could confuse a real-time clock, and a roadless
  interior where hubs connect by trail or by a long off-map drive (Dog Canyon, Salt Basin).
