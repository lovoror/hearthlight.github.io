# Saguaro National Park — `SAGU`

> Arizona · established October 14, 1994 · 375.9 km² (92,877 acres) · 847,749 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 664–2,641 m · NPS region IMR (DOI Region 8, Lower Colorado Basin)
>
> Two separate forests of giant saguaro cacti on either side of Tucson: the low, rugged Tucson Mountain District to
> the west and the Rincon Mountain District to the east, whose cactus forest climbs into a pine-topped « sky island ».
> In Hearthlight it should feel like strolling through a crowd of friendly green giants with raised arms, in the gold
> light of a Sonoran sunset, with monsoon thunder rolling off the mountains.

## Reading the map

The park is **two districts with a city between them**. **Saguaro West**, the **Tucson Mountain District** (TMD), is a
compact block on the west flank of the low Tucson Mountains, looking west over the flat Avra Valley. Pima County's
Tucson Mountain Park borders it to the south, with the Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum, Old Tucson and Gilbert Ray
Campground. **Saguaro East**, the **Rincon Mountain District** (RMD), is a larger square covering the western half of
the **Rincon Mountains**, where Tucson's street grid (Speedway, Broadway, 22nd Street) stops at its west boundary.
The Coronado National Forest wraps it on the north and east; Rincon Valley ranchland lies to the south. In between,
~40 km of city, crossed by the dry Santa Cruz River and the Rillito.

**West.** **Kinney Road** enters from the south-east (via Gates Pass Road or Ajo Way) past the Desert Museum to the
**Red Hills Visitor Center** (777 m) and the Desert Discovery Trail, then runs north-west to Sandario Road. The **Bajada
Scenic Loop** (graded dirt) leaves Kinney Road north on one-way **Hohokam Road** (Valley View Overlook, the Hugh Norris
trailhead, Sus picnic area) and returns west on **Golden Gate Road** past **Signal Hill** and its petroglyphs.
Eastward, Golden Gate Road continues as a trail to Ez-Kim-In-Zin and the Sendero Esperanza trailhead. The Hugh Norris
Trail follows the crest east to **Wasson Peak** (1,428 m), the Tucson Mountains' high point. A northern annex along
Picture Rocks Road holds the Cam-Boh picnic area, Panther and Safford peaks, and residential access points (A1–A10).

**East.** From the Old Spanish Trail entrance, the **Rincon Mountain Visitor Center** (942 m) opens onto the **Cactus
Forest Loop Drive**, 8 mi (12.7 km), paved and mostly one-way, through the densest saguaros: Mica View picnic area
(north), Loma Verde, Javelina picnic area (south), the Desert Ecology and Freeman Homestead trails. A web of flat
trails crosses the **Cactus Forest** between the Broadway, Speedway (Douglas Spring) and Wildhorse trailheads. East of
it the land rises into **wilderness**: the **Tanque Verde Ridge Trail** climbs from Javelina to Juniper Basin and
Tanque Verde Peak (2,148 m), and the Douglas Spring Trail passes Bridal Wreath Falls. Both lead to **Manning Camp**
(2,420 m, a 1905 cabin) and **Mica Mountain** (2,641 m, the park's high point) in pine forest. **Rincon Peak**
(2,585 m) guards the south-east corner. The **Arizona National Scenic Trail** crosses the district from Rincon Valley
over Mica Mountain to the north boundary (16.8 mi / 27 km). A southern tongue along Rincon Creek (Loma Alta
trailhead, Hope Camp) was added in the 1990s. Six permit-only backcountry campgrounds are the park's only lodging.

### Water

No permanent rivers. The **washes** (Javelina, Monument, Bajada, King Canyon, Rincon Creek) are dry sand paths most of
the year and run as brown torrents in monsoon storms; the Santa Cruz River and the Rillito cross Tucson dry. Water
shows up as **seasonal falls and pools** in the Rincons (Bridal Wreath and Ernie's falls, Devil's Bathtub),
**springs** (Douglas, Italian, Manning Camp) and ranchers' old **stock tanks and dams** (Garwood Dam, Three Tank).
Drinking water only at the visitor centres (carry 1 L per hour of hiking).

### Relief

From **664 m** on the TMD's western flats to **2,641 m** on **Mica Mountain**. The **Tucson Mountains** are a low,
jagged volcanic range, dark ridges rising ~650 m above the bajadas, with Wasson, Safford and Panther peaks as
landmarks. The **Rincons** are a big granite-and-gneiss dome: from the Cactus Forest foothills (~900 m) long ridges
(Tanque Verde Ridge) and canyons (Box, Chimenea, Madrona) climb to a forested summit plateau around Mica Mountain and
Spud Rock, with Rincon Peak a separate pyramid to the south-east. In the game: bajadas = long gentle banks studded
with saguaros; TMD ridges = low cliffs and scree; the Rincon climb = a staircase of biome bands (cactus → grass → oak
→ pine); Wasson Peak, Tanque Verde Peak, Mica Mountain and Rincon Peak are 3D peaks.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: desert scrub (660–1,200 m): saguaro, foothill palo verde, ocotillo, teddybear and
  chain-fruit cholla, prickly pear, fishhook barrel, creosote, brittlebush, mesquite and ironwood in washes ·
  grassland (1,200–1,400 m): sotol, beargrass, agave · oak woodland (1,400–1,800 m): Emory oak, alligator juniper,
  Mexican pinyon · ponderosa pine above ~1,800 m, Douglas-fir and white fir on Mica Mountain's north slopes.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#C9A27A` rocky bajada soil · `#8E5A45` dark volcanic Tucson Mountains ·
  `#B3AE9A` Rincon granite · `#5F7F4B` saguaro green · `#9DB36A` palo verde bark · `#E8D8A0` backlit cholla ·
  `#D9C27A` dry grass · `#E86F3C` sunset sky behind the silhouettes · `#8C6A4E` monsoon-wet wash sand.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): javelina (groups at dusk) · coyote · Gila monster (spring, monsoon) · western
  diamondback rattlesnake · Sonoran desert tortoise · Gambel's quail · cactus wren (Arizona's state bird) · Gila
  woodpecker and gilded flicker (they carve saguaro nest holes) · elf owl (in old woodpecker holes) · lesser long-nosed
  bat (saguaro nectar, May–June nights) · roadrunner · Coues white-tailed deer · black bear, coati and Mexican spotted
  owl (Rincon high country) · spadefoot toads (monsoon nights).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): saguaro (white flowers May–June, open at night; red fruit June–July; rare crested form)
  · ocotillo (red tips, March–April) · foothill palo verde (yellow, April–May) · Mexican gold poppy and lupine
  (Feb–March, wet years) · brittlebush (March) · fishhook barrel cactus · teddybear cholla · Emory oak · ponderosa pine.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter: mild days, rare snow on the saguaros, snow on the Rincons · spring: wildflowers, then
  cactus blooms · May–June: the hottest, driest weeks · monsoon (July–mid-September): afternoon storms, lightning,
  flash floods, a green flush · autumn: warm, clear, hiking season returns.
- **Night**: Tucson's glow to the west, darker skies in the Rincon backcountry; saguaro flowers opening for bats; owls
  in saguaro holes; toads after storms.
- **Sounds**: the cactus wren's rattle · Gambel's quail « chi-CA-go » · Gila woodpeckers' churrs · wind through cholla
  spines · coyotes · cicadas in June · thunder rolling off the Rincons · washes running after rain · the city's far hum.

## Moments

- **Saguaro bloom** (May–June): white flower crowns open at night for nectar bats and close by the next afternoon.
- **Saguaro fruit harvest** (June–July): red fruits split open on the arm tips; for the Tohono O'odham the harvest
  marks the new year, just before the rains.
- **Monsoon** (early July–mid-September): thunderheads over the Rincons, lightning behind the cactus forest, washes
  flooding, toads singing all night.
- **Sunset silhouettes**: the arms-up saguaro skyline against orange skies from Valley View Overlook, Signal Hill and
  Gates Pass.
- **Spring wildflowers** (February–April, after wet winters): poppies, lupines and brittlebush under the saguaros.
- **Snow on the saguaros**: a few winter mornings each decade; snow lingers on Mica Mountain.
- **Up the sky island**: one long day on the Tanque Verde Ridge Trail turns cactus into juniper, then oak.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a wide **1024 × 512**-tile map holding both districts, with Tucson reduced to a thin stylised
  strip (a few streets, the dry Santa Cruz) between them, or two linked vignettes (TMD ~384 × 384, RMD ~512 × 384).
  Near true scale (~1.5–2 m per tile): the Red Hills Visitor Center and the Desert Discovery Trail, Signal Hill, the
  Valley View Overlook, the Cactus Forest inside the 8 mi loop (squeezed ~3×). Compressed ~10×: the Hugh Norris crest
  to Wasson Peak and the Rincon wilderness, a ramp of biome bands from cactus to pines around Manning Camp.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the two visitor centres, the 1930s CCC stone ramadas (Sus, Signal Hill), Manning Camp's
  cabin. 3D landmarks: giant saguaros (each one different: arms, holes, a rare crested one), Signal Hill's petroglyph
  boulders, the Freeman Homestead walls, lime kilns, Wasson Peak, Tanque Verde Peak, Mica Mountain, Rincon Peak.
- **Activities**: a saguaro census (count arms, find the crested one, spot woodpecker holes and elf owls); photo spots
  (sunset silhouettes, the petroglyphs, a saguaro in bloom, the Rincons behind the cactus forest, snow on cactus); the
  field guide (javelina, Gila monster, nectar bats); the herbarium (a bloom calendar); a multi-day sky-island backpack
  to Manning Camp; ranger programmes and the Junior Ranger booklet at both visitor centres; vehicles: car (the two loop
  drives), bike (Cactus Forest Loop and trails), horse (most RMD trails); storm-watching from a ramada.
- **Passport stamps**: the official list has Tucson, Rincon Mtn. District, Tucson Mtn. District, Manning Cabin
  (8,000 ft, backcountry), a Mobile Visitor Center stamp and the Santa Cruz Valley National Heritage Area.
- **With friends / with the world**: a sunset gathering at Valley View Overlook; a « biggest saguaro » hunt; the night
  bloom watch with bats; a group backpack to Manning Camp; a bike ride round the Cactus Forest Loop; group photos with
  arms raised beside a saguaro.
- **Rules of the park**: never damage or carve a saguaro (a crime in Arizona); stay on trails (no off-trail hiking
  below 4,500 ft in the RMD); carry water and hike early in summer; mind rattlesnakes and cholla; keep away from
  javelina and never feed them; don't touch the petroglyphs; pets only on roads, picnic areas and two paved trails;
  backcountry camping only in the six campgrounds, with a permit.
- **Implementation challenges**: procedural saguaros (growth stages, arms, holes, night flowers, fruit) as instanced
  3D models · nectar bats and night blooms · a sky island's biome ramp · a city between two districts (a transit
  abstraction or two maps) · monsoon storms and running washes · spiny plants as obstacles (cholla « jumping » onto the
  player as a gag).
