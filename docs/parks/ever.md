# Everglades National Park — `EVER`

> Florida · established December 6, 1947 (authorized May 30, 1934) · 6,106.5 km² (1,508,938.57 acres) · 778,198 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 0–2.4 m · NPS region SER (Southeast)
>
> The largest subtropical wilderness in the United States: a shallow "River of Grass" tens of kilometres wide that slides
> south-west into mangrove rivers, the Ten Thousand Islands and the milky shallows of Florida Bay. In Hearthlight it should
> feel like crossing a flat, shining sea of sawgrass on boardwalks and by canoe, where every tree island hides a heron or an
> alligator, the sky (towering storm clouds, sunsets over Florida Bay) does the job mountains do elsewhere, and the season
> decides where the water — and therefore the wildlife — is.

## Reading the map

North is up and the park fills the southern tip of the Florida peninsula like a broad shield ~110 km across. Its **north edge** is only partly straight: from Shark Valley eastward it follows **US 41, the Tamiami Trail**, to the Krome Avenue / L-31N canal area west of Miami; west of Shark Valley the beige **Big Cypress National Preserve** pushes south into a rectangular notch (its corner at about 25.62 N, -81.13) and the boundary then steps north-west to **Everglades City** and Chokoloskee in the far north-west corner. The **east edge** is a stair-stepped line against Homestead's farmland ("Agricultural and rural development zones"); the Ernest F. Coe Visitor Center sits right on it. The **south** is Florida Bay, which the park owns almost entirely, down to the back shore of the Florida Keys, where US 1 draws the south-east limit from Key Largo to Lower Matecumbe. The **west** is the Gulf of Mexico coast, from the Ten Thousand Islands to the three capes of Cape Sable.

**Roads.** There are three separate road entries and no road between them. (1) The **Main Park Road (SR 9336)** leaves Florida City westward, reaches the Coe Visitor Center and the entrance station 11 mi from Homestead, and then runs 38 mi / 61 km to Flamingo. Its first leg goes due west across the pinelands of Long Pine Key: the **Royal Palm** spur (south, 4 mi from Coe) with the Anhinga and Gumbo Limbo trails; the Daniel Beard Center and the **Nike Missile Base HM-69** on a side road to the south; the **Long Pine Key** campground and lake (south, mile ~6); the **Pinelands** trail (north, mile 7); Pine Glades Lake (north) and the **Rock Reef Pass** boardwalk and "3 ft" sign. At mile 13 the **Pa-hay-okee Overlook** spur heads north onto the open River of Grass. The road then bends south-west and south through dwarf cypress and sawgrass past Sisal, Ficus and Sweet Bay ponds, the **Mahogany Hammock** spur (west, mile 20), **Paurotis Pond**, **Nine Mile Pond** (mile 27), the Noble Hammock and **Hells Bay** canoe launches, **West Lake** (mile 31), Coot Bay Pond, Mrazek Pond and the Snake Bight / Rowdy Bend / Christian Point trailheads, and ends at **Flamingo** (mile 38) on Florida Bay. The dashed **Old Ingraham Highway**, now a dirt hike-and-bike route closed to cars, cuts south-west from Royal Palm through the "Hole in the Donut" restoration area. (2) **Shark Valley** sits mid-way along US 41: from the visitor centre a 15-mi paved **tram road** loop drops straight south (the west road) 7 mi to the **Observation Tower** and wiggles back north (the east road) — no private cars. The Miccosukee Cultural Center and reserve lie just west; three airboat concessions (Everglades Safari Park, Gator Park, Coopertown) line US 41 to the east; Chekika, in the north-east corner, is closed. (3) **SR/CR 29** runs south from US 41 at Carnestown through **Everglades City** (on the Barron River) to **Chokoloskee** island; the **Marjory Stoneman Douglas Visitor Center (Gulf Coast)** stands on Chokoloskee Bay at 815 Oyster Bar Lane.

**Water routes do the rest.** From Everglades City the dashed **Wilderness Waterway** threads 99 mi south-east behind the outer islands — Lopez River, Sunday, Huston and Chevelier bays, Alligator and Plate Creek bays, Lostmans Five, Onion Key, Third/Second/First bays, Big Lostmans Bay, Rodgers River Bay, the Broad, Harney and Shark rivers, Tarpon Bay — into **Whitewater Bay** and through Coot Bay and the **Buttonwood Canal** to the Flamingo marina. Chickees (elevated platforms) and ground or beach campsites dot it: Crooked Creek, Lopez River, Watson Place, Darwins Place, Plate Creek, Lostmans Five, Willy Willy, Rodgers River, Camp Lonesome, Broad River, Harney River, Canepatch, Shark River, Oyster Bay, Joe River, South Joe River, Hells Bay, Lard Can, Pearl Bay. On the Gulf side, beach campsites sit on the outer keys (Tiger, Picnic, Jewell, Rabbit, Mormon, New Turkey, Turkey, Hog keys) and at Highland Beach, Graveyard Creek, Northwest Cape, Middle Cape and East Cape. Florida Bay's many keys are closed to landing except a few campsites (North Nest Key, Little Rabbit Key) and Bradley Key (day use).

**Water and relief organise everything.** The map is dead flat — the highest ground is 8 ft / 2.4 m — so what reads as landscape is water depth and habitat. Fresh water enters across US 41 from the Water Conservation Areas and moves south-west down the wide **Shark River Slough** (drawn as pale green with streaks of tree islands aligned with the flow) to the Gulf rivers, and south down the narrow **Taylor Slough** past Royal Palm into Florida Bay. Between the two sloughs lies the higher marl prairie and the pine island of Long Pine Key. A teal band of mangrove, 10–25 km wide, wraps the whole coast; west of Flamingo it opens onto the coastal prairie and the beaches of Cape Sable, the southernmost point of the US mainland. Florida Bay (the south-east third of the park) is a white-blue lagoon mostly 0–3 ft deep, cut by darker channels and dotted with mangrove keys.

### Water

Everything flows south-west, very slowly. Fresh water from the Water Conservation Areas north of US 41 spills under the Tamiami Trail bridges and creeps down the **Shark River Slough** — a sawgrass "river" up to ~30 km wide and a few decimetres deep, moving only tens to a few hundred metres a day — to the tidal **Shark, Harney, Broad and Lostmans rivers** and the Gulf. East of it the narrow **Taylor Slough** (under the Anhinga boardwalk) drains south to Florida Bay; in the east, the canal C-111 area feeds Joe Bay. Where fresh meets salt, the mangrove belt holds brackish lakes and bays: **West Lake**, **The Lungs**, **Long Lake**, **Cuthbert Lake**, **Seven Palm Lake**, **Whitewater Bay**, **Coot Bay**, Bear Lake and Mud Lake near Flamingo. Old 20th-century drainage and access canals (Buttonwood, Homestead, East Cape and Middle canals) cut the Flamingo–Cape Sable area. **Florida Bay** is a 0–1 m deep lagoon of seagrass meadows, mud banks and ~200 mangrove keys with almost no tide (it is sheltered by the Keys); the **Gulf coast** has twice-daily tides of roughly a metre that decide which creeks are passable (the waterway's "Nightmare"). Seasons move the water: the wet season (mid-May–Nov) floods the prairies ankle- to knee-deep; by the end of the dry season (Apr–May) only sloughs, ponds and alligator holes hold water and canoe trails like Nine Mile Pond and Mud Lake can become impassable. No falls, no springs, no ice.

### Relief

The lowest point is sea level and the highest natural ground only 8 ft / 2.4 m; the famous **Rock Reef Pass** sign on the Main Park Road reads "Elevation 3 ft". Relief comes in centimetres: the **Long Pine Key** pine island sits on a low ridge of pitted oolitic limestone (solution holes, pinnacle rock); **tree islands** (hammocks, bayheads) rise a few decimetres above the marsh, teardrop-shaped and aligned with the flow; **cypress domes** make green bumps whose tallest trees stand in the wettest centre; the Gulf coast has **shell mounds** raised by the Calusa and earlier peoples (Chokoloskee, Watson Place, Sandfly Island; Hamilton and Johnson mounds) and low beach ridges on Cape Sable. For the game: use only three or four height levels — water (−1), marsh & prairie (0), hammock / pineland / beach ridge (+1, a one-level walkable bank), shell mound (+2) — with no cliffs. The vertical accents are built things and plants: the Shark Valley tower, the raised Pa-hay-okee platform, the stilted Flamingo Lodge, chickees on posts, royal palms and slash pines as tall silhouettes, and thunderheads.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation** (by centimetres and by water): seagrass meadows (turtle grass) under Florida Bay → red mangroves on arching prop roots at the water's edge, black mangroves with pencil-like breathing roots, white mangrove and buttonwood behind → salty coastal prairie of succulents and grasses (Flamingo, Cape Sable) → sawgrass sloughs and spikerush with floating periphyton mats → marl prairie with dwarf "hat-rack" pond cypress → cypress domes → teardrop tree islands of gumbo limbo, live oak, mahogany, strangler fig, poisonwood and paurotis palm hung with bromeliads → pine rockland (South Florida slash pine over saw palmetto, silver palm and coontie) on the highest limestone.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#C8B46E` dry-season sawgrass gold · `#8FA65A` wet-season sawgrass green · `#D8CFAE` marl and periphyton cream · `#5E7D6A` clear, tea-tinted slough water · `#2E5B3C` hammock / tree-island green · `#4F7D4A` mangrove canopy · `#8A5A3C` red-mangrove prop roots · `#CBC3AE` pitted oolite limestone · `#A9D8CF` milky turquoise Florida Bay shallows · `#B9A984` low-tide mud flats · `#F2A65A` sunset glow over the bay.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **American alligator** (everywhere fresh: Anhinga Trail, the Shark Valley canal; basks on winter mornings) · **American crocodile** (brackish and salt water: Flamingo marina, West Lake, Florida Bay — the only place both crocodilians share) · **anhinga** (drying its wings on the Anhinga Trail rails; nests in winter) · **roseate spoonbill** (Snake Bight flats at low tide, Paurotis Pond, winter) · **wood stork** (Paurotis and Mrazek ponds, winter–spring) · **great blue heron**, including the all-white Florida Bay form · **white ibis**, great egret, tricolored heron, purple gallinule (marsh edges) · **snail kite** (Nine Mile Pond, Shark Valley; eats apple snails) · **West Indian manatee** (Flamingo marina and canals, Florida Bay) · **bottlenose dolphin** (Florida Bay, Ten Thousand Islands tours) · **Florida panther** (pinelands and hammocks; almost never seen — a legendary entry) · **river otter** (Shark Valley canal) · **Florida softshell turtle** and Florida gar (Anhinga Trail) · **Burmese python** (invasive; "report, don't touch") · the odd **American flamingo** (Snake Bight, rare) · loggerhead turtles nesting on Cape Sable beaches (summer).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): **sawgrass** (a razor-edged sedge up to ~3 m; gold in the dry season) · **royal palm** (Royal Palm) · **gumbo limbo**, the peeling red "tourist tree" (hammocks) · **West Indian mahogany** (Mahogany Hammock) · **paurotis palm** (Paurotis Pond) · **pond cypress**, bare and rusty in winter, green in summer (Pa-hay-okee road) · **red mangrove** and its floating propagules (all year) · **South Florida slash pine** and saw palmetto (Long Pine Key; fire-adapted) · **bromeliads and orchids** on hammock trees (spring–summer blooms) · **swamp lily** and yellow bladderwort flowers in the wet season.
- **Weather & seasons**: *Dry season* (Dec–mid-May) — clear skies and breezes, cool fronts that can bring 5–10 °C mornings, sawgrass turning gold, water shrinking into sloughs and gator holes, smoky late-season fires. *Wet season* (mid-May–Nov) — steamy mornings, cumulus towers by noon and violent thunderstorms most afternoons, sheets of water spreading over the prairie, clouds of mosquitoes. *Storms* — hurricanes (Andrew 1992, Wilma 2005, Irma 2017, Ian 2022) flatten hammocks and wreck coastal buildings; the Gulf Coast VC was lost twice. No snow, ever.
- **Night**: Flamingo and Long Pine Key have some of the darkest skies in South Florida (the Milky Way over Florida Bay; ranger full-moon and meteor-shower bike rides); the Miami glow sits on the north-east horizon. Flashlights pick out alligator eyeshine along the Anhinga boardwalk; frogs roar in wet-season nights; far-off storms flicker silently.
- **Sounds**: dry sawgrass rustling in the wind, alligator bellows (spring), pig-frog grunts and tree-frog choruses, croaking ibis, clicking anhingas, the whine of mosquitoes, rain hammering on the marsh and thunder rolling across it, mangrove water lapping and mullet jumping, the roar of an airboat fan on US 41, tram narration at Shark Valley, halyards and outboards at the Flamingo marina.

## Moments

- **The dry-season gathering (Dec–Apr, best Jan–Mar mornings)** — as the marsh dries, fish, alligators, turtles and wading birds crowd into Taylor Slough right under the Anhinga Trail boardwalk.
- **Rookery season (winter–spring)** — wood storks, egrets and roseate spoonbills nest at Paurotis Pond; the pond is closed while they nest and viewed from the road.
- **Low tide at Flamingo** — the mud flats in front of the Guy Bradley VC and in Snake Bight fill with feeding birds; crocodiles bask on the marina banks on sunny mornings.
- **The rains return (late May–June)** — the first big storms of the wet season; within weeks the prairie floods, the grass greens up and the mosquitoes explode.
- **The afternoon storm (Jun–Sep, almost daily)** — towers build by noon over the sawgrass, lightning and downpours at 2–4 pm, washed-clean sunsets.
- **Sunset over Florida Bay** from the Flamingo campground and marina; **sunrise at Pa-hay-okee** over mist on the River of Grass.
- **Alligator courtship (Apr–May)** — bellowing choruses; hatchlings chirp at nests in late summer (Aug–Sep).
- **Fire in the pines** — prescribed burns in the Long Pine Key pinelands in the dry season, lightning fires at the start of the wet season.
- **Nike Site Festival (early March; March 7 in 2026)** — the Cold War missile base opens for a day of classic cars, K-9 demos and veterans' talks.
- **Tram tour at Shark Valley (daily, 2 h)** — the ride to the tower and the view of up to 20 mi of sawgrass.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: **1536 × 1280 tiles** (~1.5 m per tile at the hotspots). The three districts, unconnected by road in reality, are joined across the wilderness by water. Layout, north up: US 41 across the top with **Shark Valley** at x≈900 and its tram loop compressed from 15 mi to ~350 tiles north–south (the tower at the south end); **Everglades City / Gulf Coast** in the north-west corner with a maze of 40–60 mangrove islets for the Ten Thousand Islands; the **Main Park Road** entering at the east edge (Coe VC, x≈1350, y≈750), running west across Long Pine Key and then south-west to **Flamingo** (x≈700, y≈1150) — 38 mi compressed to ~1,000 tiles of road, with each stop's spur and boardwalk kept at near true size (Royal Palm ≈ 330 × 270 tiles; Anhinga loop ≈ 270 tiles of boardwalk; Mahogany Hammock ≈ 120-tile loop). The **Wilderness Waterway** becomes ~1,200 tiles of channels and bays down the west coast with 6–8 chickees (≈1:80); **Florida Bay** a 400 × 250-tile sheet of shallows with 20–30 keys and the Keys as a horizon strip. Cut: most of Florida Bay's keys, the farmland east of the park, the interior of Big Cypress (a beige border), Chekika.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: **Coe VC** (arrival, film room, junior-ranger desk), **Royal Palm** (the busiest boardwalk), **Flamingo** (the social hub: the pink Mission 66 Guy Bradley VC, marina, container lodge on stilts, eco-tents, campground on the bay), **Shark Valley** (tram depot, bike rental, tower), **Gulf Coast** (new VC, boat-tour dock). 3D landmarks: the Shark Valley Observation Tower (concrete spiral ramp to a round deck), the Pa-hay-okee platform, the Rock Reef Pass sign, the Flamingo Lodge on stilts, the Nike missile barns with a Hercules missile, chickees, airboats, royal palms. Interiors: Coe VC, Guy Bradley VC, the Nike missile assembly building, the Flamingo marina store and lodge restaurant.
- **Activities**:
  - *Hiking*: the five boardwalks as bite-size walks; Snake Bight at high tide; the long Coastal Prairie walk to camp at Clubhouse Beach; a ranger-led "wet walk" knee-deep into a cypress dome.
  - *Photo spots*: an anhinga spreading its wings on the Anhinga rail; the gator under the Anhinga bridge; Pa-hay-okee at sunrise; the tower deck panorama (and group photo); the Rock Reef Pass "3 ft" sign; the champion mahogany; Flamingo sunset; a crocodile on the marina bank (from a distance); the missile on its launcher; the white beach of East Cape.
  - *Wildlife & ranger programmes*: a dry-season bird count on the Anhinga Trail, a low-tide spoonbill hunt at Snake Bight, manatee nostrils at the marina, a night eyeshine walk, Nike missile tours, full-moon bike rides.
  - *Junior Ranger*: the real book is shared by Everglades, Biscayne and Big Cypress (English, Spanish, Haitian Creole). Game tasks: find all nine habitats of the map legend; read a water gauge in dry vs wet season; drop a leaf in the slough and follow the River of Grass; pass the boater-education quiz; learn why Guy Bradley, a warden who protected plume birds, gave his name to the Flamingo VC.
  - *Snorkelling*: not an Everglades activity — swimming is discouraged (alligators, crocodiles, murky tannic water). Offer instead a "look-down" view from boardwalks, canoe and tour boat: gar, bass, turtles and periphyton in clear slough water.
  - *Boating*: Flamingo tour boats (the 90-min Whitewater Bay backcountry tour via the Buttonwood Canal; the Florida Bay tour Nov–Apr) and the Ten Thousand Islands pontoon tour; rent a skiff or a **houseboat** (a moving campsite) after a boater-education mini-quiz; motor off and pole across the Snake Bight pole/troll zone; idle speed in canals. An **airboat** ride on US 41 as a loud novelty vehicle that scatters birds.
  - *Paddling*: canoe trails as pole-marked mazes — Nine Mile Pond (follow numbered white poles; periphyton slows you), Noble Hammock (tight mangrove tunnels), Hells Bay (160 poles to the chickees), West Lake (open lakes, wind, crocodiles), the Sandfly Island loop (tide-dependent). The **Wilderness Waterway** is the park's epic: 99 mi played as a one- to two-hour expedition with nightly chickee camps and a tide table.
  - *Vehicles & camping*: car on the Main Park Road, tram and bikes at Shark Valley, bikes on the Long Pine Key and Snake Bight trails, seasonal Homestead trolley (shuttle), boats, houseboats, canoes and kayaks; campgrounds at Long Pine Key (dry season) and Flamingo, plus wilderness beach, ground and chickee sites by permit.
- **Passport stamps**: Homestead (Ernest F. Coe VC) · Royal Palm · Flamingo (Guy Bradley VC) · Gulf Coast (Marjory Stoneman Douglas VC, Everglades City) · Shark Valley · Nike Missile Base (HM-69) · and three stamps at the US 41 airboat concessions: Coopertown, Everglades Safari Park, Gator Park.
- **With friends / with the world**: the **Shark Valley tram** carries a whole party of eight to the tower; a canoe convoy on Nine Mile Pond where each canoe must find the next pole; a chickee overnight for up to four with storms passing; a Wilderness Waterway relay (one team starts at Everglades City, one at Flamingo, they meet at Watson Place); a spotting contest on the Anhinga boardwalk. In the shared world strangers naturally meet on the **Anhinga Trail boardwalk**, on the **tower deck** and at the **Flamingo marina at sunset** — the group photo spots.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace as gameplay): keep 15 ft / 5 m from all wildlife (alligators and crocodiles get a visible comfort ring — step inside and they slide away or hiss); never feed anything; stay on boardwalks and trails; no pets on trails; no swimming; no drones; motors off in pole/troll and no-motor zones, idle speed in canals, never scar the seagrass; stay out of rookeries and closed keys; pack out all trash; no collecting plants or shells; cover your car against the black vultures that peck rubber at Royal Palm (the park lends tarps); watch for poisonwood; carry water and repellent; wilderness camping only with a permit.
- **Implementation challenges**: **seasonal water levels** that redraw the map (flooded vs dry prairie, canoe trails that close in late dry season); **tides and mud flats** in Florida Bay and on the Gulf; near-zero relief (height levels barely used: hammocks as +1 banks, pinnacle limestone as texture); animated sheet flow through grass; **mangrove tunnels** under a closed canopy (needs canopy fade with the oblique camera); boardwalks over water; wildlife AI with comfort radii and crocodilian behaviour; airboats; mosquitoes as a weather-like pest system; prescribed fire; hurricanes; stitching three disconnected entrances into one map; a very large, mostly empty extent.
