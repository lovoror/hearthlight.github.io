# Hot Springs National Park — `HOSP`

> Arkansas · established March 4, 1921 (Hot Springs Reservation since April 20, 1832 — the oldest area in the National Park System) · 22.5 km² (5,554 acres) · 2,494,611 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 126–428 m · NPS region MWR (Midwest; DOI Region 4, Mississippi Basin)
>
> A national park wrapped around a spa town: a row of grand 1892–1923 bathhouses on Central Avenue fed by 143 °F spring water that fell as rain 4,400 years ago, with forested Ouachita ridges, scenic drives and an observation tower rising right behind them. In Hearthlight it should feel like a warm, steamy old resort town where you fill a jug at a hot fountain, take a bath, then climb a switchback road to a pagoda and a tower above the rooftops.

## Reading the map

North is up. The park is not a block but a **ring of forested ridges around downtown Hot Springs**: the city fills the valleys in the middle and south (grey streets, "CITY OF HOT SPRINGS"), and the green park land drapes over the mountains around it like a horseshoe open to the south. The ridges run south-west to north-east, typical of the folded Ouachitas: in the east **Hot Springs Mountain** and **North Mountain** (with **Indian Mountain** beyond Gulpha Gorge, no public access); in the west **West Mountain** and, beyond it, **Music Mountain** (the park's high point, 1,405 ft / 428 m, at the far south-west); in the north-west **Sugarloaf Mountain** with Balanced Rock; and a long northern arm running up to the park's north edge (Fordyce Peaks). The **Sunset Trail** follows this ring for ~10 miles through the most remote parts.

The heart is the narrow notch between West Mountain and Hot Springs Mountain where Hot Springs Creek runs south (now in a tunnel under the street). Here **Central Avenue** (SR 7) runs north–south, and **Bathhouse Row** lines its east side at the foot of Hot Springs Mountain: from south (Reserve Street) to north (Fountain Street) the Park Administration Building, Lamar, Buckstaff, Ozark, Quapaw, **Fordyce** (the visitor centre and museum), Maurice, Hale and Superior, with the grand **Arlington Hotel** (private) closing the north end. Behind the bathhouses, the brick **Grand Promenade** runs along the hillside above the springs; the springs themselves are capped in green boxes on the slope, except the open **Display Springs** behind the Maurice, the **Maurice Historic Spring Area**, and the steaming **Hot Water Cascade** on Arlington Lawn. Thermal-water jug fountains stand on Reserve Street and along the row. The former Army-Navy General Hospital looms on the hill south-east of the row.

Two scenic drives climb out of town: **Hot Springs Mountain Drive** (one-way switchbacks up from Fountain Street) to the Pagoda lookout, a picnic pavilion and the **Hot Springs Mountain Tower** on the summit, then on around the **North Mountain Loop** (Goat Rock overlook) and back down; **West Mountain Drive** from Whittington and Prospect avenues up to the **Summit Loop** with three overlooks. East of North Mountain, Gorge Road runs up the valley of **Gulpha Creek** past **Gulpha Gorge Campground** and picnic area. North-west of downtown, Whittington Avenue runs up a valley to **Whittington Park** (spring, park maintenance area) and continues as Blacksnake Road into the remote west. Relief governs everything: the ridges are steep, 150–300 m above the town, and the town sits at ~180–200 m in the creek valleys.

### Water

- **Thermal springs**: 47 active springs emerge along a fault on the lower western slope of Hot Springs Mountain, over ~2.8 acres along Bathhouse Row and the Grand Promenade, at an average **143 °F (62 °C)**; together they yield about **700,000 gallons (2.6 million litres) a day**. The water fell as rain on the Zigzag Mountains (the recharge zone north-east of the park) some **4,400 years** ago, sank 4,500–7,500 ft (1.4–2.3 km) along folded and faulted rock, heated up and rose quickly back to the surface. 27 springs are captured in sealed green boxes and piped to a reservoir under the Administration Building, pumped to the bathhouses and hilltop tanks, and cooled by heat exchangers for bathing (98–100 °F).
- **Where to meet the water**: open springs (Display Springs, Hot Water Cascade, Maurice Historic Spring Area, the Promenade seep), hot jug fountains (Reserve Street, Administration Building, between Hale and Maurice, the Noble Fountain), the steaming Fordyce fountains, and two cold-spring fountains (Happy Hollow, Whittington). Calcium carbonate deposits grey, spongy **tufa** where the water cools.
- **Creeks**: Hot Springs Creek runs south under Central Avenue in an 1880s tunnel; Gulpha Creek flows south through Gulpha Gorge; small ponds (Ricks Pond) and lakes (Dillon, Bethel, Sanderson) lie just outside the north boundary; Lake Hamilton and Lake Ouachita are south and west of town.
- No natural lakes or falls in the park.

### Relief

Lowest point 126 m (Bull Bayou, 415 ft); highest 428 m (Music Mountain, 1,405 ft). The park sits in the **Zigzag Mountains** of the Ouachitas: rock layers of sandstone, shale, chert and hard white **novaculite** folded ~300 million years ago into tight SW–NE ridges with steep sides and rocky crests, separated by narrow creek valleys where the town grew. Summits: Music Mountain 428 m, Sugarloaf 369 m, West Mountain (359 m high point, 335 m summit overlook), North Mountain 341 m, Hot Springs Mountain ~317 m (tower base). The town sits at ~180–200 m. In the game: 3–5 levels of stepped ridges with cliffy novaculite crests (painted white-grey faces), switchback roads as walkable banks and stairs (Hot Springs Mountain Drive, the Stevens Balustrade), the row's backyard as a steep terraced hillside of tufa with steaming pools; the tower and pagoda as 3D landmarks on the summit.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: town and promenade — magnolias, oaks and lawns, mosses and blue-green algae around the hot pools; lower slopes and hollows — oak, hickory, sweetgum, beech along Gulpha Creek; upper slopes and ridges — oak–hickory with **shortleaf pine** on dry exposed crests, rocky novaculite outcrops with lichens and glade flowers; understory of flowering dogwood, redbud, sparkleberry; rare Ozark chinquapin and Graves spleenwort.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#E9E4DA` pale novaculite · `#B7ADA0` grey tufa · `#8D6B4F` weathered sandstone · `#3D5E3A` oak–pine forest · `#7FA35A` spring leaves · `#C8A233` / `#B5532B` November oaks and sweetgums · `#6FB3B0` steaming hot-spring pools with `#5E8C4A` algae · `#C9B79C` bathhouse stucco and brick (`#9A4A35`) · `#E0D8C8` steam.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): white-tailed deer (ridges and campground at dawn), groundhog (lawns), eastern chipmunk, gray and fox squirrels, nine-banded armadillo (night, leaf litter), gray fox, raccoon, opossum, coyote, wild turkey (ridges), pileated woodpecker, turkey and black vultures (soaring over the ridges), Carolina wren, summer tanager, copperhead (common along trails — one of five venomous snakes), three-toed box turtle, five-lined skink, and 50+ mammal, 100+ bird and 70+ reptile and amphibian species overall.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): flowering dogwood (late March–April), eastern redbud (March), shortleaf pine (all year), wild azalea (April), Ozark chinquapin (rare, June catkins), fire pink and spiderwort (spring), black-eyed Susan (summer), goldenrod (fall), mosses and blue-green algae in the thermal pools (all year).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — dogwoods and redbuds, thunderstorms and rain; summer — hot and very humid, cicadas, afternoon storms; fall — long mild season, oaks and sweetgums turning into November; winter — mostly mild with cold snaps, occasional ice storms, and **steam clouds** billowing from the springs and fountains on cold mornings.
- **Night**: a lit city; stars from the ridges only; the tower lit; whip-poor-wills and katydids in summer; fireflies in the hollows (June); the Superior brewery and the Arlington glowing on Central Avenue.
- **Sounds**: trickling hot water and hissing fountains, the clink of jugs being filled, the tram-like hum of Central Avenue traffic, footsteps on the brick promenade, cicadas and katydids, woodpecker drumming, the wind on the ridge at the tower, a church bell downtown.

## Moments

- **Steam mornings**: on cold winter mornings the Hot Water Cascade, Display Springs and jug fountains smoke with steam.
- **Take the waters**: a traditional bath at the Buckstaff (since 1912) or the thermal pools of the Quapaw; free hot water at the jug fountains any time.
- **The Tower**: the Hot Springs Mountain Tower's open-air deck (1,256 ft ASL) looks over ~140 square miles of Ouachitas; sunrise from Goat Rock, sunset from the West Mountain summit.
- **Dogwood and redbud bloom** in late March–April; fall colour peaks late October–November.
- **Ranger-led walks** on Bathhouse Row and the Grand Promenade; self-guided tours of the Fordyce's three floors (music room, gymnasium, bath halls).
- **Total solar eclipse of April 8, 2024** passed over the park (a special passport stamp remembers it).
- **Iron Ranger Challenge**: hike, bike, swim or paddle 100 miles on Arkansas public lands.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a compact map **768 × 576 tiles** (~1.15 × 0.86 km at 1.5 m/tile), north up, with downtown and Bathhouse Row in the lower middle at **near true scale** (the ~450 m row, the Grand Promenade, Arlington Lawn, a few blocks of Central Avenue) and the ring of ridges compressed ~6–8× around it: Hot Springs Mountain with the tower and North Mountain to the east, Gulpha Gorge beyond, West Mountain to the west, Sugarloaf and Music Mountain as the far western rim, the northern arm cut short.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Bathhouse Row (main hub), the tower summit, Gulpha Gorge campground. 3D landmarks: the eight bathhouses (Spanish Colonial, Neoclassical and Mission facades: the Fordyce's stained glass, the Quapaw's tiled dome, the Ozark's sculpted creatures), the Arlington Hotel's twin towers, the Hot Springs Mountain Tower (216 ft), the 1911 pagoda, stone shelters from the 1920s, the green spring boxes, jug fountains with people filling bottles. Interiors: the Fordyce (bath halls, music room, gymnasium, bowling alley), the Buckstaff baths, the Quapaw pools, the Superior brewery, the Lamar store, the tower's upper deck.
- **Activities**: bathing as a rest mechanic (restores the hero); filling a jug at the fountains as a collectible ("thermal water"); photo spots — the row from West Mountain's lower overlook, steam at the Hot Water Cascade, the Fordyce's stained-glass ceiling, the tower view, Balanced Rock, dogwoods on the promenade; hiking the ring (Sunset Trail as the long quest), switchback drives by car; camping at Gulpha Gorge; birding; junior ranger (booklet at the Fordyce); winter "steam hunt".
- **Passport stamps**: the 2025 list gives "Hot Springs" and "Total Solar Eclipse April 8 2024", stamped at the Fordyce Bathhouse Visitor Center. In the game: at the Fordyce front desk (with the eclipse stamp as an event reward).
- **With friends / with the world**: the party books a bath together (the Quapaw pools), a jug-filling queue at the fountains where strangers chat, a "promenade stroll" at dusk, group photo on the Stevens Balustrade stairs, a race up the Tufa Terrace to the tower; open-world gathering on Arlington Lawn by the cascade.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): don't touch or bathe in the open springs (too hot at the source, and fragile tufa); take water only from fountains, never sell it; stay on trails (copperheads!); don't carve the novaculite or remove rocks; respect the private hotels and businesses on the row; pack out trash; quiet in the bath halls.
- **Implementation challenges**: a real town inside the park (streets, cars, private buildings, the tunnelled creek); steam and hot water (animated vapour, warm pools); dense detailed historic facades and interiors; steep switchback roads; many small interiors (eight bathhouses); a hidden-water story (rain → deep underground → hot springs) worth visualising.
