# Joshua Tree National Park — `JOTR`

> California · established October 31, 1994 · 3,217.9 km² (795,156 acres) · 2,932,644 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 163–1,772 m · NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10, California–Great Basin)
>
> Two deserts meet here: the high Mojave in the west, a plateau of shaggy Joshua trees and piles of giant rounded
> granite boulders, and the lower Colorado Desert in the east, a hot basin of creosote, cholla and ocotillo. In
> Hearthlight it should feel like a sunny, surreal playground: scrambling over boulder castles among Seussian trees
> by day, then lying on warm rock under one of the darkest skies in Southern California.

## Reading the map

The park is a long east–west block, wider than tall, shaped (says the park's own map description) like a thumbs-up
turned on its side and pointing left. Its **north edge** runs just south of CA 62 and the desert towns strung along
it: Yucca Valley, Joshua Tree, Twentynine Palms. Its **south-west edge** follows the crest of the **Little San
Bernardino Mountains**, which drop about a mile to the Coachella Valley, Palm Springs and the San Andreas Fault.
I-10 runs along the south side. A dotted band crossing the map from north-west to south-east marks the **Transition
Zone**. North-west of it lies the **Mojave Desert** (above ~900 m: Joshua trees, boulder piles, every campground but
Cottonwood). South-east of it lies the **Colorado Desert**: the vast, empty **Pinto Basin**, ringed by the Pinto (north),
Hexie (west), Cottonwood (south), Eagle (south-east) and Coxcomb (far east) mountains.

The road network is a **T**. **Park Boulevard** is the top bar: it enters at the **West Entrance** (5 mi south of the
town of Joshua Tree), runs south-east 8.8 mi (14.2 km) past Quail Springs to the **Hidden Valley** cluster
(Intersection Rock, Hidden Valley, the Barker Dam and Keys Ranch spurs), 1.7 mi (2.7 km) more to **Cap Rock**, where
**Keys View Road** branches 5.5 mi (8.9 km) south-west to the crest. Then it curves east for 10.3 mi (16.6 km) through
Lost Horse and Queen valleys (Ryan Mountain, Sheep Pass, the Geology Tour Road, Jumbo Rocks, Skull Rock, Split Rock)
to the **Pinto Y**, and turns north 4.6 mi (7.4 km) to the **North Entrance** and Twentynine Palms. Its mile markers
count down from 25 at the West Entrance to 0 at the North Entrance. **Pinto Basin Road** is the stem: from the Pinto
Y (its mile 0) it passes Belle and White Tank (Arch Rock), drops through Wilson Canyon to the **Cholla Cactus
Garden** (~mile 10) and the Ocotillo Patch, crosses the Pinto Basin south-east over Fried Liver, Porcupine and
Smoke Tree washes, then climbs to **Cottonwood** (29.7 mi / 47.8 km from the Y) and reaches I-10 about 7 mi further
at the South Entrance.

Everything busy sits in the north-west quadrant, within 5 km of Park Boulevard. Short dead-end roads reach it from
the northern towns: **Black Rock Canyon** (Yucca Valley, the park's west corner, Joshua tree forest and a nature
center), **Indian Cove** (off CA 62, a campground tucked into the north face of the **Wonderland of Rocks**, the
boulder maze north of Barker Dam) and **Fortynine Palms** (a trail over a ridge to a palm oasis). Two visitor centres
are in the towns, outside the park: the **Joshua Tree Visitor Center** (town of Joshua Tree) and the **Oasis Visitor
Center** at the **Oasis of Mara**, park headquarters in Twentynine Palms (named « Joshua Tree National Park Visitor
Center » on the 2023 map). The **Cottonwood Visitor Center** is the only one deep inside, at the south gate.

Dirt roads (grey) and 4WD tracks (dashed) fill in: the one-way **Queen Valley** and Big Horn Pass roads, the
**Geology Tour Road** south from Park Boulevard into Pleasant Valley (4WD beyond its first miles), **Covington Flats**
and Eureka Peak in the west (from La Contenta Road), **Berdoo Canyon** down to the Coachella Valley, **Pinkham** and
**Thermal** canyons west of Cottonwood, and **Old Dale** and **Black Eagle Mine** roads into the eastern Pinto Basin.
The **California Riding and Hiking Trail** crosses the whole north-west from Black Rock to the North Entrance, and the
**Boy Scout Trail** links Park Boulevard to Indian Cove. The east third (Pinto Basin, Eagle and Coxcomb mountains) is
roadless wilderness; the 2023 map crops it and shows it only in the small wilderness inset.

### Water

There are no rivers or lakes. Water shows up as **five fan palm oases** where faults push groundwater to the surface
(the **Oasis of Mara** in Twentynine Palms, **Fortynine Palms**, **Cottonwood Spring**, **Lost Palms Oasis** and
Munsen Canyon), as small **rancher-built tanks** that fill after storms (**Barker Dam**, White Tank, Twin Tanks,
Live Oak and Stirrup tanks), and as **tinajas**, rain pockets in the granite of the Wonderland of Rocks. Rain comes
in winter storms and in summer monsoon cloudbursts. Then the dry **washes** (Fried Liver, Porcupine and Smoke Tree
washes in the Pinto Basin) run as sudden flash floods, and the air fills with the resin smell of wet creosote. Far
below Keys View shines the **Salton Sea** (−70 m), outside the park. In game terms: water is a resource (carry 4 L
per person per day), and the oases are the only green.

### Relief

From **163 m** at the south-east corner by the Coxcomb Mountains to **1,772 m** on **Quail Mountain**. The west is a
high **plateau of sandy valleys** at 1,100–1,400 m (Lost Horse, Queen, Pleasant valleys, Covington Flats), studded
with **inselbergs**: piles of rounded monzogranite boulders, domes, fins and arches up to ~100 m high (the Wonderland
of Rocks, a maze of ~30 km² north of Barker Dam; Jumbo Rocks; Hidden Valley; White Tank). Its south-west rim is the
crest of the **Little San Bernardino Mountains** (Keys View 1,581 m, Eureka Peak 1,682 m), which falls ~1,300 m in
one escarpment to the Coachella Valley and the San Andreas Fault. Eastward the land steps down through Wilson Canyon to
the **Pinto Basin** (~350–600 m), a broad sandy plain with a small dune field, framed by the Pinto, Hexie, Cottonwood,
Eagle (Eagle Mountain 1,631 m) and Coxcomb ranges (Aqua Peak 1,346 m). In the game: the valleys are flat tiles, the
rock piles are **3D boulder sets to climb** (not tile cliffs), the escarpment is one tall cliff line seen from Keys
View, the Pinto Basin a flat with washes as shallow channels; Ryan, Queen and Quail mountains are 3D peaks.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: Colorado Desert below ~900 m: creosote bush, burrobush, teddybear cholla gardens,
  ocotillo, smoke tree, blue palo verde and ironwood in the washes, chuparosa · Transition Zone (~900–1,100 m): both
  worlds mixed, pencil cholla, Mojave yucca · Mojave above ~1,100 m: Joshua tree woodland, blackbrush, Mojave yucca,
  Parry's nolina, cactus among the boulders · high ridges (1,400–1,770 m): single-leaf pinyon, California juniper,
  scrub oak (Live Oak's big oak), the largest Joshua trees at Covington Flats · oases: California fan palms,
  Fremont cottonwoods.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#E8D9B5` pale sandy valley floor · `#D2B48C` weathered monzogranite boulders ·
  `#B89A78` shadowed granite · `#8C7A6B` dark Pinto gneiss · `#5E6B45` Joshua tree leaf clusters · `#9C8B6E` Joshua
  bark · `#A7A58A` creosote grey-green · `#F2E6C8` backlit cholla spines · `#3F3A3A` Malapai basalt · `#6E8FA8` the
  hazy Coachella Valley and Salton Sea seen from Keys View.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): desert bighorn sheep (Wonderland of Rocks, Barker Dam, Indian Cove) · coyote
  (everywhere, dawn and dusk) · black-tailed jackrabbit · white-tailed antelope squirrel (hot days) · Merriam's
  kangaroo rat (night) · desert tortoise (spring, washes and flats; threatened) · chuckwalla (on rocks, puffs up in
  crevices) · desert iguana (creosote flats) · Mojave rattlesnake and sidewinder (warm nights) · greater roadrunner
  · Gambel's quail (coveys at the oases) · cactus wren (nests in cholla) · Scott's oriole (nests in Joshua trees,
  spring) · common raven · desert tarantula (males wander in autumn) · the tiny yucca moth, the Joshua tree's only
  pollinator.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): Joshua tree (creamy flower clusters Feb–April, only in years with good rain and a winter
  freeze) · Mojave yucca · teddybear cholla (yellow-green flowers in spring, glowing spines at dawn) · ocotillo (red
  flame tips March–April) · creosote bush (yellow flowers, the smell of rain) · California fan palm (the oases) ·
  beavertail cactus (magenta, spring) · desert sand verbena and dune primrose (Pinto Basin carpets after wet winters,
  Feb–March) · chuparosa (red, loved by hummingbirds, winter–spring) · Parry's nolina (tall flower stalks, May).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter: crisp blue days, freezing nights, an occasional snowfall frosting the Joshua trees ·
  spring: the busiest season, blooms climbing from the low Colorado Desert (Feb) to the high Mojave (April–May),
  wind · summer: fierce heat, empty trails, monsoon towers and flash floods (July–Sept) · autumn: warm days, cool
  nights, climbing season opens, tarantulas on the roads.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park: the Milky Way arching over boulder silhouettes, four designated
  stargazing lots (Quail Springs, Hidden Valley, Cap Rock, Ryan Mountain), the darkest skies at Cottonwood and along
  Pinto Basin Road; kangaroo rats hopping in headlights, owls, scorpions; red lights only. An annual Night Sky
  Festival.
- **Sounds**: wind through Joshua tree leaves and around boulders · ravens' clicks and croaks · the cactus wren's
  rattling « car-engine » song · Gambel's quail calling at the oases · coyote choruses at dusk · climbers' calls
  (« on belay! ») echoing off the rock · crunching decomposed granite underfoot · rattling palm fronds · near silence
  on the Pinto Basin at night · distant thunder and the rush of a flash flood.

## Moments

- **Sunset at Keys View**: the sun sinks behind San Jacinto, the Coachella Valley fills with violet haze and the
  Salton Sea glints; everyone lines the low wall.
- **Sunrise at the Cholla Cactus Garden**: the low sun backlights thousands of cholla spines into a golden glow.
- **Joshua tree bloom** (Feb–April, only in some years, after good rain and a winter freeze): creamy clusters on
  the branch tips, yucca moths at work.
- **Spring wildflowers** (Feb–May): carpets of sand verbena and primrose in the Pinto Basin after a wet winter, then
  ocotillo, cactus and Mojave blooms climbing to the high valleys.
- **The Milky Way over the rocks** (moonless nights, best April–October): on the designated stargazing lots, with the
  annual Night Sky Festival.
- **Snow on the Joshua trees**: a few winter storms each decade dust the boulders and trees white for a morning.
- **Barker Dam fills**: after winter storms the reservoir mirrors the rocks and bighorn sheep come down to drink.
- **Monsoon cloudburst** (July–September): thunderheads build over the ranges, a wash turns into a brown river for an
  hour, and the whole desert smells of creosote.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a **1024 × 768**-tile map of the visited park, Black Rock to Cottonwood (~60 × 45 km).
  Near true scale (~1.5–2 m per tile): the Hidden Valley–Intersection Rock–Barker Dam cluster (~2 × 1.5 km, squeezed
  a little), Jumbo Rocks–Skull Rock–Split Rock (~1.5 km), Arch Rock, the Cholla Cactus Garden loop (400 m), Keys View,
  the Oasis of Mara and Cottonwood Spring (~500 m each). Compressed ~8–15×: Park Boulevard's 25 mi (→ ~900 tiles, every
  pullout kept), Keys View Road, Pinto Basin Road's 30 mi (→ ~500 tiles). Reduced to a backdrop: the eastern Pinto
  Basin, the Eagle and Coxcomb mountains, and the Coachella Valley seen from Keys View (a painted distance with the
  Salton Sea). The CA 62 towns become a thin gateway strip along the north edge.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Intersection Rock and Hidden Valley (the climbers' crossroads), the Oasis Visitor Center
  under the Mara palms, the Joshua Tree Visitor Center in town, Jumbo Rocks campground, Cottonwood. 3D landmarks:
  Skull Rock, Arch Rock, Split Rock, Cap Rock, Heart Rock, Intersection Rock, the Wonderland of Rocks maze, Barker
  Dam's wall, Keys Ranch (house, school, rusting cars and machines), the Lost Horse and Wall Street mills, Ryan and
  Queen mountains. Interiors: Keys Ranch house (guided tour), the visitor centres, the Black Rock Nature Center.
- **Activities**: boulder scrambling and a light climbing mini-game (roped routes on the domes, bouldering problems
  in Hidden Valley; the park counts thousands of routes); hiking; photo spots (Skull Rock, Arch Rock under the Milky
  Way, the Cholla Garden at dawn, Keys View at sunset, Heart Rock, Cap Rock, Barker Dam's reflection, a lone Joshua tree
  against the sky); the field guide (a tortoise in spring, bighorn at water, tarantulas in autumn); the herbarium
  (log Joshua trees in bloom, spot the yucca moth); stargazing with a star chart on the four lots; ranger tours of Keys
  Ranch and night programmes; Junior Ranger (booklet for ages 5–13, plus the Junior Ranger Night Explorer badge);
  camping in nine campgrounds (bring water); vehicles: car, bike (Geology Tour and Queen Valley dirt roads), horse
  (California Riding and Hiking Trail, Black Rock and Ryan horse camps), a 4WD for Berdoo and Pinkham canyons; no
  shuttle.
- **Passport stamps**: the official list has Twentynine Palms (Oasis Visitor Center), Oasis of Mara, Joshua Tree
  (Joshua Tree Visitor Center), JTNPA-West, Black Rock Nature Center, Indian Cove, Keys Ranch and Cottonwood.
- **With friends / with the world**: a roped party on a dome (one climbs, one belays, the rest cheer from the
  boulders); hide-and-seek in Hidden Valley, the « rustlers' hideout »; a boulder-pile summit at sunset; stargazing
  parties on the Cap Rock lot with shared telescopes; the Keys View wall at sunset is where strangers gather; group
  photos framed in Arch Rock; a shared campfire at Jumbo Rocks.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): carry 1 gallon / 4 L of water a day (a water gauge); stay on
  trails and « don't bust the crust » (living soil crust on the sand); never climb or hang on Joshua trees; don't
  touch or lift tortoises and check under the car before driving off; keep your distance from bighorn at water; no
  camping or fires in rock shelters; no collecting rocks, plants or mine relics; stay out of abandoned mines; red
  lights only at night, no lights on wildlife; pets only on roads and campgrounds; no off-road driving.
- **Implementation challenges**: the boulder piles are rounded 3D rock sculptures, not tile cliffs, and must be
  climbable · Joshua trees need a procedural branching generator (every tree a unique silhouette, forests of them
  instanced) · a Transition Zone that blends two plant palettes across the map · the Keys View escarpment and a
  valley a mile below as a distant backdrop · heat and water as a survival-lite mechanic · a starry sky rich enough to
  be a destination · flash floods in washes · dirt and 4WD roads with a jeep.
