# Great Basin National Park — `GRBA`

> Nevada · established October 27, 1986 · 312.3 km² (77,180 acres) · 161,210 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 1,888–3,982 m · NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10, California–Great Basin)
>
> A lone high mountain rising out of the sagebrush desert: Wheeler Peak, the second-highest summit in Nevada, with the
> state's only glacier, alpine lakes, groves of ancient bristlecone pines, a marble cave full of rare shields, and
> some of the darkest night skies in the country. In Hearthlight it should feel like a quiet climb from a desert ranch
> town into cool aspen and spruce, then above the trees to twisted pines that are thousands of years old, and back
> down into a glittering cave.

## Reading the map

The park is a tall, stepped rectangle over the crest and eastern slopes of the **southern Snake Range**, with the flat
**Snake Valley** (and Utah) to the east. The high ground runs north–south along its west side: **Bald Mountain**
(3,504 m) and **Wheeler Peak** (3,982 m) in the north, then Doso Doyabi, Baker Peak, Pyramid Peak, **Mount Washington**
(3,539 m) and Lincoln Peak, with the **Highland Ridge** running on south into BLM wilderness. Creeks drain east in
parallel canyons (Strawberry, Lehman, Baker, Snake, Big Wash, Lexington) and sink into a valley with no outlet.

Everything visitors use is in the **north-east corner**. From **Baker** (a ranch town at 1,621 m with the Great Basin
Visitor Center), NV 488 climbs 5 mi (8 km) west up the fan to the **Lehman Caves Visitor Center** (2,080 m: café,
cave entrance, astronomy amphitheatre). There the paved **Wheeler Peak Scenic Drive** begins: 12 mi (19 km) of
switchbacks north-west past **Lower** and **Upper Lehman Creek** campgrounds, **Mather Overlook** (~2,740 m) and
**Wheeler Peak Overlook** to **Wheeler Peak Campground** (~3,000 m), under the peak's north-east face. No vehicles
over 24 ft beyond Upper Lehman Creek; the upper road is snowbound from about November to June. From the top parking,
trails reach Stella and Teresa lakes, the **bristlecone grove**, the **rock glacier** and ice under the cirque wall,
and the summit.

South of the drive, gravel **Baker Creek Road** climbs 3 mi (5 km) to the Grey Cliffs and Baker Creek campgrounds
and the Baker Lake, Johnson Lake and Timber Creek trailheads; a short road reaches Pole Canyon picnic area. The long
gravel **Snake Creek Road** enters from NV 487 further south, past free primitive sites, to the Shoshone campground
and the Johnson Lake and Snake Divide trails. The southern half (Big Wash, Mount Washington's bristlecones, Lincoln
Canyon) is roadless but for 4WD tracks, and **Lexington Arch** stands alone in the far south-east corner, reached by
a high-clearance road from the Utah side.

### Water

The creeks (Strawberry, Lehman, Baker, Snake, Lexington) run east off the range from snowmelt and springs, lined with
aspen, willow and cottonwood, and disappear into the Snake Valley: this is the **Great Basin**, where no water reaches
the sea. High up lie small **cirque lakes** (Stella, Teresa, Brown, Baker, Johnson, Dead lakes), frozen from about
November to June. **Wheeler Peak Glacier**, a small ice field shrinking under the north-east cirque wall, feeds a **rock
glacier** (ice-cored rubble). Inside **Lehman Caves** (the longest cave system in Nevada) water drips, pools and
slowly builds formations in a constant ~11 °C. Native **Bonneville cutthroat trout** have been restored to several
creeks (fishing needs a Nevada licence). The 1880s–90s **Osceola Ditch** carried water ~18 mi around the range for a
gold-mining town.

### Relief

From **1,888 m** at the park's lowest edge (Snake Creek) to **3,982 m** on **Wheeler Peak**: ~2,100 m of relief inside
the park and ~2,400 m above the Snake Valley floor, within ~16 km. The range is a tilted block of quartzite, limestone
and granite: long alluvial fans rise from the valley to the foot of the mountains (~2,000 m), then steep canyons climb
to cirques carved by Ice-Age glaciers, knife-edged aretes, talus slopes and moraines, and the broad, rocky summit
ridge. The limestone and marble belt around the Lehman Caves area and Grey Cliffs hides the caves. In the game: the
fan = long gentle banks; canyons = stepped cliffs; the cirque under Wheeler Peak = a tall horseshoe cliff with a
glacier and rock glacier at its foot; the scenic drive's switchbacks = stairs of road; Wheeler Peak, Doso Doyabi and
Bald Mountain are 3D peaks; Lexington Arch a 3D arch; the cave a separate interior.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: valley (1,600–2,000 m): big sagebrush, rabbitbrush, bitterbrush, bunch grasses ·
  foothills (2,000–2,400 m): single-leaf pinyon, Utah juniper, curl-leaf mountain mahogany · montane (2,400–3,000 m):
  quaking aspen (gold in late September), white fir, Douglas-fir, creek willows · subalpine (3,000–3,400 m): Engelmann
  spruce, limber pine, gnarled Great Basin bristlecones on rocky moraine · alpine (3,400–3,982 m): cushion plants,
  alpine flowers, lichen-covered talus.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#C9C2A5` sagebrush grey-green · `#8C9A6B` pinyon-juniper olive · `#E8C547` aspen
  gold · `#2F4A3A` spruce green · `#B89572` bristlecone silver-bronze wood · `#A9A39A` quartzite talus · `#E9E6DF`
  limestone and snowfields · `#5C7D94` alpine lake blue · `#D8C8B0` cave flowstone cream.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): mule deer (campgrounds at dusk) · pronghorn (Snake Valley) · yellow-bellied marmot
  and American pika (talus) · Clark's nutcracker (pine seeds) · mountain bluebird · golden eagle · greater sage-grouse
  (valley sagebrush) · Townsend's big-eared bat (Lehman Caves) · Bonneville cutthroat trout (restored creeks) · Great
  Basin rattlesnake · short-horned lizard · mountain lion and bobcat (rarely seen) · coyote.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): Great Basin bristlecone pine (living up to ~5,000 years) · limber pine · quaking aspen ·
  Engelmann spruce · single-leaf pinyon (pine nuts in autumn) · big sagebrush · Indian paintbrush and lupine
  (July–August) · Lewis flax · alpine columbine and phlox · mountain mahogany.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter: snow closes the upper drive; snowshoes and skis on the unplowed road; the cave stays
  open · spring: warm valley, deep snow above · summer: warm days, cool nights, daily thunderheads in July–August ·
  autumn: golden aspen, crisp air, first snow on the peak.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park (2016), among the darkest skies in the lower 48: a Milky Way bright enough
  to cast shadows, ranger telescope programmes at the Lehman Caves amphitheatre, a research observatory, an annual
  astronomy festival.
- **Sounds**: wind combing bristlecone needles · Clark's nutcrackers · marmot whistles and pika « eeps » · Lehman
  Creek · quaking aspen leaves · thunder echoing in the cirque · in the cave: dripping water, then total silence.

## Moments

- **Sunrise on Wheeler Peak's north-east face** from Mather or Wheeler Peak overlook, the cirque turning pink.
- **Ancient trees at golden hour**: the bristlecone grove's twisted trunks glow bronze at sunset.
- **Aspen gold** (late September–early October): the scenic drive and Lehman Creek lined with yellow.
- **The Milky Way over the Snake Range** (moonless nights, summer–autumn), with ranger telescope nights and the
  annual astronomy festival.
- **Summer thunderstorms** (July–August): clouds boil up over the peak by early afternoon; hikers turn back.
- **The Grand Palace**: on the 90-minute tour, the Parachute Shield and hundreds of shields glow in the ranger's
  lights, in a cave that has welcomed visitors since 1885 (tours all year, 20 people each).
- **Snow walk**: in winter, snowshoes up the closed scenic drive through silent fir forest.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a portrait **512 × 768**-tile vignette of the north-east corner and the crest, from Baker (east)
  to Wheeler Peak (west) and Snake Creek (south). Near true scale (~1.5–2 m per tile): the Lehman Caves Visitor Center,
  the drive's end with the Alpine Lakes Loop, the bristlecone grove and the cirque (~2 km). Compressed ~5×: the
  switchbacks and the 8 km fan down to Baker. The south (Mount Washington, Lexington Arch) becomes a small corner or a
  detached vignette. **Lehman Caves** is a separate interior (~128 × 128 tiles) of rooms linked by narrow passages.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the Lehman Caves Visitor Center (café, exhibits, the cave door, the amphitheatre), Baker's
  single street. 3D landmarks: Wheeler Peak's cirque with the glacier, Doso Doyabi, the bristlecones (each half-dead,
  half-living, sculpted by wind), Lexington Arch, the Johnson Lake mine cabins. Interiors: Lehman Caves (Gothic Palace,
  Music Room, Lodge Room, Grand Palace with the Parachute Shield), the visitor centre, the Rhodes Cabin.
- **Activities**: ranger-guided cave tours (the only way in: 20 people per tour, tickets on recreation.gov); the
  scenic drive's overlooks; alpine lake walks; the summit hike; a bristlecone story (count rings on fallen wood; learn
  of Prometheus, a ~4,900-year-old tree cut for research in 1964 whose loss rallied support for protecting the range);
  photo spots (Wheeler Peak over Stella Lake, bristlecones against the sky, the Parachute Shield, aspen gold, Lexington
  Arch, the Milky Way over the cirque); fishing for native cutthroat; ranger telescope nights; the Junior Ranger badge;
  vehicles: car, bike (the drive is a famous climb), horse on the lower trails; winter snowshoe and ski.
- **Passport stamps**: the official list has Baker (Great Basin Visitor Center) and Lehman Caves (Lehman Caves Visitor
  Center).
- **With friends / with the world**: a stargazing party with shared telescopes; a cave tour following a ranger's
  light; a summit team on the talus ridge; a group photo by a bristlecone; Mather Overlook at sunrise.
- **Rules of the park**: enter the cave only on a ranger tour, touch nothing inside (skin oils kill formations), bring
  no bags, food or drink, and pass the mandatory white-nose syndrome screening that protects the bats; never climb,
  cut or take bristlecone wood, living or dead; stay on trails on the fragile tundra and moraines; no camping or fires
  in the Wheeler Peak and Lexington Arch zones; red lights at night; turn back when thunderstorms build.
- **Implementation challenges**: a cave interior (lantern light, formations as props, the engine's first cave) · a
  glacier and a rock glacier in a cirque · a 2,400 m climb through five vegetation bands on a small map · ancient trees
  as unique hand-shaped models · snow closing the upper road seasonally · a sky dark enough to be a destination.
