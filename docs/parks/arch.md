# Arches National Park — `ARCH`

> Utah · established November 12, 1971 · 310.3 km² (76,678.98 acres) · 1,511,740 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 1,205–1,723 m · NPS region IMR
>
> More than 2,000 natural stone arches, fins and balanced rocks strung along one 18-mile road above the Colorado River,
> five miles north of Moab. In the game it should feel like a red-rock sculpture garden: every pullout a new shape,
> slickrock under your boots, the snowy La Sal Mountains on the horizon, and a pilgrimage uphill to Delicate Arch at sunset.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a jagged, roughly rectangular block about 20 km wide and 27 km tall. Its south-west corner is pinned to US-191 in Moab Canyon, 5 miles north of Moab; its southern edge follows the north side of the Colorado River (UT-128 runs along the river outside the park). The **only entrance**, the visitor centre and entrance station, sits in that south-west corner at the foot of the Moab Fault, a cliff the park road climbs straight away by a pair of switchbacks.

From the top of the cliff the red park road runs north-east and then north through the park's spine. First come the **Park Avenue** viewpoint and trailhead (a street of stone "skyscrapers"), the La Sal Mountains Viewpoint, and the **Courthouse Towers** viewpoint with Three Gossips, Sheep Rock, the Tower of Babel and the Organ. The road then runs north beside **the Great Wall** (a long cliff on its west side, with the Rock Pinnacles), crosses Courthouse Wash, passes the **Petrified Dunes** viewpoint (fossil sand dunes to the east) and reaches **Balanced Rock** at mile 9.2. There the paved Windows Road forks east 2.5 miles past the Garden of Eden to a loop at **the Windows Section** (North and South Windows, Turret Arch, Double Arch, the Parade of Elephants), under Elephant Butte, the park's high point.

North of Balanced Rock the main road climbs to **Panorama Point** (picnic area, stargazing) and then forks again: the Delicate Arch Road runs east 1.2 miles to **Wolfe Ranch** (the Delicate Arch trailhead, a pioneer cabin and a Ute rock art panel) and 1 more mile to the Lower and Upper **Delicate Arch Viewpoints**; Delicate Arch itself stands north-east of the ranch on the rim of a slickrock bowl, across Salt Wash. The main road bends west across Salt Valley Wash to the Salt Valley Overlook and the **Fiery Furnace** viewpoint (a maze of fins east of the road), then runs 5 miles north-west along the edge of Salt Valley past Sand Dune Arch, Broken Arch and Skyline Arch to the **Devils Garden** Campground and the road-end loop at the Devils Garden trailhead, 18 miles from the visitor centre. The Devils Garden trail heads north-west into the fins to Landscape Arch, Double O Arch and the Dark Angel spire.

The west half of the park is the long, open **Salt Valley**, running north-west: the unpaved Salt Valley Road leaves the main road near Skyline Arch and follows it 7.2 miles to a 1-mile spur to the **Klondike Bluffs** (Tower Arch, the Marching Men), then leaves the park towards I-70. Four-wheel-drive tracks cross the south-west (Willow Springs Road from Balanced Rock to US-191; the Herdina Park route past Eye of the Whale Arch, to be driven north to south). The east of the park — Cache Valley, Salt Wash and Lost Spring Canyon, whose side canyons cut the boundary into fingers — is roadless. Every visitor place hangs off the single road spine, so crowds concentrate at seven parking lots.

### Water

Arches is dry country drained by three washes that flow only after rain or snowmelt. **Courthouse Wash** starts in the north-west, crosses the park road south of Petrified Dunes and runs south-east to join the **Colorado River** by the US-191 bridge; its lower reach keeps pools, cottonwoods and willows. **Salt Valley Wash** drains Salt Valley south-east past the Fiery Furnace to Cache Valley, and **Salt Wash** comes down from Lost Spring Canyon in the north-east past Wolfe Ranch (the Delicate Arch trail crosses it on a footbridge) and south to the Colorado. The Colorado itself stays just outside, along the south and south-east edge: a broad brown river in a cliff-walled corridor, with the park's lowest ground (~1,205 m) at its bank near Moab. Summer monsoon storms turn the washes into brief red floods and close the dirt roads.

The most important water is the smallest: **potholes** (ephemeral pools) in the slickrock, from a hand's width to a few metres, which fill after rain and wake up communities of fairy shrimp, tadpole shrimp and spadefoot toad tadpoles that lived as dormant eggs in the dry mud. Springs are few and small: Willow Spring (west), Freshwater and Salt springs (near Cache Valley). No swimming anywhere in the park.

### Relief

The park runs from ~1,205 m where it meets the Colorado River corridor (the NPS quotes the visitor centre's 1,245 m as its low point) to 1,723 m at Elephant Butte. Relief is shallow but everything is vertical at a small scale. The **Moab Fault** makes a ~150 m cliff right behind the visitor centre, climbed by the road's switchbacks. Above it lies a rolling sandstone plateau at 1,300–1,600 m, cut into "rooms" by washes. Deep underground a thick bed of salt once flowed and swelled into a long ridge that later dissolved and collapsed: that is **Salt Valley**, a broad trough running north-west through the park, and the flexing sandstone along its flanks cracked into parallel joints that eroded into **fins** (Devils Garden, the Fiery Furnace, Klondike Bluffs) — narrow walls 5–30 m high and a few metres thick, in rows. Fins thin into **arches**; some leave **balanced rocks** on softer pedestals. Other landforms: the **Great Wall** (a long cliff beside the road), **Petrified Dunes** (humps of fossil dune), the raised mass of the **Windows Section**, and **slickrock bowls** like the one under Delicate Arch. The rock bands: dark-red wavy Dewey Bridge beds at the bottom (the pedestals), thick salmon-orange Entrada "Slick Rock" in which most arches form, a pale cream cap (the Moab Member) on many fins.

In the game: the fault cliff is a tall painted face with a two-switchback road; fins are 2–4-tile-thick walls, 8–15 levels tall, in parallel rows forming corridors; slickrock domes and bowls are stacks of one-level banks (smooth, no terraces); the washes are sand-floored lowlands one or two levels down. Arches, Balanced Rock, the Courthouse Towers, the Three Gossips, Dark Angel, Turret Arch and the Parade of Elephants are 3D models on top of the terrain.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: relief is small, so plants follow soil rather than height. Deep red sand: blackbrush, Indian ricegrass, sand sagebrush, narrowleaf yucca, Mormon tea, with dune evening primrose and sand verbena on the dunes. Shallow soil and slickrock cracks (the whole park): gnarled Utah juniper with silver dead snags, pinyon pine, cliffrose, single-leaf ash, prickly pear and claret cup cactus. Washes: Fremont cottonwood, willows, rabbitbrush and invasive tamarisk. Everywhere between plants: black, knobbly **biological soil crust**.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#C4552B` Entrada sandstone, arches and fins · `#E08A4F` sunlit slickrock · `#8E3A2A` Dewey Bridge pedestals and shadowed walls · `#EAD9B6` cream fin caps and petrified dunes · `#C98A5E` red sand · `#3D3530` biological soil crust and desert varnish · `#5E7348` juniper and pinyon · `#9BA58A` sagebrush and blackbrush grey-green · `#8C6E4E` muddy Colorado River · `#7C8FB5` distant La Sal Mountains (`#F3F3F0` snow).
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): mule deer at dawn along the road; desert bighorn sheep on the cliffs by US-191 and the river; kit fox and coyote at night; black-tailed jackrabbit and desert cottontail; white-tailed antelope squirrel darting between shrubs; Ord's kangaroo rat hopping in headlights; ravens at every viewpoint; pinyon jays in flocks; white-throated swifts screaming round the fins; canyon wren; golden eagle over Salt Valley; collared lizard on sunny rocks in summer; western whiptail; midget faded rattlesnake (rare, nocturnal); spadefoot toads and tadpole shrimp in potholes after summer storms.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): biological soil crust (all year — the park's hidden garden); cliffrose (cream, fragrant, April–May); globemallow (orange, spring); dune evening primrose (white, opens at dusk, spring); sand verbena (purple, spring); claret cup cactus (scarlet, April–May); narrowleaf yucca (cream spikes, May–June); sacred datura (night trumpets, summer); Utah juniper (berries, winter); Fremont cottonwood (gold along Courthouse Wash in late October).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — windy, blooming, busy; summer — 38 °C afternoons, hikes start at dawn, July–September thunderstorms with lightning, flash floods and filled potholes; fall — the best season, cottonwoods gold, cool nights; winter — quiet, cold, the La Sals white behind red arches, occasional snow on the fins and ice on the Delicate Arch slickrock.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park (2019): the Milky Way over Balanced Rock, the Windows and Panorama Point (ranger stargazing events); moonlight bright enough to hike by; kangaroo rats, owls and bats; toads calling after rain.
- **Sounds**: wind whistling through arches and fin corridors; ravens' croaks and wing-beats; the descending song of the canyon wren; the swifts' screams; boots scraping slickrock; the murmur of a crowd at Delicate Arch at sunset; thunder rolling over Salt Valley; car doors at full parking lots; spadefoot toads bleating after a storm.

## Moments

- **Delicate Arch at sunset** — nightly, hundreds of people fill the slickrock bowl to watch the arch turn orange then red against the La Sal Mountains; the walk back is by headlamp.
- **Sunrise through North Window** — from the far side of North Window, the rising light frames Turret Arch; Landscape Arch is at its best in early-morning light.
- **Pothole life after a storm** — July–September: within hours of rain, potholes fill and fairy and tadpole shrimp hatch from years-old eggs; spadefoot toads chorus that night; washes run red and the dirt roads close.
- **Snow on the fins** — a few winter days a year, white snow lies on red fins with the La Sals white behind.
- **Spring bloom** — April–May: cliffrose, globemallow, primrose and cactus flowers.
- **The Fiery Furnace at golden hour** — the fins glow like embers from the viewpoint at sunset (hence the name).
- **Meteor nights** — Perseids (August 12–13) and Geminids (December 13–14) in a certified dark sky.
- **A reminder that arches fall** — Wall Arch in Devils Garden collapsed in August 2008; slabs fell from Landscape Arch in the 1990s: the trail now stops short of it.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a 768 × 1024-tile map, north up, with the park road as its spine. The 29 km road shrinks to ~850 tiles of driving (~1:25 on the long straights of the Great Wall and Salt Valley), while the rock "rooms" stay near true size at ~1:3–1:5: Park Avenue (~1.5 km of canyon → ~250 tiles), the Windows Section (~150 × 150 tiles), the Delicate Arch bowl (the trail's last 400 m and the bowl → ~120 tiles; the arch ~10 tiles tall), the Fiery Furnace (a ~200 × 150-tile maze), Devils Garden (fins over ~2 km → ~300 tiles). The roadless east (Salt Wash, Lost Spring Canyon) becomes a narrow strip; Moab and the Colorado River corridor form the south edge as a gateway town and a river view; the 4×4 tracks except Salt Valley Road are cut.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the visitor centre at the foot of the cliff (the only gateway, with an entrance line that moves); Balanced Rock picnic area; the Windows parking loop; Wolfe Ranch; Devils Garden campground and amphitheatre. 3D landmarks: Delicate Arch, Landscape Arch, Double Arch, North & South Windows, Turret Arch, Balanced Rock, Double O Arch, Skyline Arch, Tower Arch, the Courthouse Towers (Three Gossips, the Organ, Tower of Babel, Sheep Rock), Dark Angel, the Parade of Elephants, the snowy La Sal Mountains as a skybox. Interiors: the visitor centre (exhibits, film), the Wolfe Ranch cabin and root cellar.
- **Activities**: hiking the table above; an **arch journal** — the park has over 2,000 arches, so finding and naming small hidden ones off the main trails is a natural collection; the **Fiery Furnace** as a maze with a ranger (a group that must stay together) or a day permit, with squeezes, jumps and stemming; fin-top scrambles to Double O Arch; photo spots (Delicate Arch from the bowl and from the Upper Viewpoint, Turret Arch through North Window, Double Arch from below, Balanced Rock at dusk, Landscape Arch at sunrise, Park Avenue from the top, the Three Gossips, Fiery Furnace from the viewpoint, Skyline Arch from the road); ranger patio talks, campground evening programmes and stargazing at Panorama Point; Junior Ranger booklet (five exercises and three adventures); biking the park road and the Moab Canyon Pathway; a high-clearance drive down Salt Valley Road; pothole-life spotting after rain; canyoneering and rock climbing by permit (never on arches).
- **Passport stamps**: Arches Visitor Center (the park's only stamp location).
- **With friends / with the world**: the Delicate Arch sunset is the natural world event — strangers gather on the slickrock bowl and take turns for the photo under the arch; group photos through North Window and under Double Arch; a party in the Fiery Furnace helping each other up ledges (one boosts, one pulls); the primitive loop's pool crossing; campfire talk at Devils Garden. Gathering spots: the visitor-centre line, Balanced Rock picnic tables, the Windows parking loop, Panorama Point after dark.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): walk only on trails, bare rock or wash sand — the soil crust keeps your footprints for decades; never climb, stand on or rappel from an arch; don't touch the rock art or add graffiti; don't drink from, wade in or step through potholes, even dry ones; don't build or knock down cairns; carry a gallon of water on summer hikes; when a parking lot is full, move on rather than circle or park on plants; permits for the Fiery Furnace; pets only on roads and in the campground.
- **Implementation challenges**: arches are holes through rock — overhangs the tile heightmap can't make, so they must be 3D models blended into fins; very thin, tall fin walls and narrow corridors seen from a 45° north-looking camera (occlusion, see-through walls in the Fiery Furnace); smooth slickrock domes and bowls rather than stepped terraces; potholes that fill after rain; persistent footprints in the soil crust; rock-art decals; a crowd-capacity system (lots that fill, an entrance line) for the shared world.
