# Badlands National Park — `BADL`

> South Dakota · established November 10, 1978 (national monument January 25, 1939) · 982.4 km² (242,756 acres) ·
> 1,139,361 recreation visits (2025) · 721–1,018 m · NPS region MWR
>
> A 100 km wall of striped, eroding buttes and pinnacles rising out of mixed-grass prairie, packed with 30-million-year-old
> mammal fossils, grazed by bison and bighorn; its southern half is Oglala Sioux Tribe land. In Hearthlight it should
> feel like walking on another planet at the edge of an ocean of grass: boardwalks into pastel spires, prairie dogs
> chirping, thunderheads over the Wall, and a night sky full of stars.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is three pieces. The **North Unit** is a long band running west-north-west from Cedar Pass to Sage
Creek, ~60 km long and 8–15 km wide, surrounded by the **Buffalo Gap National Grassland** (patchworked with private
land). Its spine is the **Badlands Wall**, a ragged escarpment of banded buttes, ~100 km long, that separates the
higher prairie (north, towards I-90 and the town of Wall) from the lower White River basin (south). The **Badlands Loop Road
(SD 240)** runs along the Wall for 39 mi: from the **Northeast Entrance** (I-90 exit 131) south past the **Big Badlands
Overlook** and the Door, Window and Notch trailheads to **Cedar Pass** and the **Ben Reifel Visitor Center** (Cedar Pass
Lodge and campground below it, the Interior Entrance 2.5 mi south towards the town of **Interior**), then west-north-west
along the top of the Wall past the Fossil Exhibit Trail, the White River Valley, Big Foot Pass, Panorama Point, Prairie
Wind, Burns Basin, Homestead, Conata Basin, Yellow Mounds and Ancient Hunters overlooks to the **Pinnacles Overlook**
and the **Pinnacles Entrance**, 8.5 mi south of the town of Wall (I-90 exit 110).

West of the Pinnacles, the gravel **Sage Creek Rim Road** follows the rim of the **Badlands Wilderness Area** (64,144
acres, the park's west end) past the Badlands Wilderness and Hay Butte overlooks, the **Roberts Prairie Dog Town** and the
**Sage Creek Basin Overlook** to the free, primitive **Sage Creek Campground** where the South Fork of Sage Creek comes out,
then leaves the park towards Scenic. The Wilderness is roadless: Sage Creek and its forks drain it north-west, and bison
roam it. South of the Loop Road, **Conata Road** (gravel) drops from Conata Picnic Area across Conata Basin to SD 44,
the highway along the park's south side (Interior–Scenic). The White River runs east along the south side, past Interior.

The **Stronghold Unit** (South Unit, ~one-half of the park) lies south-west of the North Unit, entirely within the **Pine
Ridge Indian Reservation** on Oglala Sioux Tribe trust land, co-managed with the Tribe since a 1976 agreement. It is a
great block of grassy tables and badlands: **Sheep Mountain Table** (reached by a rough road from SD 589, 1,000 m), the
**Stronghold Table**, Plenty Star, Blindman, Galigo and Red Shirt tables, with **Red Shirt Table Overlook** at its
south-west corner on BIA 41 and the **White River Visitor Center** (open seasonally) on SD 27 at its south-east corner by
the White River. Most of it is roadless and part of a former WWII bombing range. The small **Palmer Creek Unit** sits
further south-east, isolated among private reservation land.

### Water

- **White River**: rises in Nebraska and flows north-east through the Pine Ridge country, past the White River Visitor
  Center, then east along the North Unit's south side and south of Interior, muddy grey-white with badlands clay (hence the name), lined with
  cottonwoods; it separates the North Unit from the Stronghold country.
- **Sage Creek** and its South and Middle forks drain the Badlands Wilderness north-west to the Cheyenne River;
  **Battle Creek**, **Cedar Creek** and **Cottonwood Creek** cross the Stronghold Unit; **Palmer Creek** its own unit.
  All are intermittent, alkaline and undrinkable; after storms the clay turns to slick gumbo and the washes run brown.
- Small springs and seeps (Cliff Shelf's slump, juniper draws) and stock ponds; the park has no lakes. Bring all water.

### Relief

Lowest ~721 m (2,365 ft) in a runoff channel south-east of the Ben Reifel Visitor Center; highest ~1,018 m (3,340 ft) on
Red Shirt Table. The land steps in two levels: the **upper prairie** (the "tables", ~900–1,000 m: Sheep Mountain,
Stronghold, Red Shirt, and the prairie north of the Wall) and the **lower basins** (~730–800 m: the White River valley,
Conata Basin, Sage Creek Basin), joined by the **Wall**, a 50–150 m escarpment of soft, banded sediments (grey, pink,
buff, yellow-mound bands) carved into buttes, spires, knife-edge ridges, gullies, slumps and hoodoos with caprocks.
Everything erodes about 2.5 cm a year. In the game: the Wall as a long 3–5-level terraced cliff with painted stripes,
spires and pinnacles as 3D props on the plateau edges, the tables as flat grassy plateaus with crumbling edges, walkable
banks on the slumps and the prairie, a log ladder as stairs on the Notch Trail, boardwalks over the clay.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation**: mixed-grass prairie everywhere the soil holds — western wheatgrass, green needlegrass, blue grama,
  buffalograss, little bluestem, with soapweed yucca, prickly pear, silver sage and wildflowers; Rocky Mountain juniper
  in the draws and on slumps (Cliff Shelf); cottonwoods, willows and chokecherry along the White River and Sage Creek;
  the badlands themselves are bare clay and sandstone with a few pioneer plants.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#D9D0C1` pale grey-white volcanic-ash mudstone · `#E2B9A0` pink-salmon band ·
  `#C98E6C` rust-red fossil-soil stripe · `#D8B34A` Yellow Mounds mustard · `#9C8F85` grey shale of the lower beds ·
  `#EDE3CE` sunlit caprock sandstone · `#A7A26A` summer wheatgrass · `#C8B77E` late-summer golden prairie · `#6F7F52`
  juniper · `#B9AE9A` the chalky White River.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): American bison (Sage Creek; brought back from Theodore Roosevelt NP in 1963),
  bighorn sheep (Pinnacles and Cedar Pass, lambs in May–June), black-tailed prairie dog (Roberts town, pups in spring),
  black-footed ferret (night; reintroduced in 1994, one of North America's rarest mammals), swift fox, pronghorn, mule
  deer, coyote, burrowing owl (prairie-dog towns, summer), golden eagle, western meadowlark, cliff swallow (the Wall),
  prairie rattlesnake and bullsnake.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): pasque flower (April), soapweed yucca (cream bells, June), prickly pear (yellow, June),
  scarlet globemallow (May–June), prairie coneflower and purple coneflower (July), sunflowers (August), western wheatgrass
  (all year, gold in autumn), Rocky Mountain juniper.
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — wind, rain, green prairie, newborn bison and lambs; summer — heat, gnats, violent
  afternoon thunderstorms with hail and lightning (and rare tornadoes); autumn — golden grass, clear cool days; winter —
  snow dusting the pink spires, bitter wind, bison crusted with frost.
- **Night**: dark prairie skies (ranger night-sky programs at the Cedar Pass amphitheater; the Badlands Astronomy
  Festival in summer, most years); coyotes howling, ferret-spotlighting researchers' lights on the grassland.
- **Sounds**: prairie-dog alarm chirps and "jump-yips", meadowlarks, wind in the grass, the crunch of dry popcorn clay,
  rolling thunder, bison grunting at Sage Creek, coyote choruses at night.

## Moments

- **Sunrise at the Door and Big Badlands Overlook** (east-facing) and **sunset at Pinnacles or Yellow Mounds** (west):
  the bands glow orange and pink.
- **Bison at Sage Creek**: the herd grazes around the campground; rut in July–August.
- **Bighorn lambs** (May–June) on the spires at Pinnacles and Cedar Pass.
- **Prairie-dog pups** emerging (May–June) at Roberts Prairie Dog Town; burrowing owls in summer.
- **Summer thunderstorm over the Wall**: lightning on the prairie, then the clay turns to slick gumbo.
- **Fossil prep lab** (summer): paleontologists cleaning fresh finds behind glass at Ben Reifel; visitors who spot a
  fossil report it, never pick it up.
- **Night sky programs and the annual astronomy festival** (summer) at Cedar Pass.
- **Winter**: snow lines picking out every ridge of the spires; bison in a white basin.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: the North Unit as the playable map, **1024 × 384 tiles**, north up: the Loop Road ribbon from
  Cedar Pass (east) to the Pinnacles and Sage Creek (west). Keep Cedar Pass near true scale (visitor center, lodge,
  Door-Window-Notch complex within ~2 km → ~350 × 200 tiles); the overlooks along the 39 mi road as compressed stops
  every ~40 tiles (~1:20); Sage Creek and the Wilderness at ~1:10 (a bison basin ~250 tiles across). The Stronghold
  Unit as a separate, smaller vignette map (~384 × 256) with the White River Visitor Center, Sheep Mountain Table and
  Red Shirt Table, reached by a road link; the Palmer Creek Unit cut.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Ben Reifel Visitor Center (interior with the fossil prep lab window), Cedar Pass Lodge
  (cabins, restaurant), the amphitheater; set pieces: the Door's boardwalk opening onto spires, the Notch's log ladder,
  Yellow Mounds, the Pinnacles, Sage Creek's bison campground, Sheep Mountain Table's grassy flat; 3D props: spires,
  hoodoos, toadstool caprocks, fossil casts on the Fossil Exhibit Trail.
- **Activities**: short signature hikes (Door, Window, Notch, Castle, Cliff Shelf) and open off-trail hiking; overlook
  photo spots (Big Badlands at sunrise, Yellow Mounds, Pinnacles at sunset, Roberts Prairie Dog Town, the Notch ladder,
  Sheep Mountain Table); wildlife spotting (bison, bighorn, prairie dogs, pronghorn, the rare ferret at night);
  **fossil finding** (spot fossils eroding out, photograph and report them — a Junior Paleontologist badge); Junior
  Ranger; ranger talks and night-sky programs; horseback riding (open riding in the Wilderness); biking the Loop Road;
  camping at Cedar Pass and Sage Creek; winter snow walks.
- **Passport stamps**: "Interior", "White River VC" and "Ben Reifel VC – Interior, SD" (Eastern National list,
  August 2025).
- **With friends / with the world**: sunrise gathering at the Door trailhead, a group climb of the Notch ladder, a
  bison-watch night at Sage Creek Campground, a fossil hunt where each player covers a gully, the Cedar Pass amphitheater
  star party.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): never collect fossils (report finds at the visitor center), 100 yd
  from bison, 25 yd from other wildlife, don't feed prairie dogs, stay off fragile formations and slopes, no water in the
  backcountry — carry it, beware rattlesnakes and gumbo after rain. In the Stronghold Unit, respect Oglala Lakota land
  and sacred sites (Stronghold Table), stay on public routes, and keep clear of the old bombing range (unexploded
  ordnance).
- **Implementation challenges**: soft striped badlands at big scale (many small spires and gullies — a procedural
  erosion painter), gumbo mud after rain (slippery state), bison herds moving through camp, a burrow-colony system for
  prairie dogs, respectful representation of the South Unit as Lakota land (consult, avoid invented sacred content), and a
  long linear map along one road.
