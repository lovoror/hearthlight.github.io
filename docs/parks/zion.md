## Reading the map

North up, the park is a staircase-shaped diagonal running from the north-west to the south-east. Interstate 15 runs down the left edge along the foot of the Hurricane Cliffs. At the top left, a small separate-feeling block, **Kolob Canyons**, sits just east of I-15 Exit 40: the Kolob Canyons Visitor Center is right at the exit, and a 5-mile road climbs east past Taylor Creek and Lee Pass to the Kolob Canyons Viewpoint and Timber Creek Overlook, looking into a row of parallel red "finger" canyons (the North, Middle and South Forks of Taylor Creek) cut into Horse Ranch Mountain (8,726 ft / 2,660 m, the park's summit), Nagunt Mesa, Timber Top Mountain and Gregory Butte. La Verkin Creek runs along their south side; Kolob Arch hangs above it. Nothing links this block to the rest of the park by road.

From the Kolob block the boundary narrows into the **Hop Valley** corridor and then widens into the large main block, which fills the centre and south-east of the map. Its west half is the high **Kolob Terrace**: the Kolob Terrace Road leaves UT-9 at the town of Virgin (bottom left) and winds north, in and out of the park, past the Right Fork, Grapevine and Left Fork trailheads (the Left Fork of North Creek is the Subway), the Wildcat Canyon trailhead and the Northgate Peaks viewpoint facing the white domes of the North and South Guardian Angels, up to Lava Point (7,890 ft) and Kolob and Blue Springs reservoirs just outside the north boundary. The upper road is not plowed in winter. Between this plateau and Zion Canyon lies the roadless **Horse Pasture Plateau**, crossed from Lava Point to the Grotto by the West Rim Trail.

The heart of the map is **Zion Canyon**, in the lower right: the North Fork of the Virgin River comes down from the north-east (Chamberlain's Ranch, outside the park) through **the Narrows** and Orderville Canyon, then turns south and cuts the main canyon, flanked by named walls and temples. UT-9 enters from Springdale at the **South Entrance**; the visitor centre, Watchman Campground and the Pa'rus Trail sit on the river's east bank there, the South Campground and Nature Center on its west bank. The road runs north 2 km to the Human History Museum and **Canyon Junction**. There the green shuttle line of the **Zion Canyon Scenic Drive** continues north, shuttle-only from spring through fall, up the canyon floor past the Court of the Patriarchs, Zion Lodge, the Grotto, Weeping Rock and Big Bend to the **Temple of Sinawava**, where the road ends and the Riverside Walk leads into the Narrows. Angels Landing is the fin on the west side of the river opposite Big Bend; the Great White Throne rises on the east side opposite the Grotto; the Emerald Pools are on the west side behind the lodge.

At Canyon Junction UT-9 becomes the **Zion–Mount Carmel Highway**: it crosses the river and climbs the east wall by a stack of switchbacks into Pine Creek canyon, enters the 1.1-mile tunnel, and comes out at the Canyon Overlook trailhead, then crosses the slickrock **East Side** past Checkerboard Mesa to the East Entrance and on to Mount Carmel Junction and US-89 (Bryce Canyon, Kanab, the Grand Canyon). North of the highway the **East Rim** plateau carries Observation Point, Cable and Deertrap mountains. South of Springdale, the East Fork of the Virgin comes in from Parunuweap Canyon and joins the North Fork; the combined Virgin River then flows west past Rockville, the Grafton ghost town and Virgin toward La Verkin and Hurricane. The south-west corner of the park is low desert: Coalpits Wash (the park's lowest point, 3,666 ft) and the Chinle and Huber Wash trails.

About 84 % of the park is designated wilderness; everything outside the three road corridors (Zion Canyon + UT-9, Kolob Terrace Road, Kolob Canyons Road) is reached on foot.

### Water

The **North Fork of the Virgin River** rises on the Markagunt Plateau east of the park and flows south-west past Chamberlain's Ranch into the Narrows, where 300 m walls close to a few metres apart; it then turns south to carve Zion Canyon, running past the Temple of Sinawava, Big Bend, the Grotto, the Lodge and Canyon Junction to Springdale. Just south of town it meets the **East Fork**, which comes west along the south boundary through Parunuweap Canyon; the joined Virgin flows west past Rockville, Grafton and Virgin toward Hurricane, and on to Lake Mead and the Colorado. Normal flows are low (often under 100 cfs), but spring snowmelt raises and browns the river, the Narrows close above ~150 cfs, and monsoon storms can send a flash flood down the slot in minutes. The park issues cyanobacteria advisories for the North Fork: no drinking untreated water, no dunking heads, dogs out of the river.

Water also comes out of the rock: rain soaking into the Navajo Sandstone takes centuries to reach an impermeable layer and seeps out along it, feeding **Weeping Rock**, the hanging gardens of the Riverside Walk and the **Emerald Pools** (three pools stepped below Heaps Canyon, with seasonal falls pouring over the lower pool's overhang). Big Spring feeds the upper Narrows. After a storm, temporary waterfalls pour off every rim — at the Temple of Sinawava, Emerald Pools and along the Scenic Drive. On the Kolob Terrace, the Left Fork of North Creek runs through the Subway past Archangel, Keyhole and Double Falls. In Kolob Canyons, the three forks of Taylor Creek and La Verkin Creek flow west and south-west out of the finger canyons. Kolob Reservoir (8,118 ft) and Blue Springs Reservoir sit on the plateau just north of Lava Point, outside the park. There is no lake or large pool anywhere you may swim in the park; the river itself is the swimming hole.

### Relief

The lowest point is Coalpits Wash, 1,117 m (3,666 ft); the highest is Horse Ranch Mountain, 2,660 m (8,726 ft). The park sits on one step of the Grand Staircase: a thick layer of Navajo Sandstone (up to ~670 m) forms the sheer cream upper walls, standing on redder, ledgy slopes of Kayenta and Moenave rock that form benches at the walls' feet. **Zion Canyon** is a flat-floored gash 600–900 m deep: the floor climbs gently from 1,197 m at the visitor centre to 1,347 m at the Temple of Sinawava, while the rims stand at ~2,000–2,380 m (West Temple, Great White Throne, Observation Point). It narrows northward, from about a kilometre wide at Springdale to a slot a few metres wide in the Narrows. **Angels Landing** is a free-standing fin 450 m above the river, joined to the west wall by the Scout Lookout saddle. The **East Side** is a rolling sea of cross-bedded slickrock domes and hoodoos at 1,550–2,050 m, cut by Pine Creek's slot below the tunnel. The **Kolob Terrace** is a plateau rising north from ~1,550 m (Left Fork) to 2,400 m (Lava Point), its white domes (the Guardian Angels, Tabernacle Dome) standing above; basalt from old cinder cones (Firepit Knoll) caps parts of it. **Kolob Canyons** is the edge of the Hurricane Cliffs fault: the finger canyons end in 600 m red walls facing I-15.

In the game: the canyon floor is the base level; the walls become tall cliff faces (20–30 levels, painted in two bands — red benches below, cream sandstone above, black varnish streaks); Walter's Wiggles, the Canyon Overlook ledges and the Riverside Walk's steps become stairs; the benches under the walls (Sand Bench, the Kayenta ledge) are one-level banks. Model as 3D peaks: the Watchman, West Temple, Towers of the Virgin, the three Patriarchs, Lady Mountain, Great White Throne, Angels Landing, the Organ, the Pulpit, Checkerboard Mesa, the Guardian Angels and the Kolob fingers.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: canyon floor (1,100–1,400 m) — Fremont cottonwood, box elder, velvet ash, coyote willow and grassy meadows along the river; hanging gardens at seeps with maidenhair fern, golden columbine, scarlet monkeyflower and shooting stars. Slopes and slickrock (1,300–1,900 m) — pinyon pine, Utah juniper, Gambel oak, singleleaf ash, manzanita, cliffrose, yucca, prickly pear. South-west desert below 1,300 m — blackbrush, sand sagebrush, Mojave species at their northern edge. Plateaus and rims (1,900–2,660 m) — ponderosa pine, Douglas-fir and white fir in shady canyons, quaking aspen and sagebrush meadows on the Kolob, bigtooth maple in side canyons.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#EFE2C8` upper Navajo sandstone, white temple caps · `#E2A47E` salmon-pink mid-walls and slickrock · `#B5482E` red Kayenta/Moenave benches, Kolob finger canyons, the red asphalt of the Scenic Drive · `#4B3B35` desert-varnish streaks on the walls · `#D8B98E` sandy washes and trails · `#7FA650` cottonwood and meadow green (`#E3B23C` in November) · `#56713F` pinyon–juniper dots · `#6E8C7B` jade-grey Virgin River (`#9C7B5A` when flooding) · `#3E3A38` basalt on Lava Point · `#6FA8DC` desert sky.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): mule deer on the canyon-floor meadows at dusk; Rio Grande wild turkeys on the Lodge lawn and at the Grotto; rock squirrels begging at every trailhead (their bites are a real hazard); desert bighorn sheep on the East Side slickrock near Checkerboard Mesa and the tunnel; California condors (with numbered wing tags) soaring near Angels Landing and Big Bend; peregrine falcons nesting on the big walls in spring; Mexican spotted owls in cool side canyons (rare); canyon wrens singing down the scale; American dippers bobbing in the Narrows; canyon treefrogs in pools and potholes; the Zion snail, found only in the park's seeps; desert tortoises in the south-west desert in spring; tarantulas wandering in September–October; ringtails in the campgrounds at night.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): golden columbine and maidenhair fern (hanging gardens, spring–summer); sacred datura (white night-blooming trumpets, summer); prince's plume (yellow spikes, spring); claret cup cactus and Indian paintbrush (April–May); sego lily (May–June, rims); Fremont cottonwood (gold in early–mid November); bigtooth maple (red in October, Kolob and side canyons); quaking aspen (Kolob, gold in October); cliffrose (cream flowers, May).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — snowmelt swells the brown river, the Narrows often stay closed into June, seasonal falls run, wildflowers. Summer — 38–41 °C on the canyon floor; from mid-July, afternoon monsoon storms, lightning on the rims, instant waterfalls and flash floods. Fall — clear, cool, cottonwoods and maples turn. Winter — mild canyon days, ice on the Riverside Walk and Angels Landing's chains, a few mornings when snow dusts the temples (white on red); the Kolob plateau is snowbound. Occasional wildfire smoke from the plateaus.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park (2021): the Milky Way over the Watchman from the Pa'rus, the walls cutting the sky into a narrow ribbon of stars; full moons light the cliffs; bats over the river, canyon treefrogs calling in bleating choruses, ringtails raiding camps, datura flowers opening.
- **Sounds**: the Virgin River's steady rush (louder in spring), the canyon wren's falling whistle, ravens, wind in the cottonwoods, dripping at Weeping Rock; the shuttle's diesel-electric hum, air brakes and the recorded stop announcements; thunder echoing wall to wall; horse hooves on the Sand Bench trail; a rockfall's clatter; the boom of cars in the tunnel.

## Moments

- **The Watchman at sunset** — every clear evening the peak over the visitor centre glows orange; the classic view is from the Pa'rus footbridges near Canyon Junction (the NPS asks people not to stop on the road bridge).
- **Sunrise on the temples** — first light creeps down the Towers of the Virgin from the Human History Museum patio, and into Zion Canyon from the Canyon Overlook.
- **Monsoon waterfalls & flash floods** — mid-July to September, afternoon storms: within minutes of heavy rain, ribbons of red water pour off the rims; the visitor centre posts the day's flash-flood potential, and the Narrows can rise in minutes.
- **Spring runoff** — April–June the North Fork runs high and brown; the Narrows close above ~150 cfs and reopen when flows drop (often June).
- **Fall colour** — bigtooth maples and aspens turn in Kolob Canyons in mid-October; the cottonwoods of Zion Canyon peak in early to mid November.
- **Condors over Angels Landing** — year-round, California condors ride the thermals along the walls near Big Bend.
- **Snow on red rock** — a few winter mornings a year the temples are dusted white; the Scenic Drive then opens to private cars on days the shuttle is off.
- **The permit lottery** — Angels Landing's day-before lottery opens at 12:01 am, closes at 3 pm, results at 4 pm; the seasonal lottery covers months ahead.
- **Tarantula season** — September–October evenings, males cross roads and trails looking for mates.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a 1024 × 1024-tile map keeping the brochure's NW–SE diagonal. Zion Canyon from Canyon Junction to the Temple of Sinawava (9.7 km) becomes a ~520-tile corridor (~1:12), but its stops are near true size at ~1:2–1:3: the visitor-centre plaza and campgrounds, the Lodge lawn, the Grotto, the Emerald Pools basin (~500 m across → ~150 tiles), Weeping Rock's alcove, the Temple amphitheatre; the Narrows stay 5–25 tiles wide between 25-level walls. The East Side (15 km of road) shrinks to ~250 tiles (switchbacks, tunnel, Canyon Overlook, Checkerboard Mesa, East Entrance). The Kolob Terrace is a ~300-tile strip (Left Fork, Northgate Peaks, Lava Point) and Kolob Canyons a ~250 × 200-tile corner in the north-west, reached by a "drive" link along the map's west edge (I-15 and UT-9 are cut). The south-west desert becomes one small wash; Springdale is a gateway strip with shuttle stops.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the visitor-centre plaza (shuttle queue, footbridge from Springdale, rangers), Zion Lodge and its lawn (the natural meeting place), the Grotto picnic area, the Temple of Sinawava, the Kolob Canyons Visitor Center, Lava Point. 3D landmarks: the Watchman, West Temple, Towers of the Virgin, the three Patriarchs, Lady Mountain, Great White Throne, Angels Landing with its chain posts, the Organ, the Pulpit, Checkerboard Mesa, the Kolob finger canyons, Kolob Arch, the Guardian Angels, the Cable Mountain draw-works on the East Rim. Interiors: Zion Lodge (lobby, dining room), the visitor centre, the Human History Museum (film theatre), the Nature Center; the tunnel itself (a roofed road with light spilling through gallery windows).
- **Activities**: hiking the list above; **wading the Narrows** (walking stick, current, depth, Wall Street); permits as items (Angels Landing past Scout Lookout, the Subway, top-down Narrows); riding the **shuttle** (9 stops, the park's fast travel inside the canyon) and biking the Pa'rus and Scenic Drive; guided horse rides from the Lodge corral along the Sand Bench trail (mid-March–October); canyoneering rappels in the Subway and Keyhole Falls; watching big-wall climbers at Big Bend; ranger talks at the Watchman and South campground amphitheatres; Junior Ranger booklet (ages 4+) with a ranger programme at the Nature Center; stargazing on the Pa'rus. Photo spots: the Watchman from the Pa'rus bridges, Court of the Patriarchs, the curtain at Lower Emerald Pool, Scout Lookout, the Angels Landing summit, Big Bend, Weeping Rock's alcove, the Temple of Sinawava, Wall Street, Canyon Overlook, Checkerboard Mesa, Timber Creek Overlook, Double Arch Alcove, Kolob Arch, the Subway's tube, Lava Point.
- **Passport stamps**: Zion Canyon Visitor Center, Zion Human History Museum, Kolob Canyons Visitor Center (stamp collectors also list a bonus stamp at the Springdale post office).
- **With friends / with the world**: the shuttle ride as a shared scene (everyone boards, the driver narrates each stop); a party walking the Narrows together (the current slows whoever wades deepest); Angels Landing's chains in single file, yielding at the wide spots; picnics on the Lodge lawn; a group photo at the Watchman bridge at sunset. In the open world strangers gather in the visitor-centre shuttle line, on the Lodge lawn, at Scout Lookout (where those without a permit wait and watch), at the river entry at the end of the Riverside Walk, and at Lava Point for sunrise.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): don't feed the rock squirrels (a bite ends your photo); stay out of the Emerald Pools; read the flash-flood forecast before any slot and leave when the water browns; permit checks at Scout Lookout, Big Spring and the Subway; don't drink or dunk in the river (cyanobacteria); keep 25 yd from bighorn sheep and never approach a condor; stay on trail to spare biological soil crust; stop only at pullouts, never in the tunnel or on the Canyon Junction bridge; bikes pull over for shuttles; pets only on the Pa'rus; pack out everything, no graffiti, no cairns.
- **Implementation challenges**: walls 600–900 m tall that must read as vertical from a 45° camera looking north (compressed to 20–30 levels, north walls hidden behind their tops, south walls facing the camera); a walkable, flowing river floor with depth and current (the Narrows) and a flood state that raises and browns the water; temporary waterfalls spilling off rims after rain; single-file exposed ridges with chains; a roofed tunnel; scheduled shuttle buses with boarding at stops; hanging gardens and seeps painted on cliff faces; snow dusting only the tops of the temples.
