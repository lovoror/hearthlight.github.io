# Petrified Forest National Park — `PEFO`

> Arizona · established December 9, 1962 · 895.9 km² (221,390 acres) · 315,951 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 1,585–1,900 m · NPS region IMR (DOI Region 8, Lower Colorado Basin)
>
> A high, windswept grassland over banded badlands, strewn with 225-million-year-old logs turned to rainbow stone,
> with ancestral Puebloan ruins and rock art, a 1930s pueblo-style inn and the only stretch of Route 66 in a national
> park. In Hearthlight it should feel like a road trip through deep time: one road north to south, a string of short
> walks among glittering logs and candy-striped hills, and a wide, wide sky.

## Reading the map

The park is an irregular block on the high plateau, **cut in two by I-40**, the old Route 66 alignment and the BNSF
railway, which run together south-west to north-east across its northern third beside the **Puerco River**. **North
of I-40** lies the **Painted Desert**: a rim of overlooks above a vast basin of red, orange and lavender badlands (the
Black Forest, Chinde Mesa, Pilot Rock at 1,900 m), almost all wilderness. **South of I-40** a long tongue of grassland
and badlands holds the log fields (Blue Forest, Jasper Forest, Crystal Forest, Rainbow Forest). Private and state
blocks inside the 2004 authorized boundary show as pale « no access » areas.

One road does everything: the **park road**, 28 mi (45 km) from I-40 exit 311 to US 180. From the **Painted Desert
Visitor Center** (park HQ, 1,759 m) it loops north along the rim past Tiponi, Tawa and Kachina points and the
**Painted Desert Inn**, swings west to Chinde Point and back south past Pintado, Nizhoni, Whipple and Lacey points.
It crosses the **Route 66** alignment (a rusting 1932 Studebaker marks it) and passes over I-40 without an
interchange, then the railway and the Puerco. South of the river come **Puerco Pueblo**, **Newspaper Rock**, **The
Tepees** and the 3.5 mi **Blue Mesa** loop (east). Next are **Agate Bridge**, the **Jasper Forest** spur (west) and
**Crystal Forest**, below The Flattops and Martha's Butte, and finally the **Rainbow Forest Museum** (1,669 m) with
Giant Logs behind it and Long Logs and Agate House to the south-east; the south entrance and US 180 are 2 km on.

The two **wilderness areas** (designated in 1970, among the very first in the national park system) are open to
off-trail hiking: the **Painted Desert Wilderness** from Kachina Point (Lithodendron Wash, Onyx Bridge, the Black
Forest) and the **Rainbow Forest Wilderness** behind The Flattops (access at milepost 24). The edge dirt roads are
mostly closed or by permit (Devil's Playground by permit via I-40 exit 303).

### Water

A dry grassland: the **Puerco River** (usually a braided sandy channel lined with cottonwoods and tamarisk) crosses
the park from east to west between I-40 and Puerco Pueblo, flowing to the Little Colorado at Holbrook. **Lithodendron
Wash** (« stone-tree » wash) drains the Painted Desert north to south, and other washes (Dry, Dead, Jim Camp,
Cottonwood) run only after storms. Monsoon cloudbursts in July–August send flash floods down them and melt the soft
bentonite badlands into mud. Ranchers' stock tanks dot the grasslands (Rock, Blue, Cedar, Delaney tanks). There is no
swimming and no reliable water outside the visitor centres.

### Relief

A plateau between **1,585 m** (where the railway and the Puerco leave the park) and **1,900 m** (Pilot Rock). The
land is flat grassland capped by hard sandstone and lava layers. Where the cap breaks, the soft clays of the Chinle
Formation erode into **badlands**: the Painted Desert's 100–150 m rim above a basin of red hills, and the smaller
banded cones and mesas of Blue Mesa, The Tepees, The Flattops and Martha's Butte. The logs lie where erosion has
freed them from the clay, scattered on slopes and wash floors, some left balanced on pedestals. In the game: the
Painted Desert rim is one long cliff with overlooks; badlands are banded banks and cones in stripes of blue, purple,
red and white; mesas and buttes (Pilot Rock, The Flattops, The Battleship, Twin Buttes) are 3D landmarks; logs are
props lying on the ground, broken into segments like cut sausages.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: little relief (1,585–1,900 m), so plants follow the ground instead: shortgrass prairie
  (blue grama, galleta, Indian ricegrass) with four-wing saltbush, rabbitbrush, Mormon tea and snakeweed on the
  plateau · soapweed yucca, plains prickly pear and claret cup cactus on rocky benches · almost bare badlands ·
  cottonwoods, willows and tamarisk along the Puerco · a few junipers on the highest mesas.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#C8573C` Painted Desert red · `#E39A62` orange badland band · `#8E7BA6` lavender
  clay · `#7F97AE` Blue Mesa blue-grey · `#EDE5D6` white ash bands · `#B79B6B` dry grass · `#C0443A` jasper-red log ·
  `#E7C35A` yellow-gold log · `#6C5A7C` amethyst log heart · `#5E4A3A` basalt caprock on the rim.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): pronghorn (grasslands, dawn and dusk) · coyote · bobcat · Gunnison's prairie dog ·
  badger · black-tailed jackrabbit · common raven and golden eagle over the rim · burrowing owl · western meadowlark ·
  collared lizard · plateau striped whiptail (an all-female lizard species) · prairie rattlesnake · and the Triassic
  animals of the museum: phytosaurs, metoposaurs, early dinosaurs.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): globemallow (orange, spring–summer) · Indian paintbrush · evening primrose (after rain)
  · claret cup cactus (red, May) · plains prickly pear (June) · soapweed yucca (June) · rabbitbrush (gold, autumn) ·
  and the fossil flora: conifer logs (Araucarioxylon / Agathoxylon), ferns and cycads.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter: cold and clear, snow dusting the badlands · spring: wind, dust, wildflowers ·
  summer: heat, then July–August monsoon storms with lightning and rainbows · autumn: golden grass, calm air.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park (2018); the road closes at 5 p.m., so the stars belong to ranger night
  programmes and wilderness backpackers under a dome of stars over the Painted Desert.
- **Sounds**: wind over the grass and around the overlooks · ravens · meadowlarks on fence posts · trains rumbling and
  horning on the railway · the far hum of I-40 · thunder rolling over the Painted Desert · clay crunching underfoot.

## Moments

- **Sunset and sunrise on the Painted Desert**: from Kachina, Tawa and Pintado points the red basin turns crimson and
  purple as the sun goes down.
- **The solstice marker** (about two weeks around June 21): at Puerco Pueblo, sunrise light and shadow pass across
  the rings of a small circular petroglyph, an ancestral calendar.
- **Monsoon storms** (July–August): black clouds, lightning and rainbows over the badlands, then the Blue Mesa clays
  steaming and gleaming after rain.
- **Snow on the badlands** (winter): white frosting on red and blue hills, logs dusted with snow.
- **Route 66 centennial** (2026): the Mother Road turned 100 this year; the park added a special 1926–2026 stamp.
- **Pronghorn on the plains**: small herds sprint across the grass beside the road at dawn.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a portrait **512 × 1024**-tile map following the park road north to south. Near true scale
  (~1.5–2 m per tile): the Painted Desert rim from Tawa to Kachina Point and the Inn (~1 km), Puerco Pueblo, Newspaper
  Rock, the Blue Mesa trail, Agate Bridge, Crystal Forest, and the Rainbow Forest Museum with Giant Logs, Long Logs and
  Agate House (~2 km). Compressed ~15×: the road between stops and the grasslands. The Painted Desert basin becomes a
  vast backdrop below the rim; the I-40 corridor, a band with a bridge, passing trucks and trains.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the Painted Desert Visitor Center (1960s modern courtyard), the Painted Desert Inn (pueblo
  revival walls, Kabotie murals, a trading-post counter), the Route 66 Studebaker, the Rainbow Forest Museum (fossil
  skeletons, the wall of letters from people who returned stolen wood). 3D landmarks: Agate Bridge, The Tepees, Blue
  Mesa's pedestal logs, Newspaper Rock, Puerco Pueblo's walls, Agate House, Pilot Rock, The Flattops.
- **Activities**: short walks among logs (sort them by colour: red jasper, yellow, amethyst, crystal cores); photo spots
  (the Painted Desert at sunset, Agate Bridge, The Tepees, Blue Mesa, the Studebaker, Newspaper Rock through a scope);
  the field guide (pronghorn, prairie dogs, collared lizards); a look-don't-take fossil hunt reported to a ranger; the
  solstice programme at Puerco Pueblo; Junior Ranger booklet, a natural home for the NPS Junior Paleontologist badge;
  vehicles: car (and a classic Route 66 car), bike on the park road, horse; permit backpacking in the wilderness.
- **Passport stamps**: the official list has Petrified Forest, The Painted Desert, the Painted Desert Inn and the new
  « 1926–2026 Route 66 Centennial » stamp, kept at the visitor centres, the Inn and the Rainbow Forest Museum.
- **With friends / with the world**: sunset on the Painted Desert rim is the gathering; a convoy of classic cars down
  Route 66 and the park road; a « find the rainbow » log-colour challenge; the solstice sunrise at Puerco Pueblo;
  group photos at Agate Bridge and the Studebaker.
- **Rules of the park** — **leave every piece of wood**: taking petrified wood is theft, and the game can make it a
  story (a pocketed chip brings « bad luck » until returned to the museum's conscience pile, as real visitors write).
  Also: stay on trails at archaeological sites; never touch petroglyphs; keep off Agate Bridge and pedestal logs;
  respect closed areas and private lands inside the boundary; be out by 5 p.m. unless you hold a wilderness permit.
- **Implementation challenges**: banded badlands (stripes following the height levels) · logs as many varied props
  (segments, cross-sections with rings and crystals) · a huge basin seen from a rim, below the camera · an interstate
  and a railway crossing the map without access · day-only park hours · a museum interior that reads well.
