# North Cascades National Park — `NOCA`

> Washington · established October 2, 1968 · 2,042.8 km² (504,781 acres; the whole complex with Ross Lake and Lake Chelan
> national recreation areas ~2,770 km², ~684,000 acres) · 46,925 recreation visits (2025, the national park unit only) ·
> ~110–2,810 m · NPS region PWR (today DOI Region 9, Columbia–Pacific Northwest)
>
> The « American Alps »: a sea of jagged, glacier-hung granite and gneiss peaks (300+ glaciers, the most in the lower 48)
> cut by deep valleys, run as one complex with two recreation areas — the Skagit River's chain of turquoise reservoirs
> along the North Cascades Highway, and the roadless village of Stehekin at the head of Lake Chelan. In Hearthlight it
> should feel like a mountain road trip that ends in a boat: a highway threading between spires and milky-green lakes, then
> a ferry to a valley where the road goes nowhere.

## Reading the map

North up, the complex is an irregular block of the Cascade Range about 70 km square pressed against the Canadian border, drawn as **three pieces of green that touch**. The **Ross Lake National Recreation Area** is a narrow corridor that runs from the west edge (near Marblemount) east along the Skagit River and then north up the long, thin, north–south **Ross Lake** to the border — it splits the national park in two. North of the highway and west of Ross Lake is the park's **north unit**: the Picket Range (a wall of spires with glaciers on its north face), Mount Shuksan on the far north-west corner (seen from the Mount Baker ski area, outside the park), Copper Ridge and the Chilliwack valley. South of the highway is the **south unit**: Eldorado Peak, the Boston Glacier (the park's largest), Forbidden Peak, **Cascade Pass** and Goode Mountain (2,810 m, the highest point). At the south-east corner the **Lake Chelan National Recreation Area** follows the Stehekin River valley down to the head of **Lake Chelan**, a 50-mile fjord-like lake whose far end (the town of Chelan) is off the map to the south-east.

The spine is **SR 20, the North Cascades Highway**, drawn in yellow from west to east. From Marblemount (the Wilderness Information Center) it follows the Skagit upstream into the recreation area, past the Copper Creek raft launch and the Goodell Creek campgrounds to **Newhalem**, the Seattle City Light company town where the **North Cascades Visitor Center** sits across the river. Then it enters the Skagit gorge: **Gorge Lake** and Gorge Dam, Gorge Creek Falls, the village of **Diablo** and Diablo Dam, then the turquoise, many-armed **Diablo Lake** (the Environmental Learning Center on its north shore, **Colonial Creek** campground on its Thunder Arm, the **Diablo Lake Overlook** above it). Past the Ross Dam trailhead and the **Ross Lake overlooks**, the highway leaves the lakes at Ruby Creek and climbs south-east out of the complex through national forest along Granite Creek to **Rainy Pass** (1,480 m, where the Pacific Crest Trail crosses) and **Washington Pass** (1,669 m, overlook under Liberty Bell and the Early Winters Spires), then drops east to Mazama and Winthrop. It is closed in winter from about milepost 134 (Ross Dam) to the east side.

Everything else is reached on foot or by boat. **Ross Lake** (the reservoir behind Ross Dam) runs 22 mi / 35 km north from the dam to **Hozomeen** at the Canadian border — with the floating cabins of **Ross Lake Resort** at its south end (reached by trail over Ross Dam or by the resort's boat-and-truck shuttle from Diablo Lake), 19 boat-in camps along its shores, the East Bank Trail on its east side and Desolation Peak's fire lookout above it. From Marblemount the gravel **Cascade River Road** runs ~23 mi south-east into the south unit to the **Cascade Pass trailhead** under Johannesburg Mountain; the trail climbs to Cascade Pass and continues down the other side into the Stehekin valley — the classic crossing of the range. **Stehekin** itself has no road from outside: the ferries come up the lake to **Stehekin Landing** (Golden West Visitor Center, lodge, post office), and a single valley road runs ~12 mi / 20 km north-west past Harlequin campground, Buckner Orchard and Rainbow Falls to **High Bridge**, where the PCT crosses and the road ends.

Relief and water organise everything: deep, steep-walled U-shaped valleys at 100–600 m (the Skagit, Thunder Creek, Big Beaver, the Stehekin) separate crest after crest of 2,400–2,800 m peaks. The wet west side (Skagit) and dry east side (Stehekin, Washington Pass) look like two different parks. The lakes along the highway are reservoirs of the Skagit hydroelectric project, their famous milky-turquoise colour coming from glacial flour.

### Water

- **The Skagit River** flows west out of Canada, through Ross Lake, Diablo Lake and Gorge Lake (three reservoirs behind Seattle City Light's Ross, Diablo and Gorge dams) and on past Newhalem and Marblemount to Puget Sound. The lakes are a startling milky turquoise from glacial flour carried down by Thunder Creek and others. The Skagit is the only Washington river with all five Pacific salmon species; in winter chum carcasses draw hundreds of bald eagles to the river below Marblemount.
- **Ross Lake**: ~22 mi / 35 km long, reaching into British Columbia; boat-in camps, a floating resort, cold clear water.
- **Diablo Lake**: small, many-armed, the brightest turquoise, with Thunder Arm reaching south to Colonial Creek.
- **Lake Chelan**: a glacier-carved fjord lake ~50 mi / 80 km long and ~450 m deep (one of the deepest lakes in the US, its floor below sea level), fed at its head by the Stehekin River.
- **Glaciers**: 300+ glaciers and countless snowfields — the most heavily glaciated area in the US outside Alaska, most of them shrinking fast. Boston Glacier (south unit) is the largest; others: Neve, Colonial, Inspiration, Klawatti, McAllister, Sahale, the Challenger and Terror glaciers of the Pickets.
- **Falls**: Ladder Creek, Gorge Creek, Rainbow (Stehekin, 95 m), countless snowmelt ribbons on valley walls.
- **Rivers & creeks**: Thunder Creek, Big Beaver and Little Beaver (into Ross Lake), the Cascade River (west), the Chilliwack (north unit, into Canada), the Stehekin River (south-east, into Lake Chelan), Bridge Creek, Agnes Creek.

### Relief

From ~110 m on the Skagit near Marblemount to 2,810 m at Goode Mountain, in some places 2,000 m of relief in 5 km. The North Cascades are not volcanoes (except Mount Baker outside the park) but an old crystalline range carved by ice: horns, arêtes, spires and cirques (the Picket Range, Liberty Bell, the Early Winters Spires), hanging glaciers on north faces, and deep glacial trough valleys with flat floors and near-vertical walls — the Skagit gorge, Thunder Creek, the Stehekin. Passes (Cascade, Rainy, Washington, Easy) are notches at 1,500–2,100 m. In the game: valley floors as long flat corridors with river and road; walls as stacked cliff levels; spires and horns as 3D peaks (Liberty Bell, Shuksan, the Pickets); dams as big 3D structures with water stepping down from lake to lake; the Cascade Pass trail as long switchback stairs; Lake Chelan as a long narrow water channel on the south-east edge.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: west side — old-growth western redcedar, western hemlock and Douglas-fir with moss, sword fern and devil's club on the Skagit floor (100–600 m) → Pacific silver fir forest → subalpine fir, mountain hemlock and heather-huckleberry meadows (1,300–1,900 m) → rock, snow and ice. East side — ponderosa pine and Douglas-fir with open grass in the Stehekin valley and around Lake Chelan (300–900 m), then Engelmann spruce, subalpine fir and **subalpine larch** basins near the crest (Maple, Cutthroat, Blue Lake), then granite.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#3FA8A0` Diablo Lake's glacial turquoise · `#2B6F8F` Ross Lake's deep blue-green · `#8FB9B0` milky Skagit / Thunder Creek · `#2D4A30` west-side old-growth · `#C9A227` October larch gold · `#BDB8AE` pale granite and gneiss (Liberty Bell, the Pickets) · `#E9F0F4` glacier white · `#6E8B4E` subalpine meadow · `#C8B48A` sunny east-side grass and pine needles (Stehekin) · `#B83A2E` Buckner Orchard apples.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): black bear (valley bottoms and huckleberry slopes) · mountain goat (cliffs of the passes; some were moved here from Olympic in 2018–2020) · hoary marmot & American pika (Cascade Pass, Sahale Arm) · black-tailed and mule deer (west vs east side) · gray wolf (packs have returned to the region) · wolverine (rare) · Cascade red fox · bald eagle (Skagit, December–February) · American dipper (every creek) · harlequin duck (fast rivers) · Clark's nutcracker (east-side passes) · northern spotted owl & marbled murrelet (old growth) · chinook, coho, chum, pink and sockeye salmon, steelhead and bull trout (the Skagit). Grizzly bears are nearly gone from the ecosystem (a 2024 federal decision plans to restore them).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): western redcedar (Trail of the Cedars) · devil's club · subalpine larch (gold late September–mid October) · pink mountain heather & partridgefoot · glacier lily (at melt-out) · subalpine lupine & magenta paintbrush (Cascade Pass, July–August) · mountain huckleberry · ponderosa pine (Stehekin) · Buckner's apple trees.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter closes the highway and buries the passes (avalanche country); Newhalem stays low and rainy, Stehekin gets snow and sun. Spring brings waterfalls pouring off every wall and the May reopening of SR 20 (snow walls at Washington Pass). Summer: the high country melts out in July; hot and dry on the east side, wildfire smoke possible in August. Autumn: golden larches, red huckleberry, salmon runs; the first snows by October.
- **Night**: dark skies over Ross Lake, Colonial Creek and Stehekin (ranger astronomy programmes in summer); the lights of the dams and of Newhalem; stars over the boat-in camps.
- **Sounds**: roaring snowmelt creeks and waterfalls on every wall; the hum of the powerhouses at Newhalem and Diablo; the ferry's engine and horn on Lake Chelan; loons and paddles on Ross Lake; marmot whistles at Cascade Pass; glacier ice cracking; eagles and ravens over the Skagit in winter.

## Moments

- **The larch turn**: subalpine larches on the east side (Maple Pass, Cutthroat Pass, Blue Lake) turn gold for two or three weeks from late September to mid October.
- **Diablo Lake's turquoise**: most vivid in mid to late summer, when glacial melt is highest.
- **The highway opens**: SR 20 usually reopens in early May after crews clear the avalanche chutes and snow walls at Washington Pass; it closes again in late November.
- **Salmon and eagles**: salmon spawn in the Skagit in late summer–autumn (pink salmon in odd years); hundreds of bald eagles gather on the river from December to February.
- **The Stehekin ferry**: the Lady of the Lake's run up the 50-mile lake, arriving at the landing mid-day, is the daily event of the village.
- **Wildflowers at Cascade Pass and Sahale Arm**: late July–August.
- **Lookout summers**: fire lookouts on Desolation, Sourdough, Copper Ridge and Hidden Lake — Jack Kerouac spent 1956 on Desolation Peak.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a **1280 × 1152-tile** map for a ~75 × 90 km complex (~40× compression) built as **two linked halves**: the **Highway 20 corridor** across the middle (Marblemount → Newhalem → Gorge → Diablo → Ross Lake → Rainy & Washington passes) with Ross Lake reaching north to Hozomeen, and **Stehekin** in the south-east at the head of a long Lake Chelan channel that leaves the map (the ferry arrives from off-map). The Cascade Pass trail links them across the south unit. Near true scale: Newhalem and the VC, the Diablo Lake Overlook with the lake below, Colonial Creek campground, Ross Dam and the resort, Washington Pass hairpin and overlook, Cascade Pass, Stehekin Landing and the first mile of valley. Compressed ×10–30: Ross Lake (35 km → ~1.2 km), the highway between hubs, the Stehekin valley road, Lake Chelan. The north unit's Pickets and Chilliwack can be a mostly visual, climbable backdrop.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: hubs at Newhalem (VC, company town), Diablo Lake (Colonial Creek and the overlook), Ross Lake Resort (floating cabins), Rainy/Washington passes, and Stehekin (landing, VC, bakery, orchard). 3D landmarks: the three dams (Gorge, Diablo's arch, Ross), Liberty Bell and the Early Winters Spires, Mount Shuksan, the Picket Range, the fire lookouts (Sourdough, Desolation, Hidden Lake, Copper Ridge), the Diablo Lake ferry dock, Rainbow Falls, the Old Stehekin School, the ferry *Lady of the Lake*. Interiors: the North Cascades VC, a Ross Lake Resort floating cabin, a fire lookout cab (Osborne fire finder, glass walls), the log schoolhouse, the Golden West VC.
- **Activities**: hiking (above); **photo spots**: Diablo Lake Overlook, Ross Lake Overlook, Gorge Creek Falls, Washington Pass Overlook, Cascade Pass, Sahale Arm, Rainbow Falls, Buckner Orchard, the ferry at Stehekin Landing, a lookout at sunset; **water**: canoe/kayak Diablo and Ross lakes (boat-in camps), Skagit Tours boat trips on Diablo Lake, rafting the Skagit from Goodell Creek/Copper Creek, the ferry up Lake Chelan, fishing; **bike** the Stehekin valley road and the highway; **horse** trips from Stehekin; **ranger programmes**: evening talks at Newhalem and Colonial Creek, Stehekin walks; **Young Ranger** ideas: follow a drop of meltwater from a glacier through three dams, measure a glacier's retreat from old photos, identify five salmon species, spot a fire from a lookout and report its bearing with the fire finder, learn the valley's homesteading history at Buckner; **climbing** (Liberty Bell, Sahale, Eldorado) as guided mini-expeditions; winter: the highway closed, snowshoeing at the Newhalem end and in Stehekin.
- **Passport stamps**: the official cancellation list (2025) gives North Cascades NP stamps at Newhalem, Marblemount, Stehekin, Sedro-Woolley and Chelan; Ross Lake NRA at Newhalem, Sedro-Woolley and Marblemount; Lake Chelan NRA at Newhalem, Stehekin, Sedro-Woolley and Chelan. In practice: the North Cascades Visitor Center (Newhalem), the Marblemount WIC, the Golden West VC (Stehekin), the Forest Info Center & park HQ in Sedro-Woolley and the Chelan ranger station.
- **With friends / with the world**: a **Cascade Pass crossing** as a party quest — drive to the trailhead, cross the pass, catch the Stehekin shuttle and the ferry home; a **Ross Lake paddle** with a boat-in camp; a lookout network where friends on different summits spot « smokes » and triangulate them; a larch-season photo hunt. Open-world gathering spots: the Diablo Lake Overlook, Colonial Creek campground, Stehekin Landing when the ferry arrives, Washington Pass overlook, the Ross Lake Resort docks. Group photos: on Diablo Dam, at Cascade Pass, on the bow of the ferry.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): permits for every night in the backcountry; bear canisters or hangs; stay on the trail in the fragile meadows at Cascade Pass (the park restored them after decades of trampling); never feed marmots, jays or goats; keep away from goats on narrow trails; pack out everything; check avalanche and road status; boat-in camps only where designated; respect the Canadian border at Hozomeen.
- **Implementation challenges**: **extreme relief** (spires and horns, 2,000 m walls, hanging glaciers) that must read from a valley road; **glacier-flour turquoise water** in stepped reservoirs with dams (water on several levels); a **ferry-only village** and a long lake that leaves the map; a highway that **closes for the winter** (seasonal map); **two climates** across the crest (wet green west, sunny dry east) and larches that change colour; fire lookouts with 360° views.
