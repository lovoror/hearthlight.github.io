# Kobuk Valley National Park — `KOVA`

> Alaska · established December 2, 1980 (national monument since December 1, 1978) · 7,084.9 km² (1,750,716 acres) · 7,786 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~9–1,451 m · NPS region AKR (Alaska)
>
> A wide Arctic river valley 35 miles north of the Arctic Circle where golden sand dunes 100 feet high rise out of the spruce and tundra, and where the Western Arctic caribou herd swims the Kobuk River at Onion Portage, a crossing Iñupiat hunters have used for about 9,000 years. In Hearthlight it should feel like a desert that should not exist: land on the sand, walk barefoot on warm dunes under a midnight sun, then sit on a bluff above the river waiting for the caribou.

## Reading the map

On the Western Arctic Parklands map (north up), Kobuk Valley is the compact, dark-green block in the south-centre, much smaller than the pale-green Noatak National Preserve that wraps around it to the north and west. The park is roughly square with jagged northern and eastern edges. Its **north** is the crest of the **Baird Mountains** (the label « B A I R D  M O U N T A I N S » arches across the top), with the park's high point **Mount Angayukaqsraq** (4,760 ft / 1,451 m) on the northern boundary; the smaller **Akiak Mountains** rise in the north-east. Its **south** is the valley floor of the **Kobuk River**, which crosses the southern third of the park from east to west in wide meanders; beyond the southern boundary the **Waring Mountains** close the valley. The Jade Mountains stand just outside the north-east corner.

Rivers come down from the Baird Mountains to the Kobuk: from west to east the **Kallarichuk**, the **Salmon River** (a Wild River), the Tutuksuk, the Kaliguricheark, Akiak Creek and the Akillik; the Hunt River joins from the north in the east. Two seasonal **ranger stations** (house symbols) mark the park's river gates: **Kallarichuk** at the west boundary, below the Kallarichuk Hills, and **Onion Portage** at the east boundary, at a sharp bend of the Kobuk under the Jade Mountains. South of the river, dotted outlines mark the **Great Kobuk Sand Dunes** (between Kavet Creek and the east) and the **Little Kobuk Sand Dunes** (south of Onion Portage); a third field, the Hunt River Dunes, is not labelled on this sheet.

Around the park, the map shows the Iñupiat villages that live from this valley: **Ambler** just east of Onion Portage, Shungnak and Kobuk farther up-river, **Kiana** and **Noorvik** down-river to the west, and **Kotzebue** (« Park Headquarters and Information Center ») on the coast of Kotzebue Sound at the far west, about 80–100 miles away, where the Kobuk ends in a wide delta in Hotham Inlet. The dashed **Arctic Circle** runs across the bottom of the sheet, south of the Waring Mountains; Selawik National Wildlife Refuge lies south of the park, Gates of the Arctic to the east.

### Water

The **Kobuk River** flows west across the park for ~61 miles (98 km) in big loops with oxbow lakes, cutbanks and sand and gravel bars, on its way to Hotham Inlet and Kotzebue Sound; it is the park's highway in summer (boats) and winter (snowmachines on the ice). It carries chum salmon (mid-July to early September), sheefish (inconnu), whitefish, pike and grayling, fished from nets at family fish camps. Clear tributaries come down from the Baird Mountains (Kallarichuk, Salmon, Tutuksuk, Kaliguricheark, Akiak, Akillik, Hunt); the Salmon River is a designated Wild River. Near the dunes, springs and creeks (Kavet Creek) soak the sand and can turn it into « quicksand ». The river freezes in October and breaks up in May; permafrost underlies the tundra. River bluffs expose Ice Age sediments with mammoth and other fossils.

### Relief

From ~9 m on the Kobuk at the park's west boundary to **Mount Angayukaqsraq, 1,451 m**. The land is a broad valley: flat river terraces and meander belt (10–80 m), the dune fields on a sandy plateau just south of the river (active dunes rising ~30 m / 100 ft above their bases, with steep slip faces and wind-rippled backs), then rolling forested hills and tussock slopes climbing north to the rounded, treeless Baird Mountains (1,000–1,450 m) and south to the Waring Mountains. In the game: a river ribbon with one-level banks and sandbars, a dune field made of stacked sand terraces with slip-face « cliffs » that you slide down, a forest-edge transition where trees swallow old dunes, and rounded 3D mountains as the northern skyline.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: river terraces with white spruce, paper birch, aspen and balsam poplar, willow thickets on the bars; the dunes are bare sand edged by sparse grasses, wild rye, sedges and the endemic **Kobuk locoweed**, then mosses and lichens, shrubs and finally forest creeping in; tussock tundra and dwarf shrubs on the slopes; alpine tundra and rock on the Baird Mountains. It is the northern edge of the forest.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#E2C27A` sunlit dune sand · `#C9A45C` sand in shade and ripples · `#2F4A36` spruce forest · `#9DB26A` summer tundra · `#C44E6E` fireweed pink · `#D08A2E` autumn birch and aspen · `#6E8F9C` Kobuk River water · `#A9A38F` gravel bars · `#B7C2B0` reindeer lichen · `#8A8F92` Baird Mountains rock.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **caribou** of the Western Arctic Herd (hundreds of thousands; they cross the Kobuk and the dunes in spring and especially late summer–autumn), moose, grizzly and black bears, gray wolf, wolverine, red fox, lynx, porcupine, beaver, muskrat, snowshoe hare; birds — sandhill crane, tundra swan, golden eagle, spruce grouse, willow ptarmigan, migrating waterfowl; fish — **sheefish** (inconnu), chum salmon, whitefish, northern pike, Arctic grayling, Arctic char.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): Kobuk locoweed (*Oxytropis kobukensis*, found only on these dunes, June–July), fireweed (turns the tundra pink in July), salmonberry / cloudberry (*aqpik*, August), blueberry, crowberry and cranberry (August–September), Eskimo potato (*masru*), sourdock, wild rye on the dunes, white spruce.
- **Weather & seasons**: May break-up and spring caribou heading north; June–July midnight sun, heat and mosquitoes, thunderstorms, sand blowing on windy days; August–September berries, fall colours and the big caribou crossing; October freeze-up; winter −20 to −46 °C, short grey days, the valley silent under snow, travel by snowmachine and dog team.
- **Night**: no sunset from about June 3 to July 9; at the winter solstice the sun shows for only ~1.5 hours; aurora from late August through spring over the dunes; very dark skies.
- **Sounds**: wind hissing over sand ripples, the dry crunch of dune slopes sliding, the click of caribou tendons and splashing as a herd swims the river, sandhill cranes' rolling calls, an outboard motor and voices at a fish camp, a floatplane on the river, total silence at night.

## Moments

- **The caribou crossing at Onion Portage** (late August–October southbound, spring northbound): hundreds to thousands of caribou swim the Kobuk at the bend; Iñupiat hunters still wait for them there, as people have for about 9,000 years.
- **Midnight sun on the dunes** (June 3–July 9): golden light at 2 am on 100-foot dunes; summer afternoons can reach ~38 °C (100 °F) on the sand.
- **Caribou tracks across the sand**: in spring and autumn the Great Kobuk dunes are stamped with hoof prints.
- **Fish camp season** (mid-July–early September): salmon and sheefish drying on wooden racks along the river.
- **Berry time** (August–September): families picking salmonberries, blueberries and cranberries on the tundra.
- **Fireweed pink** (July) and **autumn gold** (late August–early September) along the valley.
- **Aurora and the dark season** (late August–April): northern lights over the dunes, ~1.5 hours of sun at midwinter.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a curated map of **~1,024 × 768 tiles**. The **Great Kobuk Sand Dunes** fill the centre-south at ~600 × 420 tiles (the real field is ~8 × 8 km, compressed ~8×: ridges, bowls and slip faces kept at readable scale, the dune edge swallowed by forest); the **Kobuk River** runs across the top third as a meandering ribbon (~100 km of park river compressed ~1:60) with sandbars, a fish camp and the portage shelter; **Onion Portage** sits at the east end (~256 × 192 tiles: the bend, the bluff with house pits, the Giddings cabin ranger station, the Little Kobuk dunes behind), **Kallarichuk** at the west end. The Baird Mountains are a skyline of rounded 3D peaks to the north. **Kotzebue's Northwest Arctic Heritage Center** is a separate small gateway interior reached by a **plane hop** (and linked to the other Alaska vignettes: Gates of the Arctic's Bettles and Anaktuvuk Pass are next door).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: a bush plane parked on the sand (the dunes are a runway), the Onion Portage ranger station in Giddings' 1964 cabin, a family fish camp with drying racks (shown respectfully, as part of living Iñupiat subsistence), the heritage center museum interior in Kotzebue (Arctic wildlife, Iñupiaq culture); 3D landmarks: the highest dune crest, Mount Angayukaqsraq, the Jade Mountains; a boat on the Kobuk.
- **Activities**: land on the dunes and camp; dune walking with slides down slip faces and sand-temperature heat management (shade, water); track reading (caribou, bear, wolf, fox prints in the sand); watching the caribou crossing from the bluff (a timed, seasonal event — quiet, no approach); floating the Kobuk by canoe; fishing for sheefish and grayling; berry picking; photo spots — the dune crest at midnight sun, caribou swimming the Kobuk at Onion Portage, Kobuk locoweed in bloom, the aurora over the dunes; learning Iñupiaq words (Paatitaaq, aqpik, masru); Junior Ranger via the heritage center; winter mode with dog teams on the frozen river.
- **Passport stamps**: « Kotzebue » (Northwest Arctic Heritage Center, which also holds the Western Arctic National Parklands, Noatak, Cape Krusenstern, Bering Land Bridge and Gates of the Arctic stamps).
- **With friends / with the world**: a party flies in together and sets one camp on the sand; group dune races and slide contests down the slip faces; a shared silent watch at Onion Portage when the herd arrives (everyone must stay still for the crossing to happen); canoe convoys on the river; gathering spots — the plane-parking area on the dunes, the Onion Portage bluff at crossing time, the Kotzebue heritage center lobby on a weather-delay day.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): respect private land and fish camps along the Kobuk (detour around any cabin, camp or net) and never interfere with subsistence hunting or fishing; do not disturb or approach swimming caribou; use bear-resistant food containers (loaned in Kotzebue); camp on durable surfaces — sand is fine, fragile dune plants are not (stay off the locoweed); beware wet « quicksand » near springs and creeks; pack out everything; leave artifacts and fossils in place (Onion Portage is an archaeological site); prepare for weather delays and hypothermia even in summer.
- **Implementation challenges**: sand dunes as terrain (wind ripples, slip faces, sliding and sinking footsteps, drifting sand, heat shimmer); herds of hundreds of caribou swimming a river; a large meandering river usable by canoe; midnight sun and polar-night lighting; bush planes landing on sand and water; aurora; respectful depiction of living subsistence culture.
