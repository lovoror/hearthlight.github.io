# Olympic National Park — `OLYM`

> Washington · established June 29, 1938 · 3,733.8 km² (922,649 acres) · 3,584,187 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 0–2,432 m · NPS region PWR (today DOI Region 9, Columbia–Pacific Northwest)
>
> Three worlds on one peninsula: a wild Pacific coast of sea stacks and drift logs, moss-hung temperate rain
> forests in the western valleys, and a glaciated dome of mountains in the middle, all ringed by one highway with
> dead-end spur roads poking in. In Hearthlight it should feel wet, green and deep: you drive the ring, pick a
> spoke, and each spoke opens onto a completely different small world — surf, moss, lake, meadow, ice.

## Reading the map

North up, the Olympic Peninsula is a rough square with its north-west corner (Cape Flattery) poking at Vancouver Island: the Pacific on the west, the Strait of Juan de Fuca on the north, the long fjord of Hood Canal on the east. The park is two pieces. The **main unit** is a big irregular blob in the middle of the peninsula (about 60 % of the land), a dome of glaciated mountains whose edge sends « tendrils » out along the river valleys; its northern edge sits ~8 km inland from the strait and scoops in **Lake Crescent**, its western tendrils follow the Bogachiel, Hoh and Queets valleys, and its south-western arm wraps the north shore of **Lake Quinault**. The **coastal strip** is a separate ribbon along the Pacific, from Shi Shi Beach in the north (just south of the Makah Reservation) to South Beach below Kalaloch, broken only where the Quileute (La Push, at the Quillayute River) and Hoh reservations sit on the river mouths; the Quinault Reservation fills the coast south of it. Between the two pieces lie logged national-forest and state lands and the timber town of **Forks**.

The road network is a **ring with spokes**. US 101 runs all the way round the outside and never enters the heart of the park. On the north side it passes Sequim and **Port Angeles** — the park headquarters and the **Olympic National Park Visitor Center** with the Wilderness Information Center sit on the town's south edge — then runs 20 km west to follow the south shore of Lake Crescent (Storm King ranger station, Lake Crescent Lodge, Fairholme at the west end), swings south through the Sol Duc valley to Forks, and on down to the Hoh. There it bends west, reaches the ocean at **Ruby Beach**, and runs south along the coastal strip past the numbered Kalaloch beaches and **Kalaloch Lodge** before leaving the coast at Queets, crossing the Quinault Reservation to Amanda Park on **Lake Quinault**, and heading south to Aberdeen. The east side of the ring is 101 hugging Hood Canal from Hoodsport up to Discovery Bay.

The spokes, clockwise from Port Angeles: **Hurricane Ridge Road** climbs 27 km from town past the Heart O' the Hills entrance and three short tunnels to the **Hurricane Ridge** meadows at 1,600 m (with a paved spur west to Hurricane Hill and the gravel, cliff-edge **Obstruction Point Road** 12 km east); **Deer Park Road** climbs a steep gravel ridge to Blue Mountain in the rain shadow; **Olympic Hot Springs Road** goes up the Elwha but is gated to cars at Madison Falls; **Sol Duc Road** leaves 101 at the west end of Lake Crescent and runs 22 km up the Sol Duc to the hot-springs resort and the trail to Sol Duc Falls. On the coast, WA 112 follows the strait west to Sekiu, from where the **Hoko–Ozette Road** reaches the **Ozette** ranger station on the north tip of Lake Ozette; WA 110 goes west from Forks and splits into **Mora Road** (north bank of the Quillayute: Mora campground, **Rialto Beach**) and **La Push Road** (south bank: Third and Second Beach trailheads, the Quileute village of La Push). South of Forks the **Upper Hoh Road** runs 29 km up the Hoh to the **Hoh Rain Forest Visitor Center**; gravel roads go up the Queets; **North Shore** and **South Shore roads** circle Lake Quinault to the Quinault Rain Forest ranger station, Graves Creek and North Fork. On the east side, short roads climb from Hood Canal: to **Staircase** (via Hoodsport and Lake Cushman), the Hamma Hamma, the Duckabush and the washed-out Dosewallips.

Everything inside the ring is roadless wilderness (95 % of the park is the Daniel J. Evans Wilderness). **Mount Olympus** (2,432 m) stands west of centre with its glaciers feeding the Hoh; the Bailey Range curves north-east of it; the Elwha runs north through the middle; the eastern peaks (Mount Deception, Mount Constance, The Brothers) are steeper and drier. The only ways across are long trails: the Hoh River Trail to Glacier Meadows under Mount Olympus, and the Elwha–Low Divide–North Fork Quinault route that crosses the park north to south. The coast has no road except 101's Kalaloch stretch: the North Coast (Shi Shi to Rialto) and South Coast (Third Beach to Oil City) are beach routes cut by headlands, rope ladders and river mouths that can't be forded, governed by the tide.

Water organises everything: rivers radiate from the central dome in every direction like spokes of their own — the Elwha north to the strait, the Sol Duc, Bogachiel and Calawah west into the Quillayute, the Hoh, Queets and Quinault south-west to the ocean, the Skokomish, Hamma Hamma, Duckabush and Dosewallips east to Hood Canal, the Dungeness and Gray Wolf north-east. The west-facing valleys (Hoh, Queets, Quinault, Bogachiel) catch the Pacific storms and hold the rain forests; the north-east (Deer Park, Sequim) sits in a rain shadow.

### Water

- **Rivers** radiate from the central dome: the **Elwha** flows north through the middle to the strait west of Port Angeles (its two dams — Elwha and Glines Canyon — came down in 2011–2014, the largest dam removal in US history; salmon are back); the **Sol Duc**, **Bogachiel** and **Calawah** flow west and join as the short **Quillayute**, which meets the sea between Rialto Beach and La Push; the **Hoh** (milky with glacial flour from Mount Olympus), **Queets** and **Quinault** flow south-west to the ocean; the **North Fork Skokomish**, **Hamma Hamma**, **Duckabush** and **Dosewallips** flow east into Hood Canal; the **Dungeness** and **Gray Wolf** north-east to the strait. The river mouths of the Quillayute and Hoh can't be forded (map notes « No river ford possible »).
- **Lakes**: **Lake Crescent** (~19 km along its bend, 190 m deep officially, strikingly clear blue because it is poor in nitrogen; landslide-dammed, draining by the Lyre River to the strait; home of the endemic Beardslee trout); **Lake Ozette** (Washington's third-largest natural lake, draining north by the Ozette River to the sea); **Lake Quinault** (on the Quinault River, owned and managed by the Quinault Indian Nation); **Lake Cushman** (a reservoir at Staircase, outside the park); dozens of cirque lakes (Seven Lakes Basin, Lake Angeles, Grand Valley, Royal Basin).
- **Coast**: ~73 mi / 117 km of park coast. Sand and cobble beaches, drift-log barricades, sea stacks and arches, headlands that close at high tide (overland trails with rope ladders, marked by round black-and-red targets), offshore islands that are national wildlife refuges and closed to people. Mixed tides: two highs and two lows of unequal height a day, ~2.5 m range (more at spring tides); the lowest daytime minus tides fall in late spring–summer mornings, winter minus tides at night.
- **Falls**: Marymere (27 m), Sol Duc, Madison, Merriman and Bunch (Quinault), the cliff falls of Enchanted Valley, the Salmon Cascades.
- **Ice**: ~180 glaciers (2009 count, down from 266 in 1982), the biggest on Mount Olympus (Blue, Hoh — ~4.9 km long, the park's longest — White, Humes, Jeffers, Hubert), others in the Bailey Range (Carrie) and on Mount Anderson (Eel, Anderson — nearly gone).
- **Springs**: Sol Duc Hot Springs (developed pools); undeveloped Olympic Hot Springs up Boulder Creek, now a long walk from the Elwha gate.

### Relief

From the surf line (0 m) to Mount Olympus (2,432 m on the brochure; recent surveys ~2,428 m) in about 50 km. The mountains are not a single range but a round dome of peaks cut by deep, steep-walled, flat-floored U-shaped valleys: the Hoh valley floor at the visitor centre is only 177 m, 20 km from the glaciers. Ridges and meadows on the north rim (Hurricane Ridge, Klahhane Ridge, Obstruction Point) sit at 1,600–1,900 m, a flat-topped high country above sheer north faces. The eastern peaks (Deception 2,374 m, Constance 2,360 m, The Brothers 2,093 m) rise steeply straight out of Hood Canal. On the coast: low forested terraces 20–60 m high end in eroding bluffs over the beaches, with headlands (Hoh Head, Teahwhit Head, Toleak Point, Cape Johnson) and hundreds of sea stacks. In the game: the coastal bluffs and headlands become one- and two-level cliffs with rope-ladder stairs; the river valleys are wide flat floors with high banked walls; Hurricane Ridge is a plateau with a steep north face and the road's switchbacks as stairs; Mount Olympus is a 3D glaciated peak visible from the ridge, the High Divide and the Hoh; Mount Storm King and Hurricane Hill as small 3D summits; sea stacks and Hole-in-the-Wall as 3D props.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: beach and drift logs (sea palm and kelp on the rocks) → a narrow Sitka spruce fringe bent by salt wind on the bluffs → lowland old-growth of western hemlock, western redcedar and Douglas-fir (0–600 m), which becomes **temperate rain forest** in the west valleys: giant Sitka spruce, bigleaf maples draped in clubmoss and licorice fern, sword-fern carpets, nurse logs with rows of saplings, elk-grazed open floors → montane silver fir (600–1,200 m) → subalpine fir and mountain hemlock clumps in wildflower meadows (1,200–1,800 m) → rock, snowfields and glaciers. The dry north-east (Deer Park) has open grassy slopes and lodgepole/Douglas-fir.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#2E5A36` deep spruce–hemlock canopy · `#86B04C` luminous clubmoss & moss carpets · `#4F6F3C` sword fern & salal understorey · `#B9B2A3` silvered drift logs · `#4A4F52` wet basalt of sea stacks and cobbles · `#C2A98B` grey-tan beach sand (`#B7856F` Ruby Beach's garnet tint) · `#2F6E8E` Lake Crescent's deep blue · `#8FB5B9` glacial-milk Hoh River · `#E6EFF3` glacier ice · `#C8BE7A` late-summer subalpine meadow.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): Roosevelt elk (Hoh, Queets, Quinault floors; herds in the Hoh campground; bugling Sept–Oct) · Olympic marmot (endemic; Hurricane Hill, Obstruction Point, May–Sept) · black-tailed deer (Hurricane Ridge meadows) · black bear (summer meadows, Seven Lakes Basin, Enchanted Valley, also on the beaches) · cougar (present, rarely seen) · banana slug (rain forest, every wet day) · coho and chinook salmon, steelhead (Sol Duc cascades, the Elwha) · sea otter and harbor seal (north coast, around Cape Alava) · gray whale (spring migration off the coast) · bald eagle (river mouths, Rialto) · tufted puffin & common murre (offshore islands in summer, with binoculars) · ochre sea star, giant green anemone, gooseneck barnacles (tidepools) · Pacific wren & varied thrush (the rain-forest voices) · American dipper (rivers). Mountain goats were introduced in the 1920s and mostly moved out in 2018–2020.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): Sitka spruce · western redcedar (the record Big Cedars) · bigleaf maple with clubmoss · sword fern · skunk cabbage (March–April in the Ozette bogs) · avalanche lily (right behind the melting snow on Hurricane Ridge, June–July) · Piper's bellflower (endemic, in rock cracks, July–Aug) · Flett's violet (endemic, June–July) · magenta paintbrush & subalpine lupine (July–August) · vine maple (red in October) · evergreen huckleberry & salal (berries late summer).
- **Weather & seasons**: winter is the long wet season — atmospheric rivers, rivers in flood, coastal storms with king tides and flying logs, snow on Hurricane Ridge (9–11 m a year) with the road open only on weekends. Spring is showery; the coast turns green, skunk cabbage glows in the bogs, whales pass. Summer (July–Sept) is dry and mild (18–24 °C in the lowlands), with morning fog on the coast and wildflowers up high. Autumn brings the first storms, salmon, elk bugling and yellow maples. Fire is rare in the rain forest but the dry east side burned in 2025 (Bear Gulch Fire).
- **Night**: dark skies on Hurricane Ridge and Deer Park (summer astronomy programmes), the Milky Way over the sea stacks, the glow of Port Angeles and Victoria to the north; owls (barred, northern spotted in the old growth), Pacific tree frogs chorusing at Ozette, stars reflected on Lake Crescent.
- **Sounds**: surf booming and cobbles rattling in the backwash; gulls, ravens and eagles on the coast; drip of rain from moss onto ferns; the varied thrush's long single note and the Pacific wren's endless trill; the roar of the Sol Duc falls and glacial rivers; elk bugles echoing in autumn; marmot whistles on the ridge; a creaking old-growth trunk in wind.

## Moments

- **Minus tides**: two tide cycles a day; the lowest daytime tides (below 0) come on spring-tide mornings from ~May to July, opening the tidepools at Hole-in-the-Wall, Second Beach and Kalaloch's Beach 4 and making Hole-in-the-Wall walk-through. At high tide headlands close and walkers take the ladders.
- **Salmon leap the Salmon Cascades** on the Sol Duc: coho jump the falls after the first autumn rains, late September to November (best in October). Chinook and coho also run up the freed Elwha.
- **The elk rut**: Roosevelt elk bulls bugle and gather harems in the Hoh and Quinault valleys, September–October; calves are born in late May–June.
- **Hurricane Ridge in bloom**: avalanche lilies right after snowmelt (June–July), then lupine, paintbrush and bistort, peaking mid-July to mid-August; marmots active mid-May to mid-September.
- **Gray whales migrate** north past the coast in March–May (visible from Kalaloch, Rialto and Cape Alava); a few stay all summer.
- **Winter storm season**: November–January swells, king tides and storm-driven drift logs at Rialto, Ruby and Kalaloch — storm-watching from the lodge; the same months bring 30-odd feet of snow to Hurricane Ridge (rope tows run on winter weekends).
- **Sunset behind the sea stacks** at Ruby Beach, Rialto Beach and Second Beach; sunrise over the Bailey Range from Hurricane Ridge.
- **Mount Olympus unveiled**: on clear summer mornings the glaciers light up pink from Hurricane Ridge and the High Divide.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a **1536 × 1280-tile** map (~2.3 × 1.9 km at ~1.5 m a tile) for a real peninsula of ~120 × 100 km, so ~50× compression on average — the heaviest in the connective forest and the long 101 drives, the lightest at the hotspots. Keep the **ring-and-spokes** structure: a single ring road around a roadless mountain core, with 8–10 spur valleys, and the coastal strip as a separate band along the west edge joined to the ring only at Ruby Beach/Kalaloch and by the Mora and Ozette spurs. Near true scale: the Hoh VC with the Hall of Mosses loop (1.3 km) and the Spruce trail; Rialto Beach to Hole-in-the-Wall (2.4 km → ~1 km); Ruby Beach; Kalaloch lodge, bluffs and Beach 4; Hurricane Ridge's meadow loops and the Hurricane Hill ridge (2.6 km → ~1 km); Sol Duc resort to the falls; Marymere Falls. Compressed ×10–20: Lake Crescent (19 km → ~1.5 km, still long and bent), the Ozette triangle (14.5 km → ~1.8 km), Lake Quinault. Heavily compressed or reduced to one valley: the Queets, the east-side valleys, the Bogachiel. Cut: Deer Park and Dosewallips could become trailhead « doors » only.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: gathering hubs at Port Angeles VC, Hurricane Ridge (a new lodge or the temporary ranger kiosk — the 1952 lodge burned in 2023), Lake Crescent Lodge, Sol Duc resort, the Hoh VC, Kalaloch Lodge, Mora campground and Lake Quinault. 3D landmarks: Mount Olympus with its glaciers, Mount Storm King, Hurricane Hill, the sea stacks and Hole-in-the-Wall arch, Abbey Island at Ruby Beach, Point of the Arches, the Glines Canyon spillway, Destruction Island's lighthouse offshore, the Kalaloch Tree of Life (a spruce standing on air over a washed-out gully), the record giant trees. Interiors: the Port Angeles VC, Lake Crescent Lodge's lobby and sunporch, the Sol Duc pools' changing house, the Hoh VC, the Enchanted Valley chalet, Humes Ranch cabin.
- **Activities**: hiking (the trails above); **photo spots**: Hurricane Ridge with Mount Olympus, Marymere Falls, Lake Crescent from Storm King, Sol Duc Falls from the bridge, Hall of Mosses, Rialto's driftwood, Hole-in-the-Wall at low tide, Ruby Beach sunset, the Tree of Life, the Ozette boardwalk, Enchanted Valley's chalet; **wildlife** spotting with a « three worlds » checklist (a marmot, an elk, a sea star); **tidepooling** with a tide clock; ranger talks (tidepool walks at Kalaloch and Mora, meadow walks at Hurricane Ridge, rain-forest walks at the Hoh, astronomy at the ridge); **Young Ranger** ideas: read a tide table and cross a headland before the water returns, count banana slugs on the Hall of Mosses, match the three elk-browsed « lines » in the rain forest, sort beach finds (look, don't take), spot a salmon jump at the cascades; **water**: kayak and canoe on Lake Crescent and Lake Ozette (paddle to Ericsons Bay), swim at the Devil's Punchbowl and Fairholme, soak at Sol Duc, raft the Elwha or Hoh; **bike** the Spruce Railroad Trail and the car-free Elwha road; winter snowshoeing, sledding and rope-tow skiing at Hurricane Ridge.
- **Passport stamps**: the official cancellation list (2025) has 13 for Olympic: Hoh Rain Forest · Hurricane Ridge · Eagle Ranger Station (Sol Duc) · Elwha Ranger Station · Ozette Ranger Station · Staircase Ranger Station · Kalaloch Ranger Station · Mora Ranger Station · Port Angeles WIC · Quinault Ranger Station · Storm King Ranger Station · Sol Duc Hot Springs · Hoodsport WIC. The park's own page lists where they are kept: the Port Angeles VC (all year), Hurricane Ridge and Hoh VCs (seasonal), Kalaloch RS (summer; at Kalaloch Lodge when closed), Staircase and Mora RS (summer, limited hours), Quinault RS. Junior Ranger badges at Port Angeles, the Hoh and Kalaloch.
- **With friends / with the world**: a **tide run** — the party must get round a headland before the tide closes it, one player reading the tide clock while others spot the target markers; carrying a drift log together to build a beach shelter; a relay around the ring (one friend at the ridge, one at the Hoh, one on the beach, each taking a « three worlds » photo at the same in-game hour); a salmon-jump count at the cascades. Open-world gathering spots: the Hurricane Ridge terrace at sunset, the Rialto driftwood (bonfire nights at Mora campground), the Hoh VC queue and Hall of Mosses, Lake Crescent's beaches, the Sol Duc pools. Group photos: in front of Hole-in-the-Wall, on the Hurricane Hill summit, under a Big Cedar.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): check the tide before walking the coast and never climb on logs in the surf; stay 50 m (150 ft) from elk, bears and all wildlife, never feed marmots or jays; walk gently in tidepools — touch lightly, never pry animals off, never take shells, driftwood or rocks; don't touch the petroglyphs; stay off the offshore islands (wildlife refuges; boats keep 200 yards away); store food in a canister on the coast (raccoons open anything); stay on the boardwalks and trails in the meadows; pack out everything; respect tribal lands at La Push, Hoh and Neah Bay.
- **Implementation challenges**: **tides** (a moving shoreline, headlands that open and close, stranding, arches walkable only at low water); **surf and drift logs** (animated waves on a sea edge, log piles as walkable terrain); **near-constant rain and fog** with dripping, moss-hung trees (hanging-moss meshes, fern-carpet ground); a large **glaciated 3D massif** readable from far away; seasonal **snow** and closed roads on the ridge; **salmon leaping** and hot-spring steam; a long, deep, clear **lake**; the **ring layout** (long drives want fast travel or a compressed car/bus ride); river fords and impassable river mouths.
