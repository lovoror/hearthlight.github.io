## Reading the map

The park is three pieces of Maine coast. The main one is **Mount Desert Island (MDI)**, a big granite island ~24 km across, joined to the mainland at its north-west corner by the Trenton bridge (ME 3). A narrow fjord-like inlet, **Somes Sound**, cuts 8 km north into the island from the south coast, splitting it into two lobes like a lobster claw. The **east side** is the busy heart of the park: a north–south row of granite mountains (Cadillac in the middle, Dorr and Champlain east of it, Pemetic, the Bubbles, Penobscot and Sargent to the west), with long glacial lakes lying between them in parallel troughs — **Eagle Lake**, **Bubble Pond**, **Jordan Pond**. The **west side** ("the quiet side") has a second, lower range (Beech Mountain, Acadia and St. Sauveur mountains, Western Mountain's Bernard and Mansell peaks) around Echo Lake and Long Pond, and runs south to **Bass Harbor Head Light** at the island's southern tip. The towns (Bar Harbor on the north-east shore, Northeast Harbor and Seal Harbor on the south coast, Southwest Harbor and Bass Harbor on the west lobe, Somesville at the head of the Sound) sit in the gaps between park land; the island's northern third is private.

The **Park Loop Road** (27 mi / 43 km, dark red on the map) is the organising line of the east side. It starts at the **Hulls Cove Visitor Center** on the north shore, runs south two-way above Bar Harbor, then — just past the ME 233 junction and the Cadillac Mountain entrance — turns into a one-way road heading east and south (clockwise): **Sieur de Monts** (nature centre, spring, wild gardens) at the foot of Dorr Mountain, then down the east coast as "Ocean Drive": the fee station, **Sand Beach** between the Beehive and Great Head, **Thunder Hole**, Monument Cove, **Otter Cliff** and **Otter Point**, across the Otter Cove causeway, past **Blackwoods Campground** and Little Hunters Beach, then west inland above Seal Harbor to the "End one-way traffic" sign at the **Jordan Pond** gatehouse. From the **Jordan Pond House** (tea and popovers at the pond's south end) the road runs two-way north between Jordan Pond and Bubble Pond, past Eagle Lake, back to the start. Off it, the 3.5 mi **Cadillac Summit Road** switchbacks up to the summit (seasonal vehicle reservations). Inside the loop, the yellow web of the **carriage roads** (45 mi) circles Eagle Lake, Jordan Pond, the Bubbles and Sargent Mountain, crossing streams and motor roads on 17 cut-stone bridges; the Island Explorer buses stop at the carriage-road entrances.

West of Somes Sound, the west side is reached by ME 102 (Somesville → Echo Lake → Southwest Harbor) and ME 102A (Bass Harbor → **Seawall** → Manset): Echo Lake Beach (the swimming lake), Beech Mountain's fire tower above Long Pond, the Carroll Homestead, then on the south coast the Seawall campground and picnic area, the Wonderland and Ship Harbor tide-pool walks and the lighthouse on Bass Harbor Head. Unpaved fire roads (Long Pond, Western Mountain, Seal Cove roads) thread the Western Mountain woods and close in winter.

Across Frenchman Bay, 10 km east of Bar Harbor, the **Schoodic Peninsula** is the park's only mainland district: a 6 mi one-way loop road around a wooded peninsula from Winter Harbor to Birch Harbor, with **Schoodic Point**'s wave-washed ledges at the tip, Schoodic Head (134 m) inland, the Schoodic Woods campground and 8 mi of bike paths. **Isle au Haut**, 30 km south-west of MDI beyond Deer Isle, is roadless for visitors: half the island is park, reached by the mail-boat ferry from Stonington to the Town Landing (and, in summer, Duck Harbor, with its five-shelter campground). Smaller park pieces dot the islands south of MDI: Little Cranberry (the Islesford Historical Museum), Baker Island and Bear Island lights. The whole region map reads as three detail boxes spread along 50 km of island-studded coast, with ferries as dashed blue lines between them.

### Water

Everything is shaped by the sea and by glaciers. The **Gulf of Maine** surrounds the park: **Frenchman Bay** on the north-east (Bar Harbor, the Porcupine Islands, Schoodic across it), the open Atlantic on the south-east (Ocean Drive), the **Western Way / Eastern Way** channels and the Cranberry Isles to the south, **Blue Hill Bay** to the west. **Tides** rise and fall 2.4–3.7 m twice a day: at low tide the Bar Island bar, tide pools, mudflats and seaweed ledges appear; the hour or two before high tide is when Thunder Hole booms. **Somes Sound** is an 8 km, 40 m-deep glacial fjard nearly splitting the island. Inland, glaciers left long lakes in north–south troughs between the ridges — Eagle Lake (the largest), Jordan Pond (the deepest, 46 m, crystal clear, both public water supplies with no swimming), Bubble Pond, Echo Lake (swimming beach), Long Pond (the longest, west side), Seal Cove and Hodgdon ponds — plus small tarns and bogs (the Tarn, Sargent Mountain Pond, the Great Meadow and Big Heath wetlands). Streams are short and quick: Duck Brook, Jordan Stream, Hadlock Brook (falls under Waterfall Bridge), Man O'War Brook falling straight into Somes Sound. Salt marsh at Bass Harbor Marsh and Northeast Creek. Water colours shift from dark tannic ponds to the clear green-blue of Jordan Pond and the grey-green, foam-edged sea.

### Relief

From sea level to 466 m on Cadillac — low numbers, but the mountains rise straight from the water, so every summit sees the ocean. Relief is a north–south grain carved by the ice sheet: a line of **bare, rounded granite domes** (Cadillac, Dorr, Champlain, Pemetic, Sargent, Penobscot on the east side; Beech, St. Sauveur, Acadia, Bernard, Mansell on the west) separated by steep-walled **U-shaped valleys** holding lakes and "notches" (Great Notch, the Gorge). Their north slopes are long and gentle, their south faces steep and cliffy (glacial plucking), which is why the rung trails (Beehive, Precipice, Jordan Cliffs, Dorr Ladder) climb south and east faces. The coast is **cliffs and ledges of pink granite** (Otter Cliff, Great Head, Schoodic Point) broken by coves, cobble beaches, sea stacks and chasms (Thunder Hole), with dark basalt dikes striping the granite at Schoodic. Glacial erratics (Bubble Rock) sit on summits. In the game: the domes are stacks of smooth one-level banks capped with bare rock and a few pitch pines, the valleys flat and wooded with a lake at the bottom, the coast a band of stepped granite ledges (walkable banks) ending in true cliffs at Otter Cliff, Great Head and the Beehive; rung trails as ladder props on cliff faces; the Cadillac road as switchbacks; Somes Sound as a narrow sea channel between two ridges.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: the shore is a strip of bare pink granite, rockweed and barnacles below the tide line, beach rose and bayberry above it; then dense **red spruce – balsam fir** forest with white cedar in wet hollows (the "boreal" look, dark and mossy); on the east side, the **1947 fire** replaced much of that with **birch, aspen and maple** (the brightest autumn colour); on the ridges and summits, open **granite ledges** with pitch pine, jack pine at Schoodic, lowbush blueberry, huckleberry, sheep laurel, three-toothed cinquefoil and cushions of lichen and moss; bogs and heaths (the Great Meadow, Big Heath) with sphagnum, pitcher plants and sundews.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#D9A89A` pink Cadillac granite in sun · `#9E8B86` shaded/wet granite · `#2F3A38` black basalt dike · `#8A6F3A` rockweed band at low tide · `#E9DCC0` Sand Beach shell sand · `#27432F` spruce-fir forest · `#C9A227` birch-and-aspen gold in October · `#7B9C3C` blueberry-barren green (`#A33B32` in fall) · `#3E6C7A` Gulf of Maine water · `#9FB9A7` Jordan Pond shallows · `#D8DEE0` summer fog.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **harbor seal** (hauled out on offshore ledges at low tide; from the shore and boat tours) · **common loon** (Echo Lake, Jordan Pond, Eagle Lake, summer) · **peregrine falcon** (nesting on the Precipice and Jordan Cliffs, spring–summer; ranger scopes at the Precipice lot) · **bald eagle** and **osprey** (over the harbours) · **common eider** and **black guillemot** (Ship Harbor, Schoodic) · **herring gull** (everywhere on the rocks) · **beaver** (Sieur de Monts, Witch Hole Pond; lodges and dams) · **snowshoe hare** (white in winter, brown in summer) · **white-tailed deer** (fields at dusk) · **red fox** · **porcupine** · **river otter** (Otter Cove, ponds) · **spring peeper** (bog chorus in May) · tide-pool life: **green crab, periwinkles, dog whelks, blue mussels, sea stars, barnacles** (Wonderland, Ship Harbor, Bar Island at low tide) · **harbor porpoise** and whales offshore (whale-watch boats from Bar Harbor). 338 bird species have been recorded; fall **hawk migration** passes Cadillac.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): rhodora (magenta, May, bogs) · lupine (roadside purple-and-pink fields, June; not native but iconic) · beach rose (pink, June–August, the shore) · pitch pine (twisted on the ledges) · lowbush blueberry (white bells in May, ripe in August, scarlet leaves in October) · pitcher plant (bogs, summer) · bunchberry (white carpets in June) · paper birch (white trunks, gold in fall) · red spruce and balsam fir (the Christmas-tree smell of the woods) · rockweed (the brown seaweed band).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring is late, cool and muddy (April–May), with warblers arriving and carriage roads closed during mud season; June–July bring fog banks that swallow the coast and burn off by noon (foghorns, dripping spruce), then warm clear days; August is blueberries and crowds; late September–mid-October is the park's glory, with maple red and birch gold under blue sky; winter (December–April) closes most of the loop road to cars and opens the carriage roads to skiers, with ice on the ponds, snow on the domes and nor'easter surf on the coast; storms and rogue waves are a real danger at Thunder Hole and Schoodic Point.
- **Night**: dark skies over the sea — the Milky Way from Sand Beach, Seawall, Jordan Pond and Schoodic Point; the summit road closes at 9 pm so Cadillac at night is for hikers and cyclists; lighthouse beams (Bass Harbor Head's red flash, Baker Island, Egg Rock) sweeping the water; bioluminescent plankton sparkling in the coves on late-summer nights; owls calling; the Acadia Night Sky Festival each September.
- **Sounds**: surf and the boom of Thunder Hole; cobbles rolling at Boulder Beach and Little Hunters Beach; foghorns and bell buoys; herring gulls, the wail of loons at dusk; lobster-boat diesels leaving harbour at dawn; horses' hooves and carriage wheels on broken stone, bicycle bells; wind over bare granite summits; the crunch of snow under skis in winter.

## Moments

- **First sunrise in the United States**: from October 7 to March 6, Cadillac's summit is the first place in the country to see the sun rise (at other times of year it is still among the first); the summit fills an hour before dawn, and cars need a timed reservation (May 20 – Oct 25 in 2026).
- **Thunder Hole booms**: best one to two hours before high tide, and on stormy days — the tide table is the schedule (two high tides a day, ~50 minutes later each day).
- **Low-tide windows**: the Bar Island bar opens for about 1.5 hours either side of low tide (miss it and you wait ~9 hours); tide pools at Wonderland, Ship Harbor and Schoodic at every low tide; spring tides at new and full moon.
- **Tea and popovers on the Jordan Pond House lawn**: a tradition since the 1890s, mid-May to late October, with the Bubbles reflected in the pond.
- **Peregrine season**: falcons nest on the Precipice cliffs from spring to midsummer (the trail closes and a ranger "Peregrine Watch" sets up scopes at the base); the fall **hawk watch** on Cadillac counts migrating raptors from mid-August to mid-October.
- **Autumn colour**: peaks roughly early-to-mid October, strongest on the east side where the 1947 fire left birch and maple.
- **Summer fog**: June–July mornings when the coast vanishes and only the foghorns speak, then the fog lifts off Otter Cliff.
- **Lupine and blueberry seasons**: lupines in June, wild blueberries ripe on the summits in August.
- **Sunset at Bass Harbor Head Light**: the red-and-white lighthouse against the western sky, photographers packed on the rocks.
- **Winter carriage roads**: when the snow is deep enough, volunteers groom the carriage roads for cross-country skiing (December–March).
- **Acadia Night Sky Festival** (September): star parties on the beaches and summits.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a **1536 × 1024-tile** map. Mount Desert Island fills most of it (~1,100 × 1,000 tiles, i.e. compressed ~15× overall), keeping its claw shape, Somes Sound and the north–south grain of domes and lakes. Near true scale (1 tile ≈ 1.5 m): Thunder Hole and its stairs, the Jordan Pond House and lawn, the Cadillac summit loop (~800 m path, cut to ~300 tiles), Bass Harbor Head Light and its rocks, a stretch of carriage road with one full stone bridge, Sand Beach (~300 m, kept at ~150–200 tiles). Compressed ×3–5: Ocean Path (Sand Beach → Otter Point), Jordan Pond itself, the Beehive/Precipice cliffs (height exaggerated). Heavily compressed: the west-side woods, the island's private north third (a thin band with Bar Harbor as a small village hub), Frenchman Bay. The **Schoodic Peninsula** sits across a narrowed Frenchman Bay at the east edge (~250 × 300 tiles), reached by ferry or a long road round the head of the bay; **Isle au Haut** becomes a small island (~200 × 300 tiles) in the south-west corner reached by the mail boat; Little Cranberry a ferry stop. Cut: most private land, the Cranberry villages beyond the museum.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: hubs at **Hulls Cove** (visitor centre up its stairs, bus stop), **Jordan Pond House** (lawn tables, gift shop, boat launch, carriage-road entrance — the social heart), **Sand Beach** (beach, bus stop, trailheads), **Cadillac summit** (parking loop, gift shop, sunrise ledges), **Bar Harbor Village Green** (bus hub, ferry pier, the sandbar), **Echo Lake Beach**, **Schoodic Point**. 3D landmarks: Cadillac's pink dome with the summit road switchbacks; the two Bubbles; the Beehive's cliff; Otter Cliff; Bass Harbor Head Light (white tower, red lantern, keeper's house); the **17 carriage-road bridges** (a kit of cut-granite arches: Duck Brook's triple arch, Cobblestone, Waterfall Bridge framing Hadlock Falls, the Triad-Day Mountain bridge over the Loop Road); the two **gate lodges** in French-chateau style; the Beech Mountain fire tower; the Sieur de Monts spring canopy; Thunder Hole's slot with its railings; the Otter Cove causeway; Island Explorer buses. Interiors: Hulls Cove Visitor Center (3D relief model of the island), the Jordan Pond House dining room, the Sieur de Monts Nature Center, the Carroll Homestead kitchen, the Islesford museum.
- **Activities**: hiking the rung trails (Beehive, Precipice, Jordan Cliffs, Dorr Ladder) as climbing mini-games with iron-rung props; summit walks on bare granite following Bates cairns; biking and walking the carriage roads by signpost numbers (a "find all 17 bridges" collection); horse-drawn carriage rides from Wildwood Stables; the Island Explorer as the open-world shuttle (flag it down anywhere, bikes on the rack); photo spots (Cadillac sunrise, Bass Harbor Head Light at sunset, the Bubbles from the Jordan Pond House lawn, Thunder Hole spray, Otter Cliff, Sand Beach from the Beehive, Duck Brook Bridge, Schoodic Point surf, Somes Sound from Acadia Mountain); tide pooling with a timer (what's out at low tide), walking the Bar Island bar before the sea returns; swimming at Sand Beach (cold!) and Echo Lake; canoe/kayak on Long Pond, Echo Lake and Jordan Pond (no swimming); sea kayaking and a ranger boat cruise to Little Cranberry; fishing boats and lobster buoys as scenery; peregrine and hawk watches with a ranger's scope; ski the groomed carriage roads in winter; stargazing on Sand Beach. Junior ranger ideas: pick your popover flavour after a hike (the reward), count Thunder Hole's booms before high tide, match tide-pool creatures to their zones, identify the rock at Schoodic (pink granite vs black dike), learn a word of the Wabanaki languages, deliver a lost signpost number to the right junction.
- **Passport stamps**: the park's official list — Hulls Cove Visitor Center ("Bar Harbor, Maine", plus Junior Ranger, Bark Ranger and "Tick Check" stamps), Acadia Gateway Center, Sieur de Monts Nature Center, Jordan Pond House gift shop, Blackwoods and Seawall campground ranger stations, Cadillac Mountain gift shop, Thunder Hole gift shop, the Bar Harbor Chamber of Commerce (winter only), Schoodic Woods ranger station ("Schoodic Peninsula" and "Schoodic Woods"), Rockefeller Hall welcome centre at the Schoodic Institute, the Islesford Historical Museum, and the Duck Harbor ranger station on Isle au Haut ("Isle au Haut" and "Duck Harbor").
- **With friends / with the world**: the Cadillac summit at sunrise is the natural mass gathering (everyone in the world waiting on the ledges for the same dawn, then a cheer); the Jordan Pond House lawn is the tea-time plaza; Sand Beach and the Island Explorer bus stops are where strangers mix. Co-op: a party climbing the Beehive or Precipice together (one player at a time on a rung, others spotting), a carriage-road bike ride through the numbered junctions, a group crossing to Bar Island racing the tide back, a party-sized horse-drawn carriage, a shared tide-pool bingo, a synchronised "wave" at Thunder Hole's boom for the group photo, a lobster-boat ferry ride to Isle au Haut.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on the trail and off roped-off summit plants (Cadillac's "re-vegetation areas"); don't stack or topple cairns (Bates cairns guide hikers — moving one is a real offence); no swimming in Jordan Pond or Eagle Lake (drinking water); keep back from the edge at Thunder Hole and Schoodic Point in big surf; don't take rocks or cobbles from the beaches; peregrine closures mean detours; bikes yield to horses on carriage roads (and no e-bikes over 20 mph); leave tide-pool animals where you found them (turn rocks back over); park only in lots or the right lane of the loop; pets leashed, and never on rung trails.
- **Implementation challenges**: **tides** (a sea level that moves 3 m on a real schedule, exposing and covering bars, ledges and pools, and driving Thunder Hole's spray); **surf** breaking on cliffs; **fog** that rolls in and out along the coast; a one-way loop road with parking in its right lane; pink granite domes that must read as bare rock, not grass; rung-and-ladder vertical climbs on cliffs; the carriage-road **stone bridges** passing over roads and streams (bridge decks over lower tiles); the Island Explorer as a flag-stop bus system; ferries between three separate land pieces; winter snow turning roads into ski routes; a lighthouse beam at night.
