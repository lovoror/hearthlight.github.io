# Glacier National Park — `GLAC`

> Montana · established May 11, 1910 · 4,100.0 km² (1,013,126.39 acres) · 3,136,557 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~960–3,190 m · NPS region IMR
>
> The « Crown of the Continent »: two ranges of layered, glacier-carved peaks split by the Continental Divide, long turquoise lakes in every valley, the last small glaciers, and one of the world's great mountain roads cut across the middle. In Hearthlight it should feel like crossing a wall: a mossy cedar-and-lake west side, a switchback climb along a cliff to a flower-covered pass, and a windy, sunlit east side of prairie-edge lakes, chalets and red buses.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a lozenge about 90 km across. Its **north edge** is the straight Canadian border; above it the brochure also draws Waterton Lakes National Park, whose long **Upper Waterton Lake** crosses the border into the park, ending at Goat Haunt. The **west edge** follows the North Fork of the Flathead River, running NNW–SSE, with the gravel roads to Polebridge alongside. The **south edge** follows the Middle Fork of the Flathead, with US 2 and the railway beside it, from West Glacier (south-west corner) east over Marias Pass to East Glacier. The **east edge** is a straight diagonal line where the mountains meet the plains of the Blackfeet Indian Reservation, from Chief Mountain (north-east) down to East Glacier (south-east). Inside, two ranges run north-west–south-east: the **Livingston Range** in the north-west half and the **Lewis Range** along the east half, joined in the middle. The **Continental Divide** (orange) follows the Lewis Range crest north from Marias Pass, through Logan Pass and along the Garden Wall, then crosses west over Flattop Mountain to follow the Livingston Range into Canada. **Triple Divide Peak**, south-east of Logan Pass, sends its water to three oceans.

Between the ranges and on both flanks, the valleys are long glacial troughs, each holding a lake. On the west side they drain south-west: **Kintla**, **Bowman**, **Quartz** and **Logging** lakes to the North Fork, and **Lake McDonald**, the biggest (16 km long, beside the park's main road), to the Middle Fork. On the east side they open onto the prairie: **Waterton**, **Belly River**, **Many Glacier** (Swiftcurrent Lake, Lake Josephine, Lake Sherburne), **St. Mary** (St. Mary Lake, 16 km), **Cut Bank** and **Two Medicine**. The brochure scatters small white glaciers among the peaks: Grinnell and Salamander above Many Glacier, Jackson and Blackfoot south-east of Logan Pass, Sperry above Lake McDonald, Agassiz and Rainbow in the north-west.

The **Going-to-the-Sun Road** (80 km) is the one road across the middle, running south-west to north-east then east. From the West Entrance (3 km) past Apgar at Lake McDonald's foot, it follows the lake's east shore to Lake McDonald Lodge and up McDonald Creek to Avalanche Creek. There the long climb begins: the road bends back in a single hairpin, **The Loop**, then runs along a shelf blasted into the **Garden Wall** past the Weeping Wall, Bird Woman Falls, Haystack Creek, Triple Arches and a tunnel, to **Logan Pass** (2,025 m, 51 km from Apgar). It drops east through a second tunnel, past Siyeh Bend, the Jackson Glacier Overlook, St. Mary Falls, Sunrift Gorge and Sun Point, along St. Mary Lake's north shore past Wild Goose Island and Rising Sun, to **St. Mary** (29 km from the pass). Everything else hangs off the park's edges. From St. Mary, US 89 runs north through Babb (14 km) to the Many Glacier Road (19 km west up the Swiftcurrent valley) and on to Canada, while the Chief Mountain Highway branches north-west to the Belly River and Waterton. South of St. Mary, US 89 and MT 49 lead to Cut Bank and Two Medicine (14 km from its junction) and to East Glacier. US 2 closes the loop along the south. On the west, the Camas Road (18 km) crosses from Apgar to the North Fork, where rough gravel roads reach Polebridge, Bowman Lake (10 km in) and Kintla Lake (22 km north).

The **hubs** are Apgar (west gate), Lake McDonald Lodge, Avalanche Creek, Logan Pass, Rising Sun and St. Mary on the road; Many Glacier and Two Medicine in their own valleys off the east edge; Polebridge in the North Fork; Goat Haunt reachable by boat from Waterton. The **roadless heart** is the rest: the Livingston Range's lakes and passes, the Belly River, the Nyack and Coal Creek valleys south of the road, and the Lewis Range between the Going-to-the-Sun Road and Two Medicine. Two chalets from the 1910s, Granite Park and Sperry, are reached only on foot. **Relief** is steep everywhere: valley floors at 960–1,500 m, walls and horns rising 1,200–1,800 m above them, and passes at 2,000–2,400 m.

### Water

Glacier sits on the **Continental Divide** and on a second, rarer one: **Triple Divide Peak** (2,444 m) sheds water to the Pacific (the Flathead and Columbia), to the Gulf of Mexico (Cut Bank Creek to the Marias and Missouri) and to Hudson Bay (the St. Mary River to the Saskatchewan). West-side valleys drain south-west into the **North Fork** (the west boundary, a wild and scenic river) and the **Middle Fork of the Flathead** (the south boundary, rafted from West Glacier); **McDonald Creek** feeds **Lake McDonald** (961 m, 16 km × 2.4 km, ~141 m deep, the park's largest), whose shallows show red, green and blue argillite pebbles. North-west lakes in parallel troughs: Kintla, Bowman, Quartz, Logging. East-side lakes open to the plains: **St. Mary Lake** (1,366 m, 16 km, and Lower St. Mary Lake outside), **Swiftcurrent Lake**, **Lake Josephine** and **Lake Sherburne** (a reservoir) at Many Glacier, **Two Medicine Lake**, and **Upper Waterton Lake** crossing into Canada. Glacier-fed lakes (Grinnell, Upper Grinnell, Iceberg) are milky turquoise from rock flour. About **26 glaciers** still counted as active in 2015 (of ~150 around 1850), all shrinking: Grinnell, Salamander, Sperry, Jackson, Blackfoot, Harrison, Agassiz, Rainbow. Waterfalls everywhere: Bird Woman Falls (~150 m, from a hanging valley), the Weeping Wall pouring onto the road, St. Mary, Virginia, Baring, Running Eagle, Twin, McDonald, Swiftcurrent and Ptarmigan falls. The snowpack is the park's reservoir: the **Big Drift** east of Logan Pass can be 20 m or more deep in spring.

### Relief

From ~960 m (Lake McDonald and the Flathead forks at the south-west corner) to 3,190 m (Mount Cleveland, in the north above Waterton Valley). The mountains are layered sedimentary rock — red and green argillite, grey limestone with fossil stromatolites — pushed east over younger rock along the **Lewis Overthrust** (Chief Mountain is an isolated block of the older layers standing on the plains). Glaciers carved them into a textbook: U-shaped troughs with lakes, knife-edge arêtes (the **Garden Wall**), horns (Clements, Reynolds, Mount Wilbur, Mount Gould), cirques with tarns, and hanging valleys with waterfalls. The west valleys are low and deep; the east face ends abruptly on the prairie at ~1,400 m. Main summits: Cleveland (3,190 m), Stimson (3,091 m), Kintla (3,079 m), Jackson (3,064 m), Siyeh (3,052 m), Going-to-the-Sun (2,939 m), Gould (2,911 m), Heavens Peak (2,739 m).

In game terms: valley floors at levels 0–1 (west lower than east), the east-side prairie a flat level-2 strip; valley walls as tall painted cliffs (the layered bands of red, green and grey are the park's look); the Going-to-the-Sun Road as a shelf road climbing a cliff face, with the one hairpin, two tunnels, arches and a wall of snow in early season; Logan Pass as a small high plateau between two 3D horns; cirques as bowls with tarns; stairs where trails climb headwalls (Hidden Lake boardwalk steps, the Highline's ledge). 3D peaks: Clements and Reynolds at the pass, the Garden Wall arête, Going-to-the-Sun Mountain, Mount Grinnell and Mount Wilbur at Many Glacier, Sinopah at Two Medicine, Mount Cleveland in the north, Chief Mountain beyond the edge.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: west-side valleys (960–1,300 m): western red cedar and western hemlock (inland rainforest with devil's club and moss at Avalanche Creek), western larch, Douglas-fir, black cottonwood by the creeks; lodgepole pine and wide burned areas with silver snags and fireweed (the Loop's 2003 burn, Reynolds Creek 2015 by St. Mary Lake, Sprague 2017, Howe Ridge 2018 on Lake McDonald's north shore, the North Fork's older fires). East side (1,350–1,600 m): aspen parkland, fescue prairie, limber pine, willow flats. Subalpine (1,600–2,100 m): Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir, then meadows of beargrass and glacier lilies; krummholz near the passes. Alpine (above ~2,100 m): tundra, scree, cliffs, snowfields and glaciers.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#A5483A` red argillite · `#6C8D6A` green argillite · `#8C8A84` grey limestone band · `#4DB6C0` glacier-flour turquoise · `#3E7F9C` Lake McDonald blue-green · `#F2F5F8` snowfield · `#EFE8C8` beargrass plume · `#8FA85F` subalpine meadow · `#2F4B38` cedar-hemlock green · `#D8A93A` larch gold · `#B3241E` red-bus red.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): mountain goat (the park's emblem animal: Logan Pass, Hidden Lake, the Goat Lick in May–July), bighorn sheep (Many Glacier, Logan Pass), grizzly bear (Many Glacier's slopes digging lily bulbs in June–July, berries in August–September), black bear, hoary marmot and pika (the Logan Pass talus), Columbian ground squirrel, moose (Many Glacier's ponds, Fishercap Lake), elk (North Fork, Two Medicine), mule and white-tailed deer, gray wolf (North Fork), Canada lynx and wolverine (rare), harlequin duck (McDonald Creek, April–June), white-tailed ptarmigan, Clark's nutcracker, golden eagle (autumn migration along the Divide), American dipper, bull trout and westslope cutthroat.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): beargrass (tall cream plumes in July, massive blooms every few years), glacier lily (June–July at the snow's edge), Indian paintbrush, pink monkeyflower (seeps), fireweed (burns), pasqueflower (east-side prairie, May), western red cedar, western larch (gold in October), huckleberry (August), whitebark pine, subalpine fir, alpine forget-me-not, mountain heath.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter (November–April) buries the high road under many metres of snow and closes the valleys; spring (May–June) is the plowing season — the road open to cyclists and walkers before cars, waterfalls at full roar, avalanche debris, bears waking; summer (July–August): the whole road open, meadows in flower, afternoon storms, wildfire smoke some years; autumn (September–October): larches and aspens gold, first snow, elk bugling in the North Fork, the alpine road closing mid-October. The east side is famous for wind (Chinooks that can gust over 100 km/h); the west side for rain and mist.
- **Night**: an International Dark Sky Park with Waterton (2017): the Milky Way over St. Mary Lake, summer star parties at Logan Pass and Apgar, northern lights on strong solar nights; owls; the lodges' lit windows on the lakes.
- **Sounds**: water everywhere (the Weeping Wall drumming on car roofs, falls, creeks), marmots' whistles and pikas' squeaks, wind at Logan Pass and on St. Mary Lake, a red bus's engine and gear-jamming, boat motors, elk bugles in autumn, the Empire Builder's horn at West Glacier and Essex.

## Moments

- **The Going-to-the-Sun Road opens**: after ~10 weeks of spring plowing (the Big Drift last), usually late June to mid-July — before that, cyclists and walkers have the plowed road to themselves. The alpine section closes around the third Monday of October.
- **Beargrass and glacier lilies**: glacier lilies follow the melting snow at Logan Pass (late June–July); beargrass blooms in July, massively in some years.
- **Mountain goats at Logan Pass and the Goat Lick**: nannies and kids on the Hidden Lake trail all summer; goats at the US 2 mineral lick in May–July.
- **Grizzlies of Many Glacier**: digging glacier-lily bulbs on the slopes in June–July, stripping huckleberries in August–September (spotting scopes at the hotel and Swiftcurrent).
- **Sunrise at Swiftcurrent Lake and Wild Goose Island**: Mount Grinnell's reflection and the island in first light.
- **Larch gold**: mid-October on the west side and in the North Fork.
- **Harlequin ducks on McDonald Creek**: April–June.
- **Dark-sky nights**: summer star parties at Logan Pass; the aurora on rare nights.
- **Ice on Iceberg Lake**: floes drift on the lake into August.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: 1280 × 1024 tiles (40 × 32 chunks), north up, the park's lozenge with Waterton's townsite as a small hub across the top border. The Going-to-the-Sun Road is the spine and stays long — it is the attraction: ~2,500–3,000 tiles of road from Apgar to St. Mary (~4–5 min by car), with the Garden Wall climb (18 km) kept at ~×10 so the shelf, the Loop, the Weeping Wall and Triple Arches each get room. Hubs at 1 tile ≈ 3 m: Logan Pass (the visitor centre and the 2.2 km boardwalk to Hidden Lake Overlook, ~350 × 250 tiles), Apgar, Lake McDonald Lodge, Rising Sun, Many Glacier (hotel, Swiftcurrent Lake and trailheads, ~300 × 250 tiles), Two Medicine. Lake McDonald shrinks to ~400 tiles long, St. Mary Lake ~350. Many Glacier and Two Medicine sit off a compressed outer road along the east edge, where the Blackfeet Reservation's prairie is a thin strip. The North Fork is a gravel-road strip along the west edge with Polebridge, Bowman and Kintla as small lake pockets. The roadless interior (the Livingston and Lewis ranges between the valleys) is compressed ~50× into peaks and passes, with the two chalets and Grinnell Glacier kept. Cut: the Belly River (a trail spur off the Chief Mountain Highway), US 2's canyon (the south edge with the train), Waterton beyond its townsite.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Logan Pass (the open-world plaza), Apgar, Lake McDonald Lodge, Many Glacier Hotel, Rising Sun/St. Mary, Two Medicine, Polebridge. 3D landmarks: the Garden Wall arête and the shelf road, Clements and Reynolds mountains framing the pass, Going-to-the-Sun Mountain, Mount Grinnell and Mount Wilbur, Sinopah, Wild Goose Island, Many Glacier Hotel's Swiss gables, Lake McDonald Lodge, the Granite Park and Sperry chalets, fire lookouts, the Prince of Wales Hotel on its bluff in Waterton, the red buses and the historic wooden tour boats. Interiors: Lake McDonald Lodge's lobby (stone fireplace, balconies), Many Glacier Hotel's lobby with its view, the Logan Pass Visitor Center, a chalet dining room.
- **Activities**: hiking (Hidden Lake, the Highline, Avalanche Lake, Grinnell Glacier, Iceberg Lake); photo spots — Wild Goose Island, Apgar's view up Lake McDonald, the Weeping Wall, The Loop with Heavens Peak, a goat at Hidden Lake Overlook, Grinnell Lake's turquoise, Swiftcurrent Lake at sunrise, Running Eagle Falls, the red bus on the Garden Wall; wildlife spotting (goats and bighorn at the pass, grizzlies by scope at Many Glacier, harlequins on McDonald Creek); ranger programmes (Logan Pass talks, Many Glacier hotel evening talks, Apgar and St. Mary amphitheatres, Blackfeet cultural talks at St. Mary); Young Ranger ideas: match a glacier's old photo to today, name the three oceans at Triple Divide, count goats at the pass from a proper distance; water: boat tours on Lake McDonald, St. Mary Lake, Swiftcurrent Lake + Lake Josephine (two boats with a short walk between) and Two Medicine, kayaks from Apgar, rafting the Middle Fork, the border-crossing boat from Waterton to Goat Haunt; vehicles: car, the ticketed Logan Pass shuttle, the red buses, horses from Apgar, Lake McDonald and Many Glacier, bikes on the closed road in spring, Amtrak to West and East Glacier; winter: skiing and snowshoeing at Apgar and on the closed road.
- **Passport stamps**: Apgar Visitor Center, Logan Pass Visitor Center, St. Mary Visitor Center, and the ranger stations of Many Glacier, Two Medicine, Polebridge and Goat Haunt; Waterton's visitor centre offers the International Peace Park's (to confirm on the Passport stamp finder).
- **With friends / with the world**: Logan Pass is where strangers meet (the parking kiosk, the boardwalk, the goats); the Weeping Wall splashes every passing car. Party ideas: a red-bus tour where the party fills one bus (seats = players, the roof rolled back, one driver); a Highline convoy along the ledge holding the cable; a two-boat relay to Grinnell Glacier; the spring bike-up of the closed road as a group event; crossing to Goat Haunt by boat with a « passport check » for fun.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): 100 yd / 91 m from bears and wolves, 25 yd / 23 m from goats, sheep and everything else (goats come close for salt: walk away, don't let them lick you); stay on the Hidden Lake boardwalk and trails (alpine plants take decades to heal); never feed ground squirrels or marmots; bear spray and food lockers; respect the size limits and timed parking; Blackfeet sacred places (Chief Mountain, the Two Medicine valley) are told the way the tribe and park tell them, without gameplay on them.
- **Implementation challenges**: a shelf road cut into a cliff, with one hairpin, tunnels, arches and a waterfall onto the road, readable from a north-facing camera (the Garden Wall faces west); layered, banded cliff faces; 3D horns and arêtes; glaciers and a seasonal snow wall along the road; the road's late opening as a season state (and shuttle tickets or timed parking if the game keeps them); turquoise glacial lakes; wind and Chinook weather; the international border (Waterton, a passport moment) and the reservation between the east-side hubs; Triple Divide Peak's three-way drainage; burned forests of silver snags.
