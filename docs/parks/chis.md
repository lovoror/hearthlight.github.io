# Channel Islands National Park — `CHIS`

> California · established March 5, 1980 (Channel Islands National Monument, Anacapa and Santa Barbara islands, since
> April 26, 1938) · 1,009.9 km² (249,561 acres, about half of it ocean: the park reaches 1 nautical mile offshore) ·
> 227,186 recreation visits (2025) · 0–740 m · NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10, California–Great Basin)
>
> Five islands off the Santa Barbara Channel coast — sea cliffs, sea caves, kelp forests and grassy tablelands where
> isolation bred a dwarf fox, a bigger and bluer jay and some 145 species found nowhere else — reached only by boat.
> In Hearthlight it should feel like an expedition: a crossing with dolphins at the bow, a ladder up to a gull-loud
> clifftop, whole islands to yourself until the boat's horn calls you back.

## Reading the map

The brochure map is mostly sea. The mainland runs along the top — the Santa Ynez Mountains falling to Santa Barbara, Carpinteria, Ventura and the Oxnard plain — and the islands lie across the lower half: the four northern islands in a west–east chain, the tops of one drowned ridge (20,000 years ago, when the sea was ~107 m lower, they were a single island, « Santarosae »), and Santa Barbara Island alone to the south-east. Each island is ringed twice, by the park boundary and the sanctuary boundary. From west to east:

**San Miguel** (westernmost, windiest, 38 km²) is a low plateau ~150 m high (San Miguel Hill 253 m, Green Mountain 249 m) with its only landing on the north side at **Cuyler Harbor** — a beach reached by skiff, Prince Island in its mouth. A steep trail up Nidever Canyon leads to the **Cabrillo Monument**, the **Lester Ranch** site, the campground and the ranger station on the bluff; beyond, only with a ranger (the Navy owns the island and there may be unexploded ordnance): the white **caliche forest** in the middle and **Point Bennett** at the west tip, one of the great seal and sea-lion rookeries of the world.

**Santa Rosa** (215 km²), 8 km east across the San Miguel Passage, rolls in grassy hills (Vail Peak 484 m, Soledad Peak 480 m, Black Mountain 396 m) cut by canyons. Boats land at the pier in **Bechers Bay** on the north-east, by the Vail & Vickers ranch; a road runs south to the **Water Canyon** campground and its 2-mile white beach; the **Torrey pine** grove stands on the bluffs to the south-east (burned in the 2026 fire); **Lobo Canyon**, **Cherry Canyon** and **Carrington Point** lie to the north-west. A 2026 wildfire burned about a third of the island.

**Santa Cruz** (~250 km², the largest island in California), across the Santa Cruz Channel, is ~35 km long, with two ranges on either side of a **Central Valley** along the Santa Cruz Island Fault; **Devils Peak** (740 m) on the northern range is the park's summit. The Nature Conservancy owns the western 76 % (no landing without its permit); the NPS owns the eastern 24 %, marked by a fence between Prisoners Harbor and Valley Anchorage. The two landings are **Scorpion Anchorage** on the east end (pier, the 1880s **Scorpion Ranch** with the visitor centre, a campground up Scorpion Canyon, the kayak and snorkel outfitter) and **Prisoners Harbor** mid-north (pier, ranch, wetland, the Del Norte trail and camp). From Scorpion, trails and ranch roads reach **Cavern Point**, the **Potato Harbor** overlook, **Smugglers Cove** over the ridge and **Montañon Ridge**. The north coast is a string of anchorages and sea caves, with **Painted Cave** near the west end.

**Anacapa** (2.8 km²), ~6 km east across the Anacapa Passage and ~20 km off Oxnard, is an 8 km chain of three cliff-walled islets. Only **East Anacapa** is open: boats idle in **Landing Cove**, visitors climb a steel ladder and **157 stairs** to a flat top with a visitor centre, the **1932 lighthouse** and its foghorn, a small campground and a figure-eight of trails to **Inspiration Point** (the view west along Middle and West Anacapa), Pinniped Point and Cathedral Cove; **Arch Rock** stands off the east tip. Middle and West Anacapa are left to the gulls and pelicans (Frenchy's Cove beach on West Anacapa is closed by a washout).

**Santa Barbara Island** (2.6 km², the smallest), alone ~60 km south-east of Anacapa, is a triangular mesa ringed by cliffs: a small Landing Cove with a (damaged) dock, a steep trail up to the visitor centre and campground, and ~5 mi of trails to **Signal Peak** (194 m), Arch Point, Elephant Seal Cove and Webster Point, with **Sutil Island** off the south-west tip.

### Water

The sea *is* the park: half of its area lies within the 1-mile band, inside a 6-mile national marine sanctuary where a cold southward current meets warm water from the south, stirred by upwelling. **Giant kelp forests** (the plants grow up to ~0.6 m a day) ring the islands and shelter more than 1,000 species; 13 marine protected areas (~21 % of the waters, many no-take reserves: Scorpion, Anacapa, Harris Point, Carrington Point…) keep them rich. The water runs 11–21 °C with up to 30 m of visibility in autumn. The coasts are cliffs cut into **sea caves** (Scorpion's for kayakers; Painted Cave, one of the largest in the world; ~30 around Anacapa) and **arches**, with **tidepools** at Frenchy's Cove, Smugglers Cove, the Bechers Bay pier and East Point. Fresh water is scarce: seasonal creeks in the Santa Cruz and Santa Rosa canyons (Lobo Canyon's runs all year), drinking water only at Scorpion and Water Canyon; Anacapa has none, which is why it has no foxes. Crossings meet swell, wind (north-west, strongest in the afternoon and on the outer islands), fog and ship lanes.

### Relief

From **sea level** to **740 m** (Devils Peak, Santa Cruz; the brochure prints 747 m); the sea floor inside the sanctuary drops to a few hundred metres. **Anacapa** and **Santa Barbara Island** are flat-topped mesas ringed by cliffs 60–90 m high (West Anacapa rises to 285 m). **Santa Cruz** has two rugged ranges (to 740 m in the north, 551 m at Montañon on the east end) with a central fault valley and cliffs of 50–150 m on the north coast. **Santa Rosa** rolls in grassy hills to 484 m over wide marine terraces, dunes and sandstone canyons. **San Miguel** is a ~150 m plateau of sand, dunes and caliche with low hills.

For the game: the islands are made for Hearthlight's plateau system — flat tops with painted **cliff faces** all round, entered by one **stair** (Anacapa's 157 steps) or a steep canyon path; the sea arch, Scorpion Rock and the sea stacks are **3D rock** landmarks; sea caves are **interiors** entered by kayak; beaches and coves are **banks**; the camera looking north sees the islands' south-facing cliffs, while the landings (on the north shores) sit behind them and need a cut-away rim.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: here exposure matters more than height. Wind-swept terraces: coastal sage scrub, native bunchgrass and non-native annual grass (brown by summer), recovering since the sheep, cattle and pigs were removed; giant coreopsis « trees » on Anacapa, Santa Barbara Island and San Miguel; iceplant being pulled out on Anacapa. Sheltered canyons and north slopes (Santa Cruz, Santa Rosa): island oak, island ironwood groves, Bishop and Santa Cruz Island pines, a fog-fed « cloud forest » of oaks on Soledad Peak and Black Mountain; the Torrey pine grove on Santa Rosa. Dunes and beaches with sand verbena; kelp forests offshore.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#5B4A3F` dark volcanic cliffs (Anacapa, north Santa Cruz) · `#D9C7A1` sandstone and yellow banks (Santa Rosa, Smugglers) · `#EDE9DF` guano-white ledges · `#E8E2D2` caliche white · `#F2C230` coreopsis yellow · `#8DBA5A` spring grass · `#C8B27A` summer grass · `#6B6B2A` kelp olive · `#3FA9A5` shallow cove turquoise · `#1F4E79` deep channel blue · `#D5DCDF` fog.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): **island fox** (a house-cat-sized fox, a different subspecies on each of San Miguel, Santa Rosa and Santa Cruz; active by day, bold at campsites; saved from near extinction in the 2000s); island spotted skunk (Santa Cruz and Santa Rosa, at dusk); **island scrub-jay** (Santa Cruz only, bigger and bluer than its mainland cousin); island deer mouse; **island night lizard** (Santa Barbara Island); **western gull** (the world's largest colony on Anacapa, April–August); **California brown pelican** (West Anacapa and Santa Barbara Island colonies); Scripps's murrelet (Santa Barbara Island, at night); bald eagle (reintroduced, nesting again); **northern elephant seal**, **California sea lion**, harbor seal and northern fur seal (Point Bennett and the other rookeries); **gray, blue and humpback whales**; common dolphins by the thousand; **garibaldi** (the orange state fish of the kelp forest); giant sea bass; sea stars and anemones in the tidepools.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): giant coreopsis (yellow « tree sunflower », late January–March), Torrey pine, island oak, island ironwood, Santa Cruz Island pine, Santa Barbara Island live-forever and other Dudleya on the cliffs, island barberry (a dozen plants left, Santa Cruz), lupine and poppies in spring, giant kelp.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter brings rain storms, green hills, coreopsis bloom, gray whales and elephant seal pups; spring is windy and flowery, with gulls nesting on Anacapa from April; late spring and early summer are foggy (« May Gray, June Gloom »); late summer and autumn are the calm, warm, clear season of blue whales and diving; Santa Ana winds can blow from October.
- **Night**: camping on the islands under dark skies (the mainland's glow to the north-east); the Anacapa light flashing twice every minute and the foghorn in the murk; Scripps's murrelets, Cassin's auklets and ashy storm-petrels that visit their colonies only in the dark; foxes and skunks prowling camp; the swell booming in the sea caves.
- **Sounds**: the din of ten thousand gulls on Anacapa in May–July, sea lions barking and elephant seals belching at the rookeries, surf and wind in the grass, the boat's diesel and horn, the foghorn, ravens, fox yips at dusk, the scrub-jay's harsh call, paddles echoing inside a sea cave.

## Moments

- **Gray whales**: mid–late December to March–April, southbound then northbound with calves.
- **Blue whales**: June–December, peak July–October, feeding off San Miguel and Santa Rosa; **humpbacks** mid-May to mid-September; **common dolphin** superpods all year.
- **Giant coreopsis bloom**: late January–March, turning Anacapa, Santa Barbara Island and San Miguel yellow.
- **Gull nesting on Anacapa**: April to mid-August; eggs in April–May, fluffy chicks along every trail in May–June.
- **Seal and sea lion seasons**: elephant seals breed December–March (San Miguel, Santa Barbara Island); harbor seal pups February–April; California sea lion pups June–July; northern fur seal pups June.
- **Island fox pups**: April–May.
- **Channel Islands Live**: rangers dive Anacapa's Landing Cove with a live camera, ~May–September.
- **Calm season**: August–October, flat seas and 30 m visibility for kayaking the sea caves and snorkelling the kelp.
- **The tomol crossing**: Chumash paddlers have crossed from the mainland to Santa Cruz Island (Limuw) in a plank canoe every year since 2001.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a boat-hub vignette of ~1,280 × 768 tiles: Ventura Harbor and its visitor centre on the north-east edge, a crossing squeezed from 20 km of sea to ~300 tiles of open water (the ride itself is a set piece: dolphins, whales, swell), **East Anacapa** near true scale on a 1:2 grid (~600 × 120 tiles of cliff-top, the ladder and the stairs), and the **Scorpion end of Santa Cruz** (Scorpion Valley, Cavern Point, Potato Harbor, Smugglers Cove, ~6 × 4 km) at ~1:4 (~1,000 × 600). Santa Rosa (the Bechers Bay corner), San Miguel (Cuyler Harbor to the caliche forest) and Santa Barbara Island become separate small maps (~384 × 256 each) reached by longer boat trips on a timetable.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the Lagomarsino Visitor Center (tidepool tank, lookout tower) and Island Packers dock; Scorpion Ranch (visitor centre in the ranch house, blacksmith shop, the kayak shack); East Anacapa (lighthouse, foghorn, visitor centre with the old lens); the Vail & Vickers ranch; 3D landmarks: Arch Rock, the lighthouse, Scorpion Rock, the Cavern Point sea caves, the coreopsis « forest », the Torrey pines, the Cabrillo Monument, the caliche forest's white casts; interiors: Painted Cave and the Scorpion sea caves (by kayak), the ranch house, the lighthouse.
- **Activities**: the boat crossing (whale and dolphin spotting from the bow), sea kayaking through the Scorpion caves, snorkelling and diving in the kelp (find the garibaldi), tidepooling, hiking, island camping (reserve, carry all water, fox-proof food boxes), photo spots (Inspiration Point, Arch Rock, Cavern Point, Potato Harbor, Smugglers Cove, Water Canyon Beach, Signal Peak, Point Bennett's seals); wildlife (a three-island fox field guide, the scrub-jay only on Santa Cruz, the night lizard only on Santa Barbara Island); ranger programmes (Channel Islands Live, tidepool talks in Ventura, the ranger-led Point Bennett hike); Junior Ranger (the park booklet plus national themed books such as Underwater Explorer and Night Explorer); vehicles: boat, kayak.
- **Passport stamps**: seven cancellations — Ventura (Lagomarsino Visitor Center), Santa Barbara (Outdoors Santa Barbara Visitor Center), Anacapa Island, Santa Cruz Island, Santa Rosa Island, San Miguel Island and Santa Barbara Island — so a full set means an island-hopping campaign.
- **With friends / with the world**: the shared boat is the open world's natural lobby (everyone rides out together and must be back on the dock when the horn sounds); a kayak party through the sea caves timed between swells; a night on Anacapa with the foghorn; bow-spotting contests for whales; a snorkel hunt in the kelp; a group photo on the 157 stairs; a relay of foxes counted on three islands.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): no pets (they carry disease to the foxes); never feed the foxes and lock food in the fox boxes (foxes and ravens raid packs); pack out every scrap — there are no bins; carry your own water; stay on the trails (required on Anacapa, Santa Barbara Island and San Miguel) and well back from haul-outs and nesting gulls; take nothing, from tidepools or beaches; clean boots and bags before landing (no hitchhiking seeds); 100 yards from whales; no fishing in the marine reserves; stay with the ranger on San Miguel (old ordnance); and be on the dock on time.
- **Implementation challenges**: an ocean map where boats and swell carry the play; landings by ladder and by skiff through surf; sea caves entered by kayak (interiors with surging water); an underwater kelp forest (the engine swims on the surface only); tides and tidepools; islands as separate maps linked by a boat timetable; strong wind (San Miguel) and fog; seabird colonies of thousands (instanced birds, noise); a 3D sea arch; closed and permit-only zones (the Nature Conservancy's 76 % of Santa Cruz, the pelican areas, the escort-only parts of San Miguel).
