# White Sands National Park — `WHSA`

> New Mexico · established December 20, 2019 · 592.2 km² (146,344 acres) · 659,742 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 1,185–1,255 m · NPS region IMR
>
> The southern half of the world's largest gypsum dunefield (275 sq mi / 712 km² in all), white as snow and cool
> underfoot, in a basin ringed by mountains and wrapped in a missile range. In Hearthlight it should feel like a bright,
> silent sea of white waves: one road in, then sledding, barefoot dune walks, pink sunsets and moonlit nights.

## Reading the map

North up (the brochure must be turned a quarter-turn anticlockwise), the park is a lopsided polygon in the southern
Tularosa Basin, about 32 km across. Its **south-east edge runs along US 70**, the highway between Las Cruces (south-west)
and Alamogordo (north-east); the **visitor center**, a Pueblo Revival adobe complex, sits at the park's eastern corner
right on the highway, with the entrance station and the Border Patrol checkpoint beside it. Holloman Air Force Base
lies just north-east, outside; everything else around the park — including the San Andres Mountains that wall the
west horizon — is the **White Sands Missile Range**.

A single road, **Dunes Drive**, leaves the visitor center heading west-north-west. Its first 2.3 mi cross the
vegetated edge of the dunefield (Playa Trail and Dune Life Nature Trail face each other at mile ~2.3), then it passes
the **Interdune Boardwalk** and the **end of pavement** (~mile 4.3–5) and runs on packed white gypsum, plowed like snow,
to the **Heart of the Sands**: at mile ~6 the road splits into a long loop (~4 mi around) lined with the Primrose,
Roadrunner and Yucca picnic areas, the Backcountry Camping Loop trailhead, the group-use area, the Horse Area, the
West Filming Area and the Amphitheater; at its far north-west tip (mile 8.0 / 12.8 km) is the **Alkali Flat Trail**
trailhead. The drive is a dead end: 16 mi / 26 km round trip.

West of the drive the dunes become bare, higher and wilder, running ~5 km to the edge of **Alkali Flat**, the dry bed of
Ice Age Lake Otero, in the north-west. A north–south line across the middle of the park (~106.32° W) marks the
**cooperative-use zone**: everything west of it — the rest of Alkali Flat and **Lake Lucero**, the seasonal lake in the
south-west corner where the gypsum crystals form — is restricted and visited only on ranger tours (a few times a year) that
drive around through the missile range. Streams come off the San Andres Mountains (west) and the Sacramento Mountains (east,
beyond Alamogordo) and die in the basin; the Lost River reaches the park's north-east corner near Holloman.

The map's two arrows tell the whole story: water carries dissolved gypsum down from the mountains into Lake Lucero; the
prevailing south-west wind lifts the crystals off the dry lakebed and piles them into dunes that march north-east
toward the visitor center.

### Water

- **Lake Lucero** (south-west, restricted): a shallow seasonal lake/playa, the lowest point (~1,185 m / 3,887 ft). Rain
  and snowmelt carry dissolved gypsum down from the San Andres Mountains; as the water evaporates, **selenite crystals**
  grow in the mud; wind and weathering break them into sand grains. Most of the year it is a white crust.
- **Alkali Flat** (north-west): the dry bed of Ice Age **Lake Otero**, which covered ~1,600 sq mi of the basin; its
  shoreline muds preserve the fossil footprints.
- **The water table** lies only centimetres to a metre under the dunes; it keeps the sand damp and packed (the dunes'
  "glue") and after heavy rain it rises into the interdunes as temporary ponds (Playa Trail). The groundwater is too
  salty to drink; the visitor center is the only water source.
- Streams from the San Andres (west) and Sacramento (east) mountains die in the basin; the **Lost River** (north-east,
  near Holloman) is a short salty stream with the White Sands pupfish, the basin's only native fish.

### Relief

Almost flat: the basin floor lies at ~1,185–1,255 m (Lake Lucero 3,887 ft is the low point; the park's survey high point
NE30, 4,116 ft, is in the north-east; the NPS hiking page's "4,235 ft" is higher than the survey data). All the relief
is **dunes**, 3–18 m high (up to ~60 ft), moving up to ~9 m (30 ft) a year north-east: dome dunes at the upwind (west)
edge, transverse ridges and barchans in the Heart of the Sands, and plant-anchored parabolic dunes at the downwind
(east) edge by the visitor center. The mountains stand far off: the San Andres (2,000–2,500 m) 15–25 km west, the
Sacramento (to ~2,700 m) beyond Alamogordo to the east. In the game: dunes as smooth one- and two-level banks that are
all walkable (no cliffs), steep slip faces on their north-east sides for sledding, flat interdunes between them, and
the mountain ranges as distant 3D backdrops on the horizon.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation**: the dune edge and interdunes carry soaptree yucca (stems stretch to stay above burying sand),
  skunkbush sumac and rosemary-mint on sand pedestals, four-wing saltbush, Indian ricegrass, sand verbena, a few lone
  Rio Grande cottonwoods; the Heart of the Sands is almost bare; the playas grow pickleweed and iodine bush; desert
  scrub (creosote, mesquite) outside the dunes.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#F7F5EF` fresh gypsum dune · `#E9E4D8` shaded slip face · `#DCD2BE` damp
  interdune crust · `#C9BFA8` trampled sand and tracks · `#E3C9C2` dunes at sunset · `#AFC0D8` dunes under moonlight and
  shadow · `#7F8F5A` yucca and sumac green · `#B9A98C` alkali playa crust · `#9FB7B5` shallow water on Lake Lucero ·
  `#8A7A8E` the San Andres Mountains in haze.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): kit fox (night; dens under sumac pedestals), coyote, bleached earless lizard and
  Apache pocket mouse (white forms that evolved to match the sand), greater roadrunner, burrowing owl, darkling beetles
  (their tracks at dawn), white endemic moths (night), black-tailed jackrabbit, porcupine, White Sands pupfish (Lost
  River), and the odd gemsbok (oryx) wandering in from the missile range. Over 800 animal species, 220 birds.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): soaptree yucca (cream flower stalks, May–July), sand verbena (pink, spring–summer after
  rain), hoary rosemary-mint (purple, spring), skunkbush sumac (red berries, early summer; red leaves in autumn), Rio
  Grande cottonwood (gold in November), Indian ricegrass (autumn seed heads).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — the windy season, sand streaming off crests, dunes on the move, dust storms; summer —
  hot glare, monsoon storms, temporary ponds in the interdunes; autumn — calm and mild, the best hiking; winter —
  cold clear nights, rare snow (white on white).
- **Night**: the dunes glow under the full moon (Full Moon Nights with extended hours, May–October); dark skies and the
  Milky Way on moonless nights; kit foxes, owls and moths come out; tracks tell the night's story at dawn.
- **Sounds**: wind hissing over the crests, the squeak of gypsum underfoot, a raven, a far jet from Holloman, sled
  laughter at the loop, and deep silence away from the road.

## Moments

- **Sunset**: the dunes turn peach, pink and lavender as the sun sets behind the San Andres; ranger Sunset Strolls on
  Fridays–Sundays (a Dunes Discovery morning hike on weekdays).
- **Full Moon Nights** (monthly May–October; park open two hours late, sometimes music and talks) and **moonlight hikes**
  (March–December, the night before the full moon).
- **Sledding** on waxed saucers from the park store, all year, down the slip faces at the Heart of the Sands.
- **Lake Lucero tour** (a few times a year): a caravan through the missile range to the crystal lakebed.
- **Missile-test closure**: the gate closes, Dunes Drive empties for up to three hours; the visitor center stays open.
- **White Sands Balloon Invitational** (usually a September weekend): hot-air balloons over the white dunes.
- **After a storm**: the interdunes flood into mirror ponds; fossil footprints can show as dark "ghost tracks" on damp
  playas; dawn reveals a new page of animal tracks after every windy night.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a vignette of **512 × 384 tiles**, north up, with the visitor center at the east corner and Dunes
  Drive running west-north-west. Keep the Heart of the Sands near true scale (the loop is ~1.3 × 1 km → ~350 × 250
  tiles, dunes ~6–12 tiles high on the height grid), squeeze the first 5 miles of the drive ~1:6 (~130 tiles) with its
  three trail stops, keep the Alkali Flat Trail's 4 km outbound at ~1:3 (~250 tiles of dunes). The restricted west
  (Lake Lucero, the footprint playas) as a small gated zone reached only in a ranger-tour event; the San Andres and
  Sacramento mountains as skyline backdrops.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: the adobe **visitor center** (interior with the store and its sled rack); the loop's
  **picnic shelters** (the park's signature arched mid-century shade shelters, instantly recognisable in white sand);
  the **Amphitheater**; the **Interdune Boardwalk**; the **Alkali Flat** trail's red posts marching over the dunes; Lake
  Lucero's crystal field. Few 3D landmarks: the mountains on the horizon do the framing.
- **Activities**: sledding (the signature — saucer physics on slip faces), dune hikes following colored posts (red
  diamonds, blue clubs, orange…), photo spots (ripples in low light, a yucca on a pedestal, the picnic shelters, the
  boardwalk, sunset from a dune crest, a moonlit dune), tracking at dawn (match tracks to animals for the field guide),
  ranger programs (Sunset Stroll, full moon hike, Lake Lucero tour), Junior Ranger (the "Junior Dunes Ranger" booklet
  with Riley the mascot), backcountry camping at the 10 dune sites, horseback riding (horse area, permit), biking
  Dunes Drive, the Balloon Invitational.
- **Passport stamps**: one cancellation, still printed "White Sands NM — Alamogordo", at the visitor center store
  (Eastern National list, August 2025).
- **With friends / with the world**: sledding races on one dune, a Full Moon Night gathering at the Amphitheater, group
  sunset on a crest, a picnic-shelter lunch, a tracking hunt at dawn, a "don't lose the posts" Alkali Flat convoy.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): carry water (none beyond the visitor center; heat above 30 °C),
  keep the next trail post in sight and turn back if you can't see it, sled away from the road and not by the boardwalk,
  don't dig tunnels (collapse), stay off the fragile interdune crust by the boardwalk, pets on leash, leave the sand
  (don't take gypsum home), respect missile-test closures.
- **Implementation challenges**: dune fields that migrate (a slowly shifting height map), sand physics for sledding, a
  blinding white palette that must still read with toon outlines, glare and night/moon lighting on white sand, footprints
  that fill in with wind, drifting sand over the road, a scheduled closure mechanic, and a restricted zone.
