# Rocky Mountain National Park — `ROMO`

> Colorado · established January 26, 1915 · 1,075.8 km² (265,847.74 acres) · 4,171,431 recreation visits (2025) ·
> ~2,390–4,346 m · NPS region IMR
>
> A high block of the Front Range split by the Continental Divide, a third of it above treeline, crossed by the highest continuous paved road in North America. In Hearthlight it should feel like climbing through the life zones in one drive — elk meadows and ponderosa, then spruce and lakes under granite walls, then a windswept tundra with marmots and pikas at 3,700 m — with the choice of a gentle lake walk or Longs Peak's summit.

## Reading the map

North up, the park is a jagged block about twice as long as it is wide, set between two gateway towns: **Estes Park** outside its east edge and **Grand Lake** at its south-west corner, with national forest and wilderness all round. The **Continental Divide** enters in the north-west near La Poudre Pass, runs south-east over Milner Pass to the middle of the park, then south along the crest of the Front Range, past Flattop Mountain and Hallett Peak above Bear Lake, and out through the south boundary into the Indian Peaks. So the park has two unequal sides. The **east side** is higher-relief and busier: big meadows at the foot of the mountains (Horseshoe Park, Moraine Park, Upper Beaver Meadows), glacial valleys climbing west to the lakes under the Divide, the Mummy Range in the north-east corner, and Longs Peak (4,346 m) standing apart in the south-east. The **west side** is the long, straight Kawuneeche Valley, where the **Colorado River** is born, under the Never Summer Mountains that form the park's north-west edge.

**Trail Ridge Road** (US 34, 77 km from Estes Park to Grand Lake) is the spine. It draws an upside-down U across the middle of the park: west up from the Fall River and Beaver Meadows entrances, which join at Deer Ridge Junction; along the crest of Trail Ridge above Forest Canyon (Many Parks Curve, Rainbow Curve, Forest Canyon Overlook, Rock Cut, Lava Cliffs); over the road's high point (3,713 m) to the **Alpine Visitor Center** at Fall River Pass (3,595 m), the highest visitor centre in the park system, at the top of the U. It comes down past Medicine Bow Curve to **Milner Pass** on the Divide (3,279 m, Poudre Lake), then down a flight of switchbacks (Farview Curve) to the Colorado River trailhead. From there it runs south down the Kawuneeche Valley past Timber Creek and the Holzwarth Historic Site to the Kawuneeche Visitor Center and the Grand Lake Entrance. Its alpine section, from Many Parks Curve on the east to the Colorado River trailhead on the west (~48 km), is closed from mid-October to Memorial Day. Distance markers: Beaver Meadows Entrance → Deer Ridge Junction 5 km; Fall River Entrance → the Alluvial Fan junction 3 km; Deer Ridge Junction → Alpine Visitor Center 31 km; Alpine Visitor Center → the valley floor near the Colorado River trailhead 19 km; then ~13 km down the Kawuneeche toward the Grand Lake Entrance. **Old Fall River Road**, the park's first road, climbs 14 km of gravel from Endovalley up Fall River's valley to the Alpine Visitor Center, one-way uphill only, early July to September.

The **Bear Lake Road** (15 km) is the second great corridor. It leaves US 36 just inside the Beaver Meadows Entrance and runs south-west past the Moraine Park Discovery Center and Moraine Park (campground, stables, the Cub Lake and Fern Lake trailheads), Hollowell Park, the **Park & Ride** shuttle hub at Glacier Basin, Sprague Lake, the Bierstadt Lake and Glacier Gorge trailheads, to a dead end at **Bear Lake** (2,890 m), under Hallett Peak and Flattop Mountain. Free shuttles run it in summer. The other hubs are Beaver Meadows (park headquarters, visitor centre open all year), the Fall River entrance (Aspenglen campground, Fall River Visitor Center), Horseshoe Park (Sheep Lakes, the Alluvial Fan), the Alpine Visitor Center, Timber Creek and Holzwarth on the west side, and three outlying east-side areas off CO 7: **Lily Lake**, **Longs Peak** (trailhead, tent campground) and **Wild Basin** (a gravel road to a trailhead of waterfalls). North-east, **Lumpy Ridge** (granite knobs, the Twin Owls, Gem Lake) sits just above Estes Park.

The **roadless parts** are the Mummy Range and the Comanche Peak and Cache la Poudre country in the north; the high lake basins and the Divide west of Bear Lake; the south-west (East Inlet, North Inlet, the Paradise Park research area); and Wild Basin's upper lakes. The Never Summer range's east flank carries the **Grand Ditch**, a 19th-century water canal visible as a line across the slopes. **Relief** organises it all: meadows at 2,400–2,600 m on the east and 2,650–2,750 m in the Kawuneeche, forested slopes and U-shaped valleys to ~3,400 m, then broad rolling tundra (Trail Ridge) and granite summits from 3,700 to 4,346 m.

### Water

The **Continental Divide** splits the park's water between two oceans. West of it the **Colorado River** rises near La Poudre Pass in the north-west and flows south through the **Kawuneeche Valley** — meandering among willows and beaver ponds, with moose — past Timber Creek and the Grand Lake Entrance, out of the park to Grand Lake, Shadow Mountain Lake and Lake Granby, on its way to the Gulf of California. The **Grand Ditch** (dug 1890–1936) contours along the Never Summer Mountains, catching their creeks and carrying the water over La Poudre Pass to the east side. East of the Divide, the **Cache la Poudre** heads north from Poudre Lake at Milner Pass; the **Fall River** runs east through Horseshoe Park (Chasm Falls, the Alluvial Fan's falls); the **Big Thompson** drains Forest Canyon and Moraine Park to Estes Park; **Glacier Creek** carries the Bear Lake–Glacier Gorge lakes down past Sprague Lake; the **North St. Vrain** drains Wild Basin (Copeland Falls, Calypso Cascades, Ouzel Falls). **Lakes**: more than 150, from the lily ponds of the montane (Cub, Nymph) to the cirque tarns under the Divide (Emerald, Sky Pond, Chasm, Frozen); Bear, Sprague and Lily lakes are the easy ones. A few small **glaciers** and permanent snowfields survive in north-facing cirques: Tyndall (above Emerald Lake), Andrews, Taylor, Sprague, Mills (under Longs Peak), Rowe (Mummy Range). **Lawn Lake**'s dam failed in July 1982 and the flood dropped the Alluvial Fan into Horseshoe Park.

### Relief

From ~2,390 m (7,840 ft; the park's figure, at Beaver Meadows on the east edge) to 4,346 m (Longs Peak). The east side climbs in big steps: glacial meadows at 2,400–2,700 m (Moraine Park is a valley dammed by its glacier's lateral and terminal moraines — long forested ridges on each side), then U-shaped valleys and gorges with stairs of lakes and falls up to cirques at 3,000–3,400 m, then the granite crest of the Divide (Hallett, Otis, Taylor, Powell, McHenrys) at 3,800–4,000 m. **Trail Ridge** is the opposite shape: a broad, rounded tundra ridge at 3,500–3,700 m with **Forest Canyon** cut 750 m deep below its south side. **Longs Peak** stands east of the Divide with its flat top and sheer east face (the **Diamond**, ~300 m of vertical granite above Chasm Lake). The **Mummy Range** (Hagues 4,133 m) makes a separate cluster in the north-east; the **Never Summer Mountains** (volcanic, reddish) wall the west side of the Kawuneeche Valley.

In game terms: meadows at levels 0–1 (Kawuneeche at 1), forested moraines as 1–2 banks with long ridges, the valleys stepping up in banks and short cliffs with a lake on each step (Nymph → Dream → Emerald is a three-step stair), the tundra plateau as a wide flat level 5 crossed by the road, Forest Canyon as the one deep cut beside it, the Divide's granite as cliffs to level 6–7 with 3D peaks. 3D peaks: Longs Peak (flat-topped, the Diamond), Hallett Peak, Flattop, the Mummy Range, the Never Summer summits, Deer Mountain, the Twin Owls on Lumpy Ridge.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: montane (below ~2,750 m): ponderosa pine on warm south slopes (sweet-smelling bark, Abert's squirrels), Douglas-fir and lodgepole on cooler ones, aspen groves, meadows of grass and sedge with willow along the rivers; subalpine (2,750–3,470 m): dense Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir up to 30 m, with wildflower meadows (arnica, elephant's head, fairy slipper) and bogs; treeline (~3,400–3,500 m): wind-flagged krummholz of spruce, fir and limber pine; alpine (above ~3,470 m): cushion plants, sedges and lichens on thin soil, fellfield, boulder fields, snowfields.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#C9B48A` montane meadow tan · `#9FB27A` summer tundra green · `#B7A15E` autumn tundra gold · `#2E4636` spruce-fir green · `#E4B93C` aspen gold · `#A48A7A` pink-grey granite · `#5E4C4A` Lava Cliffs tuff · `#E3703A` orange lichen on boulders · `#3F7E8E` subalpine lake blue-green · `#F3F5F8` snowfield.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): elk (Moraine Park, Horseshoe Park, Upper Beaver Meadows, the Kawuneeche; rut mid-September to mid-October), bighorn sheep (over 350: Sheep Lakes in May–August, the tundra in summer), moose (the Kawuneeche Valley's willows), mule deer, black bear, mountain lion (rarely seen), coyote, yellow-bellied marmot and American pika (Rock Cut, the Alpine Ridge Trail), white-tailed ptarmigan (tundra all year), Abert's squirrel (ponderosa), golden-mantled ground squirrel, beaver and river otter (the Colorado), American dipper (Wild Basin's streams), Steller's jay, Clark's nutcracker, broad-tailed hummingbird, greenback cutthroat trout.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): Colorado blue columbine (the state flower, July in aspen and subalpine meadows), alpine forget-me-not and moss campion (June–July on the tundra), five-petaled alpine avens (tundra, July), sky pilot, elephant's head (wet meadows, July), fairy slipper orchid (June, deep forest), quaking aspen (gold in late September), ponderosa pine, limber pine (krummholz at treeline), Wood's rose, kinnikinnick.
- **Weather & seasons**: winter (November–April): deep snow above the meadows, wind scouring the tundra, Trail Ridge Road closed and the east meadows open (snowshoeing at Bear Lake, sledding at Hidden Valley); spring (May–June): snow walls on Trail Ridge when it opens on Memorial Day, elk calving, bighorn at Sheep Lakes; summer (July–August): tundra flowers, near-daily afternoon thunderstorms with lightning (be below treeline by noon), crowds and timed entry; autumn (mid-September–October): aspens gold, elk bugling at dusk, first snow on the peaks, Trail Ridge closing mid-October. Fire scars from 2020 (the Cameron Peak and East Troublesome fires) mark the north and the Kawuneeche/Moraine areas.
- **Night**: dark skies over Trail Ridge and the Kawuneeche, the Milky Way over the Divide, astronomy programmes with volunteer telescopes (on the brochure), elk bugling under the stars in autumn, great horned owls.
- **Sounds**: elk bugles echoing across Moraine Park, the marmot's whistle and the pika's squeak on the tundra, wind on Trail Ridge, thunder rolling off the Divide in the afternoon, waterfalls in Glacier Gorge and Wild Basin, the Colorado's riffles in the Kawuneeche, Steller's jays and nutcrackers, the shuttle's hiss at Bear Lake.

## Moments

- **The elk rut**: mid-September to mid-October, at dawn and dusk in Moraine Park, Horseshoe Park, Upper Beaver Meadows and the Kawuneeche; bulls bugle and gather harems, some meadows close to people from 5 pm to 7 am, and volunteers manage the crowds.
- **Trail Ridge Road opens and closes**: it opens on or near Memorial Day through walls of plowed snow, and closes to cars in mid-October (for the season with the first big storms).
- **Bighorn sheep at Sheep Lakes**: May to August, mostly mornings; rangers stop traffic so the herd can cross to the mineral soil.
- **Tundra flower peak**: early to mid-July, a carpet of forget-me-nots, moss campion and avens around Rock Cut and the Alpine Visitor Center.
- **Aspen gold**: mid to late September (Bear Lake Road, the Kawuneeche, Horseshoe Park).
- **Sunrise at the lakes**: Hallett Peak and the Divide lit orange over Dream Lake and Sprague Lake.
- **Longs Peak's window**: the Keyhole route is a non-technical hike only from about mid-July to mid-September; summit parties start around 2–3 am to be off the top before the afternoon storms.
- **Afternoon thunderstorms**: July–August, lightning on Trail Ridge almost daily; a real rule of the tundra.
- **Pikas' haypiles**: August–September, pikas stack flowers under the rocks for winter.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: 1024 × 1024 tiles (32 × 32 chunks), north up, the park's tall block with Estes Park and Grand Lake as edge hubs. Trail Ridge Road's upside-down U is the spine: ~1,800–2,200 tiles of road (Estes Park to Grand Lake, ~3.5 min by car), its alpine stretch kept long enough to feel high and exposed. Hubs at 1 tile ≈ 3 m: the Bear Lake corridor's upper end (Bear Lake, Nymph, Dream and Emerald lakes, 2.9 km of trail → ~300 × 200 tiles; the Glacier Gorge lakes beside it), Moraine Park (~300 tiles across), the Alpine Visitor Center with the Alpine Ridge stairs, Rock Cut, Sheep Lakes, Holzwarth. Bear Lake Road compressed ~10× (Beaver Meadows → Bear Lake ~60 s by car or shuttle). The Kawuneeche Valley compressed ~15× as a long green corridor along the west edge. Longs Peak as the map's summit in the south-east with a compressed Keyhole route (Boulder Field, Keyhole, Ledges, Narrows) — a signature climb. Cut or reduced: the Mummy Range (views and one trail to Lawn Lake), the Cache la Poudre valley, the far south-west (East Inlet as a short waterfall spur), Lumpy Ridge as a corner with the Twin Owls.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Bear Lake (the open-world plaza, with the shuttle stop), the Alpine Visitor Center (the high plaza, snack bar, the stair to 3,660 m), Moraine Park, Beaver Meadows, Kawuneeche Visitor Center, Estes Park and Grand Lake at the edges. 3D landmarks: Longs Peak with the Diamond, Hallett Peak's north face, the Twin Owls, Lava Cliffs, the Mushroom Rocks at Rock Cut, the Beaver Meadows Visitor Center (a low 1967 modernist building by Taliesin Associated Architects), the Moraine Park Discovery Center's 1923 lodge, the Holzwarth cabins, the Alluvial Fan's boulders, the Grand Ditch's line on the Never Summer slopes, the Trail Ridge Road's snow walls in spring. Interiors: the Alpine Visitor Center, the Moraine Park Discovery Center, a Holzwarth ranch cabin.
- **Activities**: hiking (the lake chains, Flattop, Sky Pond, Chasm Lake, Wild Basin's falls); photo spots — Dream Lake with Hallett Peak, Sprague Lake at sunrise, a bull elk bugling in Moraine Park, bighorn at Sheep Lakes, a marmot at Rock Cut, the Diamond from Chasm Lake, the snow walls on Trail Ridge, Forest Canyon Overlook; wildlife spotting (the elk rut with a volunteer-style « bugle corps » role, moose in the Kawuneeche, ptarmigan hidden in the tundra); ranger programmes (elk talks, tundra walks at the Alpine Visitor Center, night-sky telescopes, geology at Moraine Park); Young Ranger ideas: name the three life zones on a drive, spot a pika's haypile, find where the water parts at Milner Pass, stay on the tundra trail; water: fishing for greenback cutthroat, no swimming culture (cold lakes), canoes on Grand Lake outside; vehicles: car on Trail Ridge, the free shuttles (Bear Lake, Moraine Park) and the Hiker Shuttle from Estes Park, horses from the Moraine Park and Glacier Creek stables, bike on the roads (and up Old Fall River Road before it opens to cars); winter: snowshoe and ski from Bear Lake, sledding at Hidden Valley; climbing: Lumpy Ridge, the Diamond for experts.
- **Passport stamps**: Beaver Meadows Visitor Center, Alpine Visitor Center, Kawuneeche Visitor Center, Fall River Visitor Center, Moraine Park Discovery Center, the Wild Basin and Longs Peak ranger stations (to confirm on the Passport stamp finder).
- **With friends / with the world**: Bear Lake's shore and the shuttle queue at Park & Ride are the natural meeting places; the Alpine Visitor Center terrace is the high plaza where everyone pauses; Moraine Park at dusk in autumn gathers a crowd for the rut. Party ideas: a pre-dawn Longs Peak ascent where the party must summit and get below treeline before the afternoon storm timer; a « bugle corps » where players keep crowds back from elk; a tundra photo hunt (marmot, pika, ptarmigan) on the Tundra Communities Trail; riding the shuttle together to hop between trailheads.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on the trail in the tundra closure zones along Trail Ridge Road (off-trail walking is banned within 100 yd / 91 m of trails and parking lots there); 25 yd / 23 m from elk, moose and bighorn, 100 yd / 91 m from bears; no pets on trails, tundra or meadows; never stop or park on the road — use pullouts; get below treeline when storms build; pick no wildflowers; feed nothing (the Clark's nutcrackers and ground squirrels will beg).
- **Implementation challenges**: the three life zones and treeline as a visible band that the road climbs through; a high tundra plateau with a deep canyon beside it; snow walls along the road in late spring and the seasonal closure; a timed afternoon-thunderstorm system with lightning above treeline; the elk rut's crowds and meadow closures; shuttles with stops and a timed-entry rule (or a gentler in-game equivalent); stepped lake chains (lake → bank → lake); the Continental Divide as a named line where water splits; fire-scarred forest from 2020; Longs Peak's exposed scramble (ledges, a narrow keyhole, a « homestretch » slab) as a safe but thrilling climb.
