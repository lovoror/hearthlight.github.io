# Indiana Dunes National Park — `INDU`

> Indiana · established February 15, 2019 (national lakeshore since November 5, 1966) · 65.8 km² (16,268 acres) · 2,629,497 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 176–274 m · NPS region MWR (Midwest; DOI Region 3, Great Lakes)
>
> Fifteen miles of singing-sand beaches and high dunes on the southern tip of Lake Michigan, strung between steel mills, beach towns and Chicago's commuter railroad, with bogs, marshes and oak savannas hidden behind the dunes. In Hearthlight it should feel like a summer seaside on an inland sea — climb a dune, see the Chicago skyline across the water, then duck into a quiet marsh — with the smokestacks always on the horizon.

## Reading the map

North is up and **Lake Michigan** fills the top half of the sheet. The Indiana shore is a nearly straight line running from lower left to upper right: from Miller Beach in Gary (west, 41.61 N) to Michigan City (north-east, 41.72 N), it climbs about 12 km over its ~40 km length. The national park is not one block but a **string of green units** pressed against that shore, interrupted by beach towns and by heavy industry, with the state park in the middle and a few inland units behind. A single transport corridor runs the whole length a few hundred metres to 2 km inland: **US 12 (the Dunes Highway)** with the **South Shore Line** tracks beside it; US 20 runs parallel further inland and I-94 further still.

From west to east: (1) the **Miller/Gary unit** — the Paul H. Douglas Center for Environmental Education and the Miller Woods trail through black oak savanna to the beach, beside Gary's own Marquette Park, with the Grand Calumet River lagoons behind; the Marquette bike trail runs east from here. (2) The **West Beach unit** at the end of County Line Road — West Beach (bathhouse, lifeguards, a 642-car lot), the Dune Succession Trail stairs, the Long Lake interdunal lake and a jack-pine pinery; across US 12 to the south, **Tolleston Dunes** with its overlook and trail. The town of Ogden Dunes follows. (3) **Portage Lakefront and Riverwalk**, a small reclaimed industrial site at the mouth of the Burns Waterway, with a pavilion and a riverwalk to a breakwater light. (4) Then the **industrial gap**: 7–8 km of Port of Indiana–Burns Harbor, the Burns Harbor steel mill (grey blocks on the map) and the NIPSCO Bailly power plant, with no park land on the shore.

(5) The **central unit** around Porter: behind the gated town of Dune Acres, the Cowles Bog trail (off Mineral Springs Road) loops through ponds and savanna to a quiet beach; south of US 12 sit Park Headquarters and the historic core — Bailly Homestead, Bailly Cemetery, Chellberg Farm, the Dunes Learning Center and Mnoké Prairie along the Little Calumet River — with the Porter Brickyard bike trail heading south-east. Porter Beach is at the end of the town's lakeshore road. Next comes **Indiana Dunes State Park** (a state park inside the national park's shoreline), with the highest dunes — the Tremonts: Mount Tom, Mount Holden and Mount Jackson — its beach pavilion, nature center and campground; SR 49 runs south from it to the shared **Indiana Dunes Visitor Center** at US 20 in Porter. (6) The **east unit** from Kemil Road to Michigan City: Kemil, Dunbar, Lake View and Central Avenue beaches strung along Lake Front Drive in the town of Beverly Shores, the five 1933 Century of Progress houses, the Dune Ridge Trail; inland of the town, the **Great Marsh** (stippled wetland between Beverly Shores and US 12) with its trail; south of US 12, the Calumet Dunes (an old shoreline ridge), the Glenwood Dunes horse-and-ski trails and Dunewood Campground; and at the far north-east corner, next to the Michigan City power plant's cooling tower, **Mount Baldy** and its beach.

Inland, the map shows two more units in the lower right: the **Heron Rookery** (a strip along the Little Calumet River between 450 East and 600 East roads, 10 km south-east of the state park) and **Pinhook Bog** (a glacial kettle bog near I-94, 23 km east-south-east, visited on ranger-led tours). The area map adds Hobart Prairie Grove and Hoosier Prairie far to the south-west. The relief reads in bands parallel to the shore: beach, then foredunes and high dunes (30–60 m), then interdunal wetlands, then older forested dune ridges that were shorelines of glacial Lake Chicago (Tolleston, Calumet, Glenwood), and finally the Valparaiso Moraine inland.

### Water

- **Lake Michigan** (176–177 m above sea level): the whole northern edge; a freshwater sea with waves, sandbars, **rip currents** on north-wind days, no tides; water ~20–24 °C in August. The park boundary runs 100 yards (91 m) offshore. In winter **shelf ice** and ice volcanoes build along the beach (never walk on it). On clear days the Chicago skyline shows across the water to the north-west.
- **Longshore drift** carries sand from north to south along the lake's east shore and then westward along Indiana; harbour piers (Michigan City) starve Mount Baldy's beach of sand.
- **Little Calumet River** (East Branch): flows **west** through the Heron Rookery and past Bailly/Chellberg, then turns north into the lake through the man-made **Burns Waterway** at Portage. The **Grand Calumet River** lagoons lie behind Miller Woods (flowing west toward Indiana Harbor). Dunes Creek drains the state park; Trail Creek reaches the lake at Michigan City.
- **Wetlands**: the Great Marsh (largest wetland complex in the lake's watershed, behind Beverly Shores), Cowles Bog (a fen/swamp complex), Long Lake (interdunal), Pinhook Bog (a true acid bog in a kettle), marshes at Tolleston and Miller.
- No waterfalls or springs of note; beaches may close for high bacteria after storms.

### Relief

Lowest point 176 m (Lake Michigan, 577 ft); highest ~274 m (900 ft, the Upland Trail on the Valparaiso Moraine at Pinhook Bog). Along the shore the land rises in parallel ridges: beach (0–2 m), marram-grass foredunes (5–15 m), then the high dunes and blowouts of the state park and West Beach — Mount Tom 192 ft (59 m) above the lake, Mount Holden 184 ft, Mount Jackson 176 ft, Mount Baldy 126 ft (38 m) — with sand faces too steep to walk up except on stairs. Behind them, flat interdunal wetlands, then three lower forested dune ridges that mark older, higher shorelines of glacial Lake Chicago (Calumet ~12,000 years, Glenwood, Tolleston), and finally the moraine. In the game: dunes are walkable sand slopes of 1–4 levels with painted sand faces, blowouts as bowls, stairs/boardwalks where trails climb (Dune Succession, Mount Tom, Central Avenue Beach); wetlands as flat LIQUID-edged marsh; Mount Tom and Mount Baldy as 3D sand peaks (Baldy half-bare, sliding onto trees); the steel mills and cooling tower as distant 3D silhouettes.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: beach (bare, with sea rocket); foredunes — marram grass, sand reed, sand cherry, cottonwoods; high dunes and blowouts — jack pine (a northern relic at West Beach), bearberry, junipers, black oak; behind the dunes — black oak savanna with lupine and prickly pear (Tolleston, Miller Woods), sedge-cattail marsh and buttonbush swamp (Great Marsh, Cowles Bog), tamarack-sphagnum bog (Pinhook); old dune ridges and moraine — beech-maple and oak forest with white pine; river floodplain woods with spring wildflowers (Heron Rookery). Over 1,100 flowering plants and ferns: plant diversity is among the highest of all national parks.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#E8D9B0` pale quartz beach sand · `#D9C38A` dry dune crest · `#8FAF5A` marram grass · `#3F6E3A` oak and beech woods · `#6B8F4E` marsh sedge · `#4E8FB8` Lake Michigan in summer (with `#2F5E7E` deep water) · `#C9DCE0` shelf ice · `#7B4A2E` Porter brick (Chellberg house) · `#8A8F96` steel-mill grey · `#B5452F` South Shore train trim.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): white-tailed deer (savanna edges at dusk), coyote, red fox, beaver and muskrat (Great Marsh), sandhill crane (Great Marsh, spring and fall), great blue heron (marshes, Heron Rookery), ring-billed and herring gulls (every beach), bald eagle (lakeshore in winter), migrating hawks (fall, along the shore), red-headed woodpecker (black oak savanna), six-lined racerunner (lizard of the open dunes), Fowler's toad (dune pools), Blanding's turtle (marshes), monarch butterfly (fall migration along the shore), Karner blue butterfly (lupine savanna; now very rare). More than 350 bird species; the southern tip of the lake funnels migrants.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): marram grass (the dune-builder, all year), wild lupine (late May–June), eastern prickly pear (yellow flowers, June–July), butterfly weed (July), rough blazing star (prairie, August), marsh marigold and spring beauty (Heron Rookery, April), pitcher plant and round-leaved sundew (Pinhook Bog, summer), tamarack (bog, golden in October), jack pine (West Beach), sassafras (red-orange in October).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — cold lake fog, migrating warblers, wildflowers; summer — hot humid beach days, thunderstorms rolling in over the lake, rip-current flags; fall — hawk flights, gold savannas, crisp sunsets; winter — lake-effect snow, shelf ice and ice volcanoes, frozen marsh, snowshoes and cross-country skis on Glenwood Dunes.
- **Night**: surprisingly dark to the north over the lake; Kemil Beach's lot stays open all night for stargazing and Beverly Shores is an International Dark Sky Community; the Milky Way over the water, the orange glow of the mills and Chicago on the horizon; fireflies in the savannas in June–July; the singing chorus of spring peepers and chorus frogs in the marsh.
- **Sounds**: waves on sand, the squeak of "singing sand" underfoot, gulls, wind hissing through marram grass, the South Shore's horn and electric hum, distant mill clangs, red-winged blackbirds and sandhill-crane bugles over the Great Marsh, spring peepers, ice groaning along the shelf in winter.

## Moments

- **Sunset over Lake Michigan** every clear evening; on the clearest days the **Chicago skyline** rises across the water (from Mount Tom's platform, West Beach and the Portage Riverwalk).
- **3 Dune Challenge**: Mount Jackson, Mount Holden and Mount Tom in 1.5 miles (state park, all year; a badge-style challenge).
- **Spring migration** peaks in May; the **Indiana Dunes Birding Festival** is held the third weekend of May; fall hawk and songbird flights follow the shore in September–October.
- **Spring wildflowers** carpet the Heron Rookery woods in April–May; **lupine** turns the savannas blue in late May–June; prickly pear blooms in June–July.
- **Ranger-led Mount Baldy summit hikes** (scheduled tours only) and **Pinhook Bog open houses** on summer weekends.
- **Century of Progress homes tour** (the last weekend of September, with Indiana Landmarks; shuttle from the Visitor Center).
- **Shelf ice** season (December–March): white ridges and "ice volcanoes" along the shore — look, never walk.
- **Maple sugaring at Chellberg Farm** in late winter (traditional March weekends), and farm animals in summer.
- **Night skies at Kemil Beach**, the park's 24-hour beach; summer fireflies.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a long, shallow map, **1280 × 576 tiles** (~1.9 × 0.85 km at 1.5 m/tile), north up with the shoreline as a diagonal from the lower left to the upper right; the ~40 km of shore compressed ~20×. Near true scale: West Beach with its stairs and Long Lake, the state park's three dunes and pavilion (~800 m), Mount Baldy and its beach (~500 m), the Beverly Shores lakefront with the Century of Progress houses, the Bailly–Chellberg farm cluster, Cowles Bog's loop. Compressed: US 12 frontage, town blocks, the industrial gap (a short stretch of mill fence, stacks and a port crane silhouette), the inland distance. Inland outliers (Heron Rookery, Pinhook Bog) become two small pockets along the map's south edge reached by a road; Hoosier Prairie and Hobart Prairie Grove are cut or become a "prairie" corner.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Indiana Dunes Visitor Center, West Beach, the state park pavilion and Mount Tom, Beverly Shores (Lake View beach + station), Douglas Center. 3D landmarks: Mount Tom's viewing platform, Mount Baldy (a bald sand hill half-swallowing trees), the Century of Progress houses (a pink stucco Florida Tropical House, a glass House of Tomorrow, a steel Armco-Ferro House, a log Cypress House, a Rostone house), Chellberg's brick farmhouse and barn, the Bailly log buildings, the South Shore train and stations, the Michigan City cooling tower and the Burns Harbor mill as silhouettes. Interiors: Visitor Center, Douglas Center (live animals), Chellberg farmhouse, state park nature center, West Beach bathhouse.
- **Activities**: swimming and beachcombing (with lifeguards, rip-current flags); dune climbs (3 Dune Challenge as a timed challenge), photo spots — the skyline from Mount Tom, the Dune Succession stairs, Mount Baldy's slope, the Century of Progress houses, sunset at Portage Riverwalk, shelf ice, lupine fields at Tolleston; birding (the Great Marsh, migration events); ranger programs (Mount Baldy summit tour, Pinhook Bog open house, night sky at Kemil Beach); junior ranger (activity guide, Beachcomber sheet, Night Explorer); kayaking the Lake Michigan Water Trail; biking the Calumet trail and taking the South Shore back; horse riding at Glenwood Dunes; winter snowshoes and skis.
- **Passport stamps**: the 2025 list gives "Porter", "West Beach", "Chellberg Farm", "Bailly Homestead", "Pin Hook Bog", "Mount Baldy" plus two Amtrak Trails & Rails stamps ("Wolverine", "Blue Water") and a 2024 eclipse stamp. Stamps are kept at the Indiana Dunes Visitor Center (Porter) and the Paul H. Douglas Center. In the game: Visitor Center, West Beach bathhouse, Chellberg Farm, Bailly Homestead, Pinhook Bog trailhead, Mount Baldy lot; the Trails & Rails stamps on the train.
- **With friends / with the world**: a party beach day (volleyball, sandcastle building contest that the waves wash away), the 3 Dune Challenge as a race, a synchronized group photo on Mount Tom's platform at sunset with the skyline, a birding "big day" at the Great Marsh in May, meeting strangers on the South Shore train and at West Beach's boardwalk; stargazing party at Kemil Beach.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on marked trails and stairs (marram grass holds the dunes — trampling set Mount Baldy moving); stay off Mount Baldy except with a ranger; never walk on shelf ice; heed rip-current flags and swim near lifeguards; take no rocks, shells or fossils; no fires on the beach; don't feed the gulls; Pinhook Bog only with a ranger (the mat is fragile); respect private homes in Beverly Shores, Ogden Dunes and Dune Acres.
- **Implementation challenges**: sand that moves (a dune creeping over trees; windblown sand), steep loose sand slopes you slide down, waves and rip currents on a big lake, seasonal shelf ice along the shore, a busy commuter train, dense towns and heavy industry right against a park (fences, stacks, private land), a diagonal coastline on a square grid, and a sky-line of a distant city across water.
