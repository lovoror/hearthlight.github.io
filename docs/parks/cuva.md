# Cuyahoga Valley National Park — `CUVA`

> Ohio · established October 11, 2000 (national recreation area since December 27, 1974) · 132.0 km² (32,607 acres) · 3,025,325 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 180–355 m · NPS region MWR (Midwest; DOI Region 3, Great Lakes)
>
> A green, lived-in river valley between Cleveland and Akron, threaded by four parallel arteries: the winding Cuyahoga River, the Ohio & Erie Canal Towpath, a scenic railroad and a country byway, with villages, farms, waterfalls and sandstone ledges on either side. In Hearthlight it should feel like a model-railway diorama: you bike the towpath, wave the yellow-and-red train down to ride home, and poke up side ravines to find falls and ledges.

## Reading the map

The park is a long, narrow ribbon, north up, laid along the Cuyahoga River where it flows **north** from Akron to Cleveland (Lake Erie is ~20 km beyond the top edge). The green NPS land is narrowest in the north, where it squeezes between Independence and Valley View (2–4 km wide), bulges around Brecksville, pinches again at Jaite, and is widest (up to 10 km) in the south-centre around Peninsula and the Virginia Kendall uplands; below Everett it narrows to a 4–5 km strip and ends at Botzum, 3 km above Akron, with only the rail and river corridor running on to Akron Northside. It is a patchwork: Cleveland Metroparks reservations (Bedford, Brecksville) and Summit Metro Parks (Furnace Run, Deep Lock Quarry, O'Neil Woods, Hampton Hills) sit inside the boundary in a darker blue-green, and villages, farms, ski resorts, golf courses and private land are sprinkled throughout.

Four arteries run the whole length side by side along the valley floor and organise everything. From west to east, roughly: the **Cuyahoga Valley Scenic Railroad** (on the west bank), the byway (**Ohio & Erie Canalway America's Byway**, yellow: Canal Road → Valley View Road → Chaffee Road → SR 82 → Riverview Road → Merriman Road → Memorial Parkway into Akron), the **Cuyahoga River** meandering in the middle, and the **Towpath Trail** with the old canal on the east bank. The river swaps sides with them in places, and the byway jogs east over Chaffee Road above Station Road Bridge. The railroad's 8 stations, north to south: Rockside (Independence, park's north edge, next to Lock 39), Canal Exploration Center, Brecksville (Station Road Bridge), Boston Mill (the visitor centre), Peninsula Depot, Indigo Lake (Hale Farm), Botzum, and Akron Northside (downtown Akron, off the park). Akron-Peninsula Road is the second north–south road, on the east side of the river south of Peninsula.

East–west roads climb out of the valley at regular intervals and carry you to the side attractions on the rims: from north to south Rockside Road, Tinkers Creek Road (to Bedford Reservation and Tinkers Creek Gorge), Pleasant Valley/Alexander Road, SR 82 (Chippewa Road, on a tall concrete arch bridge above Station Road Bridge; west to Brecksville Reservation), Highland/Vaughn Road (Red Lock, Jaite), Boston Mills Road (Boston, Blue Hen Falls, Brandywine Falls via Brandywine Road), then two **interstate high bridges** crossing the valley just south of Boston (I-271 to the north, the Ohio Turnpike I-80 to the south), then SR 303 through Peninsula (east to Happy Days Lodge, Ledges and Kendall Lake via Truxell/Kendall Park Road), Everett Road (Everett Covered Bridge), Ira Road (Beaver Marsh, Hale Farm), Bath Road (the heronry) and Smith Road/Portage Trail at the south end. Nothing is one-way and nothing is closed seasonally except Hunt House and some facilities; the Towpath may flood in spring.

The visitor hubs line the valley floor: **Canal Exploration Center** (north, at Canal and Hillside roads, seasonal, lock demonstrations at Lock 38), **Boston** in the exact middle (Boston Mill Visitor Center, the main one, on the west bank beside the station; Boston Store and M.D. Garage across the river; Boston Mills ski slopes on the west wall; Brandywine Falls 2.5 km east up Brandywine Creek), **Peninsula** 2.5 km south (Depot, Lock 29, Trail Mix store, a real village), and **Hunt House** in the south (seasonal information by Beaver Marsh, off Bolanz Road). The uplands on either side hold the scenic places reached by short drives: Tinkers Creek Gorge, Bridal Veil and Great Falls in Bedford Reservation (north-east), Brecksville Nature Center (north-west), Brandywine Falls (east of Boston), Blue Hen Falls (west of Boston), the Ledges, Octagon, Happy Days and Kendall Lake in the former Virginia Kendall park (east of Peninsula), and Hale Farm & Village with Indigo Lake and Everett Covered Bridge (south-west).

Relief is simple and strong: a flat floodplain 180–230 m high, 0.5–1.5 km wide, walled by 60–120 m wooded bluffs cut by side ravines (Tinkers Creek, Chippewa Creek, Brandywine Creek, Furnace Run, Yellow Creek) that drop to the river over sandstone lips as waterfalls; on top, a rolling plateau of fields and woods at 280–350 m with the Sharon conglomerate cliffs of the Ledges near its eastern edge. There is no true wilderness: the "wild" parts are the forested walls and ravines between the roads, notably the Brecksville–Jaite stretch and the plateau south of Kendall Lake.

### Water

- **Cuyahoga River**: the "crooked river" makes a U: it rises east of Cleveland, flows south to Cuyahoga Falls and Akron (the Gorge), turns sharply north at Akron and flows **north** through the whole park to Lake Erie in downtown Cleveland. Inside the park it meanders freely across a 0.5–1.5 km floodplain for ~25 mi / 40 km, dropping from ~220 m at Botzum to ~180 m at Rockside; shallow riffles and gravel bars, tea-brown after rain. Famous for catching fire in 1969 (in Cleveland), now clean enough for otters and eagles. Floods in spring cover the floodplain and parts of the towpath.
- **Tributaries** from the east: Tinkers Creek (gorge, Bridal Veil & Great Falls), Brandywine Creek (Brandywine Falls), Boston Run, Salt Run, Langes Run; from the west: Chippewa Creek (Brecksville), Furnace Run (Everett Covered Bridge, Brushwood Lake), Yellow Creek (Botzum), Deer Lick Creek. They reach the valley floor over sandstone lips: **waterfalls** at Brandywine (18 m), Blue Hen, Buttermilk, Bridal Veil, Great Falls of Tinkers Creek.
- **Ohio & Erie Canal** (1825–1832; Akron–Cleveland opened 1827): a watered ditch beside the towpath in the north (Lock 39 to Station Road), dry or marshy sections further south; locks 39 (Rockside), 38 (Canal Exploration Center), Red Lock, 29 (Peninsula), 28 (Deep Lock); Tinkers Creek Aqueduct; Stumpy Basin.
- **Still water**: Beaver Marsh (beaver-made, lilies), Kendall Lake, Indigo Lake, Horseshoe Pond, Armington Pond, Brushwood Lake, Goosefeather Pond, Wyoga Lake (outside, east).
- No coast, no tides; Lake Erie is off-map to the north (visible only as a distant horizon from the north end in the brochure painting).

### Relief

Lowest point 180 m (the river at the north boundary, 590 ft); highest 355 m (1,164 ft, uplands at Black & Brush roads, Richfield). The land steps in three levels: (1) the flat floodplain at 180–230 m with the river, canal, towpath, railroad and byway; (2) steep wooded valley walls 60–120 m high, cut every kilometre or two by V-shaped side ravines whose creeks fall over **Berea sandstone** caprock above soft Bedford and Cleveland shale; (3) a rolling glaciated plateau at 280–350 m with fields, pine plantations and, near its eastern lip, the 10–20 m **Sharon conglomerate** cliffs of the Ledges (crevices, slump blocks, Ice Box Cave). In the game: the valley walls become one tall cliff band with painted shale/sandstone faces broken by walkable banks where roads climb (SR 303, Boston Mills Rd, Everett Rd); side ravines are narrow notches with a waterfall where they meet the plateau lip; stairs at Brandywine Falls and at the Ledges; the Ledges are a ring of 2–3-level HIGH plateau with a narrow slot corridor. No 3D peaks needed — landmarks here are bridges (the SR 82 arch, the I-271/I-80 viaducts 40 m above the river), the Everett Covered Bridge and the ski slope on the Boston valley wall.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: floodplain (180–230 m): sycamore, cottonwood, box elder and silver maple along the river, hayfields, wet meadows with cattails and goldenrod, beaver-marsh lilies; valley walls (230–300 m): mixed-mesophytic forest (about 80 % of the park) — oak-hickory, beech-maple, tulip tree, Ohio buckeye, with **eastern hemlock** in cool ravines (Brandywine Gorge, Ledges, Tinkers Creek); plateau (280–355 m): farm fields, old-field meadows (Kendall Hills), red and white pine plantations (Oak Hill, Pine Grove), oak woods on the ledges.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#7FA35A` floodplain hayfield · `#3F6B35` summer hardwood canopy · `#C8652B` / `#E0A23A` October maple & oak · `#CFC6AE` crushed-limestone towpath · `#7A7456` tea-brown river after rain · `#4F6B4A` marsh water with lily pads · `#B9A488` Berea sandstone at the falls · `#8C8577` grey Sharon conglomerate (with `#6F8A4A` moss) · `#9C3B2E` barn red · `#F2C230` CVSR locomotive yellow.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): great blue heron (Beaver Marsh, heronries along Bath Road; nests Feb–July), bald eagle (Station Road Bridge area, year-round, nesting Feb–June), beaver (Beaver Marsh at dusk), river otter (marsh and river, winter mornings), muskrat (canal), white-tailed deer (meadows at dawn), coyote (fields, heard at night), red fox, wood duck (canal and ponds, spring), painted turtle (basking on canal logs, Apr–Sep), northern water snake (canal), spring peeper (chorus in March–April wetlands), spotted salamander (vernal pools on rainy March nights), pileated woodpecker (mature woods), eastern bluebird (meadow nest boxes), barred owl (ravines at night). About 250 bird species; the second week of May is the migration peak.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): skunk cabbage (floodplain, Feb–Mar), bloodroot & spring beauty (April), large-flowered trillium and Virginia bluebells (late April–May), Ohio buckeye (flowers May, nuts September), eastern hemlock (evergreen ravines), fragrant water lily (Beaver Marsh, summer), goldenrod & wingstem (roadsides, Aug–Sep), big bluestem (the one prairie, by the Turnpike, fall), sugar maple (October colour; maple sugaring demonstrations at Hale Farm in March).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — rain, rising river, wildflowers, frogs; summer — humid, green, afternoon thunderstorms, farmers' markets and concerts; fall — mid-October colour on both valley walls, morning river fog; winter — lake-effect snow squalls (heavier in the north end), frozen waterfalls, ski lifts running, sledding and cross-country skiing.
- **Night**: not a dark-sky park (city glow from both ends), but meadows full of fireflies in late June–July, spring peeper and bullfrog choruses, barred owls calling in the ravines, bats over the canal (Ice Box Cave is closed to protect them), the lit ski slopes and the glow of Blossom concerts in summer.
- **Sounds**: the CVSR's diesel horn and bell echoing between the valley walls; bike bells and gravel crunch on the towpath; river riffles; spring peepers; wood thrush flutes at dusk; honking geese; the hum of trucks high on the I-80/I-271 viaducts; waterfalls; distant orchestra music on summer evenings; ski-lift clanks in winter.

## Moments

- **The train**: the Cuyahoga Valley Scenic Railroad runs through the whole valley; a full scenic excursion takes about 4¼ hours; the Explorer program (May 23–Labor Day, Wednesday–Sunday in 2026) lets hikers, bikers and paddlers ride one way for $10 ($15 with a kayak) — you stand on the platform in view of the crew to flag it. Fall colour trains in October, holiday trains in November–December.
- **Lock demonstrations** at Lock 38, Canal Exploration Center, on summer weekends: volunteers in period dress and visitors push the balance beams to fill and empty the lock.
- **Heron season**: great blue herons return to their treetop colonies in February–March and raise chicks until July; bald eagles nest near Station Road Bridge (first chick recorded 2007).
- **Beaver Marsh at dusk**: beavers, muskrats and herons along the boardwalk, lilies in bloom in summer.
- **Fall colour** peaks around mid-October; the Ledges Overlook (facing west over the valley) is the sunset spot all year.
- **Spring wildflowers** in April–May on the ravine slopes; skunk cabbage in February; spring migration peaks the second week of May.
- **Countryside Farmers' Market at Howe Meadow**: summer Saturday mornings beside the tracks — passengers wave from the train.
- **Winter**: icicle curtains at Brandywine Falls, snow on the valley walls, Boston Mills/Brandywine ski slopes lit at night, sledding at Pine Hollow/Kendall Hills, cross-country skiing on the towpath and Kendall Lake trails.
- **Summer evenings**: fireflies in the hayfields (late June–July), the Cleveland Orchestra at Blossom (July–August).

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a tall map, **512 × 1152 tiles** (~0.8 × 1.7 km at 1.5 m/tile): the 26 km core compressed ~15× north–south and ~8× east–west. Near true scale: Boston village (~350 m across: visitor centre, station, store, river bridge, ski slope), Peninsula village (~400 m), the Brandywine Falls gorge and boardwalk (~300 m), Beaver Marsh boardwalk (~250 m), Canal Exploration Center with Lock 38 (~150 m), Station Road Bridge under the SR 82 arch, the Ledges ring (~500 m, slightly squeezed). Compressed: the forested walls and farm plateau between hubs (one ravine per real group of ravines), the interstates reduced to the two viaducts over Boston plus I-480 as the north edge. Cut: golf courses, most of Bedford and Brecksville reservations (keep Tinkers Creek Gorge overlook, Bridal Veil Falls and the Brecksville Nature Center as a north-east and north-west pocket), the Akron tail (the train leaves the map at Botzum into a tunnel of trees).
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Boston (main gathering hub), Peninsula, Canal Exploration Center, Hunt House/Beaver Marsh, Kendall Lake. 3D landmarks: the yellow-and-red CVSR train with a dome car, the SR 82 concrete arch bridge and the 1881 iron truss beneath it, the I-271 and I-80 viaducts on tall pillars, the red Everett Covered Bridge, canal locks with wooden balance beams, Brandywine Falls with its boardwalk, the Jaite yellow houses, Peninsula's red depot, the Blossom pavilion roof, Hale Farm's brick house and barns. Interiors: Boston Mill Visitor Center (tactile valley model), Canal Exploration Center, Boston Store, Hunt House, Happy Days Lodge, Hale Farm village workshops, the Inn at Brandywine Falls.
- **Activities**: bike the towpath and ride the train back (a signature loop); photo spots — Brandywine Falls from the boardwalk, Station Road Bridge with the arch behind, Everett Covered Bridge, Beaver Marsh boardwalk, Ledges Overlook at sunset, Tinkers Creek Gorge overlook, the train crossing a meadow at Howe Meadow, the viaducts over Boston; wildlife spotting (herons, eagles, beavers, otters, turtles); ranger lock demonstrations and bird walks; junior ranger: operate a lock, identify 5 towpath animals, pick a farm crop, ride the train; canoe/kayak the Cuyahoga River Water Trail between access points; horse riding on the bridle trails from Everett and Wetmore stables; winter skiing, sledding, snowshoes; berry picking and farm stands (Countryside farms).
- **Passport stamps**: the 2025 cancellation list gives "Boston Store", "Brecksville", "Boston", "Canal Exploration Center", "Hunt House", "Peninsula" and "Cuyahoga River Water Trail" (plus the 2024 eclipse and 50th-anniversary stamps). They are kept at the Boston Mill Visitor Center desk (all year) and at Canal Exploration Center and Hunt House (seasonal); the scenic railroad has its own "Akron" stamp. In the game, place each at its namesake: Boston Mill VC, Boston Store, Station Road/Brecksville, Canal Exploration Center, Hunt House, Peninsula Depot, a river launch.
- **With friends / with the world**: the lock needs four players pushing two pairs of balance beams to raise a boat; the party boards the same train car and hops off at different stations for a scavenger hunt; a towpath relay (bikers vs. the train); group photos at the covered bridge and on the Brandywine boardwalk. Strangers naturally meet at the Boston Mill station platform, on the Beaver Marsh boardwalk at dusk, at the Ledges Overlook for sunset and at the Saturday market at Howe Meadow.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on the Beaver Marsh boardwalk and don't disturb lodges; keep back from heron and eagle nest trees in season; Ice Box Cave is closed (bats, white-nose syndrome); no climbing or carving on the Ledges; bikes yield and ring on the towpath; respect private homes and farms inside the boundary; pick berries only at the farms, never wildflowers; leash dogs; pack out trash; don't feed the geese.
- **Implementation challenges**: a scheduled train that stops at stations, can be flagged and carries bikes (a moving vehicle on rails with timetable); canal locks with changing water levels; high viaducts and an arch bridge spanning the valley well above the ground (multi-level geometry, shadows); a narrow meandering river with frequent bridges; dense mixed land use (villages, farms, private land, ski slopes) inside a park; snow on slopes and frozen falls; spring floods over the towpath.
