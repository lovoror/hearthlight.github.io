# Canyonlands National Park — `CANY`

> Utah · established September 12, 1964 · 1,366.2 km² (337,597.83 acres) · 796,057 recreation visits (2025) ·
> 1,128–2,170 m · NPS region IMR
>
> Utah's largest national park: a vast stepped desert where the Green and Colorado rivers meet in a Y of deep canyons
> that splits the land into three districts no road connects — a mesa-top "island in the sky", a garden of banded
> spires, and a roadless labyrinth. In the game it should feel like standing on the edge of the world: enormous views
> from a mesa, then the slow, rewarding descent through three layers of rock down to a green river.

## Reading the map

The whole map is organised by an upside-down **Y of rivers**. The **Green River** enters at the top centre-left (Mineral Bottom) and meanders south through Labyrinth and **Stillwater Canyon**; the **Colorado River** enters at the top right (near Potash, downstream of Moab) and loops south-west through **Meander Canyon**. The two meet near the centre of the park at **the Confluence** (1,175 m), and the combined Colorado runs south-west through **Cataract Canyon**'s rapids (Brown Betty, Mile Long, Big Drop) and leaves the park at the bottom left toward Lake Powell. The rivers are sunk 600 m below the surrounding land, and **no road crosses them inside the park**: the three wedges of land between the arms are three separate districts, each reached from a different side of the map, and a place that looks 15 km away across the canyon is 2–6 hours away by car.

**Island in the Sky** is the wedge **between the two rivers, north of the Confluence**: a flat-topped mesa (~1,800– 1,900 m) shaped like a hand pointing south. Its only link to the outside is **the Neck**, a road-wide isthmus at its north-east corner; UT-313 comes in from US-191 and Moab (north-east), passes Dead Horse Point State Park's turnoff and reaches the **visitor centre** just before the Neck. The paved road runs south 6 miles to a fork: west 5 miles past the Green River Overlook spur, Aztec Butte and Whale Rock to **Upheaval Dome**; south 6 miles past Mesa Arch, Candlestick Tower Overlook, Buck Canyon and Orange Cliffs overlooks to the road-end at **Grand View Point**. Some 350 m below the mesa's cliffs lies a second level, the **White Rim** — a pale sandstone bench that rings the mesa, carrying the 100-mile **White Rim Road** (reached down the Shafer Trail switchbacks from the Neck, or from Mineral Bottom); below the bench, canyons drop another 300 m to the rivers.

**The Needles** is the wedge **east of the Colorado and south of the Confluence**, the park's south-east quarter, reached by UT-211 from US-191 (past Newspaper Rock and Indian Creek). The road ends in a short network: the **visitor centre**, the Stone Storehouse, Cave Spring, Wooden Shoe Arch Overlook, Pothole Point, the Slickrock Trail and **Big Spring Canyon Overlook**, plus the Needles Campground and the dirt road to **Elephant Hill**. Beyond it, trails fan out south-west into red-and-white banded spires (Chesler Park, Druid Arch, the Joint Trail), the **grabens** (Devils Lane, parallel sunken valleys), Salt Creek and Horse Canyon (Angel Arch, cultural sites), and north-west to the Confluence Overlook.

**The Maze** is the land **west of the Green and the combined river**, the park's west side. It is reached only from the far west: from UT-24, a 46-mile dirt road leads to the **Hans Flat Ranger Station** on the Orange Cliffs, and from there four-wheel-drive tracks (the Flint Trail's switchbacks) descend to the **Maze Overlook**, the **Land of Standing Rocks**, the **Doll House** and Chimney Rock — a labyrinth of slickrock canyons above Spanish Bottom on the river. Glen Canyon NRA's Orange Cliffs unit borders it on the west. Finally, **Horseshoe Canyon** is a small detached unit farther north-west, a single canyon with the Great Gallery rock-art panel, reached by 30 miles of graded dirt from UT-24.

### Water

Two big rivers and their meeting define the park. The **Green River** (from the north, out of Wyoming) runs calm and slow in tight meanders through Labyrinth and Stillwater canyons along the west side of the Island; the **Colorado River** (from the north-east, past Moab) loops through Meander Canyon along the Island's east side. Both are flatwater above **the Confluence**, where they meet in a flat, cottonwood-lined bend 600 m below the rims; the two colours stay side by side for a while. Below it, the combined Colorado accelerates into **Cataract Canyon**: 14 miles of Class III–V rapids (Brown Betty, Mile Long, the three Big Drops) that end in the slack water of Lake Powell near Hite. High water comes with snowmelt, early May to late June; there is no vehicle access to either river near the Confluence.

Away from the rivers, water is rare and precious: **potholes** on the slickrock (Pothole Point) fill after rain and come alive with fairy shrimp, tadpole shrimp and spadefoot toads; **springs** (Cave Spring, Peekaboo Spring, Neck Spring, Cabin Spring, Soda Springs) supported Ancestral Puebloan farmers and later cowboys; seasonal creeks flow in Salt Creek (with the Upper and Lower Jump falls), Horseshoe Canyon and Big Spring Canyon. Summer storms send flash floods down every wash and pour waterfalls off the Island's cliffs.

### Relief

The park runs from 1,128 m, where the Colorado leaves Cataract Canyon at the southern boundary, to 2,170 m at Cathedral Point on the Needles' southern tip. The land is a staircase of three levels, clearest around the Island in the Sky: **the mesa top** (1,800–1,900 m: Navajo and Kayenta sandstone on top of ~300 m of cliffs — sheer Wingate sandstone over steep Chinle slopes), **the White Rim bench** (~1,450–1,550 m, a hard pale sandstone shelf), and **the rivers** (1,175–1,210 m) in canyons cut another 300 m below the bench. Towers and buttes (Candlestick, Airport, the Totem Pole, Junction Butte) stand on the benches. The **Needles** are banded red-and-white Cedar Mesa Sandstone cut into spires, fins and arches by fractures, with **grabens** — long parallel valleys where blocks slid down on faults — and narrow **joints** you can walk through. The **Maze** is a maze in the literal sense: slickrock canyons 100–200 m deep below the Orange Cliffs, with the Land of Standing Rocks' towers. **Upheaval Dome** is a 5 km circular structure of upturned rock rings on the Island.

In the game: three stacked height levels with tall painted cliff faces (mesa → White Rim → river), linked by switchback stairs (the Shafer Trail, the Flint Trail, the Gooseberry and Murphy trails); spires and towers as 3D models; grabens as long one-level troughs; joints as one-tile slots; slickrock as smooth banks with potholes.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: river corridors (1,150–1,250 m) — Fremont cottonwood, coyote willow, invasive tamarisk, reeds and sandy beaches. Benches and canyon floors (1,250–1,600 m) — blackbrush, shadscale, Mormon tea, four-wing saltbush, sand sagebrush, Indian ricegrass; cottonwoods in Salt Creek and Horseshoe Canyon. Mesa tops and high benches (1,600–2,170 m) — pinyon pine and Utah juniper woodland, cliffrose, yucca, grassland (Grays Pasture, Chesler Park). Everywhere: black knobbly biological soil crust between plants; hanging-garden ferns at seeps.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#B4553A` Wingate cliffs (red-orange walls) · `#E8DCC4` White Rim cap rock and the Needles' white bands · `#C1683F` the Needles' red bands · `#D69A63` Navajo slickrock on the mesa · `#8C5A3C` Organ Rock and Moenkopi slopes (brick-brown) · `#D7C29B` sand and dust · `#6E8A4B` pinyon–juniper green · `#8B6A45` red-brown Colorado River · `#7E8E6B` greener Green River · `#9DB06F` riverside cottonwoods (`#E2B43C` in October).
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): desert bighorn sheep on the White Rim and in the river canyons; mule deer; coyote; kit fox; ringtail at night; beaver along the rivers; black-tailed jackrabbit and desert cottontail; ravens at every overlook; peregrine falcons and golden eagles on the cliffs; white-throated swifts; canyon wrens; great blue herons on the river bars; collared and side-blotched lizards; midget faded rattlesnake (rare); spadefoot toads and tadpole shrimp in potholes after rain; the Colorado River's endangered native fish (Colorado pikeminnow, razorback sucker, humpback chub).
- **Flora** (the herbarium): biological soil crust (all year); cliffrose (April–May); Indian paintbrush and globemallow (spring); claret cup and prickly pear cactus flowers (May); sacred datura (summer nights); Utah juniper and pinyon pine (cones and nuts in fall); narrowleaf yucca (May–June); rubber rabbitbrush (yellow, September); Fremont cottonwood (gold in late October along the rivers and in Horseshoe Canyon).
- **Weather & seasons**: spring — windy, blooming, rivers rising to their May–June peak; summer — 38 °C+, afternoon thunderstorms, flash floods, waterfalls off the rims, red mud on the roads; fall — the best season, cottonwoods gold, clear air; winter — snow on the Island and the Needles' spires, ice on the Shafer switchbacks, the La Sal Mountains white on the eastern horizon.
- **Night**: a gold-tier International Dark Sky Park: the Milky Way over Grand View Point, zero lights to the horizon; ranger night-sky programmes; bats over the rivers; owls and ringtails.
- **Sounds**: wind across the mesa and in juniper branches; ravens' croaks carrying across the void; the canyon wren's falling song; the far-off roar of Cataract Canyon's rapids; the river lapping at a sandbar; a 4×4 grinding up Elephant Hill; thunder echoing through the canyons; utter silence at night on the White Rim.

## Moments

- **Sunrise at Mesa Arch** — every clear dawn the rising sun lights the arch's underside a glowing orange against the White Rim and the La Sals; photographers crowd the rim before first light.
- **Sunset at Grand View and Green River overlooks** — the White Rim and the towers turn pink, the canyons fill with blue shadow.
- **High water** — early May to late June the rivers run fast and brown and Cataract Canyon's Big Drops are at their wildest.
- **Potholes wake up** — after summer rains at Pothole Point, fairy and tadpole shrimp hatch within hours from eggs that waited in dry dust for years.
- **Monsoon storms** — July–September: lightning over the Maze, waterfalls pouring off the Island's cliffs, washes running red.
- **Horseshoe Canyon's cottonwoods** — gold in October; rangers lead hikes to the Great Gallery on spring and fall weekends.
- **Snow on the spires** — a few winter days a year the Needles and the Island's rim are white over red rock.
- **The river colours** — at the Confluence the greener Green and the redder Colorado flow side by side before mixing.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: a 1024 × 1024-tile map built around the Y of rivers, heavily compressed (~1:30 overall): the Island in the Sky mesa (~20 × 25 km) becomes ~400 × 450 tiles with its viewpoints and short trails near true size, the White Rim as a ring bench one level below, the rivers two levels further down; the Needles' front country and Chesler Park (~300 × 300 tiles) in the south-east; the Maze (Maze Overlook, Doll House, Spanish Bottom) as a ~250-tile labyrinth on the west side; the Confluence at the centre. Horseshoe Canyon becomes a separate small vignette map (or a pocket at the map's north-west corner). Districts are linked only the way they really are: by long "drives" that leave the map edge (UT-313, UT-211, the Hans Flat road) — or by boat on the rivers, the one physical link.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: Island in the Sky visitor centre and the Neck; Grand View Point; the Needles visitor centre and campground; Hans Flat Ranger Station (a lonely outpost); Spanish Bottom (river camp and meeting point). 3D landmarks: Mesa Arch, Upheaval Dome's rings, Candlestick Tower, the Totem Pole, Airport Tower, Musselman Arch, the Needles' banded spires, Druid Arch, Angel Arch, Wooden Shoe Arch, the Doll House, Chocolate Drops, Chimney Rock, the Great Gallery panel, the La Sal Mountains on the horizon. Interiors: the two visitor centres; Cave Spring's cowboy camp under its overhang.
- **Activities**: overlook hopping on the mesa; the Shafer Trail descent and the White Rim loop by bike or 4×4 (with a day permit and campsite reservations); Needles day hikes with ladders and joints; a Maze expedition (self-rescue gear, map reading, route finding by cairns); **river trips** — canoeing the flatwater of Stillwater or Meander canyon to Spanish Bottom, then a jet-boat ride back or a raft through Cataract Canyon's rapids; pothole science at Pothole Point; rock-art and granary visits with a ranger; ranger geology talks at Grand View Point; Junior Ranger booklet; stargazing. Photo spots: Mesa Arch at sunrise, Grand View Point, Green River Overlook, the Shafer switchbacks from above, Upheaval Dome, Chesler Park, the Joint Trail, Druid Arch, the Confluence Overlook, the Doll House, the Great Gallery.
- **Passport stamps**: Island in the Sky Visitor Center, the Needles Visitor Center, Hans Flat Ranger Station (the Maze). The park's headquarters and the Moab Information Center are in Moab, outside the park.
- **With friends / with the world**: a party raft through Cataract Canyon (one steers, all paddle; a flip in the Big Drops); a canoe convoy down Stillwater Canyon, camping on sandbars; a group White Rim ride over several in-game days; the dawn crowd at Mesa Arch; meeting other travellers at Spanish Bottom or at the Confluence Overlook; helping each other up the Needles' ladders and through the Joint Trail's slots.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): stay on trails, slickrock or wash sand (the soil crust); don't climb on arches; view Ancestral Puebloan granaries and rock art from a distance — never enter, touch or climb them, and never add marks; don't drink or swim in potholes; carry a gallon of water; permits for the White Rim, Elephant Hill and all overnight trips; pack out human waste on the rivers (portable toilets) and camp on designated bars; no ATVs; keep to the road in a 4×4.
- **Implementation challenges**: three stacked levels of 300 m cliffs to render and connect; extreme compression (the park is larger than most maps) and districts that must stay unconnected by road; long river corridors with flow, rapids and boats (raft physics, flips, eddies); 4×4 driving on ledgy roads (stair-steps, backing on switchbacks); potholes; archaeological sites shown with care; route finding by cairns in a maze of look-alike canyons.
