# Redwood National and State Parks — `REDW`

> California · Redwood National Park established October 2, 1968 (expanded 1978), co-managed since 1994 with three
> California state parks: Prairie Creek Redwoods (1923), Del Norte Coast Redwoods (1925) and Jedediah Smith Redwoods
> (1929) · 562.5 km² (138,999.37 acres in the NPS figures; ~54 % federal, ~46 % state land) · 1,202,480 recreation
> visits (2025, Redwood NP's count; ~2.5 million for the four parks) · 0–994 m · NPS region PWR (DOI Region 10, California–Great Basin)
>
> A 84 km ribbon of fog coast in far-northern California where the world's tallest trees grow down to wild beaches,
> river mouths and elk meadows, strung along US 101 between Crescent City and Orick. In Hearthlight it should feel
> hushed and green: tiny heroes on fern-carpeted paths under trunks that vanish into mist, with the surf always
> somewhere to the left.

## Reading the map

The brochure map is a long, thin strip, 84 km tall and rarely more than 8 km wide, with the Pacific filling its left half. **US 101** is the thread: it enters from Oregon in the north, crosses **Crescent City** (the park headquarters and information centre at 2nd and K streets, Battery Point Lighthouse on its islet), and then runs south along or just behind the coast to **Orick** and on to Trinidad and Eureka. The park is a patchwork, drawn with separate boundaries: the national park proper (the coastal strip and the whole **Redwood Creek** basin in the south) and three state parks, each wrapped round a hub — **Jedediah Smith Redwoods** in the north on the Smith River, **Del Norte Coast Redwoods** on the cliffs south of Crescent City, **Prairie Creek Redwoods** in the middle. Old-growth redwood is shaded dark green; most of the rest is logged land growing back. The **Yurok Reservation** straddles the Klamath River across the middle (a mile each side of the river, 43 miles upstream), so the park is cut in two there.

**The north (Crescent City, Jedediah Smith).** US 199 leaves US 101 north of Crescent City and runs east along the **Smith River** to **Hiouchi**: the Jedediah Smith campground and visitor centre on the river bend, the Hiouchi Visitor Center across the road, the Simpson-Reed Grove on the highway. South of the river, the **Howland Hill Road**, a narrow, mostly gravel lane (« motor homes and trailers not advised »; ~10 mi / 16 km in all), runs east from Crescent City's edge through the heart of the old growth to **Stout Grove** and the South Fork Road, passing the Boy Scout Tree trailhead and the Grove of Titans. The Mill Creek horse trails and the Little Bald Hills lie south and east. Outside the park to the north-west: Lake Earl and Tolowa Dunes State Park; to the north-east, the Smith River National Recreation Area.

**The Del Norte coast and the Klamath.** South of Crescent City a spur (Enderts Beach Road) runs to **Crescent Beach** (picnic area; trailers not beyond), the **Crescent Beach Overlook** and **Enderts Beach**, where the Coastal Trail starts south along the bluffs. US 101 climbs inland through Del Norte Coast Redwoods past Vista Point and the side road to the **Mill Creek** campground, then runs high above the sea (Damnation Creek trail down to a cove, Sister Rocks offshore) and drops to **Wilson Creek**, **False Klamath Cove** and **Lagoon Creek** (picnic area, the Yurok Loop). The Coastal Trail continues past Hidden Beach to **Requa** and the **Klamath River Overlook** on the north bank of the river mouth. US 101 crosses the Klamath at **Klamath** town (Hwy 169 goes upriver to Klamath Glen). South of the mouth, the **Klamath Beach Road** and the narrow **Coastal Drive** (motor homes and trailers prohibited) loop along the cliffs past Flint Ridge camp, the **World War II Radar Station** (disguised as a farmhouse) and **High Bluff Overlook**.

**Prairie Creek.** South of the Klamath, US 101 turns inland (the Ah-Pah trail marks an old road being removed) and the **Newton B. Drury Scenic Parkway** splits off to run ~16 km through the old growth of Prairie Creek: **Big Tree Wayside**, the gravel Cal-Barrel Road (trailers prohibited), then the **Prairie Creek Visitor Center** and **Elk Prairie** (campground and the meadow where Roosevelt elk graze by the road). West of it a ridge falls to the sea at **Gold Bluffs Beach**, reached from the south by the gravel **Davison Road** (≤24 ft, no trailers, two stream fords) through Elk Meadow: a long sand beach under bluffs with a campground and, at its north end, **Fern Canyon**. The James Irvine and Miners Ridge trails cross from the visitor centre to Fern Canyon and the beach.

**The south (Orick, Redwood Creek, the Bald Hills).** US 101 rejoins at **Lost Man Creek** and Elk Meadow and reaches **Orick** on the Redwood Creek flats; the **Thomas H. Kuchel Visitor Center** stands on the beach at the creek mouth, the main south gateway. South of it lie **Freshwater Lagoon** and then the Humboldt Lagoons State Park (Stone Lagoon with a boat-in camp, Dry Lagoon, Big Lagoon), Harry A. Merlo SRA, Patrick's Point and Trinidad — on the map, but outside the park. The **Bald Hills Road** climbs east from US 101 just north of Orick: **Lady Bird Johnson Grove**, the Redwood Creek Overlook (640 m), the gated **Tall Trees Access Road** (permit only) down to the **Tall Trees Grove** on the creek, Dolason Prairie, then the open grassy ridge to Childs Hill Prairie, **Lyons Ranch** and Schoolhouse Peak (944 m) near the park's south-east corner. The whole Redwood Creek basin below the road is roadless national park: the Redwood Creek Trail runs 8 mi (13 km) up the creek from the Orick end to the Tall Trees; Elam and 44 camps sit in the forest; Rodgers Peak (837 m) rises west of the creek.

### Water

Three rivers cut the coast. The **Smith River** in the north — California's largest free-flowing river system, never dammed, emerald-clear, a Wild and Scenic River — loops past Jedediah Smith's campground, Stout Grove and Hiouchi (summer kayak tours on its gentle Class I–II water, snorkelling). The **Klamath River** (~257 mi / 414 km) reaches the sea at Requa in the middle of the park, through the Yurok Reservation; its four upstream dams came out in 2023–2024, the largest dam removal in US history, and salmon are running upriver again (chinook in late August–September, with orcas at the mouth). **Redwood Creek** (61.8 mi / 99.5 km) drains the whole southern basin past the Tall Trees Grove to the sea at Orick, where levees built in the 1960s squeeze its estuary. Smaller creeks — Prairie, Lost Man, Mill, Home Creek (Fern Canyon), Wilson — carry coho and chinook salmon and steelhead, spawning November–February. **37 mi (60 km) of Pacific coast**: sand beaches (Gold Bluffs, Crescent, Enderts, Wilson Creek), sea stacks, coves and tidepools at low tide, cold water, rip currents and « sneaker waves » that run far up the beach — never turn your back on the ocean. This is the most tsunami-prone coast in the lower 48 (the 1964 tsunami flooded Crescent City): after 20 seconds of shaking, go uphill. Fog is water too: it gives the redwoods ~40 % of theirs. Rain: 1,500–2,000 mm a year, mostly October–April.

### Relief

From **sea level** to **994 m** (an unnamed knob near Coyote Peak in the Bald Hills; Schoolhouse Peak, the brochure's summit, is 944 m). The coast alternates wide sand beaches under bluffs (Gold Bluffs) with steep, forested sea cliffs (Del Norte Coast, 150–300 m, where US 101 climbs high above the water) and headlands with overlooks (High Bluff 58 m, Klamath River Overlook 198 m). Behind it the Coast Range rises in ridges cut by the river valleys; the tallest trees grow on the flat alluvial terraces of the Smith, Prairie Creek and Redwood Creek. In the south the long **Bald Hills** ridge (600–990 m) carries open prairies and oak woodland above the Redwood Creek forest.

For the game: the forest floor is gently rolling — the **trees are the verticals** (to 116 m, the tallest things in the whole game); bluffs and sea cliffs become **cliff levels** with beaches as **banks** below; Fern Canyon is a narrow slot with vertical fern-covered walls (a signature wall texture); the Bald Hills are a grassy upper **plateau** reached by a long climbing road; sea stacks and the Klamath River Overlook headland are **3D rock** landmarks.

## Look & feel

- **Vegetation by elevation**: the salt-blown coastal strip: Sitka spruce, shore pine, coastal scrub and salal, beach pea and sand verbena on the dunes, stonecrop on the bluffs. The redwood belt (river flats and foggy slopes, 0–~600 m): coast redwood with Douglas-fir, western hemlock, tanoak and bigleaf maple, over an understory of sword fern, redwood sorrel, Pacific rhododendron, huckleberry and trillium; skunk cabbage in the bogs; red alder and dense young redwood on the logged land. The Bald Hills (600–990 m): coastal prairie and Oregon white oak woodland, kept open by fire.
- **Ground, rock & water colours**: `#8B3E2F` redwood bark · `#5E3A30` bark in shadow · `#4F7A3A` sword fern · `#8FBF4D` redwood sorrel · `#6F8F3A` moss · `#DCE3E4` fog · `#5A7F92` the grey-blue Pacific · `#3E8F7C` Smith River emerald · `#CDBB9A` beach sand · `#D9679B` rhododendron pink · `#C9A95C` Bald Hills grass in summer.
- **Wildlife** (the field guide): Roosevelt elk (Elk Prairie, Elk Meadow, Gold Bluffs Beach, the Bald Hills; the most seen animal); black bear; banana slug (up to 20 cm, everywhere in wet weather); yellow-spotted millipede (smells of almonds); marbled murrelet (a seabird that nests on mossy limbs high in the old growth, heard at dawn in summer); northern spotted owl; California condor (returned in 2022 with the Yurok Tribe); gray whale (December, March–May), humpback (September), orcas at the Klamath mouth (late August–September); harbor seal (pups April–May), California and Steller sea lions; coho, chinook and steelhead; coastal giant salamander and rough-skinned newt; American dipper (Fern Canyon); brown pelican, osprey, peregrine falcon; river otter; ochre sea stars in the tidepools.
- **Flora** (the herbarium): coast redwood (to 116 m, over 2,000 years, sprouting in « fairy rings » from stumps), Sitka spruce, Pacific rhododendron (mid-May–early June), western azalea (spring), giant trillium (February–May), redwood sorrel (flowers February–September), skunk cabbage (March–June), salmonberry, calypso orchid (March–July), the five ferns of Fern Canyon (five-finger, deer, sword, licorice, California polypody), bigleaf maple (gold in October–November), lupine in the Bald Hills (March–August).
- **Weather & seasons**: summer brings cool fog on the coast while Hiouchi and the Bald Hills bake in the sun; autumn has the clearest days, the elk rut and salmon; winter is storms, 1,500–2,000 mm of rain, fallen trees, swollen rivers and southbound whales; spring brings trilliums and rhododendrons, elk calves and northbound whales.
- **Night**: stars above the fog from Redwood Creek Overlook and Schoolhouse Peak; the November Candlelight Walk through the Prairie Creek old growth; spotted and barred owls; 13 bat species; banana slugs, newts and giant salamanders on wet nights; the unseen bears and mountain lions.
- **Sounds**: the hush of the old growth and fog dripping from the canopy, surf booming on the beaches, elk bugling in September–October, the marbled murrelet's « keer » at dawn, the long song of the winter wren, Steller's jays, sea lions barking at the Klamath mouth, gravel under the tyres on Howland Hill Road, rain on the ferns.

## Moments

- **Gray whales**: southbound peak in December, northbound March–May (mothers and calves close in during May); some stay all summer off the Klamath River Overlook.
- **Elk rut**: late August–October, bulls bugling and charging; **calving** late May–June (cows defend hidden calves).
- **Rhododendron bloom**: mid-May to early June, pink under the redwoods.
- **Salmon**: chinook enter the Klamath in late August–September (orcas follow); spawning in Prairie, Lost Man and Mill creeks November–February.
- **Minus tides** for tidepooling (winter and spring mornings), with sneaker-wave warnings.
- **Fern Canyon's summer bridges**: ~June–September (the parking reservation season is May 15–September 15).
- **Smith River kayak season**: summer, until the water drops (often by late July).
- **Tolowa Renewal Dance** demonstration at Jedediah Smith every July; the **Candlelight Walk** at Prairie Creek in November.
- **Bike & Hike Day**: the parkway is car-free on the first Saturday of each month, October–May.
- **Sunset above the fog** at Redwood Creek Overlook; fall prescribed burns in the Bald Hills prairies.

## For the game

- **Scale & shape**: ~640 × 1,536 tiles, a long strip with the Pacific down the west edge and US 101 as the spine. The 84 km coast is squeezed hard between the hubs, which keep near true scale: Stout Grove and the Smith River bend (~250 × 200 tiles), the Grove of Titans boardwalk, Fern Canyon (~300 tiles of wading), Prairie Creek with Elk Prairie and the Big Tree (~300 × 250), the Kuchel beach and Redwood Creek mouth, Lady Bird Johnson Grove, the Klamath River Overlook and the Tall Trees pocket at the end of its gated road. The Bald Hills ridge forms the south-east edge; most of the Mill Creek and upper Redwood Creek interiors are cut.
- **Hubs & set pieces**: five visitor centres (Kuchel on the beach, the Prairie Creek CCC lodge, Hiouchi, Jedediah Smith, Crescent City HQ); 3D landmarks: the Big Tree, the Corkscrew Tree, the Boy Scout Tree, the Del Norte Titan and the Grove of Titans boardwalk, the Libbey Tree, the Lady Bird Johnson plaque and footbridge, sea stacks (False Klamath Rock, Sister Rocks), Radar Station B-71's fake farmhouse and barn, the golden bear statues by the Klamath River bridge, Lyons Ranch; interiors: the visitor centres, the radar « farmhouse ».
- **Activities**: hiking; photo spots (Fern Canyon's walls, the Big Tree, Stout Grove's light shafts, the Titans boardwalk, Klamath River Overlook, High Bluff, Crescent Beach Overlook, elk on Gold Bluffs Beach, Redwood Creek Overlook above the fog, Lyons Ranch); wildlife (elk herds, whales, a banana-slug collection for the field guide, marbled murrelets at dawn); ranger programmes (forest walks, tidepool walks, campfires at Jedediah Smith, Mill Creek and Elk Prairie); Junior Ranger (activity book in six languages, the Redwood badge, plus the State Parks badge); water (guided Smith River kayak and snorkel trips, tidepooling); vehicles (car, bike on the car-free parkway days and the Ossagon–Coastal Trail–Davison loop, horse on the Mill Creek and Orick horse trails).
- **Passport stamps**: all five centres — Crescent City Information Center (the stamp reads « Crescent City Visitor Center »), Hiouchi Visitor Center, Jedediah Smith Visitor Center (summer), Prairie Creek Visitor Center and Thomas H. Kuchel Visitor Center.
- **With friends / with the world**: a wading party up Fern Canyon; a whale count from the Klamath River Overlook; a kayak flotilla down the Smith; the elk-watching etiquette as a group game (who can get the photo *without* crossing the 25-yard line); a « ring the giant » challenge (~12 heroes to circle a big trunk); the November Candlelight Walk as the open-world gathering; a Hyperion lesson — the map marks a closed zone, and the badge is earned by *not* going in.
- **Rules of the park** (Leave No Trace, as gameplay): 25 yards from elk, more in the rut and calving seasons; food in lockers or the car (bears); stay on trails and boardwalks in the old growth (the Grove of Titans boardwalk exists because of trampling); Hyperion and its area are off-limits; take nothing from the tidepools; never turn your back on the ocean; after 20 seconds of shaking, head uphill (tsunami); obey the Davison Road and Tall Trees permits; no pets on trails, no drones.
- **Implementation challenges**: 100 m trees in a dense canopy (camera occlusion, light shafts, drifting fog); a fog weather state as the default mood; surf, tides and tidepools that appear at low water; a wading canyon with fern walls; river kayaking; elk herds with seasonal behaviour; a long, thin map to stream; a patchwork of park, state-park and Yurok land (the reservation cuts the park in two at the Klamath); a closed area that must never be revealed.
